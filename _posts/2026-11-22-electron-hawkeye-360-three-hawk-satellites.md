Rocket Lab launched a dedicated Electron mission for HawkEye 360 on October 23, flying three of the Virginia company's "Hawk" radio-frequency (RF) sensing satellites to low Earth orbit from its New Zealand site. The mission was named "We're Not Hawking Around." This article is a catch-up on a launch that had not yet appeared on SpaceSlop.

Space.com puts liftoff at 0711 GMT on October 23 (8:11 p.m. local time in New Zealand), though one image caption in the same article says October 21, so the exact date is slightly inconsistent in that source. The satellites were headed for an orbit of about 520 kilometres. Space.com's report framed deployment as planned and did not itself confirm commissioning.

## Why it matters

HawkEye 360 operates its satellites in clusters of three, which let it geolocate emitters by comparing the signals each one hears. Per Rocket Lab, the data feeds maritime tracking, GNSS jamming detection, radar monitoring, communications mapping and space defense work. The company launched its first satellites in 2018 and has now sent close to 50 to low Earth orbit, according to Space.com, selling data to the U.S. government and allies.

The flight was also notable for how the company is launching. Space.com notes that another three Hawk satellites went up two days earlier on SpaceX's Bandwagon-5 rideshare, so HawkEye 360 is mixing dedicated small-launcher flights with rideshare slots to build out coverage.

## Electron's tally

For Rocket Lab it was the 98th launch overall and the 19th of 2026. Of this year's flights, 16 used Electron and three used HASTE, the suborbital Electron variant used for hypersonic testing. The company reached its 100th Electron flight on [November 14](/2026/11/14/rocket-lab-100th-electron/), a few weeks after this one.
