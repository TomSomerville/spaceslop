Dcubed, a German maker of satellite components, has closed a €27.2 million (about $30 million) Series A extension, with the money earmarked for a roughly fourfold increase in production capacity, according to Payload. The round comes on top of a €4.4 million Series A that closed in August 2024.

Warsaw Equity Group led the round. The European Innovation Council, HTGF Opportunity Fund, BayBG and Expansion Aerospace Ventures also took part. CEO Thomas Sinn said the company is open to a US investor joining the round, a nod to the American defense market it wants to serve more closely.

## Where the money goes

Dcubed will expand its plant in Germany and open a new production facility in Colorado, with the goal of quadrupling output of its actuators and solar arrays. Payload reports the company more than doubled both its production rate and its revenue over the past year.

The pitch is simple: satellite power hardware is in short supply. Proposed constellations, including orbital data centers and power-beaming concepts, all need far more watts than today's suppliers can easily deliver, and supply is lagging demand. Asked why Dcubed wins business, Sinn said, "we're the fastest."

## A new product line and an in-orbit demo

Part of the funding goes to ARAQYS, a new family of advanced solar arrays and satellite power systems. The first ARAQYS demonstration mission is slated for mid-next year and will fly HALO, a 2 kW radial solar array. Dcubed says HALO cuts cost per watt by one to two orders of magnitude.

HALO will also deploy a 10-meter boom made through in-space manufacturing. If that works, it points toward solar arrays built on orbit rather than folded into a launch fairing, a route that could loosen the size limits imposed by rocket payload bays.

The claims on cost per watt come from the company and have not been independently verified. The demo flight next year will be the first real test of whether the design delivers.
