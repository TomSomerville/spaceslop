Intuitive Machines said on November 9 that NASA has selected it for Commercial Lunar Payload Services 2, the agency's next-generation lunar delivery contract. It follows [ispace-U.S.'s selection](/2026/11/08/ispace-us-clps-2-selection/) a day earlier.

CLPS 2 is a multiple-award, indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract: NASA qualifies a pool of companies, then places individual task orders with them. According to Intuitive Machines, the combined ceiling across all awardees is $10 billion, rising to $15 billion if NASA exercises a five-year option. The ordering period is about 10 years, with a potential five-year extension.

## A broader scope than CLPS 1

The release says the work can include complete payload delivery missions, direct lander acquisitions, funded technology and payload development, orbital delivery, launch rideshares, surface mobility, precision landing, and lunar-night survival and payload operations. NASA wants the contract to cover Artemis and Moon Base needs such as transportation, mobility, power, habitats and logistics, so it reaches beyond the single-delivery model of the original CLPS.

CEO Steve Altemus said the broader scope "builds on our existing leadership in space infrastructure."

## Where Intuitive Machines stands

The release doesn't describe specific lander plans under CLPS 2. It points to earlier 2026 awards: a $180.4 million contract in March for the cargo-class Nova-D lander, and an award of up to $148.3 million in June for a production line-qualified Nova-C lander. The company counts six prior CLPS task orders, two of them in 2026.

The release names no other awardees, so the size of the pool is not clear from this source. A selection onto an IDIQ pool carries no guaranteed revenue; the ceiling is shared, and the money only flows through task orders that companies win. The value of the selection is the right to bid for them, and, for a company that already has a flight history, a seat at the table as NASA shifts toward its Moon Base approach.
