Elon Musk says SpaceX has completed the design of a small rooftop cellular base station meant to close the gap that satellite-to-phone service still struggles with: getting signal indoors.

Speaking at the DealBook Summit this week, Musk described the device as "a micro cell tower thing that you just put on your roof, like a Starlink dish," according to TechCrunch. The target is buildings with metal or thick roofs, where signals from orbit have a hard time penetrating. He said the units should go into production "next year," which TechCrunch reads as 2027, and floated bundling the small base stations with Starlink terminals.

## Why it matters

Direct-to-cell service lets ordinary phones talk to satellites, but the physics is unkind inside buildings. A small local base station that backhauls over Starlink would sidestep that, and it fits a position Gwynne Shotwell laid out in August: SpaceX would not build large cell towers and would instead deploy small, low-power base stations.

Musk also said SpaceX's next-generation Direct-to-Cell V2 constellation is set to launch next year and will raise bandwidth 40-fold. Both claims come from Musk himself and have no independent confirmation yet. TechCrunch notes he has a history of missing production deadlines.

## The spectrum behind it

SpaceX won FCC approval in 2024 for a direct-to-phone Starlink service offered with T-Mobile. It struck a $17 billion deal with EchoStar in 2025 and this year agreed to acquire low-band spectrum valued at $8 billion. Owning spectrum rather than leasing it is what would let SpaceX operate as something closer to a carrier than a wholesale partner.

Musk said he does not expect AT&T, Verizon or other carriers to go out of business, though he suggested they may lose some revenue. He expects bandwidth demand to keep growing, driven mainly by AI and robots.
