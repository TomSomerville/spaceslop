WalkerInSpace, a South Korean startup that wants to refuel and repair satellites in orbit, held a groundbreaking on 10 November for its own satellite assembly, integration and testing (AIT) centre in Sejong City, according to Money Today. The company is putting up 13 billion won, roughly $9.4 million, for the site.

The building will sit on 2,160 square metres inside the Public Facilities Complex in Janggun-myeon. It has one basement level and four floors above ground, with a footprint of about 857 square metres and a total floor area of 3,398 square metres. Completion is targeted for August 2027.

Inside will be a cleanroom for assembling and testing medium and large satellites, a space robotics lab, a space environment lab with thermal vacuum chambers and vibration testers, and a microgravity simulation system for rendezvous, docking and capture trials.

## Why build rather than rent

The company says outsourcing this work is hard for a vehicle like its own, and that hauling equipment around and renting third-party facilities adds cost. Owning the test chain is a familiar move for in-space servicing startups, whose spacecraft are one-off robotic machines that don't fit neatly into the test lines built for conventional satellite buses.

WalkerInSpace was founded in 2024 and is headquartered in Jinju, South Gyeongsang Province. Its first spacecraft, VEROS-1, is a multifunctional robotic satellite meant to dock with low-fuel satellites and help hold their orbits. Space environment testing is planned for 2028 and launch for 2029, per the report. CEO Kim Hae-dong said VEROS-1 is being designed to stay in orbit for more than seven years after launch, and described the goal as acting as a repair shop and refuelling station in space. Later plans include precision repairs and assembling space structures.

Local politicians turned out for the ceremony. Sejong's vice mayor for economic affairs, Park Seong-su, called the single-satellite, multi-task approach highly creative, and two Sejong lawmakers said the centre could help build a local space industry.

The 2029 launch date is the point to watch: servicing startups have a history of slipping, and a facility that only completes in late 2027 leaves about a year to build and test a flight vehicle before environmental testing.
