Mitsubishi Electric has signed a contract to fly a small demonstration satellite on the second test flight of PLD Space's Miura 5 rocket, the Spanish company announced on 18 November.

The satellite is meant to support Mitsubishi's plans for a large satellite constellation. PLD Space described the deal as a new stage in its relationship with the Japanese manufacturer, which is also its largest backer.

## An investor becomes a customer

Mitsubishi Electric put €50 million into PLD Space in March and led the company's €180 million Series C round, a deal that also gave it priority access to Miura 5 launch services. On 1 September it led a €108 million extension, taking the round to €288 million. The launch contract turns some of that financial interest into a manifest slot.

## No date, and a rocket that hasn't flown

The slot is on flight two, and flight two has no date. PLD Space says the schedule depends on how the inaugural flight goes. That first launch, from the Guiana Space Centre in French Guiana, was pushed on 19 October from late 2026 to the first half of 2027.

So the customer is committing to a rocket that has not yet left the pad. Booking a small demonstrator on an early flight is a sensible way to do that: the payload is cheap, and a failure costs less than losing a production satellite. For PLD Space, an anchor customer on the second flight gives investors and prospective European buyers a sign that its first big backer is staying close.
