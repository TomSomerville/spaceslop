Pixxel, the Bengaluru hyperspectral imaging company, has chosen its home city for its next and much larger satellite factory. The Karnataka government said in a statement on Tuesday that Pixxel will invest Rs 350 crore in a 250,000 sq ft facility, according to ThePrint.

The company applied for six acres of land on November 3, and the state's single window clearance committee approved the proposal within two weeks. Indian Startup News reports the plant, called GigaPixxel, will sit near Kempegowda International Airport on the city's outskirts.

## Scale

The new building is roughly eight times the size of MegaPixxel, Pixxel's existing factory. Indian Startup News says GigaPixxel is meant to build about 50 satellites in parallel at first, scaling to 100 at once. An inauguration in March or April 2027 was initially reported by Moneycontrol; ThePrint gives no completion date. Pixxel's first Honeybee satellite is also due to launch in 2027, per the same outlet.

Pixxel closed a $100 million Series C on September 7, co-led by Temasek and Seraphim, bringing total funding to $195 million. The factory's cost was not tied to that round in either report.

## The land fight

Karnataka was not the only candidate. Andhra Pradesh offered a site in Anantapur and Tamil Nadu suggested Hosur, with Andhra IT minister Nara Lokesh pitching it as a place where founders can spend their time building satellites rather than hunting for land. Land near Bengaluru had been a sore point: in June, co-founder and CTO Kshitij Khandelwal publicly said finding it was harder than building satellites, then deleted the post.

Karnataka's industries minister M.B. Patil said the state was delighted Pixxel chose it for its next phase of expansion. Pixxel founder and CEO Awais Ahmed said the investment "gives us the scale to turn that vision into planetary infrastructure."

## Why it matters

Indian states are now competing openly for space manufacturing. Karnataka's SpaceTech Policy 2025-2030 targets a $22 billion state space sector by 2033, and keeping Pixxel adds a large private plant next to ISRO's headquarters. For Pixxel, the plant is a bet that demand for hyperspectral data will justify building constellations at industrial pace.
