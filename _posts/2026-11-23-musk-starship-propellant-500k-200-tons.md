Elon Musk wrote on X on November 16 that "propellant cost per flight will drop below $500k and payload will exceed 200 tons" for Starship, according to TeslaNorth and New Space Economy. The post came during a discussion of how recovering both the ship and the Super Heavy booster changes launch economics.

The claim is narrower than it sounds. It covers propellant only, not total operating cost, and certainly not what a customer would pay. Musk gave no date, and did not say which Starship version he meant. New Space Economy notes the post does not explicitly tie either figure to the planned V4 vehicle, so the numbers read as long-term goals rather than a specification.

## The V4 context

The post followed a separate Musk update on Starship V4 a day earlier. As described by both outlets, V4's upper stage would be about 70 metres long, roughly 40% longer than today's ship, with about 60% more propellant, nine engines (three more Raptor Vacuum engines than now) and higher-thrust engines. Those percentages are Musk's own approximate comparisons; absolute propellant load, empty mass and a flight schedule were not provided. The V4 update did not include a payload number.

The "200 tons" figure is also undefined. New Space Economy assumes metric tonnes and calculates that, at full capacity and the stated propellant cost, propellant alone would work out to roughly $2.50 per kilogram delivered. That is a back-of-envelope illustration, not a price anyone has quoted.

## Why it matters

Starship's economics depend on full reuse, and SpaceX has just demonstrated the first step: our earlier coverage of [Flight 15](/2026/11/14/starship-ship-catch-flight-15/) reported the first catch of a Starship upper stage, though the booster was lost. Propellant is a small slice of a mature reusable rocket's cost compared with hardware refurbishment, ground operations and launch cadence, which is why a propellant target says little by itself about the price per flight.

Musk has made similar cost claims before, and none has yet been tested against an operating, fully reusable Starship. For now the post is a statement of intent from the company's chief executive, with the vehicle that would deliver it still in development.
