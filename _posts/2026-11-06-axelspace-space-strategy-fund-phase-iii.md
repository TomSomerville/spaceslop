Axelspace said on 6 November that it has been selected for Phase III of JAXA's Space Strategy Fund, under a theme covering low-cost, high-frequency on-orbit demonstration technology. The project is titled "Establishment of an Innovative On-Orbit Demonstration Platform to Accelerate the Commercialization of Customers' Components".

The plan is to develop a new versatile satellite bus plus a service platform that shortens the time from contract to launch and allows more demonstration flights. Axelspace says the problem it is attacking is familiar: companies entering the space component market lack flight heritage, yet demonstration opportunities are scarce and costly, launch options in Japan are limited and preparation takes a long time.

The company will build on its experience with RAPIS-1, a small demonstration satellite, and AxelLiner Laboratory, a service launched in 2024. Stated goals are lower service costs, shorter lead times, support for more launch vehicles and regular demonstration slots. The first step is verifying the new bus's basic functions and in-orbit operation, before moving toward commercial service.

Gakushuin University is a partner and will help revise the Green Spacecraft Standard to version 2.0 and contribute to international rulemaking on space sustainability. Axelspace plans to apply that standard to its own satellite design and operations. Minoru Kurata, head of the company's Spacecraft Technology Division, is principal investigator.

The announcement gives no award amount, launch date or flight schedule.
