China launched another Yaogan remote sensing satellite on Friday, putting Yaogan-50-04 into a retrograde low Earth orbit with a Long March 6A from Launch Complex 9A at the Taiyuan Satellite Launch Center in Shanxi province.

According to China in Space, the rocket lifted off at 13:05 UTC (21:05 China Standard Time). The vehicle was designated Long March 6A Y31, the 29th flight of the 6A and the 31st in the combined 6A/6C series. The satellite was built by the China Academy of Space Technology. The Yaogan-50 series is described as serving land surveying, crop yield estimation, and disaster prevention and mitigation.

## The numbers

China in Space counts the flight as the 286th Long March from the Shanghai Academy of Spaceflight Technology, the 682nd Long March launch overall and the 86th Chinese launch of 2026. Yaogan-50-01, -02 and -03 flew earlier this year, so this is the fourth satellite in the group. A retrograde orbit runs against Earth's rotation.

The Long March 6A is a roughly 52-metre, two-and-a-half-stage vehicle with about 530 tonnes of propellant on the pad and a stated capacity of 8,000 kilograms to low Earth orbit. The report notes that the rocket was heated to cope with Taiyuan's cold weather.
