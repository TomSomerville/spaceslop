South Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport gave final approval on November 25 to the plan for a national industrial complex dedicated to space launch vehicles in Goheung County, the area that hosts the country's existing launch site. The decision was published the same day, according to Financial News.

The park covers roughly 1.52 million square meters in Bongnae-myeon and carries a stated total project cost of 380 billion won. Financial News reports it will be split in two: one zone for companies building liquid-propellant rockets, and a second for facilities of the Agency for Defense Development (ADD) that make and research solid-propellant launch vehicles.

Eleven space companies have signed move-in agreements, the Gwangju city government says, including Hanwha Aerospace and Innospace. The developers, state housing corporation LH and the Jeonnam Development Corporation, plan to start compensating landowners in 2027 and begin construction in 2028.

## A long road to approval

Goheung was named a candidate site in March 2023 and received a preliminary feasibility study exemption in June 2024. It cleared central investment review and the industrial complex planning committee in October 2026. Some local reporting flags headwinds: figures differ between outlets, with one Korean report citing an estimated 172.9 billion won in the official notice, and the same report notes that about 83 percent of the land is privately owned, which could slow compensation, and that a water treatment plant serving the site needs to be rebuilt. We could only confirm the 380 billion won figure and the schedule from one outlet's full text, so treat the cost as reported rather than settled.

Gwangju Deputy Mayor Kang Myeong-su called the approval an important foothold for the region to become a core hub of Korea's launch vehicle industry. The city also plans a science complex, a space industry promotion agency, a space expressway linking Goheung and Gwangju, and a road widening, and it expects an announcement on a second space center next month.

## Why it matters

Korea has been building out private launch capability, from Hanwha's work on the Nuri rocket line to Innospace's small launcher, and manufacturers have lacked a dedicated cluster near the pad. Co-locating liquid and solid propulsion work next to the launch site is the same logic behind industrial parks around other national spaceports. The 2028 construction start means the practical benefit is several years away.
