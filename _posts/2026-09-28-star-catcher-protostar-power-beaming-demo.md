Star Catcher announced on September 28 that its Protostar mission is being prepared for launch. The company says the mission will attempt a power-beaming demonstration between two untethered spacecraft, which it describes as something that has never been achieved.

Protostar carries a prototype power node. It is set to fly on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission, launching from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. Star Catcher's page lists the launch for October 2026 and was last dated October 27, so the schedule may have moved; SpaceSlop did not independently confirm the Transporter-18 date.

## The idea

Star Catcher's business is selling power in orbit: a node collects energy and beams it to customer satellites, which could shrink the solar arrays and batteries those satellites carry. In August the company signed a power purchase agreement with Aethero, which wants to run advanced computing in orbit, according to the company's updates page and SpaceNews coverage listed there.

The page offers no partners, quotes or performance figures for the Protostar demo, so it is not clear what power level or distance it will try. That matters: a proof that energy can move between two free-flying spacecraft is a milestone, but the commercial case depends on delivering useful watts at useful ranges.

## What to watch

Success on Protostar would be the first real data point for the power-beaming sector, which also includes other startups and government-backed efforts. A failure or partial result would show how much work remains in pointing, tracking and conversion efficiency. Either way, the answer should come soon after launch.
