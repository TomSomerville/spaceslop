China's Shenzhou-24 mission will carry a Pakistani astronaut to the Tiangong space station, making them the first foreign visitor to the Chinese outpost. Chinese state media reported on 17 November that the spacecraft and its Long March 2F rocket have been moved to the launch area, according to headlines in our feeds; Chinese authorities have not announced a launch date, and Space.com says the flight is expected before the end of November.

## The crew

Two Chinese astronauts and one Pakistani payload specialist will fly. Space.com reports that a second Pakistani candidate is training as backup. Pakistani press named the candidates as Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud, both military pilots, but it isn't yet known which of them will fly. The launch will be from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in the Gobi Desert.

The visitor's stay is short. They are due to spend about a week aboard during the normal crew handover, then return to Earth on Shenzhou-23 together with the departing crew. Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO says the astronaut will be responsible for four research payloads picked from more than 300 proposals. The Chinese crew on Shenzhou-24 will stay about six months, while the Shenzhou-23 crew is nearing 180 days in orbit, per Space.com.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said that having the Pakistani astronaut on board China's spaceships will mark the first-ever joint mission.

## Why it matters

Tiangong is China's answer to the ISS, and until now every person aboard has been Chinese. A foreign guest turns the station into a diplomatic tool as well as a research platform, and Pakistan is a close partner of Beijing. It also gives China a concrete example to point to as the ISS approaches the end of its planned life and countries look for places to fly their astronauts.

Details are still thin. The launch date, the identity of the astronaut and the precise length of the visit have not been confirmed by Chinese authorities in the material we read, so expect updates once the date is announced.
