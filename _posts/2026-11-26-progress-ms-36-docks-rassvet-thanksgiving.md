The final Russian cargo ship of 2026 has arrived at the International Space Station. According to NASA's station blog, the Roscosmos Progress 97 spacecraft, flown as Progress MS-36, docked to the Earth-facing port of the Rassvet module at 7:08 a.m. EST on Thursday, Nov. 26. NASA had projected the docking for about 7:11 a.m., so the ship was a few minutes early. The approach and capture were automatic.

## A different port than expected

Our [earlier article on the launch](/2026/11/24/progress-ms-36-launches-baikonur/) relayed RussianSpaceWeb's plan to dock the ship at the zenith port of the Poisk module, with a later relocation to the aft port of Zvezda's transfer chamber. NASA's account of the docking names Rassvet's Earth-facing port instead. The NASA post does not say whether the relocation plan still stands, so the ship's onward moves remain unconfirmed.

The ship lifted off on a Soyuz rocket from Baikonur, Kazakhstan, at 5:17 a.m. EST on Nov. 24, according to NASA. It carries roughly three tons of food, fuel and supplies for the Expedition 75 crew. That figure is NASA's total cargo mass; the earlier RussianSpaceWeb-based article cited about 2,500 kilograms of deliverable payload, and the two numbers may count different things.

## Why the port matters

Progress ships are the station's workhorse for Russian-segment logistics and, from the aft port of Zvezda, for orbit-raising burns. Which port a ship occupies decides whether it can unload cargo, whether it blocks other vehicles, and whether it can be used for reboosts. The earlier reporting said that the hatch to Zvezda's transfer chamber has been closed over an air leak, which is why Russia has been spreading cargo and reboost duties across different ports.

The docking also lands on a US holiday, which NASA's blog noted by its headline. For the crew, it means fresh supplies arriving as the station settles into the last month of the year.
