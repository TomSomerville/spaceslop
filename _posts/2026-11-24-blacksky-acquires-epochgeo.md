BlackSky Technology announced on November 24 that it has acquired EpochGeo, a geospatial data science company founded in 2018. The release does not give a purchase price, a closing date or how the deal is financed.

EpochGeo builds AI and machine-learning tools that fuse several kinds of data, including radio-frequency (RF) and signals intelligence, for defense work. BlackSky says it plans to fold those capabilities into its large data contracts and mission programs, and to sell them through space-based intelligence and AI subscriptions or as sovereign mission solutions for governments that want their own tailored systems.

The acquisition also brings customers. EpochGeo works with U.S. combatant commands and international defense ministries, and BlackSky says it will keep supporting EpochGeo's existing U.S. government and defense prime contractor customers under their current contracts.

BlackSky CEO Brian O'Toole said EpochGeo brings "exceptional technology, world-class expertise and customer mission experience." EpochGeo CEO Lou Paladino said the company can now combine its capabilities with "industry-leading space-based intelligence."

## What it signals

BlackSky is best known for operating a constellation of imaging satellites that revisit sites frequently. The deal pushes it further into the analytics layer, where imagery is combined with RF and other feeds to produce finished intelligence rather than raw pictures. That layer is where many Earth-observation companies are trying to differentiate as imagery itself becomes easier to buy.

A filing detail in the release shows the scale of the team joining: BlackSky plans inducement stock awards covering up to 565,000 shares for up to 43 new non-executive employees from EpochGeo. Those awards fall under a 2026 inducement plan the board approved on October 29 and vest over several years, starting with one-fourth on December 10, 2027.
