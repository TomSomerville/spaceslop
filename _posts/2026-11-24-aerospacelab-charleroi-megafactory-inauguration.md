Belgian satellite maker Aerospacelab is inaugurating its "Megafactory" in the Charleroi area on Tuesday, 24 November, in a ceremony led by King Philippe, according to Italian wire service Teleborsa. The plant, in Marcinelle south of Brussels, is described as the largest satellite production site in Europe and the third largest in the world, with capacity for up to 500 satellites a year.

The report, dated 19 November, calls the event an inauguration still ahead at the time of writing. We did not find coverage confirming how the ceremony went, so treat the opening itself as scheduled rather than confirmed.

## Why it matters

Most European satellite manufacturing still works on a small-batch model, with a handful of large spacecraft built per year per prime contractor. A line designed for 500 units a year, roughly two a day at full rate, is a bet on the constellation business: mass-produced small satellites for communications and Earth observation rather than bespoke buses.

Teleborsa ties the factory to a larger pipeline. It cites a contract estimated at 2.4 billion euros covering 264 satellites for a future European secure communications network, which the plant is expected to help build. The article gives no figure for the factory's cost or floor area, and we have not independently verified the contract details, so the link between that programme and the plant is as reported by Teleborsa.

## Context

Aerospacelab announced the megafactory plan in 2022 and broke ground in 2024. Earlier reports had pointed to a faster timeline, with production expected to start around 2025, so the late-2026 opening reflects schedule slippage typical of new factories. Capacity on paper is not output: the real test is whether orders arrive at a rate that keeps a 500-a-year line busy.

Europe has been trying to build domestic satellite production capacity that can compete with US constellation manufacturers on volume and price, and a plant of this scale gives Belgium a visible piece of that effort.
