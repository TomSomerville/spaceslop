SpaceX has launched USSF-317, the classified U.S. Space Force mission we previewed [earlier](/), from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. Space.com reports liftoff came at 8:20 p.m. Pacific on November 20 (0420 GMT on November 21), and that it was the 121st Falcon 9 launch of 2026.

## What happened

The booster, B1100, flew for the 11th time and landed on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You* in the Pacific about eight and a half minutes after liftoff. Its earlier missions were NROL-105, USSF-385 and eight Starlink flights, so it is a repeat national-security flyer.

SpaceX ended its webcast right after booster touchdown, which means the upper stage's ascent and any payload deployment were not shown. Space.com says it is unclear what exactly went up.

## The open question

Neither the Space Force nor SpaceX has described the payload. Some outside experts, according to Space.com, suspect a batch of SpaceX Starshield satellites for low Earth orbit, supporting the Space Force's Space Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio. That is informed speculation, not confirmation.

Our earlier preview, citing Spaceflight Now, noted that previous missions in this run of Space Force launches each led to 23 catalogued objects. Whether USSF-317 follows the same pattern will show up in public tracking data in the coming days; until then, treat the payload as unknown.

The takeaway is the cadence: a booster on its 11th flight carrying a classified payload, with the broadcast trimmed to the landing, has become a routine way for Falcon 9 to serve national security customers.
