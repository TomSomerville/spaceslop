Puxing Aerospace (谱星航天), a Chinese satellite startup founded about a year ago, has completed two financing rounds in quick succession, a Pre-A+ and an A round, together worth nearly 300 million yuan. 36Kr reported the deal on November 9. The company's post-money valuation is described only as being in the "billions of yuan" range.

## Who put in the money

Existing shareholders Jinpu Intelligent and Lianrong Zhidao followed on in the Pre-A+ round, with Xinding Capital also joining. The A round was co-led by Changfa Fund, Minjintou and Changxing Fund, with Guizhou High-Tech Fund and Chongshan Capital participating. Puxing has now closed five rounds in a year, for cumulative funding of nearly 600 million yuan.

## What it is building

The company makes optical payloads, micro and nano satellites, and offers technical services around them. Its long-term goal is a constellation of 1,024 hyperspectral "quantitative" satellites. The new money is earmarked for hiring, R&D, and manufacturing and rapid deployment of the first orbital plane, which would hold 128 satellites.

Hardware is already moving. According to the report, the company's Changchun satellite factory is operating, with 16 satellites and more than 50 payloads in development. A science satellite for a customer is expected to launch in the first quarter of next year, and two of Puxing's own satellites are slated for April. The company plans to have more than 10 satellites in orbit by the end of 2027.

## Business picture

Puxing says it has signed contracts worth several hundred million yuan and expects to recognize revenue in the tens of millions of yuan in 2026. Headcount has grown from 20 to more than 70 this year, with nearly a third working on AI and data.

The deal fits a broader pattern in Chinese commercial space financing, where Earth-observation and satellite-manufacturing startups are raising multiple rounds within months.
