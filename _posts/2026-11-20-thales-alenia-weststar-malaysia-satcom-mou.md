Thales Alenia Space, the Thales–Leonardo satellite joint venture, has signed a memorandum of understanding with Weststar Aviation Services to explore secure satellite communications for Malaysia. The signing took place in Kuala Lumpur and was reported on November 18 and 19.

The space MoU was one of two agreements between Thales and the Weststar Group. The second covers air defence, counter-drone systems and cybersecurity, and is outside this site's beat. Malaysia's Defence Minister, Mohamed Khaled Nordin, and France's Minister for the Armed Forces, Catherine Vautrin, presided over the ceremony.

## What the space MoU covers

The memorandum is a framework for cooperation, not a contract. The two companies plan to assess satellite mission requirements with Malaysian stakeholders and then look at solutions that fit national priorities. Thales Alenia Space has proposed a telecommunications satellite platform with dual-use capability: it would serve national security and space autonomy goals while also offering civil telecom services. Asia Pacific Defence Reporter adds that the work could extend to joint business development in Earth observation, secure space systems and the associated ground segments.

Neither report gives a contract value, a satellite specification or a timeline. As these things go, an MoU of this kind is the opening step of a sales process. Whether it turns into a satellite order depends on Malaysian government funding and procurement decisions that have not been announced.

## Why it matters

The agreements are framed around Malaysia's New Defence Industry Policy, launched in 2026, which stresses sovereign capability. Government-grade communications satellites, built or at least controlled in-country, are a familiar pitch from European manufacturers to mid-sized space customers who want to avoid depending on foreign operators.

Weststar is a local partner with existing ties to Thales. The Asia Pacific Defence Reporter piece notes ongoing work on GM400α air surveillance radars for the Royal Malaysian Air Force with Global Komited, a Weststar Group subsidiary. Thales says it has been present in Malaysia for 45 years.

Pascale Sourisse, president and CEO of Thales International, said the agreements reinforce "both space resilience and national security." Weststar founder Syed Azman Syed Ibrahim said the company is "proud to deepen its strategic partnership with Thales."

Worth watching: whether a Malaysian agency or ministry funds a formal study, and whether Thales Alenia Space faces competition from other satellite builders if a procurement opens.
