MaiaSpace, the ArianeGroup subsidiary developing the partially reusable Maia rocket, announced on Nov. 25 that it has shipped full-scale mechanical models of the vehicle's first and second stages to French Guiana.

The stages left the company's Vernon facility on Nov. 19 and went to Le Havre. On Nov. 22 they were loaded aboard the *Canopée*, the ship that also carries Ariane 6 stages, for a 10-day crossing to the Pariacabo port in Kourou, according to European Spaceflight.

## What the dummies are for

The models are stand-ins, not flight hardware. They let MaiaSpace validate each step of moving a rocket from mainland France to the Guiana Space Centre, and rehearse operations that will recur in every Maia campaign. Combined testing is planned for the first half of 2027: horizontal integration of the two stages, mounting on a transporter-erector, rolling to the pad and raising the vehicle vertical, connecting and disconnecting electrical and fluid interfaces, and validating ground support systems.

Maia will fly from the former Soyuz launch facility at the spaceport, which MaiaSpace is adapting.

## A schedule that keeps moving

The milestone has slipped. In February 2026, MaiaSpace targeted a vehicle on the pad for combined testing by the end of 2026. In July it dropped a planned suborbital test flight in favor of a direct orbital attempt, then aimed at the second half of 2027. A spokesperson has now told Ouest-France the target is a first orbital flight by the end of 2027, per the report, which notes that a debut could slip into 2028.
