ThrustMe, the French maker of electric thrusters that run on iodine, said that SpaceX's Bandwagon-5 mission, which lifted off from Cape Canaveral on October 20, took the number of satellites launched with its propulsion past 100. The company's post is dated October 22, though its dateline reads October 27.

ThrustMe was founded in 2017 by Ane Aanesland and Dmytro Rafalskyi out of research at CNRS and École polytechnique, and tested its first iodine system in orbit around 2020. Its systems now fly on constellations in Europe, North America and Asia, and some manufacturers install them on production lines. Customers named in the release include OroraTech, GomSpace, Astro Digital, iQPS, PierSight and Dhruva Space. The company says demand exceeds industry production capacity, including its own.

## Regulation is pushing demand

The release ties the milestone to rules that make manoeuvrability mandatory. It cites ESA's figure of more than 4,000 satellites launched in 2025, the US FCC requirement that LEO satellites leave orbit within five years of mission end, France's reserved orbits for manoeuvrable satellites since 2024, and the proposed EU Space Act, which would require satellites above 400 km to be able to manoeuvre and is before the European Parliament and Council.

GomSpace CEO Carsten Drachmann said his company needed a supplier able to deliver in volume and on schedule. Aanesland called the 100-satellite mark "a truly symbolic milestone" showing the sector has entered an industrial era.

Next is the JPT150, billed as the first low-power Hall thruster to run on iodine, with higher thrust and total impulse for larger satellites. It is due to fly for the first time in 2027 on MarbleSat-1, an Earth observation satellite built by Reflex Aerospace for Marble Imaging. All figures here are the company's own.
