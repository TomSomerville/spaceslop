Elon Musk has widened a plan to put SpaceX stock in the hands of American children, saying he will donate another share to every child in the US each year. Search results describing the expansion say the extra pledge came after his original announcement on 25 November; we could not read the underlying posts or the Forbes report directly.

## The original pledge

According to Al Jazeera, Musk wrote on X that every child in America would receive two SpaceX shares, one from the company and one from him. SpaceX said it would give one newly issued Class A common share to each currently active Trump Account, the programme that gives Americans under 18 a stake in the stock market, and that Musk would match the number. The White House said in October that it had opened almost 70 million such accounts. SpaceX did not say whether the gifts are formally tied to those accounts.

At Wednesday's close of $127.44 a share, the pledge was worth nearly $18 billion, Al Jazeera reported. SpaceX said its shares would be transferred next month, while Musk's own would move when his lockup expires in June 2027. In October he had promised to give one of his shares to every US child. SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell and her husband separately pledged a share to each of more than two million children that month.

## Open questions

The announcement came hours after SpaceX won US approval to deploy one million satellites for space data centres, per Al Jazeera. Reports on the new yearly pledge say no eligibility rules, custody arrangement or tax treatment have been published, and it is unclear whether yearly company-issued shares would dilute other holders or whether Musk would cover the cost.

Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera see a marketing benefit: Mitrade Group's Christopher Brankin said it would "build a massive, long-term retail shareholder base." Musk held about 6.42 billion SpaceX shares as of June, according to the same report, so his own gift is small against his stake.
