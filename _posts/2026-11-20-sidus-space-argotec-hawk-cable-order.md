Sidus Space announced on November 19 that it has received a purchase order from Italian satellite maker Argotec to manufacture cable assemblies for Argotec's HAWK satellite platform. Delivery is expected in the first quarter of 2027. The company did not disclose the value of the order.

It is a small contract, but it fits a pattern: Sidus, which is listed on Nasdaq (SIDU) and runs a 35,000-square-foot facility in Cape Canaveral, Florida, is trying to turn the hardware it builds for its own LizzieSat spacecraft into a business of selling to other satellite makers. Founder, CEO and Chairman Carol Craig said the company's flight heritage is "exactly what we now bring to other satellite manufacturers," and argued that fast-growing space companies need suppliers who have already solved these problems on their own spacecraft.

Sidus says it has manufactured aerospace hardware for nearly 15 years, and builds its own cable harnesses, structures and avionics. Its LizzieSat-3 is currently hosting a non-Earth imaging payload for HEO USA, which Sidus says is actively capturing imagery.

## The customer

Argotec is headquartered in Turin, with offices in Germany and the United States, and specializes in high-reliability small satellites and subsystems. Sidus notes that in 2022 it was the only company with two microsatellites operating in deep space, on NASA's DART and Artemis I missions. Its current projects include HEO, part of Italy's IRIDE program, HENON for space weather, and MiniCOR, a NASA instrument to observe coronal mass ejections and the solar wind.

Argotec is also expanding in the US. It opened a production facility in Melbourne, Florida in 2026, with more than $25 million invested, plus a California technology hub focused on telecommunications, and expects to triple its US workforce over the next two years. Sidus says Argotec completed a €50 million capital increase through NUO Space S.p.A. in October 2026.

The details above come from Sidus's own announcement; Argotec had not been quoted on the order in the material available. For a supplier of Sidus's size, a recurring flow of orders from established satellite builders matters more than any single purchase order, and a customer ramping up US production in Florida is a natural neighbor for a Cape Canaveral cable shop.
