True Anomaly said on November 23 that it is establishing a new mission operations facility in Colorado Springs, Colorado, which will become the home of all its mission operations starting in 2027.

The site is about 30,000 square feet and is planned to hold more than 150 employees eventually. According to the company, it includes a showcase operations floor plus mixed-use workspaces for mission engineering and support. The point of the consolidation is to let teams run several missions at once as True Anomaly's mission set grows, and to give customers and partners a place to engage directly with the systems and people flying them.

## Why Colorado Springs

True Anomaly has had a mission operations team in the city since 2023, so this is a larger home for existing work rather than a new location. The company pointed to the area's concentration of national security space activity: Peterson and Schriever Space Force Bases, Cheyenne Mountain Complex and Fort Carson are all nearby.

True Anomaly builds rendezvous and proximity operations spacecraft and focuses on space security. Its Jackal autonomous orbital vehicles are the kind of hardware that needs round-the-clock operators, and a dedicated operations center near its defense customers is a logical next step as the fleet and contract base grow. The announcement did not give a cost, a lease or ownership arrangement, or the exact opening date within 2027.
