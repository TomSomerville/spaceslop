Katalyst Space said on September 25 that its LINK servicing spacecraft has deorbited after 85 days in space, without capturing NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory as planned.

LINK was built to approach Swift, grab it and raise its orbit. Swift was never designed to be serviced and has no standard docking interface, so the robot had to handle a much larger satellite without one. Katalyst won the $30 million NASA contract in September 2025 and launched LINK on July 3, 2026 on a Northrop Grumman Pegasus XL, about nine months after the award.

## What went wrong

During commissioning an electrical power fault left two of three reaction wheels unavailable and put the spacecraft into a multi-axis spin. Engineers wrote new control modes using thrusters and the one remaining wheel, using hall effect thrusters to cut the body rate from 9 degrees per second to 1.47, and restored limited attitude control.

That recovery used propellant quickly. Katalyst and NASA concluded there was too little fuel left for capture and orbit raising, so the mission turned to testing systems and gathering data. LINK got within 12 to 15 kilometers of Swift and took unresolved imagery. It also exercised robotic arm kinematics, lifetime and grapple procedures.

NASA's Shawn Domagal-Goldman called it "a high-risk, high-reward mission." NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said, "This is not the outcome we were working toward," though the attempt was still worth making. Katalyst CEO Ghonhee Lee said "This is a foundation we can build on."

## What comes next

Katalyst says the lessons feed NEXUS, its standard multi-mission production platform. Follow-on work includes a Deorbit-as-a-Service mission selected by the Defense Innovation Unit for the Space Development Agency, and ASGARD, a power-beaming mission with the Naval Research Laboratory under the OECIF program.

The episode is a reminder of how hard on-orbit servicing remains. The company moved from award to launch in nine months, but the margin for a single electrical fault turned out to be thin.
