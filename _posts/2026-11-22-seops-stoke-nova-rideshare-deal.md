SEOPS, a company that books launch capacity and resells it to satellite operators, announced on November 17 that it has signed a multi-launch agreement with Stoke Space for the Nova rocket. It also reserved a third dedicated Falcon 9 mission with SpaceX. Both go into its Waymaker rideshare program.

## What SEOPS bought

Under the Stoke deal, SEOPS plans to fly on Nova Block 2 starting in 2029 and can also use spare capacity on other Nova missions. SEOPS handles mission planning, payload integration and launch execution for its customers. Nova Block 2 is designed to lift 15 metric tons to low Earth orbit and has a five-meter fairing.

Nova has not flown. Stoke is targeting an early-2027 debut of the Pathfinder version from Space Launch Complex 14 at Cape Canaveral, with more Pathfinder flights planned through 2028, according to Aviation Week. SEOPS has not chosen the orbit for its first Nova flight, though it will likely serve mid-inclination customers.

The new Falcon 9 mission is planned for late 2028, also to a mid-inclination orbit. It follows two Falcon 9 Waymaker missions already contracted: the first due in early 2028 to mid-inclination orbits, and another later in 2028 for customers needing Sun-synchronous orbit. SEOPS also cited six launches on Isar Aerospace's Spectrum. Aviation Week reports the Spectrum commitment was made before that rocket's first successful flight and has since been increased.

## Why it matters

SEOPS president Evan Hoyt described the Nova decision to Aviation Week as "an easy decision," saying waiting would have left the company behind the demand curve. He said customers are increasingly willing to back new rockets because of a shortage of launch capacity. Coverage also ties the deal to SpaceX's plan to wind down its Falcon 9 rideshare programs around 2029, which would leave small-satellite operators looking for alternatives.

Betting on a rocket that has not reached orbit carries obvious schedule risk, which SEOPS appears to be hedging by spreading bookings across Falcon 9, Spectrum and Nova, and by exploring more providers. It is also developing a launch cost insurance product intended to make it easier for satellite makers to commit to flights.

The deal follows Planet's own multi-launch agreement for Nova on November 9, covered in [our earlier article](/2026/11/09/planet-stoke-nova-launch-deal/), and adds to the list of customers committing to Stoke before first flight.
