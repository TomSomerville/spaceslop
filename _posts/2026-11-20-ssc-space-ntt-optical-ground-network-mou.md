SSC Space and Japanese telecom group NTT have signed a memorandum of understanding to work together on optical communications between space and the ground, Via Satellite reported on Friday. No money, station counts or deadlines were disclosed, so this is an agreement to explore rather than a contract to build.

## What the two sides bring

Under the MoU, SSC Space would contribute its global ground station infrastructure and operating experience. NTT would supply its optical communications and network control technologies. Early areas of work include making NTT's technology interoperable with SSC's optical ground station network and service environments, with the stated aim of a seamless optical ground network.

The companies also plan to look at expanding that ground network, adding sites, and developing future models for integrating services.

## Why site diversity matters

SSC Space is building a worldwide network of optical ground stations for satellite data services. Optical links carry far more data than radio but can be blocked by clouds, so operators need many geographically spread stations to keep a link available when weather closes one of them. That is the reason the MoU puts so much emphasis on adding and diversifying sites.

NTT, for its part, is planning an end-to-end optical communications infrastructure. According to the report, that would combine space optical terminals, optical ground stations, terrestrial optical networks, data centers and network control technologies. A partner with ground stations in several regions would fill one of the harder gaps in that chain.

Charlotta Sund, SSC Space's president and CEO, said NTT's "world-leading position and global reach" make it a highly valued partner, and that the company looks forward to exploring how its ground station infrastructure can support NTT's space-to-ground connectivity efforts.

## What to watch

MoUs of this kind often stay at the study stage. The signal to look for is a first joint demonstration or a named station site, neither of which has been announced. For now it shows that operators building laser-based networks are lining up ground partners early, since the ground segment is where weather risk lives.
