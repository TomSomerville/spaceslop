Canada's lunar utility rover program is speeding up. Payload reports that the Canadian Space Agency (CSA) has, at least for now, set aside the four-ton rover it planned for 2034 and asked industry to design a two-ton vehicle that could arrive roughly three to four years earlier.

Three companies, Canadensys, MDA Space and Mission Control, shared C$14.6 million (about US$10.3 million) in July 2025 for a Phase 0 study that is now nearly done. A new request for proposals is expected within days and will cut the field to two companies, who would continue design work in 2027 alongside science-instrument procurements. CSA wants a fully robotic rover about the size of two pickup trucks side by side that fits on a pallet measuring 1.5 by 1 meters.

The push comes from NASA's Moon Base plans, which will need a transport network including large rovers, and CSA is expected to raise rover funding as a result. Mission Control CEO Ewan Reid said that waiting until 2034 risked the technology becoming "obsolete", a conclusion he said Canadian industry reached collectively.

MDA Space, which also builds Canadarm3, originally meant for NASA's Gateway, said lunar mobility could support Moon Base, possibly with repurposed robotics, and that is being studied under a funded task authorization. Canadensys CEO Christian Sallaberger noted his company is part of rover programs for the US and ESA.

Payload adds that Canada canceled a planned mini-rover from Canadensys but is moving ahead with sovereign launch ambitions under its Launch the North initiative, and that NordSpace plans to launch a self-funded Moon rover, TERRY, next year.
