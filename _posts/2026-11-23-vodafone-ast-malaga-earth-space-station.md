Vodafone and AST SpaceMobile have gone live with what they call Europe's first Earth-space station dedicated to research on integrated terrestrial and low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite networks. The Málaga Satellite Centre, announced by Vodafone on 22 November, sits at the University of Málaga's telecommunications engineering school, in cooperation with the university's Mobile & Aerospace Networks Lab (Mobilenet).

## What it is

The facility is a gateway: a 0.9-metre dish that links AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird satellites, which connect directly to ordinary phones, back into a terrestrial mobile network. Spain's Ministry of Digital Transformation and of Public Service authorised Vodafone to use the 700MHz band for the site, and the Spanish Space Agency provided an initial grant. Vodafone says the lab itself was set up in October 2025.

Field activations have already begun in nearby Granada, using an automated system installed with the University of Granada that captures video, data and call logs across the satellite, gateway and terrestrial networks.

## What gets tested

Vodafone lists direct-to-device smartphone broadband, safety inspection robots, connected vehicles, safety drones and crisis communications that move between land and satellite links. It also plans to test public-safety and disaster-relief radio frequencies, including interoperability between terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks. One scenario: simulate a disaster by shutting down the ground networks and see whether devices reselect a satellite channel on their own.

Marco Zangani, Vodafone's director of network strategy and architecture, said "the resilience and ubiquity of coverage is a matter of economic and social importance," and that the facility "enables EU member states to collaborate in developing essential communication systems."

## Context

Vodafone and AST SpaceMobile also run Satellite Connect Europe, a wholesale joint venture that sells the service to mobile operators. Vodafone's announcement gives no satellite count, throughput or user-capacity figures for the Málaga setup, and no date for commercial service, so this is best read as a test bed rather than a launch of a product. Its value to the venture is regulatory and technical groundwork: proving that a satellite layer can slot into a European operator's network under national spectrum authorisations.
