Eutelsat Network Solutions has received a U.S. Space Force order estimated at more than $60 million, according to a report dated 17 November. The order runs two years and falls under the Warrior Plans initiative of the Space Force's Commercial Satellite Communications Office (CSCO).

## What is in it

The German financial site Kapitalmarktexperten reports that the package covers ready-to-use OneWeb terminals, connectivity services, alternative positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) solutions, and technical and operational support for Department of War users. Alternative PNT matters because military users want options that keep working when GPS is jammed or spoofed.

The same report cautions that an estimated contract value is not the same as revenue already booked, and it does not point to a primary Eutelsat press release. I could not find an English-language confirmation of the award, so treat the figure as reported by a single outlet until Eutelsat or the Space Force publishes details.

## Context

OneWeb Technologies, Eutelsat's U.S. arm, already holds a Space Force Proliferated Low Earth Orbit (p-LEO) contract vehicle with a $900 million ceiling, awarded in 2023, which is how commercial LEO services like this are typically bought. The new order is a sizeable, if not enormous, data point for Eutelsat as it pushes its LEO constellation into government markets.

The report also says the first 32 next-generation OneWeb satellites are tentatively scheduled to launch on 30 November on a SpaceX flight from Vandenberg into polar orbit. It attributes that date to Advanced Television and SatNews, and Eutelsat has not officially confirmed it.
