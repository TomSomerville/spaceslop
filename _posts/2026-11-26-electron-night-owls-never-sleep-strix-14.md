Rocket Lab is lining up its 101st Electron launch for the evening of Thanksgiving in the US. The mission, named "Night Owls Never Sleep," will carry one StriX synthetic aperture radar satellite for Japan's Synspective from Launch Complex 1 on the Māhia Peninsula in New Zealand.

The window opens at 00:00 UTC on Nov. 27 (7 p.m. ET on Nov. 26), according to Tech Times, which says two launch trackers list a 00:05 UTC target. Stocktwits reports Synspective is targeting liftoff five minutes after the window opens. As always, weather or vehicle checks could move the time.

## The orbit is the unusual part

The satellite, roughly 100 kg per Tech Times, is bound for a 569 km orbit at 38 degrees inclination. That cannot be sun-synchronous, so rather than polar coverage it adds mid-latitude revisits. Rocket Lab's mission page lists the same inclination, the outlet notes.

## Counting launches

By Tech Times' tally this would be the 22nd Electron of 2026, though it notes neither company has stated that figure. It would be Electron's 14th launch for Synspective. Rocket Lab's count includes the two early prototypes, StriX-α and StriX-β, so it does not match Synspective's own satellite numbering.

Synspective is Electron's largest customer by manifest. Stocktwits puts its bookings at 47 flights, including a 20-launch order announced Sept. 30 for sun-synchronous missions in 2028 through 2031. Tech Times adds that Synspective also signed with Firefly Aerospace on Nov. 10 for up to 10 Alpha launches through 2030, each carrying two StriX satellites. Neither source mentions booster recovery for this flight.

Stocktwits also reports the launch team completed its practice countdown in a record 4 hours 45 minutes. Separately, five Rocket Lab executives sold 76,572 shares for about $3.85 million on Monday at an average of $50.29. The report says the sales covered taxes on vested restricted stock awards and were arranged in advance.
