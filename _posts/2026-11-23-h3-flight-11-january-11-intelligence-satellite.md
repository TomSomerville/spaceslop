Japan has set January 11, 2027 for the launch of H3 Flight 11, which will carry a government information-gathering satellite from Tanegashima Space Center. The launch window runs from 8:00 to 10:00 a.m. Japan time, according to reports of the November 20 announcements.

The payload is the Optical Temporal Diversity Satellite No. 1 (光学時間軸多様化1号機), an Earth-observation satellite operated for the Cabinet Satellite Intelligence Center, the body that runs Japan's intelligence satellite program. The sources read for this article do not give its orbit or mass.

## A change in who flies the rocket

Flight 11 is also the first H3 launch planned under a new arrangement announced the same day. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) has provided H3 launch services since Flight 4, but JAXA has remained the legal "launch operator" under Japan's Space Activities Act. For the H3-22 configuration, which uses two LE-9 Type 1A first-stage engines and two solid rocket boosters, MHI will now hold both the service role and the legal operator role, per the CNET Japan report.

Flight 6, the test flight of a different configuration, is excluded from MHI's service. JAXA plans to move other configurations, and launches using the in-development LE-9 Type 2 engine, to the private sector in stages after consulting stakeholders.

The handover follows a plan reported earlier this month for [Japan to hand H3 operations to Mitsubishi Heavy by 2027](/2026/11/20/japan-h3-launch-operations-mitsubishi-heavy/); the goals reported then and now are lower costs and more commercial satellite orders. The new information is a concrete first mission and date.

Two regional broadcasters' reports give the year as 2026, which appears to be a typo; NHK and the other coverage, along with the official announcements, say January 2027.
