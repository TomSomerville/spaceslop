Romanian InSpace Engineering (RISE) says its OrbFIX GNSS receiver is the first to process operational Galileo High Accuracy Service (HAS) corrections in space, according to a post published Tuesday by the EU Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA).

OrbFIX is a compact, radiation-tolerant receiver for small satellites. It flew on EMISAR, a 1U CubeSat launched on April 30, 2026 on SpaceX's Transporter-16 rideshare mission, and operated in a 520 km sun-synchronous orbit.

## The numbers

The onboard HAS solution reached 0.65 m of 3D accuracy after outlier removal, compared with 1.85 m for standalone GNSS, an improvement of about 65 percent. Post-processing the same raw data under continuous tracking produced an average 3D error of 0.34 m. The satellite's rotation interrupted GNSS tracking and prevented the precise point positioning solution from fully converging, though HAS still improved the result.

Galileo HAS reached Initial Service in 2023 and offers free precise-point-positioning corrections, which on the ground deliver decimetre-level accuracy under nominal conditions. EUSPA lists possible space uses including precise orbit determination, formation flying, rendezvous and close-proximity operations, space surveillance and constellation-wide timing.

## Why it matters

Small satellites usually rely on modest receivers and ground processing for precise orbits. A free, real-time correction service that a CubeSat-class receiver can use onboard could reduce that dependence. RISE says future OrbFIX experiments aim to demonstrate decimetre-level accuracy in orbit, and it will present the work at the User Consultation Platform. The results so far come from the company and EUSPA, not an independent evaluation.
