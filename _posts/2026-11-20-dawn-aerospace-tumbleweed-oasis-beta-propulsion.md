Dawn Aerospace has been selected to supply propulsion for Oasis Beta, the next orbital mission from European microgravity-access company Tumbleweed. The announcement came on 20 November at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, Germany.

Dawn's non-toxic propulsion system will perform the reentry burn that sends the Oasis Beta vehicle back toward Earth, then separate so the research modules can come home and be recovered. The companies also signed a memorandum of understanding to explore reusable propulsion for a future Tumbleweed reentry vehicle.

## A step beyond Oasis Alpha

Oasis Beta follows Oasis Alpha, which launched in July 2026 with four customer payloads. According to Dawn, the new vehicle adds the ability to return physical samples, a capability aimed at biotechnology, pharmaceutical and materials-science customers who need hardware back on the ground rather than data alone.

Dawn says its propulsion currently flies on 59 satellites. The company was founded in 2017 and has more than 150 staff across five countries.

Jeroen Wink, Dawn's chief revenue officer, said propulsion is essential to making new space missions possible. Tumbleweed chief executive Julian Rothenbuchner said making microgravity a true industrial resource means not just getting experiments into orbit, a nod to the return leg that Oasis Beta is meant to add.

## What to watch

The announcement does not give a launch date or contract value. Reentry capsules for in-space manufacturing and research are an increasingly crowded niche, and the propulsion on the way down is one of the less glamorous parts that has to work every time. The reusable-propulsion MoU is exploratory only.
