South Korea's space agency, KASA, says its fourth NewSpace Fund has already gathered about 260 billion won, well above the 200 billion won it set as the 2026 goal. M Economy News reported the figures on 27 November.

Three of the fund's five managers have completed their capital raising, and the remaining two are expected to finish next month, so the total could climb further. The fund is split into five sub-funds: two small, one mid-sized, one large and one global. The government raised its own contribution this year to 100 billion won, with private money making up the rest of the target.

## A much bigger fund than its predecessors

The scale is the story. According to the report, the first three funds, raised between 2023 and 2025, totalled 30.1 billion won. Their cumulative investment in aerospace companies through September was 22.2 billion won: 8.6 billion in 2023, 10.6 billion in 2024 and 3 billion in 2025. Fund 4 is therefore roughly eight times the size of the three earlier ones combined.

Money raised is not money invested. The report does not give a date for the first deals, and the earlier funds deployed capital slowly.

## What comes next

KASA plans a fifth fund of more than 200 billion won in 2027, and the government's 2027 budget proposal includes another 100 billion won in contributions. A new region-focused fund will also start next year, aimed at strategic local industries such as satellite and aircraft manufacturing, with investment concentrated in small and venture companies outside the capital area.

KASA Administrator Oh Tae-seok said an investment ecosystem in which capable firms can secure stable funding is important, and that the government will support the market so that its seed money draws in more private capital.

For Korean launch and satellite startups, which have mostly relied on state contracts and a thin venture market, a fund of this size matters as a source of equity. It also fits the wider push seen in recent weeks, including plans for a [second space center and reusable rockets](/2026/11/23/korea-second-space-center-reusable-rockets/).
