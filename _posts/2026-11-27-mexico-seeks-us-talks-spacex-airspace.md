Mexico's government wants formal talks with the United States over SpaceX's use of Mexican airspace, escalating a dispute that began with Starship debris landing on Mexican territory.

President Claudia Sheinbaum said at her morning news conference on November 26, as reported by Reuters, that Mexico is pushing for a meeting between U.S. and Mexican space authorities and is working through the U.S. embassy to arrange it. The account of her remarks comes from a Korean outlet's summary of the Reuters report; the Reuters text itself was not retrievable this run.

## The agenda

According to that summary, the first question Mexico wants settled is whether SpaceX should be allowed to use Mexican airspace at all. If so, the two sides would set how often launches can take place. Sheinbaum also said moving the launch site could be discussed if necessary. No relocation options were named, and the report gives no meeting date or proposed launch limits. Debris, she said, should be handled in a separate conversation.

## How it got here

Mexico says debris from rockets launched in Texas has fallen on its territory and caused environmental damage, and has also raised concerns about disruption to its airspace. Earlier this month Mexican authorities said SpaceX debris had washed up on a beach in Tamaulipas, a border state. We covered that episode in [an earlier article](/2026/11/24/mexico-presses-spacex-starship-booster-debris/) on the booster debris.

SpaceX said it would send experts to help identify the debris and support any cleanup. It also said independent tests of material from the Starship and Super Heavy booster found no chemical, biological or toxicological risks.

## What to watch

The U.S. embassy referred questions to the State Department, which had not commented, and SpaceX had not responded at the time of reporting. A formal bilateral process over airspace and launch cadence would add a new source of friction for a launch site where SpaceX wants to fly more often. The reports do not say what Mexico could actually enforce if talks fail.
