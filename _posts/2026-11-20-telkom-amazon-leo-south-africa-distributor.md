Amazon's satellite broadband service, Amazon Leo, has picked up a second distributor in South Africa. Telkom announced on 17 November that its Openserve unit signed an agreement making the Telkom Group an authorised Amazon Leo distributor. Leo is the service formerly known as Project Kuiper.

The deal splits across Telkom's three businesses. Openserve will sell Leo services and terminals to enterprise and eligible public-sector customers, directly and through sales agents, and will remain the service provider to the end customer. BCX will bundle the connection with its managed network, cloud and cybersecurity offerings. Telkom Business will pitch it to small businesses in rural and remote areas.

## Backhaul as well as broadband

Telkom also plans to use satellite for backhaul, the links that connect cell sites to the core network, as either a primary or a backup path at fixed cellular sites. Named customer sectors include mining, agriculture, energy, construction, logistics, tourism and public bodies such as health and education. Maximum configured speeds could reach 1 Gbit/s down and 400 Mbit/s up, depending on the enterprise plan and terminal, according to ITWeb.

"We'll use fibre and mobile where they reach, and satellite where they don't," Telkom Group CEO Serame Taukobong said, per both reports.

Telkom gave no pricing and did not say whether any part of the agreement is exclusive, TechCentral reported. It said it will share availability and channel details "closer to launch."

## Context

Herotel, a Maziv company, signed the first South African Leo distribution deal in July, aimed at homes and small businesses under a new brand called evry. Commercial launch is expected in 2027. Where Herotel covers consumers, Telkom's deal leans toward enterprise and infrastructure.

Starlink, by contrast, still cannot launch commercially in South Africa, because it has not obtained the electronic communications licences it needs from the regulator ICASA, ITWeb reported.

The timing is notable for Telkom itself. TechCentral reports that the announcement came a day after the company said it had cut fibre investment by more than a third in the six months to September, and that Telkom is in early-stage talks with MTN over a possible offer for the whole company. Satellite distribution lets Telkom serve remote customers without building fibre to them.
