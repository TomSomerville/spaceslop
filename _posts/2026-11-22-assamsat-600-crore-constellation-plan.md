The Indian state of Assam is scaling up its planned AssamSAT programme into a full satellite constellation, with a proposed investment of about Rs 600 crore and a target of finishing the project by mid-2027, according to NKTV.

Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma announced the plan in a post on X, saying the state wants to order a "full-fledged satellite constellation", per NKTV's paraphrase.

## The business case

NKTV reports that the state currently spends an estimated Rs 1,775 crore over five years buying satellite data from outside providers. Against that, the Rs 600 crore investment is projected to save nearly 66 per cent. That figure is a projection from the project's own numbers, and the report offers no independent check.

## How it will be built

Assam intends to pick an implementation partner under a Design, Build, Launch, Operate, Train and Transfer (DBLOTT) model, meaning the contractor would build and launch the satellites, run them for a period and hand the system over to the state. Eleven Indian space technology companies have expressed interest, and committees are evaluating their proposals. No partner has been selected yet.

The project was first announced in the state's budget, and the state invited expressions of interest earlier this year. The report does not say how many satellites the constellation will include or what orbits they will use. Earlier coverage of the programme has used different cost and design figures, so the scope is still moving.

## Why it matters

A sub-national government commissioning its own constellation, rather than buying imagery, is unusual, and it gives India's private space sector a domestic anchor customer. Whether the mid-2027 schedule holds will depend on how quickly a partner is selected and a launch is booked.
