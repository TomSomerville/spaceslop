OHB System AG signed a contract with the European Space Agency on Tuesday to build ClearSpace-1, the mission meant to find, grab and bring down a defunct satellite that is not cooperating. According to Tech Times, the deal was signed at Space Tech Expo in Bremen and is worth €102 million (about $112 million). Launch has moved out to roughly 2030.

## What the mission does

The target is PROBA-1, an ESA technology satellite launched in 2001 that weighs about 95 kilograms. ESA calls it an "unprepared and uncooperative object", meaning the servicer has no help from the target: no docking fixtures, no guidance data. ESA's mission page describes robotic rendezvous and capture with four arms, followed by a controlled descent to burn up in the atmosphere. It would be the first European mission to capture a non-cooperative object, and it is a single-satellite demonstration rather than a cleanup service.

## How it got here

The project began in 2020 with an €86 million ESA agreement led by Swiss startup ClearSpace, aimed at a different object: the Vega Secondary Payload Adapter, or VESPA, left in orbit after a 2013 flight. In August 2023 the US Space Force's 18th Space Defense Squadron told ESA a probable collision had occurred near the adapter. In April 2024 ESA's Space Safety Programme Board approved a new phase with OHB leading the consortium, and ClearSpace announced PROBA-1 as the replacement target. Tech Times quotes ClearSpace's Luc Piguet as saying the spin from the collision made capture harder.

The two contract values are not directly comparable, the outlet notes, since the lead, target and design have all changed. OHB describes the work as a comprehensive redesign.

## Open questions

Published details disagree in places. ESA's own mission page still lists a 2029 launch, while a July 2026 ESA slide deck cited by Tech Times gives the second quarter of 2030, and OHB and Aviation Week say 2030. No launch vehicle has been named; 2024 design work assumed direct injection about 10 kilometers below PROBA-1's orbit, with a possible rideshare on Vega-C or Ariane 6.

The partner list is also inconsistent. OHB's release names ClearSpace, Deimos Engenharia and OHB Sweden, with a letter of intent signed, while an ESA deck lists INDRA Deimos for guidance. OHB has not said how the money is split.

## Why it matters

Capture technology is dual use, as Tech Times points out, citing China's Shijian-21 towing a dead satellite in 2022 and a UK contract worth £15 million to Astroscale for a counter-satellite spacecraft. For ESA, the slip from the original 2025 target to 2030 leaves Europe with a demonstration that is years behind its first schedule, and the work on the grab itself still lies ahead.
