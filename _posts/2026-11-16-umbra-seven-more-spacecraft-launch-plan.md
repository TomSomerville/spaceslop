Umbra, the Santa Barbara radar-imaging company, says it will launch seven more spacecraft starting in 2027, giving it a defined launch schedule through 2028. The company announced the plan on November 16, 2026, alongside first-image results from its two newest satellites.

## What has flown, and what is next

Umbra-12, part of the company's third-generation platform, deployed in early 2026. Umbra-13 and Umbra-14 followed on October 20, 2026 aboard SpaceX's Bandwagon-5 rideshare. Umbra-14 returned its first high-resolution synthetic aperture radar (SAR) image on October 24, and Umbra-13 delivered its first on October 28, according to the release. SAR satellites image with radio waves, so they can see through cloud and at night.

The new manifest has two parts:

- **Bandwagon-6 (SpaceX), no earlier than April 2027:** Umbra-15, -16, -17 and -18.
- **SEOPS Waymaker-1 dedicated rideshare, no earlier than April 2028:** Umbra-19 and -20, plus one additional spacecraft supporting a private demonstration for what the company calls a strategic industry partner.

All seven will go to mid-inclination orbits. The release says the expansion is meant to serve U.S. defense, national security and intelligence needs along with missions worldwide.

## Why it matters

Commercial radar imaging has become a crowded market with government demand behind it, and capacity in orbit is what customers buy. Committing to rideshare launches two years out gives Umbra predictability that matters to defense buyers, who want to know how many satellites will be revisiting their areas of interest. The company has recently been in the news for U.S. government work; its own news page lists a NOAA commercial SAR data selection and a sole-source National Reconnaissance Office radar contract, though this article relies only on the November release.

The release does not say how many satellites Umbra operates today, nor does it give prices or contract values for the launches. The April 2028 date for Waymaker-1 is also a long way off, so the second batch is a plan rather than a firm date.

"Every mission challenges us to improve on the last, from the spacecraft we build to how quickly we deliver the first image," said CEO David Langan.

For SpaceX, the booking is another small customer for its Bandwagon mid-inclination rideshares. For Umbra, it is a bet that steady, frequent launches of third-generation spacecraft will keep it competitive.
