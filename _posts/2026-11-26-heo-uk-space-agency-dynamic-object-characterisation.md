HEO, the Australian company that builds a business out of photographing other people's satellites from orbit, has picked up a contract from the UK Space Agency to automate a measurement that is still largely done by hand: how a spacecraft is oriented and how fast it is spinning.

The six-month project, called "Dynamic Object Characterisation in low-Earth orbit", will develop and demonstrate a method for measuring a spacecraft's attitude and rotation rates across multiple axes from resolved images captured in space. HEO says it will draw on its existing non-Earth imaging and analytics work to do this at scale. The contract value was not disclosed.

## Why it matters

Knowing whether an object is stable, tumbling or slowly drifting is basic space domain awareness. It helps operators and defence customers tell a healthy satellite from a dead one, and spot behaviour that changes. As orbits fill up, doing that by analyst is a bottleneck, which is the gap HEO is pitching automation at. CEO and co-founder Will Crowe said space domain awareness "must keep pace with the growing number of spacecraft".

The work sits under the agency's Unlocking Space for Defence programme, which backs dual-use space technology. Results are intended to feed BOREALIS, a system that supports the UK National Space Operations Centre in characterising resident space objects, meaning the things already in orbit.

## UK footprint

As part of the award HEO says it will invest in local operations, recruit early-career space talent and develop sovereign capability in line with UK government priorities. HEO's imaging approach is to use cameras on other operators' satellites rather than flying its own fleet, so UK-based analytics work is where the company can add capacity without launching hardware.

The announcement comes from HEO and was reported by Space Connect; we have not seen the contract notice itself, so the six-month term and programme details are as the company describes them.
