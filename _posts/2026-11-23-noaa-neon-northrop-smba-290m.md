NASA has awarded Northrop Grumman Systems Corporation a $290.6 million contract to supply the Sounder for Microwave-Based Applications (SMBA) instruments for NOAA's Near Earth Orbit Network (NEON), the agency announced on Nov. 23.

The award covers design, development, production, testing and mission support for three SMBA instruments. It is a hybrid of cost-plus-fixed-fee, firm-fixed-price and time-and-materials terms, with a period of performance that runs from Nov. 23, 2026 through April 1, 2042. The work will be done at Northrop Grumman's own facility.

## What the instruments do

SMBA units are next-generation microwave sounders meant for low Earth orbit. They measure atmospheric temperature and moisture, two inputs that heavily shape weather patterns. NASA's release says the instruments "could improve the medium-range forecasts for NOAA's National Weather Service." NOAA will own and operate them and distribute the data to users worldwide.

## Who does what

NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland, is managing the acquisition. NOAA leads the NEON mission itself, supplying funding, technical requirements and post-launch operations, while NASA manages development of the NEON satellites and will launch them on NOAA's behalf. The release says the two agencies will work with commercial partners to design and build the spacecraft and instruments.

The announcement does not say how many NEON satellites will carry the three instruments, nor when they are expected to launch. It also contains no comment from officials or from Northrop Grumman.

The structure is worth noting for the industry: NEON is framed as a network built with commercial partners rather than a single large government-built platform, and the long performance period suggests the contract covers support well beyond delivery. Details such as the satellite buses and launch providers have yet to be announced.
