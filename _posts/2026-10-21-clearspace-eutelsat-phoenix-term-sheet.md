ClearSpace has signed a term sheet with Eutelsat to cooperate on Phoenix, its geostationary (GEO) life-extension mission, giving the Luxembourg-headquartered servicing company a named satellite operator as customer and partner. The signing was announced on 21 October, with the milestone highlighted a day earlier at the NEO Space event in Luxembourg.

## What the deal covers

A term sheet is not a contract, and ClearSpace is explicit that this one sets out a path rather than an order. It lays out how Phoenix's servicing development could lead toward commercial service contracts. Eutelsat will contribute operational expertise to help define service requirements, mission design and demonstration planning. The document also allows for one of Eutelsat's own satellites to be used in the in-orbit demonstration, subject to further agreement.

That last point matters. A life-extension demonstration needs a real client satellite to dock with, and operators are understandably cautious about lending one. Having a customer willing to at least explore it is a meaningful step.

## About Phoenix

Phoenix aims to show that a servicing spacecraft can rendezvous and dock with a satellite in orbit and then provide propulsion and attitude control, keeping an ageing GEO satellite useful after it would otherwise run out of fuel. ClearSpace puts the broader programme at roughly €100 million. It is funded by the European Space Agency through its ARTES programme, building on earlier support from the Government of Luxembourg and the Luxembourg Space Agency. The first demonstration is targeted for 2030, with commercial GEO life-extension services in the early 2030s.

Sabrina Andiappane, Managing Director of ClearSpace Luxembourg, said Eutelsat "brings invaluable customer insight into how we design and deliver that capability". Eutelsat's Chief Engineering Officer Frédéric Piro said life-extension technologies "could help us deliver these services for longer."

## Context

Eutelsat operates 31 GEO satellites alongside a low-Earth-orbit constellation of more than 600 satellites following its 2023 combination with OneWeb, so it has a clear stake in stretching the life of its GEO fleet. The announcement follows a busy month for ClearSpace: on 23 September it signed an ESA contract to advance Phoenix, and on 15 October the UK Space Agency selected it to lead the UK's first national active debris removal mission, which will remove two UK-registered satellites.
