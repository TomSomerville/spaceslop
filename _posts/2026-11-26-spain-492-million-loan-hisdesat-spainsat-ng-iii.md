The Spanish government has approved a direct loan of 492.2 million euros to Hisdesat, the operator of Spain's secure military and government satellite communications, to develop, build, launch and commission SpainSat NG III. The Council of Ministers cleared the Royal Decree on Nov. 24, according to Spanish trade press.

## Terms

The loan carries no interest and no guarantees, and it is disbursed over several years from 2026 to 2030. It covers the satellite itself, adaptations to the ground segment and launch insurance. The government says the new satellite, with an estimated 15-year life, will keep secure communications available for national defense and the armed forces' international missions, and will replace the original SpainSat, launched in 2006 and nearing end of life.

## Why it is needed

The SpainSat NG program launched two satellites between 2019 and 2025. SpainSat NG I has served the Defence Ministry since January 2025. SpainSat NG II suffered an anomaly while climbing to geostationary orbit, attributed to the impact of a space particle, and the mission was a total loss.

A second Royal Decree amends the program's original loan, first set at 750 million euros and later revised to 808 million. A launch insurance policy covers that loan and guarantees repayment to the Treasury if a satellite fails. With the NG II insurance payout collected, the remaining repayments are respread over 2026 to 2039. The total repaid stays at 808 million euros, so the government says the change adds no public spending.

The coverage I read gives no launch date or launch vehicle for SpainSat NG III. One report cited by Spanish outlet Autonocion, which I did not read directly, says Hisdesat had already contracted Airbus Defence and Space and Thales Alenia Space for the build on July 31, before the loan was approved; treat that detail as unconfirmed here.

The loan is a reminder of how Europe's sovereign satcom programs lean on state credit: the insurer paid out for the loss, but replacing a geostationary military satellite still needs a fresh public line of financing.
