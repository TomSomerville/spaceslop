Portugal's first satellite production facility is close to opening. Defence Minister Nuno Melo said the clean rooms at the Alverca Space Hub, an integration site at the Portuguese Air Force base near Lisbon, will be inaugurated on 11 December. "Em dezembro, já proximamente, dia 11," he said, according to Lusa reporting carried by RTP.

Melo spoke at "Inside Space," a forum in Lisbon organized by the Armed Forces General Staff and the Aerospace Technology and Innovation Centre. The plan, as he described it, is for Portugal to build satellites there with export potential. RTP says the hub is financed through Portugal's Recovery and Resilience Plan (PRR); the article gives no amounts, production capacity or satellite counts.

## The wider Portuguese push

The minister placed Alverca alongside other national space projects. These include a launch base on Santa Maria in the Azores, a network of data-collection antennas across the country, and the Atlantic Constellation, an Earth-observation satellite constellation being developed with Spain and also PRR-funded.

Earlier coverage fills in some of the picture. ECO reported in July that Finnish radar-satellite maker ICEYE is the technology partner and was opening a "center of excellence" in Lisbon; the RTP piece itself does not mention ICEYE, so the specifics of that role are worth confirming once the hub opens. Portuguese officials have also acknowledged that the country still has no launch vehicle of its own, which is framed as a later project.

## Why it matters

Small European states are racing to claim a slice of the satellite supply chain as defence-driven demand for radar and imaging satellites grows. Alverca is modest by industry standards, but a working clean room plus a launch site in the Azores would give Portugal two links in the chain that it lacked a few years ago. The test is what rolls out of the facility after the ribbon is cut, and who buys it.
