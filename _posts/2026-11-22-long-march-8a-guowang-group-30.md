China launched the 30th group of its low-orbit internet satellites on a Long March 8A rocket from the Hainan Commercial Spacecraft Launch Site at 7:59 p.m. Beijing time on Friday 20 November (1159 UTC), according to state broadcaster CGTN. The satellites reached their intended orbit, and CGTN called the mission a complete success.

As with earlier batches of the Guowang constellation, the official announcement gave no satellite count. Public launch trackers list nine payloads for this flight, matching earlier Long March 8A batches, but that figure is not officially confirmed.

## Cadence

The interesting part is the tempo. TechTimes calculates that group 30 came 26 days after group 28, which flew on the same rocket type from the same site. Gaps between Long March 8A flights since August have shrunk from 50 days to about 32 and now about 26. The pad's fastest demonstrated turnaround is 18 days 15 hours, so this flight was still about eight days off its own best.

Guowang batches overall have been flying 12 to 16 days apart when all rocket types are counted, per the same piece.

## What limits it

TechTimes cites two outside analyses, from the tracking site KeepTrack and the research institute MERICS, that blame rocket production rather than pads or satellite factories. The Hainan site's two pads were designed for about 32 launches a year combined but have flown at roughly half that rate, and the Long March 8A is expendable, so every batch consumes a whole rocket. MERICS names the lack of reusable rockets as the key chokepoint.

Neither conclusion has been independently audited. China does not publish rocket output, and the production figures in the analysis are manufacturer claims or design ratings. The Hainan pad has reportedly achieved a seven-day pre-launch cycle, and officials say weekly launches are possible, but no such cadence has been shown.

## Scale

TechTimes puts the constellation at roughly 231 to 240 satellites in orbit, depending on whether group 30 carried nine, which is under 2 percent of the 12,992 satellites in China's filing. By its arithmetic, reaching the stated 2026 goal of 310 would take eight or nine more nine-satellite batches in the 41 days left in the year. At nine satellites a flight, the full filing would need about 1,440 launches.
