NASA has published its coverage plan for Northrop Grumman's CRS-25 resupply mission to the International Space Station, targeting a Falcon 9 launch no earlier than 10:47 a.m. EST on Sunday, Nov. 29, from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida.

The spacecraft is a Cygnus XL, named "S.S. James 'Jim' A. Lovell" after the astronaut who flew Gemini VII, Gemini XII, Apollo 8 and Apollo 13. It carries roughly 11,000 pounds of science, supplies and equipment. NASA's list of research aboard includes work on drug delivery for cancer and osteoarthritis, predicting how cancer spreads, treatments for muscle loss, radiation sensing, and food production for long-duration missions.

## Arrival and stay

Astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney are scheduled to capture the Cygnus with the Canadarm2 robotic arm at about 2:35 a.m. EST on Tuesday, Dec. 1. Ground controllers will then berth it to the Earth-facing port of the Unity module so the crew can unload it. NASA says the vehicle will remain until May 2027, when it departs filled with disposal items and unneeded cargo and burns up on re-entry.

## Why a Falcon 9

Northrop Grumman's Cygnus launches on SpaceX's rocket, and this is not a first: NASA notes that CRS-24 flew a Cygnus XL on a Falcon 9 from the same pad on April 11, 2026.

## Coverage

NASA will hold a prelaunch teleconference on its YouTube channel at noon EST on Monday, Nov. 23, with NASA, Northrop Grumman and SpaceX representatives. Launch coverage begins at 10:25 a.m. on Nov. 29, and arrival coverage starts at 1:10 a.m. on Dec. 1. Streaming and blog updates will be available at nasa.gov/live. As with any launch date this far out, the 29th is a target and can move with range, weather or vehicle readiness.
