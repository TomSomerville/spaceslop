LeoLabs, the radar-based space tracking company, has acquired FishEye Software, an engineering services firm that works on U.S. government defense systems. The company announced the deal on November 16. FishEye becomes a wholly owned LeoLabs subsidiary.

LeoLabs says the purchase is meant to speed up delivery of its next-generation radars for missile warning and tracking. FishEye brings nearly 30 years of work in systems engineering, software development and field testing for government-owned radars used in space domain awareness and missile defense. LeoLabs funded the acquisition with a loan from Hercules Capital, and Mirus Capital Advisors advised FishEye. Financial terms were not given in the release.

"FishEye brings decades of experience developing and integrating software for U.S. missile defense systems," LeoLabs CEO Tony Frazier said, adding that the expertise will help adapt the company's radar technology for missile warning and tracking.

## A company changing shape

The deal fits a pattern in LeoLabs' recent announcements. On November 5 the company said it had secured up to $150 million in a conditional loan commitment from the U.S. Department of War's Office of Strategic Capital. That money is earmarked for expanding domestic radar manufacturing, with two large-scale manufacturing facilities due by 2028 and dozens of systems to be added to its global network by 2030.

LeoLabs also reports more than $100 million in bookings for 2026 from U.S. and allied customers. Its radar line includes the fixed-site Seeker and Ranger, the transportable Scout-S aimed at space domain awareness, and Scout-X, which is optimized for missile warning and tracking.

Taken together, the financing, the order book and now an in-house team that knows government radar integration describe a company moving from selling tracking data to building and delivering radar hardware at scale. Missile warning is a different customer set from the commercial and civil space tracking LeoLabs started with, and one where government-qualified software experience counts for a great deal.

What remains to be seen is how quickly the new capacity turns into delivered systems. LeoLabs frames 2027 as the year radar production ramps, and the manufacturing facilities are not due until 2028.
