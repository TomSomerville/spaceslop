Bengaluru's SatSure has raised 600 million rupees, about $7 million, from the Antariksh Venture Capital Fund, in what is the first slice of a Series B round the company expects to close by December. The report is a Reuters wire item carried by a local radio site.

The fund values SatSure at about 8.7 billion rupees post-money, roughly $93 million. The full Series B is 2 billion rupees and mixes fresh equity, venture debt and secondary share sales.

## Who is behind the money

Antariksh is a 16 billion rupee fund anchored by IN-SPACe, India's regulator and promoter of private space activity, and managed by SIDBI Venture Capital. It is a state-linked vehicle aimed squarely at the country's push to move space work out of the government's hands and into private companies.

## What SatSure will do with it

SatSure combines satellite imagery with AI analytics for banks, insurers, infrastructure firms and public agencies. Its customers include HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, ITC, Bayer and the Airports Authority of India. The company has raised 4.27 billion rupees from institutions before this, excluding grants, with Baring Private Equity Partners India, Promus Ventures and Asian Development Bank Ventures among its earlier backers.

The new funding is earmarked for three things: preparing its own satellite imaging payloads for a 2027 launch, taking part in India's planned Earth-observation satellite network, and expanding data analysis and international operations.

The hardware ambition is the notable part. SatSure has mostly been a data-and-analytics company, and moving toward its own payloads puts it in the same lane as the Indian small-satellite builders that the regulator is trying to nurture. The 2027 launch target and the planned national EO network are the things to watch; neither has a date or contract attached in the source we read.
