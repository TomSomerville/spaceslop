China has named the crew for Shenzhou-24 and confirmed the Pakistani astronaut who will become the first foreign visitor to its Tiangong space station: Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a former pilot in Pakistan's armed forces, flying as a payload specialist.

Launch is set for 8:18 a.m. Beijing time on Wednesday, Nov. 25, from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, according to the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) as relayed by Dawn and 24 News HD. Ali flies with Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi. The reports do not name a commander.

## A week aboard, then home on Shenzhou-23

Ali is expected to spend about one week on the station. He will return on Shenzhou-23, which docked in May, together with Zhang Zhiyuan and Hong Kong astronaut Lai Ka-ying, who are finishing six-month tours. Shenzhou-23's third member, Zhu Yangzhu, stays on Tiangong for another six months with Tang and Wu. 24 News HD describes that as China's first year-long orbital stay for a crew member.

The CMSA says the Shenzhou-24 crew will carry out about 90 experiments, along with the crew handover, routine station operations and maintenance, and continued payload science. Ali will work on experiments alongside the Chinese crew. Khurram Daud, the second Pakistani candidate who trained in China, is not flying.

"Now, this dream belongs to me and also belongs to Pakistan," Ali said, per Dawn.

## Why it matters

This is the first time a foreign astronaut has flown in China's crewed program, and it is the clearest sign yet that Beijing wants Tiangong seen as an international destination as the ISS approaches retirement. Pakistan gets a first national in space without building its own crew vehicle. The flight is tied to 75 years of Pakistan-China diplomatic relations.

CMSA spokesman Zhang Jingbo also said China plans to land astronauts on the Moon before 2030 and that the maiden flight of the Lanyue lunar lander is on track, per 24 News HD.
