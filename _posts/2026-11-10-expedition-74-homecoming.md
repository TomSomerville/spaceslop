NASA will hold a public welcome-home event on 17 November at 6 p.m. CST at the Johnson Space Center visitor center, Space Center Houston, for the returned Expedition 74 crew. Speakers are NASA astronauts Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway and Chris Williams, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot and cosmonauts Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergei Mikaev. An awards ceremony follows.

Two vehicles carried the crew home. The Soyuz MS-28 crew of Williams, Kud-Sverchkov and Mikaev launched from Baikonur on 27 November 2025 and landed southeast of Dzhezkazgan, Kazakhstan, on 26 July after 241 days and 3,856 orbits. For Williams and Mikaev it was a first flight. SpaceX's Crew-12 launched on 13 February from Cape Canaveral's SLC-40 and splashed down off Los Angeles on 8 October after 237 days and 3,792 orbits. Meir flew for the second time, Hathaway and Adenot for the first. Cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev, also on Crew-12, cannot attend.

NASA says the crew spent over 1,400 hours on science and technology demonstrations. That included testing metal and tissue 3D printing, studies of blood flow and of piloting under changing gravity, a compact exercise system and life-support evaluations for Artemis. Pharmaceutical work covered growing stem cells and crystallizing proteins for cancer medications.

The long durations are notable: both crews stayed roughly eight months, well beyond the traditional six, a pattern the station's partners have used to keep crew turnover manageable.
