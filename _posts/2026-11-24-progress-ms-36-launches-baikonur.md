Russia's last resupply flight to the International Space Station this year is in orbit. A Soyuz-2.1b lifted off from Pad 6 at Site 31 in Baikonur at 13:17:46 Moscow time (10:17 UTC) on Nov. 24, carrying the Progress MS-36 cargo ship, also designated ISS mission 97P. According to RussianSpaceWeb, the third stage released the spacecraft into a parking orbit about nine minutes after liftoff, aimed at roughly 240 by 193 kilometers at 51.67 degrees inclination.

The ship carries about 2,500 kilograms of deliverable payload for the Expedition 75 crew. It is the fourth and final Russian cargo flight of 2026 and, per RussianSpaceWeb, puts three Russian cargo vehicles at the station at once for the first time.

## An unusual docking plan

Progress MS-36 is scheduled to dock automatically at the zenith port of the Poisk module on Nov. 26 at 15:11 Moscow time, according to RussianSpaceWeb. SpacePolicyOnline, relaying reporting by Anatoly Zak, gave an earlier docking date of Nov. 25, so the exact day is worth confirming with NASA or Roscosmos updates.

After unloading at Poisk, the ship is to undock and re-dock at the aft port of Zvezda's transfer chamber. That split exists because the hatch to that chamber has been permanently closed over an air leak that crews have been unable to stop, so cargo can no longer be moved through it. The aft port is still the best place for orbit-correction burns, since it aligns the Progress engines with the station's main axis. Using two ports lets Russia keep both the cargo and reboost functions going.

## Delayed by a collapsed platform

The flight was originally planned for August, slipped to September, and then to November. RussianSpaceWeb ties the delay to the collapse of a service platform at Site 31 in November 2025, which affected Russia's only crew-capable launch pad. The launch time also shifted on the day, from 08:03 to 13:13 and finally 13:17 Moscow time.

RussianSpaceWeb estimates the ship will remain at the station for about six months, into 2027. The docking itself has not yet happened, and neither source reports it, so the two-port plan still has to be carried out.
