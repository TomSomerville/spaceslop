A broken piece of the International Space Station's main robotic arm is on its way to engineers on the ground. According to SpaceQ's weekly station roundup, NASA astronaut Jessica Watkins loaded the failed Canadarm2 wrist joint into a SpaceX Dragon cargo ship, which returned it to Earth for analysis.

The joint failed earlier this year and was swapped for a spare during a spacewalk on June 30. Bringing the original back matters because the arm is central to station operations: Canadarm2 grapples visiting cargo vehicles and moves equipment outside the station. Understanding why the joint failed informs how the remaining hardware is maintained and whether other joints face the same risk.

## Weather delay, then splashdown

Dragon's undocking was set for Nov. 18, but NASA and SpaceX postponed it because of rough weather in the California splashdown zones and rescheduled it for Nov. 20. The capsule splashed down in the Pacific near Los Angeles the following day, SpaceQ reported.

## What's next for the arm

Canadarm2 has more work coming. Northrop Grumman's Cygnus XL cargo ship is scheduled to launch no earlier than Nov. 29 on a Falcon 9, carrying about 5,000 kilograms of supplies and research. NASA plans to capture it with the arm on Dec. 1, with Watkins and Luke Delaney at the controls. We have a separate report on [the Cygnus XL launch plans](/2026/11/18/cygnus-xl-crs-25-nov-29-launch/).

## Rest of the week on the station

Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk spent the week on health research: a blood draw on Nov. 13, blood tests on Nov. 16 using ACTIS, a system that lets flight surgeons monitor crew health remotely, and protein-crystal preparation on Nov. 18 for a Japanese pharmaceutical study. Fellow Canadian Jenni Gibbons visited Whitehorse, Yukon, on Nov. 17 and 18 and spoke to about 300 students and community members about Canada's role in Artemis, with Kutryk joining live from orbit.

Elsewhere, Delaney harvested thale cress grown in the Kibo laboratory, and the Roscosmos crew tested a breathing monitor and serviced ventilation and water systems.

The failure analysis results, when they come, will be the part worth watching. Until then the spare joint is doing the work.
