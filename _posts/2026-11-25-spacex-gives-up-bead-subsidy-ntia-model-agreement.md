SpaceX is giving up roughly $738 million in federal rural broadband subsidies while still committing to serve the locations it won, according to Teslarati's reading of a new National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) directive. The article cites Telecompetitor, citing administration officials, for a lower figure of about $636 million. The discrepancy is unresolved, and this account rests on a single outlet's summary, so treat the details as reported rather than confirmed.

## What the deal says

The money comes from the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program. NTIA says SpaceX will serve every awarded location at no cost to the program. A senior NTIA official said SpaceX itself proposed the arrangement after struggling with contract terms that differed across all 50 states, including financial reporting demands and default penalties. NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth said it could "save taxpayers hundreds of millions of dollars."

Under NTIA's model agreement, SpaceX must meet BEAD's minimum speeds of 100 Mbps down and 20 Mbps up, pass performance testing and report every six months. New subscribers at covered locations get the dish and router free, and SpaceX must offer a low-cost plan, though monthly service is not waived. The agreement drops BEAD's financial audit, procurement and reporting provisions, and states cannot levy penalties. Their only remedy is to take the locations back and re-award them.

## Why it matters

States must either sign the model agreement or rebid the SpaceX locations. Contracts some states had already signed with SpaceX are no longer valid under the directive, and two state officials told Broadband Breakfast it caught them by surprise. The NTIA official said holding the locations at a zero-dollar award keeps other federal broadband money from reaching competitors in those areas.

Starlink's BEAD role grew after NTIA's 2025 overhaul dropped its preference for fiber. Arizona had awarded SpaceX $17.1 million for 10,749 locations, and Virginia $3.3 million for 5,579. Elon Musk reshared a summary of the terms on X with a single word: "Starlink."

This is a policy and contract story about SpaceX's broadband business rather than a launch or service update.
