SpaceX has sold about $32 billion of bonds to pay for AI chips, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, with yields topping 8%. The sale follows the [Financial Times report](/2026/11/20/spacex-lenders-chips-orbit-safeguards/) that lenders wanted guardrails against those chips ever being flown into orbit.

## How it works

SpaceX is not issuing the bonds itself. According to Bloomberg's earlier reporting, a special purpose vehicle managed by Atlas SP, which is backed by Apollo, buys the Nvidia chips and issues asset-backed securities. SpaceX then leases the hardware. Secondary summaries of the Bloomberg piece put the lease at about five years. The structure is meant to keep the debt off SpaceX's balance sheet, which Bloomberg said lets the company hold on to its investment-grade credit ratings. Goldman Sachs and Apollo were named as underwriters, per a person familiar with the matter.

Bloomberg called the deal complicated and relatively high-yielding, and said it may become a template for similar financings. Seeking Alpha's summary cites the 8% figure; secondary reports put the headline tranche near 8.3%, a figure that matches Bloomberg's earlier detail. Per-tranche amounts and the final lease terms were not available in the material we could read. Bloomberg's original is paywalled, so details here come from summaries of it.

## Why it matters

The size is the story. A rocket company, now also an AI company after folding in xAI, is raising tens of billions against GPUs rather than against launch hardware. For a space-industry reader, it says where SpaceX's capital appetite now sits: data centers first, with the orbital version of that idea still a talking point rather than collateral. The lenders' reported worry, that chips in orbit could not be repossessed, shows how awkward that pitch is for conventional credit.

Whether any restriction on moving the chips off Earth made it into the final documents remains unreported.
