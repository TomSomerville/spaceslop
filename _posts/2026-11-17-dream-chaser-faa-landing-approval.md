The FAA has approved the return to Earth of Sierra Space's Dream Chaser spaceplane, a regulatory milestone for a vehicle that has been in development for more than two decades and has yet to reach orbit. Sierra Space announced the approval in a post on X on Monday, 9 November, according to Space.com.

The approval covers a runway landing at either the Space Shuttle landing facility at NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida or Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. In its environmental assessment the FAA concluded the vehicle would not significantly impact the human environment. For a Vandenberg return it expects sonic booms that are temporary, short, and limited to 1.1 pounds per square foot or less.

## What flies next

Sierra Space is targeting spring 2027 for the debut: a robotic, free-flying test mission in Earth orbit. The first vehicle is named Tenacity. Dream Chaser has moved from Atlas V to ULA's Vulcan Centaur as its ride. It was once slated for Vulcan's second flight, but was not ready, and that launch went ahead without it in October 2024.

That dependency now looks uncomfortable. Vulcan lost a rocket and 40 Amazon Leo satellites on 15 November, and ULA has not said what went wrong. A spring 2027 date therefore rests on both Dream Chaser's readiness and a Vulcan return to flight, which Space.com's report does not address.

## Background

The roughly 30-foot (9 m) spaceplane lost NASA's Commercial Crew competition in 2014 to Boeing's Starliner and SpaceX's Crew Dragon. Sierra Nevada Corporation, then running the program, unveiled a cargo version in 2015, and NASA picked it in 2016 for at least six uncrewed ISS cargo flights. Delays pushed those missions back. The vehicle has completed a couple of drop tests from the air but has not flown in space. Sierra Space is pursuing NASA, military and private customers, including commercial station groups, and belongs to the Orbital Reef consortium.

An FAA landing approval is necessary but is not a flight date. The vehicle still has to be launched, operated and brought home, none of which it has yet done.
