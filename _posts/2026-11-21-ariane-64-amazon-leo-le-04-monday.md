Arianespace is lined up to launch 36 Amazon Leo broadband satellites on an Ariane 64 from the Guiana Space Centre on Monday, Nov. 23, according to a schedule report published Nov. 19. The window runs from 11:09 to 11:38 UTC (08:09 to 08:38 in Kourou), Advanced Television reported.

Arianespace designates the flight VA271; Amazon calls it LE-04, for Leo Europe 4. It is the fourth Arianespace launch for Amazon Leo, the constellation formerly known as Project Kuiper. NeoTeo, citing an earlier schedule notice, describes it as the tenth Ariane 6 flight overall, the fourth in the Ariane 64 configuration, and the second Ariane 64 to fly with the larger P160C solid boosters.

The Ariane 64 uses four strap-on boosters, making it the most powerful variant of Europe's current heavy launcher. NeoTeo's account gives a flight profile of about two hours and four minutes from liftoff to release of the final satellite. The earlier LE-02 mission carried 32 satellites, so this flight carries more per launch than that one did.

## Why it matters

The launch sits inside a 24-launch agreement between Arianespace and Amazon. NeoTeo reports that Amazon added six more Ariane 64 launches in September 2026. If deployment succeeds, Arianespace's cumulative total of Amazon Leo satellites in orbit would reach 136, per the same report.

For Europe, Amazon's manifest is a large share of the Ariane 6 cadence, which makes the vehicle's launch rate a question about a single American customer as much as about European institutional demand. For Amazon, which is racing to build out service coverage, every additional heavy launcher flying on schedule counts.

## What to watch

The timing details differ between sources. NeoTeo, writing in mid-October, said the final window had yet to be set; Advanced Television's Nov. 19 piece gives the window above. Arianespace has the final say, and weather or technical holds could move the date. Neither source, as read for this report, mentions any issue with the vehicle. Some launch listings have mentioned engine concerns in earlier weeks, but those are not confirmed in the material reviewed here.
