Lenders behind SpaceX's latest financing want contractual protections to stop Elon Musk from launching the Nvidia chips that secure the debt, the Financial Times reported on November 19. The concern, citing people familiar with the matter, is practical: chips running in orbital data centers would be hard to repossess and sell if SpaceX defaulted.

It is unclear whether any such restriction made it into the final loan documents. The Financial Times also reported that SpaceX has said the chips will be used in data centers on Earth. The lenders were not named. This article relies on a secondhand summary of the FT report; the original is behind a paywall and was not read.

## The package

The debt package is worth $43 billion and is called Project Saturn. It includes a $25 billion investment-grade bond backed by lease payments for the Nvidia chips. Bloomberg reported that the bond priced at about 8.3% and was rated A3 by Moody's and A-minus by KBRA.

## Why it matters

Musk has said that part of the rationale for combining SpaceX and xAI is to advance orbital data centers. That is a business line for which SpaceX's launch capacity and Starlink networks are a natural fit, and also one where the collateral logic of conventional lending breaks down. A lease or loan secured by hardware assumes the lender can physically reach the hardware. Once the hardware is in orbit, that assumption stops holding.

The episode shows how ambitions for space-based computing are starting to meet the people lending the money for it. Whether a no-launch covenant survives negotiation, and whether SpaceX would accept one given its stated plans, will say something about how seriously both sides treat orbital compute in the near term.
