NeuralSat, a German spacetech startup founded in 2026, has closed a €2.9 million pre-seed round (about $3.2 million) to build a network that moves satellite data faster and does some of the computing in orbit.

Capnamic Ventures led the round, according to Vestbee, while Pulse 2.0 names Capnamic and Bayern Kapital as co-leads. ZOHO.VC, Robin Capital, KIEMIS Holding and Mission E Capital also took part, and Pulse 2.0 additionally lists BAB, the Bremen development bank. Vestbee reports the company was founded by three space engineers: Mohd Bilal, Manohar Karnal and Rishi Jaiswal. Neither article names the company's home city.

## What it wants to build

The pitch targets a familiar bottleneck. Earth-observation and signals-intelligence satellites collect more than they can send down, constrained by downlink capacity, ground-station access and the processing hardware they can carry. NeuralSat's answer is a relay layer that sits alongside operators' existing spacecraft: satellites hand data to NeuralSat nodes over optical or radio-frequency links, and the nodes route it onward or process it first. Vestbee quotes the company's figure of up to 50 Gbit/s on those links, which is a company claim and not a demonstrated result.

A later phase would pool computing resources across the network so that customers' satellites could borrow processing capacity rather than flying all of it themselves. Pulse 2.0 says the system is aimed at civil and defence users, with emphasis on security, sovereignty and interoperability, a framing that fits Europe's push for homegrown space infrastructure.

## Timeline

The sources differ slightly on dates. Pulse 2.0 says the first network node is planned for orbit in October 2027 to demonstrate connectivity, before the company moves into distributed computing. Vestbee puts the first in-orbit demonstration in 2027 and the start of launches for its own constellation in 2028. One other outlet reported a 72-satellite constellation; I could not confirm that figure in the sources read for this article, so it is left out here.

Relay networks and orbital computing both have crowded fields, and a pre-seed round is a long way from hardware in orbit. For now NeuralSat is a funded plan with a demonstration date, which is where most European space startups at this stage sit.
