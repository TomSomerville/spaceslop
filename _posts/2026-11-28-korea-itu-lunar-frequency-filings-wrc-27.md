South Korea is one of ten countries that have submitted lunar exploration frequency filings in the International Telecommunication Union's most recently published registrations, Korean outlet Digital Daily reported on 28 November.

The ITU manages lunar exploration frequencies under its Radio Regulations, the treaty that governs radio spectrum and satellite orbit use. Until now, countries have mostly registered frequencies for links between Earth and lunar probes. Korea did so in 2022 for Danuri, its first lunar orbiter. Digital Daily says Seoul now wants spectrum for communications on the lunar surface and between the surface and lunar orbit, which become necessary once missions turn into bases rather than one-off visits.

## The planning behind it

In July, the Korea Aerospace Administration signed a joint statement of intent with NASA on building and operating a lunar base, the report says. Korea plans to launch a 500 kg-class lunar-orbit communications satellite in 2029 to demonstrate relay and navigation technology. It would then link up with lunar landers planned for 2030 and 2032.

The larger fight is still ahead. Allocation of lunar frequencies is due to be discussed at the 2027 World Radiocommunication Conference (WRC-27). The ITU is reviewing new spectrum needs and conditions of use to prevent interference between national systems, since hundreds of lunar missions are expected over the coming decades. The Korean government told the paper it is preparing for the talks and will feed domestic demand into the international process with the Korea Aerospace Administration.

The article does not list the other nine filing countries or the number of filings each made, and it covers only government bodies, so it does not say whether any commercial operators are among the filers. For lunar surface operators, spectrum is a quiet but real constraint: a crowded Moon with uncoordinated radios is a problem that is much cheaper to solve on paper than after landers are on the ground.
