A fire at an outside contractor's facility has delayed German space agency DLR's EDEN-LUNA greenhouse, European Spaceflight reports. The container was being refurbished when the fire broke out, and installation at the LUNA facility in Cologne, planned for 2026, has slipped to the second quarter of 2027.

DLR announced the new timeline on 4 September without saying why. The fire came out in the government's reply to a parliamentary inquiry from the AfD party, published by the Bundestag on 20 October. The reply did not say how bad the damage was or which components were hit.

EDEN-LUNA is meant to test technology for growing fresh food on long Moon and Mars missions. It builds on EDEN ISS, which ran near Germany's Neumayer III station in Antarctica from 2018 to 2022 and returned to Germany in April 2023. DLR's Institute of Space Systems in Bremen has since upgraded it for a second campaign, adding robotic assistance, automated plant monitoring and systems that recover nutrients from waste.

LUNA is operated jointly by DLR and ESA. It opened in September 2024 and has about 700 square meters of simulated lunar terrain for testing technology and training astronauts.

It is a modest slip in the scheme of lunar programs, but it shows how small supply-chain events can ripple through ground-based preparation for Moon missions.
