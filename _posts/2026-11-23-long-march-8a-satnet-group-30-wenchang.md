China added another batch to its state-run broadband constellation on Friday, November 20, when a Long March 8A lifted off from the Hainan commercial launch site at Wenchang at 7:59 p.m. Beijing time (11:59 UTC). Xinhua reported that the payload, the 30th group of low-orbit internet satellites, entered its designated orbits.

The Xinhua report gives no satellite count or flight number. Launch-tracking data lists the mission as SatNet LEO Group 30 from Commercial Launch Complex 1, carrying nine satellites, which is the usual size of a Guowang batch flown on the Long March 8A and Long March 12.

## A fast cadence

The launch continues a rhythm that has become routine. Group 28 flew on a Long March 8A from the same pad on October 25, and Group 29 followed on a Long March 12 from Wenchang's second commercial pad on November 8. Three groups in roughly 26 days, split between two vehicles and two pads at one spaceport, shows how China is using the Hainan commercial site as a dedicated production line for the constellation.

SatNet, operated by the state-owned China Satellite Network Group, is planned to reach about 13,000 satellites. That is the number quoted in launch-tracking listings; the constellation's size and schedule have shifted before, so treat it as a target rather than a commitment. At nine satellites a launch, the full buildout would need well over a thousand flights, which is why the pace of Long March 8A and 12 launches matters more than any single mission.

## What's next

More SatNet flights are already on the manifest. Tracking data lists a Long March 12 carrying another SatNet batch from Wenchang around December 10, though that date is unconfirmed. Elsewhere, the competing commercial Chinese constellation from Shanghai Spacesail flew its 17th polar group on a Kinetica 2 on November 3, so Chinese broadband launches are now arriving from several providers in parallel.

For Western competitors, the signal is cadence. Amazon's Leo constellation lost a Vulcan on November 15 and is leaning on Ariane 6 and Falcon 9, while Guowang launches continue to stack up with little drama.
