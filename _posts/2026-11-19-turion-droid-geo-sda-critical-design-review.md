Turion Space has completed a critical design review (CDR) for its geosynchronous space domain awareness spacecraft, the company said in an announcement dated November 16. The review, under a Strategic Financing (STRATFI) agreement with the Space Force's SpaceWERX arm and Space Systems Command, concluded on September 9, according to the release.

A CDR is the milestone at which a design is judged mature enough to stop drawing and start building. Turion says the result clears the program to move into assembly, integration and test.

## Three vehicles, two orbits

The program covers three of Turion's highly maneuverable DROID spacecraft. One vehicle is slated to launch to low Earth orbit in 2027, followed by two vehicles to geostationary orbit in 2028. All three carry the same narrow field-of-view imager, intended for rendezvous and proximity operations and for high-resolution imaging of other objects in orbit.

The ownership split is unusual. The LEO vehicle and the first GEO vehicle will be owned and operated by the contractor. The second GEO vehicle will be government-owned but contractor-operated, so the Space Force can use it to try out its own tactics, techniques and procedures.

Turion also notes that one of the GEO spacecraft may be among the first to be serviced using Northrop Grumman's Passive Refueling Module.

## Why it matters

The stated aim is to reduce risk for RG-XX, the Space Force's planned program of record for GEO space domain awareness, and to show that such capability can be delivered commercially. Capt. Kevin Chang, the SSC program manager, is quoted in the release. Close-up inspection of satellites in GEO is a growing niche, and maneuverable commercial inspectors with a government-owned vehicle in the mix would give the Space Force a way to practice without buying a full fleet up front.
