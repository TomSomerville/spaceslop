ISRO reports that the first ignition test of the full-scale gas generator for its LOX-methane engine succeeded on 9 October 2026. The agency's own page, dated 16 October, says more tests will follow to evaluate performance under different conditions. The result drew fresh coverage in Indian media this week.

The gas generator is the small combustor that burns a portion of the propellant to make the hot gas that spins the turbopump. It is made by additive manufacturing and is going through qualification tests at ISRO's Propulsion Complex (IPRC). Before the full-scale test, a sub-scale unit was tested in a lab to work out an ignition sequence using a spark torch igniter.

## The engine behind it

The generator belongs to the LME1100, a 1,100 kN engine on a gas-generator cycle that ISRO describes as designed to be industry-friendly and cost-effective. More than 70% of its components have been realised through additive manufacturing. Formal development began in 2023, building on LOX-methane work started in 2019 and a technology demonstrator, the LM20-TD, tested in 2019 to 2020. The page says the integrated engine is expected to be ready for hot tests around mid-2027.

A larger step follows. ISRO says design work on a 2,400 kN engine using full-flow staged combustion is finished. It is intended for a future heavy-lift, human-rated launch vehicle, supporting India's goal of a crewed lunar landing by 2040.

The agency has not published test duration or performance figures for the gas generator.
