HyImpulse, the German rocket company based in Neuenstadt am Kocher, said on October 1 that it has received a UK Launch Operator Licence, making it a licensed launch operator in Britain. The company says the licence covers its SR75 suborbital programme and future orbital launches of its SL1 vehicle from SaxaVord Spaceport in the Shetland Islands.

The licence completes the regulatory review, but a launch authorization for SR75 is a separate step. HyImpulse and the UK Civil Aviation Authority still have to complete operational implementation work: monitoring processes, operational reporting and verification activities required under the licence. Taking that and autumn and winter weather at SaxaVord into account, HyImpulse is now preparing to fly SR75 in spring 2027.

## Why the weather matters

SR75 is a lightweight, aerodynamically stabilized suborbital rocket for research and technology demonstration, including microgravity and hypersonic flight work. With a lift-off mass of about 2.5 tonnes, it operates within tighter wind limits than larger vehicles, and the company says those limits are frequently exceeded at SaxaVord in autumn and winter, which could mean long waits for a suitable window. The SL1 orbital vehicle, expected to weigh more than 50 tonnes at lift-off and to use thrust vector control throughout powered flight, should tolerate a much broader range of conditions.

CEO Dr. Christian Schmierer called the licence "a major milestone" and said "SR75 is ready for flight." Sue Kee of the UK CAA said momentum "continues to build towards vertical launch from the UK." SaxaVord CEO Scott Hammond called it "another milestone on the road to launch from SaxaVord."

## Context

HyImpulse is one of several European startups competing for the continent's launch market, and SaxaVord is a primary site for vertical launches from the UK. The licence follows a March launch agreement between the two and, according to the company's press page, a funding announcement of more than €50 million in September. A first flight in spring 2027 would be the first from European soil for HyImpulse. As with any early rocket, the date depends on remaining CAA sign-offs.
