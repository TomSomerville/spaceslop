MDA Space said on Friday it has completed its acquisition of Blue Canyon Technologies, the Colorado spacecraft and satellite-component maker it agreed to buy from RTX's Raytheon business earlier this year. The deal closed ahead of the end-of-2026 target the company gave when it signed.

Blue Canyon will be rebranded as MDA Space and operate as MDA Space USA, according to a summary of the company's release. The stated aim is to give the Canadian company a US base from which to pursue American government defense space missions.

## The deal

When MDA announced the agreement, it put the price at US$620 million, all cash and subject to adjustments, fully financed at signing through senior secured debt. It said the business would be accretive to adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS in 2027, and that it added US$3.5 billion to MDA's opportunity pipeline. At closing, the release summary has CEO Mike Greenley describing Blue Canyon as a profitable, cash-generating business.

## What MDA gets

Blue Canyon has about 400 employees at facilities in the Denver area. The closing release summary credits it with more than 95 spacecraft launched and 4,300 products in orbit; the signing announcement had cited more than 85 and over 3,500, so the track record has grown since June. The company makes small-satellite buses and components such as attitude-control hardware, and also offers mission services.

For MDA, best known for robotic arms and its work on Canadian space programs, the purchase is a route into the US market, where defense spending is where the satellite money is moving. A US-domiciled subsidiary may also ease access to programs that favor domestic suppliers, though the company has not spelled that out in the material reviewed here.

Note: the figures for launches and products on orbit come from a third-party summary of the closing release, not the full release text, which was not available when this was written.
