Blue Origin has another New Glenn customer. EnduroSat, the Sofia-based satellite manufacturer, announced on November 25 that it has signed a multi-launch agreement for two dedicated New Glenn 7x2 missions, the first scheduled for 2029. Both flights will leave from Cape Canaveral, Florida, and are reserved for satellites EnduroSat builds for its own customers.

The company did not disclose a price. In the press release, EnduroSat founder and CEO Raycho Raychev framed the deal as removing "the biggest bottlenecks our customers face." Jordan Charles, Blue Origin's senior vice president for New Glenn, said the rocket "gives EnduroSat the ability to bring more of its customers' spacecraft to orbit."

## Why a satellite maker is buying whole rockets

EnduroSat is moving from selling small spacecraft to running the full chain: design, manufacturing, payload integration, launch and in-orbit operations. The release ties the booking to recent orders from TrustPoint, which is building a positioning, navigation and timing constellation meant to work without GPS, and from Vantor, which is planning a next-generation Earth-imaging fleet. Owning the launch slot lets EnduroSat bundle several customers' satellites onto one ride on its own schedule, instead of competing for rideshare space.

SpaceNews connected the booking to SpaceX winding down its Falcon 9 rideshare program, which has long been the default route for smaller operators. We could only read the free preview of that piece, so the details of that link are not independently confirmed here.

## The rocket

Per the release, New Glenn 7x2 is a reusable heavy-lift vehicle with a 7-meter fairing, rated for 45 metric tons to low Earth orbit and 13 metric tons to geostationary transfer orbit, with a first stage designed for at least 25 missions. EnduroSat says its ESPA-class Frame S and Frame L buses and the larger Frame Max, which supports payloads up to three metric tons, all fit.

The deal lands days after Google booked a dedicated New Glenn launch for its Project Suncatcher satellites in early 2029 (see [our earlier report](/2026/11/19/google-blue-origin-suncatcher-new-glenn/)). Two customers targeting 2029 gives Blue Origin a growing order book, though a 2029 date is a long way from a vehicle that still has to prove a steady flight cadence.
