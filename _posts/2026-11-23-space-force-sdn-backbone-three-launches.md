The Space Force has confirmed "three successful launches in two weeks" that put the first satellites of its Space Data Network Backbone in orbit, according to Air & Space Forces Magazine.

Launch trackers list three Vandenberg Space Force Base missions, on Nov. 6, Nov. 11 and Nov. 20. All flew on SpaceX Falcon 9 rockets. Each carried 23 satellites, for 69 in total, per Space-Track.org data cited by the magazine. The FAA labeled the payloads R-4, R-5 and R-6, which implies earlier R-1 to R-3 flights on Aug. 15, Sept. 10 and Sept. 26. The magazine notes it is unclear whether those were the program's first launches.

## What the Backbone is

The Space Data Network merges two formerly separate efforts, the Space Development Agency's Transport Layer and MILNET, into one architecture mixing military and commercial satellites for secure tactical communications. The Backbone is meant to be its "foundational data backhaul layer," offering high-throughput, low-latency connectivity. The constellation will be government-owned and controlled.

SpaceX is building the Backbone satellites under a $2.29 billion contract awarded in May. In August the Space Force added five other vendors through small initial agreements. Whether SpaceX's Starshield satellites are part of the Backbone remains unclear. The Space Force says the system is "on track to deliver regional capability this year and global support by next year."

## Competition concerns

A draft Senate appropriations bill said the Space Force appeared to "abandon meaningful competition in favor of sole-source awards" for the Backbone and the Air Moving Target Indication (AMTI) program, and directed officials to keep multiple vendors. Space Force officials say competition will remain key.

SpaceX also holds a $4.2 billion AMTI contract. A classified mission, USSF-259, launched Sept. 16 with a payload the FAA labeled "TH-1", and TH-2 followed in October. The next classified Vandenberg mission, USSF-266, is scheduled for Nov. 24; we [previewed it here](/2026/11/23/spacex-ussf-266-th-3-vandenberg-preview/).
