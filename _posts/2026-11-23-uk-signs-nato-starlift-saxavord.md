The UK government said on 22 November that it has signed STARLIFT, NATO's initiative to build a network of flexible launch sites for allied military satellites. It is the UK's own announcement of a commitment that [NATO described last week](/2026/11/19/nato-starlift-launch-initiative/) as moving into implementation.

According to the government release, the UK's National Armaments Director signed in Brussels, alongside Belgium, Canada, Finland, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Türkiye, Sweden and South Korea. STARLIFT is built around commercial launch providers and infrastructure, with the aim of making access to space more resilient, responsive and cost-effective.

## Four starting projects

The release lists four initial workstreams: common ways for allies to identify, aggregate and contract launch capacity; development and testing of responsive launch operations; strengthening the infrastructure and logistics needed to keep access to space open; and tackling regulatory barriers while improving air, maritime and space traffic management.

## The SaxaVord link

London frames the move as part of the UK Space Strategy, which it says commits to collective defence through NATO, assured access to space and less reliance on any single launch provider. The release points to SaxaVord Spaceport in Shetland, which it calls the UK's only fully licensed vertical orbital launch spaceport, approved for up to 30 launches a year. In September the government announced £30 million to speed its readiness for regular orbital launches. The release also mentions the UK's recently launched Titania satellite, the second under Programme ISTARI.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said STARLIFT "will pool our strengths, help us meet the threats in this new frontier of conflict." Space Minister Liz Lloyd said it "will strengthen our hand in a contested world."

## What is still missing

Neither the release nor the coverage names launch providers or contract values, and the government does not say that any specific UK launch is planned under STARLIFT. SaxaVord is mentioned as context for UK launch capacity rather than as a named STARLIFT site. Participant counts also differ between outlets: some reports mention 15 allies involved, while the government lists the signatories above. The government release is the reference here.
