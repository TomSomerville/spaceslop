Amazon is moving up its next Falcon 9 launch for the Leo broadband constellation, from 2027 into December, as it works around the loss of ULA's Vulcan on 15 November.

KeepTrack's SpaceX brief reports that the Vulcan failure, which destroyed 40 Leo satellites, has pushed Amazon to accelerate several of the Falcon 9 launches it purchased from SpaceX, and that more flights will follow in quick succession. The mission, listed as LF-04, appears on launch schedules for 31 December from Cape Canaveral, though a date that far out and a placeholder time should be treated as tentative. The brief does not itself name LF-04; the schedule listing comes from public launch calendars.

## Why Falcon 9 and Ariane 6

According to the same brief, Amazon has booked 108 launches and 17 have flown, one of them the failed Vulcan. Blue Origin's New Glenn has been grounded since May, so Falcon 9 and Ariane 6 are the two vehicles currently carrying Amazon hardware. Ariane 6 launched 36 more satellites on 24 November, taking the fleet to 432, per KeepTrack.

Vulcan was Amazon's largest single order and the rocket is now sidelined while ULA investigates. Amazon had already said it expects initial service to begin this year, as covered in our [earlier article](/2026/11/20/amazon-leo-service-rollout-after-vulcan-loss/). Pulling Falcon 9 flights forward is the practical way to keep satellite deliveries moving, and it puts a key competitor's rocket at the center of Amazon's schedule.

This report rests on a single secondary source, and I could not confirm the schedule with Amazon or SpaceX directly.
