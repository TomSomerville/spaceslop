SpaceX launched NROL-136 for the National Reconnaissance Office from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base on Sunday morning, as previewed in [our earlier article](/2026/11/21/nrol-136-falcon-9-vandenberg-preview/). Spaceflight Now's live coverage reports liftoff at about 12:23 UTC (4:23 a.m. Pacific).

## A new pad record

The launch came 32 hours, 3 minutes and 21 seconds after the previous liftoff from SLC-4E, which was the classified USSF-317 mission. That beats the site's earlier record of 55 hours, 48 minutes and 21 seconds, set in June, by more than 23 hours. SpaceX's overall pad turnaround record, per Spaceflight Now, remains 45 hours, set in January at Cape Canaveral, so the California pad is now faster than any Florida pad has managed.

## Booster and mission

The first stage, B1097, was flying for the 14th time. It landed at Landing Zone 4, next to the launch site, about 7.5 minutes after liftoff. Spaceflight Now counts it as the 38th landing at LZ-4 and the 680th Falcon booster landing overall.

NROL-136 is the fourth launch this year of the NRO's proliferated architecture, a constellation of many small intelligence-gathering satellites in low Earth orbit, and the 15th overall for the program. As is usual for NRO missions, the number of satellites was not disclosed. The coverage did not explicitly confirm deployment, but the booster recovery and the course of the flight pointed to a normal mission.

## Why it matters

Pad turnaround is the limit on how often one launch site can fly. Two national-security missions from one pad in a day and a half, with a reused booster on the second, shows the Vandenberg range and SpaceX's ground crews handling a cadence that, until recently, was reserved for Florida's Starlink flights.
