Avio's third Vega C launch of 2026 is drifting toward the holidays. CEO Giulio Ranzo said on the company's third-quarter earnings call on 11 November that it is waiting for the last few customer satellites, which he described as "a little bit late", according to European Spaceflight.

The mission will carry six satellites for Italy's IRIDE Earth observation constellation. On 10 September Ranzo had said it was set for "the very beginning of December", so the slip is roughly two to three weeks. CNES photos show the Thales Alenia Space-built satellites reached French Guiana by air on 9 November and moved into the Guiana Space Centre's S3B payload preparation building the next day.

Avio has flown two Vega C missions this year. The latest, on 15 September, put ESA's FLEX and the European Commission's Sentinel-3C into orbit. On the September call Ranzo said the company could have flown more than three Vega C missions in 2026 but for another late satellite.

The pattern matters because Avio wants four and eventually five Vega C launches a year, a goal Ranzo ties to "if the customers maintain their deadlines". In May he floated as many as five flights in 2027, and in November he spoke of a more gradual ramp-up, which European Spaceflight reads as pointing to four. Avio is also, separately, the subject of a takeover bid from ICEYE, announced this week.
