SpaceX launched the classified USSF-266 mission for the U.S. Space Force from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base on Tuesday, following the [preview we published on Monday](/2026/11/23/spacex-ussf-266-th-3-vandenberg-preview/). The launch window opened at 1555 UTC (7:55 a.m. Pacific), and the Launch Library board used by this site showed the rocket in flight shortly after that. Anadolu Agency also carried a report that the launch took place. We could not read a full post-launch account, so the final outcome is not independently confirmed here.

## What was planned

Spaceflight Now's pre-launch coverage said the Falcon 9 first stage, booster B1081, was on its 29th flight. Earlier missions for that booster include NASA's Crew-7, PACE and TRACERS. It was to land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific, less than 8.5 minutes after liftoff. A successful landing would be the 236th on that ship and the 681st Falcon booster recovery overall. Whether the landing happened is not something we have confirmed.

## The "TH" series

USSF-266 is the third mission in a series Spaceflight Now calls "TH", after USSF-259 (TH-1, Sept. 16) and USSF-481 (TH-2, Oct. 15). The outlet reports that these flights are thought to belong to a $1.6 billion task order for 18 Falcon 9 launches under the Space Force's Space Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio, awarded to SpaceX in late July, with launches expected to wrap up by the end of 2027. The Space Force does not disclose payload details. After each earlier TH mission, the outlet says 17 satellites appeared in the orbital tracking catalogue, which gives a rough idea of what to expect.

## Why it matters

The mission is another entry in an unusually dense run of government launches from Vandenberg this month, including USSF-317 and NROL-136 within about 32 hours of each other. With the Vulcan fleet grounded after its recent failure, SpaceX is carrying much of the Pentagon's launch load, and the TH block alone commits it to roughly 15 more flights over the next year.
