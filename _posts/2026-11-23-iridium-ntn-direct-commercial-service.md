Iridium announced on November 17 that it has activated commercial service on Iridium NTN Direct, a narrowband IoT offering that lets devices built to the 3GPP cellular standard talk to its satellites. The company describes it as the first standards-based direct-to-device (D2D) IoT service to enter commercial operation over a mobile satellite services constellation.

The service runs on Iridium's existing L-band low Earth orbit fleet, so no new satellites are involved. Because it follows the 3GPP non-terrestrial network standard, some compatible chipsets can reach the constellation without proprietary hardware. Nordic Semiconductor plans a January firmware update for its nRF9151 module to support it, according to Via Satellite. Iridium claims global coverage based on its coordinated L-band spectrum, though authorization to offer the service still depends on each country's rules.

## Who it is for

Iridium is not pitching this at smartphones. The target list is smartwatches, personal safety and emergency messaging, asset tracking, fleet management, logistics, connected vehicles, agriculture, utility metering and remote industrial monitoring. Via Satellite reports that Iridium showed a Toyota vehicle sending a voice message over the service in a September demonstration. Deutsche Telekom IoT and Vodafone IoT were named earlier as roaming partners, and neither pricing nor a full partner list was published in the coverage reviewed.

CEO Matt Desch said standards are "opening the door to a new generation of connected products."

## Context

The standards route is a change of course. The effort began in 2024 as Project Stardust after Iridium's planned arrangement with Qualcomm to embed its proprietary technology in Qualcomm chips ended, Via Satellite says. That leaves Iridium competing for the satellite D2D market with a different pitch than the broadband-to-phone constellations from other operators: low data rates, low power and true global reach rather than speed.

Via Satellite also notes that Iridium is in the process of being acquired by Rocket Lab, which has pointed to D2D as an opportunity. This run did not independently confirm the status of that deal, so treat it as that outlet's reporting.

What to watch next is device uptake. Service availability will vary by country, and the commercial case depends on chipmakers and carriers shipping NTN-capable hardware at volume.
