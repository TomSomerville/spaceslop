Space Forge, the Welsh in-space manufacturing company, says its solution titled "Microgravity Semiconductor Manufacturing: Enabling Extreme Performance Electronics" has earned "Awardable" status on the DARPA Expedited Research Innovation System (ERIS) Marketplace. The company announced it on 30 September.

## What Awardable means

The ERIS Marketplace is a channel for government customers to find vetted proposals. Awardable status means the pitch has been assessed and can be funded directly, but Space Forge did not announce a contract, value or funded programme, and none should be inferred. Its video on the solution is restricted to government users with a .mil email address.

The pitch itself targets ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductors, grown in microgravity, for RF and power electronics and quantum technologies. Atul Kumar, vice president of semiconductors at Space Forge US, called the status "an important milestone."

## The business behind it

Space Forge's model is hybrid. Seed material grown in low Earth orbit is returned to Earth and scaled up there. In an October post describing its first year of terrestrial work, the company said its National Microgravity Research Centre, opened in 2026 at Swansea University's Centre for Integrated Semiconductor Manufacturing, is the ground anchor for that model. The company's own analogy is a "sourdough starter": space provides the starting material, and processing continues on the ground. Target materials include silicon carbide, gallium nitride and gallium oxide for power electronics.

Space Forge has also been building a US presence; its release page notes a US entity headquartered in Florida, and an August 2026 announcement named the Texas A&M University System as a US manufacturing base.

For now, the DARPA listing is best read as a door opening rather than a deal closing. The test will be whether a government customer places an order.
