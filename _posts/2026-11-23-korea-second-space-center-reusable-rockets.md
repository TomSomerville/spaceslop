South Korea's space agency, KASA, now expects to name the site of the country's second space center around December 2026, according to MoneyToday. Two bids are in: Jeonnam-Gwangju, which is promoting Goheung County, and Jeju Island. The Korea Times had reported in August that the announcement would come in October, so the timeline has slipped by about two months.

## What the site is for

The new center is meant to be the main launch site for reusable rockets and private commercial vehicles. The Korea Times says it would handle launch, landing and maintenance facilities on roughly 5.62 million square meters by 2034, with a target of more than 10 reusable-vehicle operations a year from the mid-to-late 2030s. Korea relies mostly on the existing Naro Space Center, which limits support for small and solid-fuel rockets from private companies.

The two bids differ. Goheung already hosts Naro, so a new center nearby could join existing facilities, a private launch complex and an industrial park into one cluster. Jeju has reportedly proposed a floating launch facility off Seogwipo, a site already used for military solid-fuel launches, including one by the defense ministry in December 2023, to fly small satellites. KASA has not named exact sites, and says it first checks that applicants meet basic requirements, then assesses launch operability and infrastructure.

KASA administrator Oh Tae-seok called the center "the country's key space logistics infrastructure in the New Space Era."

## The launch plan behind it

MoneyToday's report lays out KASA's near-term manifest. The agency plans at least one Nuri launch a year from 2027 to 2032 and will try two in 2030. Per the report, the sixth Nuri flight in 2027 carries microsatellite constellation satellites 7 to 11, and the 2030 launches include South Korea's first small lunar lander. Oh said the fifth Nuri launch separated all 14 of its satellites and established initial communications, and KASA puts the success rate of Korean-made rockets at 80 percent. Those are official claims; the report does not verify them.

The reusable-rocket program runs on its own track: a flight test of a 12-meter small-scale test vehicle in 2028, a vertical takeoff and landing test to recover a first stage in 2032, and completed reuse technology expected in 2035. MoneyToday notes its English edition is machine-translated and details may differ from the Korean original.

Construction at the new center is scheduled to start in 2028, per the Korea Times. A second slip in the decision date would not yet threaten that schedule, but it leaves private launch companies without a confirmed home for their reusable-vehicle plans.
