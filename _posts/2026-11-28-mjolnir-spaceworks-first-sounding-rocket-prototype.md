Mjolnir Spaceworks, a Sapporo startup that spun out of Hokkaido University, said on 28 November that it has built its first complete sounding-rocket prototype and used it to rehearse a launch campaign.

According to the company's press release, the vehicle brings together parts the company had so far developed separately: its hybrid rocket engine, its seamless, weld-free propellant tanks and other propulsion hardware. They now sit in one airframe, and ground support equipment was tested alongside it. The rehearsal took place in April 2026 in Minamisoma, Fukushima Prefecture, and covered both dry and wet (fuelled) runs meant to mimic a real launch, from design and assembly through ground operations.

## The vehicle

The company gives the prototype's length as about 7 metres, with a diameter of roughly 50 centimetres and a liftoff mass near 600 kilograms. Maximum thrust is in the 40-kilonewton class. A hybrid engine pairs a solid fuel with a liquid or gaseous oxidiser, which the company says makes it simpler to handle than a conventional solid or liquid motor.

The release does not give a launch date, an altitude target or a payload figure. The company says only that it will carry the results into its sounding rocket and into a future orbital launch vehicle. A headline in Hokkaido Shimbun's report on the news says the company is aiming to fly within a few years. We could not read that article in full, so that timeline is taken from its headline.

## Why it matters

Mjolnir, founded in January 2020 and led by CEO Kazuo Yonekura, is pitching itself as much as a parts maker as a launch company. The stated plan is to standardise and mass-produce engines and tanks so they can be made cheaply and repeatably, then sell anything from single components to whole rockets to domestic and overseas customers. The release frames this against launch capacity being concentrated in the United States and China, with some satellite operators waiting years for a slot.

The company credits support from NEDO's Deep Tech Startup programme, in its early-stage practical R&D phase. No new funding round is mentioned.

Yonekura called the rehearsal "a major step forward" in the release. Japan now has several small hybrid-engine startups, and Mjolnir's case rests on production engineering rather than a single vehicle. The next signal to watch is whether it announces an actual flight.
