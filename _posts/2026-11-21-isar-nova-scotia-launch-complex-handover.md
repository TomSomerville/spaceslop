Isar Aerospace and Maritime Launch Services (MLS) announced on November 21 in Halifax that a dedicated launch complex at Spaceport Nova Scotia is being handed over to the German rocket company, which will now build and run its own infrastructure there. Construction of Isar's first Canadian launch pad begins now.

According to Isar's release, the complex will serve the Spectrum rocket and include a final assembly line and a payload preparation facility. The first orbital launches from Canada are targeted for 2028. The site will be Isar's second launch location and its first in North America.

## Why it matters

The announcement turns a commercial lease into physical construction. MLS reported earlier this month that Isar signed a ten-year facilities agreement in July, which we covered in our [report on MLS's third-quarter results](/2026/11/20/maritime-launch-q3-profit-nova-scotia/). Until now, much of MLS's income has come from lease accounting rather than launches, so groundwork on a customer-run pad is a more concrete sign of progress.

Isar says it reached orbit on its second mission in September 2026, which it describes as the first time a European commercial company has delivered satellites to orbit. The company says its factory near Munich has capacity for 40 vehicles per year, and that it has over 400 employees across five locations.

## The site and the politics

Spaceport Nova Scotia sits near Canso, on the Atlantic coast. The location allows launches over open ocean to mid-to-high and polar inclinations; MLS puts the supported range at 45 to 98 degrees. The spaceport is pitched at Earth observation, communications and defence customers.

Canadian officials were quick to claim a share of the news. Defence Minister David J. McGuinty said Canada "must have the ability to launch and protect the capabilities we depend on," and the release ties the project to Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy and the Canadian Space Launch Act. Nova Scotia minister Colton LeBlanc said it would create high-value opportunities in the province.

Isar's Chief Commercial Officer Stella Guillen said that starting construction brings the company "a decisive step closer to launching from Canadian soil." MLS CEO Stephen Matier called the handover proof of the "multi-user spaceport model."

Everything here is from Isar's own announcement; no timeline beyond 2028 for first launch has been given, and the release does not state construction costs or a pad completion date.
