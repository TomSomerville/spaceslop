United Launch Alliance lost a Vulcan rocket and its 40 Amazon Leo satellites on 15 November, the first total mission failure in the company's history since it was formed by Boeing and Lockheed Martin in 2006. The launch left Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral at 12:40 p.m. EST. ULA said the vehicle "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight."

According to Spaceflight Now, the solid rocket boosters appeared to perform normally and separated around 1 minute 40 seconds after liftoff. The first stage seemed to burn longer than planned and separated from the Centaur upper stage later than expected, and telemetry showed the Centaur making unusual orientation adjustments early in its burn. ULA has not disclosed a cause. About 50 minutes after liftoff it said it would work with Amazon to find one and return to flight quickly.

## Context

It was only Vulcan's fifth flight and its first in nine months. Earlier flights, in 2024 and February 2026, had nozzle problems on Northrop Grumman's solid boosters, and the Space Force paused Vulcan launches of national security payloads while that was analyzed. ULA CEO Mark Peller said the company had addressed three possible causes. The countdown was also messy: a gaseous nitrogen plant outage, a network fault between rocket and ground, and a hold at T-minus 20 seconds. Whether any of that connects to the failure is unknown.

The first stage's BE-4 engines had been retrofitted with a Blue Origin valve fix that Peller called a "design improvement". Blue Origin's own New Glenn has been grounded since a pad explosion in May that was traced to an oxygen valve on a BE-4. Spaceflight Now draws no link between the two events, and nothing public says the valve was involved.

## Why it matters

This was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon bought, and it would have lifted the constellation's launch total to 436 satellites. Amazon needs more than 3,300 to finish the network and also has 24 New Glenn, 24 Ariane 6 and 13 Falcon 9 launches booked. With Vulcan and New Glenn both stopped, its schedule leans harder on others. Vulcan is also meant to replace ULA's Atlas V fleet. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman offered the agency's help with the investigation.
