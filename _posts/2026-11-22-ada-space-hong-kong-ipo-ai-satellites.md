Chengdu Guoxing Aerospace Technology, known in English as ADA Space, listed on the main board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on November 13 under the ticker 06935.HK. Chinese media are calling it the first "space AI" stock in the city.

The company sold about 30.1 million H-shares at HK$24.90 each, raising roughly HK$652 million (net). Shares opened at HK$24.44 and closed the first session at HK$24.02, a touch under the offer price, for a market value of about HK$14.5 billion. It was the company's third attempt at a listing, after earlier filings in January and August 2025.

## What it sells

ADA Space designs and builds commercial satellites and sells space-based services, with a focus on satellites that process data in orbit. It says it launched China's first AI-application satellite in December 2018 and its first satellite carrying a large AI model in September 2024. In May 2025 it put a 12-satellite batch in orbit that it describes as the world's first AI computing constellation. Per the prospectus-based reporting, Frost & Sullivan ranks it second among Chinese private commercial space firms with a full satellite value chain by 2025 revenue.

The headline ambition is the "Xingsuan" plan: 2,800 computing satellites. The ITU has approved orbital resources for 3,157 satellites, and the company aims to deploy the first 100 by 2028. On listing day it said it had finished assembling five satellites for the plan's third group, due to launch soon. One Chinese report notes the plan remains early and far from realization.

## The numbers

Revenue was RMB 508 million in 2023, RMB 553 million in 2024 and RMB 703 million in 2025, but gross profit fell in 2025 to RMB 143 million from RMB 210 million. First-half 2026 revenue was RMB 226 million. As of October 20 the company reported a backlog of 114 AI satellites worth about RMB 3.5 billion in contract value. 36Kr describes the company as still loss-making because of R&D and satellite build and launch costs.

Proceeds are earmarked for AI satellite R&D, a new Shenzhen research center, acquisitions along the supply chain, loan repayment and working capital.

## Why it matters

Orbital computing has become a fashionable pitch globally, and this listing gives Chinese investors a public-market way to test it. The flat debut suggests enthusiasm is measured: the revenue today comes from building and selling satellites, while the data-center-in-orbit business is a long-dated bet.
