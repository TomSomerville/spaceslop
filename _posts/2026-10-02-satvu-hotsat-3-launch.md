Satellite Vu (SatVu), the UK company building a constellation of high-resolution thermal imaging satellites, said on October 2 that HotSat-3 has launched on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California.

HotSat-3 is the company's third mid-wave infrared (MWIR) satellite with 3.5-metre resolution. It is now entering commissioning, after which it will operate alongside HotSat-2, which began delivering data in June 2026. SatVu says that with two satellites operating it expects to image any location on Earth every one to two days, day or night, with northern latitudes getting up to 12 passes a day.

## Why thermal

Thermal imagery shows heat rather than visible detail, so it can reveal whether a refinery, LNG facility, power plant or data centre is actually running, and how that changes over time. SatVu sells the data to defence and intelligence, economic intelligence and climate resilience customers. CEO and co-founder Anthony Baker said HotSat-3 "is where the constellation redefines what we can deliver for customers."

The company also has HotSat-4 and HotSat-5 in production with launches already secured, per the release. SatVu's own news page separately lists a September 17 announcement of a launch for those two satellites, which the company described as doubling thermal imaging capacity again; we have not read that release.

Commercial thermal imaging from orbit is still a small market, and revisit rate is the limiting factor: one satellite can only catch a site occasionally, which makes it hard to sell as a monitoring product. Getting to a handful of satellites is what lets the company move from one-off pictures towards the tracking of operations that its customers are buying. The release does not give funding or pricing details.
