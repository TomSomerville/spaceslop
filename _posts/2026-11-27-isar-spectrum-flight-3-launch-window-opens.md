Isar Aerospace can now attempt the third flight of its Spectrum rocket from Andøya Spaceport in northern Norway. The window opened on 26 November, but the German company has not said when it intends to fly.

The opening comes from an aviation notice published on 28 October by Avinor, Norway's air navigation service provider. It allows launch attempts every day between 20:00 and 21:30 UTC, from 26 November 2026 through 17 February 2027. That is a wide, roughly twelve-week envelope, and the company has made no announcement of a target date. Some online trackers list 26 November as a placeholder, but nothing from Isar confirms it, so treat any specific date as speculation for now.

## Where Spectrum stands

Spectrum's first flight, in March 2025, ended when the rocket fell into the Norwegian Sea less than a minute after liftoff, within sight of the pad. The second flight, on 5 September 2026, was the vehicle's first successful mission, according to European Spaceflight.

Isar says vehicles three through seven are already in production. At the press conference after the second flight, CEO Daniel Metzler said the third rocket was in "final assembly", after which it would go through acceptance testing before being shipped to Norway. Whether that work is finished is not public.

## Payloads

Isar has not released a full manifest. Austrian satellite company R-Space confirmed in June that its AT-Astra satellite will fly. The spacecraft is also expected to carry technology demonstrations for Portugal's Synopsis Planet and for Austrian companies ENPULSION and SunBooster.

## Why it matters

Europe's push for independent small-launch capability depends on Spectrum flying often, not just once. A second success in a row from the same pad would be the first real sign of a cadence. Isar has also started building a second launch site in Canada, covered in our earlier piece on the [Nova Scotia launch complex](/2026/11/21/isar-nova-scotia-launch-complex-handover/). With a window this long and no date, the practical signal to watch is a shipping announcement or a notice to mariners, which would tell outsiders that a countdown is close.
