The cargo Dragon will stay on the International Space Station for at least another day. NASA and SpaceX have stood down from Wednesday's planned undocking of the spacecraft, citing unfavorable weather at the splashdown sites off the coast of California, according to a post on NASA's station blog.

The next opportunity is Thursday, 19 November, at 12:05 p.m. EST. Mission teams are scheduled to review the weather again at noon EST on Wednesday, and NASA says it will publish more information as conditions become clearer.

## What changed

Our [earlier report](/2026/11/17/dragon-crs-35-undock-date/) had the capsule leaving the Harmony module's space-facing port on 18 November at 11:05 a.m. EST, with a Pacific splashdown early on 19 November. That plan is now off. The new target moves undocking roughly a day later, and the splashdown would follow it. NASA's post does not give a revised splashdown time, so treat any earlier figures as superseded until the agency updates its schedule.

## Why weather matters

Dragon returns by parachute into the ocean, and recovery crews need acceptable sea states and winds at the landing zone, along with usable backup zones. Teams choose an undocking time that lines up the spacecraft's path with those zones, so a poor forecast for the recovery area can move the whole departure even when the station and spacecraft are ready to go. A stand-down is a routine, conservative call rather than a sign of a problem with the vehicle, and NASA's post cites only the weather.

The mission is SpaceX's 35th commercial resupply flight for NASA. It is bringing home science and supplies from the station, including the biological samples described in our earlier article, which are time-sensitive because they need a quick handoff to researchers after splashdown. A day's delay is unlikely to change that plan in a meaningful way, but it does leave the schedule dependent on a forecast the teams will be re-checking within hours.
