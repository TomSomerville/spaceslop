NASA's first pieces of Moon Base power infrastructure are taking shape, and the hardest problem is dust. Payload reported on November 23 that Yank Technologies, a Brooklyn company best known for wireless chargers in cars, has detailed its part of the Mini-Communications and Solar Tower, or miniCAST.

NASA announced three awards for the project on November 7 during a Moon Base update at MAX POWER. Redwire will generate the power with its roll-out solar arrays (ROSA), Blue Origin's Honeybee Robotics will store it, and Yank will supply wireless power transfer hardware. Marshall Space Flight Center will assemble and test the pieces before they head to the lunar surface. Redwire's award is worth $40 million.

## Why wireless

Lunar regolith clings to exposed connector terminals and can make plugged-in connections unusable. Yank CEO Josh Yank said plugs on the Moon could be reconnected only a few times before buildup ruined them. His company's answer is contactless charging plates that pass power across a small gap, so rovers do not need to plug in. Yank is adapting plates it has used on Earth, and says the first ones delivered up to 1 kW at better than 80 percent efficiency. It is also developing dust-tolerant connectors with insulated ends for cables, which carry up to 6 kW. It is in talks with other prime contractors.

Redwire's ROSA arrays already power the ISS. On the Moon they would retract during landings and launches to avoid regolith kicked up by engine exhaust, then redeploy. The company also plans panel-cleaning scrubbers and power distribution for future rovers and habitats. Redwire's Mike Gold called regolith the Moon's top enemy: "It will attack our people. It will attack our machines."

## Next

NASA expects a separate solicitation for fully integrated power systems in early 2027, so miniCAST is a proving ground for what that larger competition will want.
