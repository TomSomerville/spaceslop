CAS Space flew its Kinetica-2 rocket for the second time on November 3, lifting off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center and delivering 18 satellites for China's Spacesail constellation, also known as Qianfan. The mission is the first time the rocket has deployed a large batch for the country's internet satellite network, according to China Daily.

The satellites made up the 21st group of Spacesail spacecraft. Yicai Global, which calls the rocket Kinetica-2 Y2, says a new release mechanism developed with CAS Space unlocked all 18 satellites simultaneously, rather than freeing them one at a time as traditional dispensers do. Public launch trackers number the batch differently ("Polar Group #17"), and the sources read for this article do not reconcile the two schemes.

## The rocket

Kinetica-2 is CAS Space's first liquid-propellant vehicle, following the solid-fuelled Kinetica-1. China Daily describes a 53-metre rocket with a multistage core and two side boosters, each 3.35 metres across, a liftoff mass of 625 tonnes and maximum thrust of 753 tonnes. Stated capacity is about 8 tonnes to a 500 km sun-synchronous orbit or 12 tonnes to a 200 km low Earth orbit. Yicai says it is China's first rocket built around a "universal booster core".

The vehicle is also intended to carry low-cost cargo to the Chinese space station; its debut flight carried a prototype cargo spacecraft, per a separate report indexed by trackers, which this article did not independently verify.

## Why it matters

Spacesail, developed by Shanghai-based Yuanxin Satellite Technology, had 274 satellites in orbit after this launch, according to Yicai. The project targets global coverage by 2027 and more than 15,000 low-orbit satellites by 2030. Reaching that scale needs far more launch capacity than the long-established state rockets alone provide, which is why a second commercial medium-lift vehicle certified for constellation batches matters.

CAS Space, a Beijing company established by the Chinese Academy of Sciences, called this its 18th orbital launch. It says it has put 139 satellites and an experimental cargo spacecraft, totalling more than 27 tonnes, into orbit. It also says Kinetica-2 will fly multi-satellite network missions next year.
