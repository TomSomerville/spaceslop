Starfish Space's Otter 24C spacecraft launched at 11:32 a.m. PDT on 1 October on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, as part of the Transporter-18 rideshare mission, according to NASA. The company had announced a week earlier, as Via Satellite reported on 25 September, that its first Otter servicing vehicle would fly on the mission. Co-founder Austin Link was quoted then saying, "This is the moment Otter becomes real."

## What it will do

Otter 24C carries NASA's SSPICY demonstration, short for Small Spacecraft Propulsion and Inspection Capability. NASA says Otter will inspect up to four inoperable US-origin satellites in low Earth orbit, assessing their surface condition, geometry and orbital characteristics. The mission uses new propulsion and autonomous guidance, navigation and control technology, and Starfish is also testing an articulating robotic boom for directing thrust and computer-vision software for safe approaches. NASA expects it to last about two years, with inspections starting in 2027.

Via Satellite describes Otter as designed for life-extension and end-of-life disposal work, able to operate across several orbital regimes and to service spacecraft that weren't built to be serviced. After launch, the company says it will commission the vehicle and then prepare to offer commercial services in low Earth orbit. NASA's note adds that Maverick Space Systems supported integration of Otter 24C, along with two other spacecraft on the flight.

## Why it matters

Satellite servicing has spent years being promised more than delivered, and a working inspection vehicle is the first step toward the less glamorous jobs: looking at a dead satellite before deciding whether to repair, move or deorbit it. SSPICY is a government-funded demonstration rather than a commercial service, but it puts a full-scale Otter in orbit ahead of other Starfish work. Neither source here covers those contracts in detail.

Neither NASA's note nor the Via Satellite report says whether Otter 24C has completed commissioning. Starfish's own news page, as read for this article, lists only press mentions rather than a dated release on the launch, so the details above come from NASA and trade reporting.
