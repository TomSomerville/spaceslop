The Senate Commerce Committee on 18 November reported out the nominations of Joshua Shoemaker as NASA chief financial officer and Robert Steinau as NASA inspector general, along with FCC nominee Danielle Thumann Severs. Each passed 15-0, according to SpacePolicyOnline, but only because no Democrats showed up.

All 15 Republicans on the committee attended and none of the Democrats did. Committee rules require a quorum that includes at least one minority-party member, but Chairman Ted Cruz (R-TX) ordered the nominations reported regardless. Cruz said there is "ample precedent for advancing nominations during a lame duck session," and pointed to December 2024, when the committee under Democratic leadership advanced eight Biden nominees before the new Congress was sworn in. Republicans did not boycott that markup.

## Why Democrats stayed away

The objections centered on the FCC pick rather than the NASA ones. Democrats complained about moving nominees in a lame-duck session and about Thumann Severs, currently senior counsel to FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, being put forward without a Democratic nominee alongside her, which breaks with long-standing practice. They want Commissioner Anna Gomez renominated; her term expired in June, though she can serve in holdover status through the end of 2027. Democrats fear she could be fired once a third Republican joins the commission, citing the Supreme Court's 29 June ruling in *Trump v. Slaughter*, which let presidents remove independent-agency officials without cause. Ranking member Maria Cantwell (D-WA), expected to take back the gavel in January, issued a statement opposing the process: "This is not a problem we created, but a problem that the White House can solve."

## What it means for NASA

Both NASA slots have been empty of confirmed officials for a long time, as we reported when the [nominees had their hearing on 10 November](/2026/11/12/nasa-cfo-inspector-general-nominees/). Shoemaker was the Republican National Committee's controller from March 2025 to March 2026 and has no apparent space background. Steinau has been in the NASA inspector general's office since 2009, was acting inspector general from December 2024 until 18 September, and stepped aside from that role when nominated; he now serves as assistant inspector general for investigations.

The committee vote only clears them for the Senate floor. How much floor time a lame-duck Senate gives them is not yet known, and the nominations would need to be confirmed before the new Congress convenes in January or be renominated.
