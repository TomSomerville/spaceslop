Mexico's federal environmental prosecutor, Profepa, sent inspectors to Playa Bagdad in Tamaulipas on Tuesday after debris believed to come from Starship's 15th test flight washed up on the beach, which sits just south of SpaceX's Starbase site across the Texas border.

## What turned up

According to the Rio Times, citing local reporting, Profepa is identifying the material and coordinating its seizure, will ask "the responsible company" to take charge of it, and has asked the public not to touch anything and to report finds. Two national commissions, Conanp and Conabio, were reportedly collecting pieces, and the Mexican Navy has kept a presence since the flight.

The pieces described are white plastic with gray tape, black rubber and fragments with blue glue. La Crónica de Hoy reported that the environmental group CONIBIO Global counted at least 1,500 pieces, including what the paper described as fragments of the inner propellant lining, and estimated that the total in the sea could reach millions. That figure is the group's estimate, not an official finding. High tides and a cold front made the beach hard to work, the paper said.

## Link to Flight 15

Flight 15 launched Nov. 13 from Starbase. The ship reached orbit, deployed 26 Starlink satellites and was caught at the launch tower the next day, as covered in our [earlier report](/2026/11/14/starship-ship-catch-flight-15/). The Rio Times says the debris followed an explosion that day; La Crónica attributes components detaching during operations to the flight and says they drifted toward Tamaulipas. Neither article establishes exactly which piece of hardware the fragments came from, and neither reports an official determination.

## What's missing

Neither outlet carried a SpaceX statement, and neither reported whether the material poses any risk. Profepa has not published a date for its request to the company or any lab results. Debris on this stretch of coast is not new, and the Rio Times notes that Mexico ruled out a lawsuit for now in July. For a vehicle meant to fly often, how Starbase handles cross-border debris is shaping up as a regulatory question to watch alongside the FAA's environmental reviews.
