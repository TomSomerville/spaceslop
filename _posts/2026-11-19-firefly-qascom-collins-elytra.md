Firefly Aerospace has signed QASCOM to fly its COLLINS navigation payload on the Elytra orbiter that will accompany the company's Blue Ghost lander to the Moon's Gruithuisen Domes, a mission targeted for no earlier than 2028. The deal was announced on Tuesday, November 17.

COLLINS is built by QASCOM for the Italian Space Agency (ASI). It is meant to show how future spacecraft can navigate near the Moon using GNSS signals and emerging LunaNet positioning, navigation and timing services, including ESA's Moonlight. It builds on LuGRE, a joint ASI-NASA payload that flew on Blue Ghost Mission 1 and was the first to track GNSS signals on the lunar surface.

## A commercial slot, not a CLPS one

The notable detail is the contracting route. LuGRE went to the Moon through NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) initiative. COLLINS is onboarded under a direct commercial agreement between QASCOM and Firefly, as part of a public-private co-investment effort between ASI and QASCOM. Financial terms were not disclosed.

On the mission, Elytra first delivers the Blue Ghost lander into lunar orbit. The lander then touches down and supports roughly 14 days of surface payload operations. Elytra relays communications for the lander and runs six months of COLLINS operations, with an option to extend. Afterward the vehicle is planned to stay in lunar orbit for five years to support Firefly's Ocula lunar imaging service. The Blue Ghost mission now carries eight payloads, six of them delivered through CLPS.

## Why it matters

Firefly is increasingly selling Elytra as a standalone lunar-orbit platform rather than only a lander's ride. Firefly CEO Jason Kim said the company is "rapidly growing spacecraft production as we continue to see increasing demand" from commercial and government customers. QASCOM CEO Oscar Pozzobon called the deployment "a step toward validating next-generation lunar positioning, navigation and timing services," with possible uses in autonomous lunar navigation, mobility and precision landing.
