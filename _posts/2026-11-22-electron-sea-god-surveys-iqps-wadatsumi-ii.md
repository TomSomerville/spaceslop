Rocket Lab launched a synthetic aperture radar (SAR) satellite for Japanese operator iQPS on an Electron from Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand, at 16:41 UTC on November 2 (5:41 a.m. local on November 3). The mission, "The Sea God Surveys," put the WADATSUMI-II satellite into a 575 km low Earth orbit, according to New Space Economy. This is a catch-up report on a flight that had not appeared on SpaceSlop.

It was Electron's 99th flight, and the one immediately before the 100th, which flew [on November 14](/2026/11/14/rocket-lab-100th-electron/) with another iQPS satellite. New Space Economy counts it as Rocket Lab's 20th mission of 2026 and iQPS's tenth consecutive successful launch. A launch tracker listing records the outcome as a success with the satellite in contact.

## The satellite

WADATSUMI-II joins iQPS's QPS-SAR constellation. By iQPS's published design, as summarised by New Space Economy, the roughly 100 kg-class spacecraft unfolds a deployable antenna from about 80 cm to 3.6 m once in orbit. It offers stripmap and spotlight imaging modes, with spotlight covering a 7 by 7 km scene at 0.46 m resolution. The orbital inclination was chosen to improve revisit coverage over the Asia-Pacific region.

## What's next

iQPS is one of Electron's steadiest customers. Rocket Lab reports eight more dedicated Electron launches for the constellation through 2030, and New Space Economy adds that iQPS has also signed a separate agreement with France's MaiaSpace for launches starting in 2029. The outlet's figures for the number of remaining flights differ slightly from other reports, so treat the exact count as approximate.
