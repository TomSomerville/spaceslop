The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality issued SpaceX an air permit on Monday, November 16, for a gas-fired power plant that would run its planned $24 billion "super-compute" campus beside Starbase. Politico first reported the approval on November 17, and a TCEQ spokesperson confirmed it, saying the agency had "resumed its normal permit review process." SpaceX did not respond to Politico's requests for comment.

## What was approved

The permit covers 35 natural gas turbines with roughly 750 megawatts of capacity on 444 acres near Brownsville, land that was annexed into Starbase in September. The plant would not connect to the state grid, which is how it sidesteps the grid-connection scrutiny that has slowed other Texas data centers. According to reporting that cites The 956 Report, the initial turbines come from GE Vernova, Mitsubishi and Solar Turbines, and could later be swapped for eight larger GE Vernova 7E units.

The emissions cap is the detail drawing the most attention. The filing allows up to about 248 tons of nitrogen oxides a year, just under the 250-ton line that triggers fuller review including a public hearing. Tech Times reports the limit is 248.86 tons and that startup and shutdown emissions are not counted toward it. The 956 Report calculated that running all 35 turbines flat out would emit around 458 tons a year; that is the outlet's own arithmetic, not a TCEQ finding.

## The complaint and the pause

Three days before the approval, FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen filed a complaint with TCEQ alleging SpaceX had started construction without a permit, which they say would violate the Clean Air Act. They cite imagery from October 9 to November 12. The regulator did not respond to that complaint in the coverage reviewed.

Timing matters here. Gov. Greg Abbott's September 21 letter had paused data center permit approvals. TCEQ resumed reviews after Abbott's office told it on November 13 that grid officials had finished collecting community-impact information. ERCOT, the state grid operator, is due to present broader findings to the Public Utility Commission on December 10.

## What's next

The air permit is separate from the money question. Cameron County commissioners recently voted down SpaceX's tax-break request, as covered in our [earlier article](/2026/11/18/spacex-starbase-data-center-tax-break/), and the Starbase city commission takes up a property tax abatement and development agreement at a 7 p.m. hearing on December 9. Two of the three commissioners voting are SpaceX executives, according to Politico via Teslarati. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who takes office January 19, has said she will block data center approvals until the legislature passes new protections, though a behind-the-meter plant like this one may fall outside that approach.
