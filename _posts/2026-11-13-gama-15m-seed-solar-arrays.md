Gama, a French startup making ultra-light deployable solar arrays, has raised a €15 million seed round, Payload reports. Investors include Transition Ventures, Planet A, Draper Associates and Manta Ray. Payload calls it one of the largest seed rounds for a European space startup in recent years.

The money will pay for a high-volume factory outside Paris able to build 100 MW of arrays a year, with the first production units, called Wings, due next year. Gama plans to hire about 60 mechanical, electrical and software engineers, deliver a first demonstration Wing in the middle of next year, and target $10 per watt.

The company began by developing solar sails for deep-space propulsion and moved to arrays to address supply-chain problems. CEO Andrew Nutter says solar cells come from a small number of providers and lead times can reach a year or more.

Gama's technical bet is to make arrays as thin and light as possible and handle the resulting flexibility in software. As arrays grow larger and more flexible they capture sunlight less well, and the usual fix is adding stiffening mass. Gama's software runs on customers' flight computers and points the whole spacecraft more precisely instead.

In September Gama signed a partnership with US satellite builder Eclipse Space to supply deployable arrays for its platforms. The target market is Europe's growing set of satellite-bus makers, and Nutter argues that pooling volume across several of them could let Gama "give the industry cheaper prices."
