CyberCUBE, a small satellite designed as an in-orbit testbed for cyber defense, has finished commissioning and moved into nominal operations. Spanish company GMV, which led the mission, handed operational control to the European Space Agency's operations team after commissioning tests at ESA's ESEC centre in Redu, Belgium, according to a November 19 post from Alén Space, the cubesat's builder.

## What it does

The mission is described as an in-orbit laboratory for testing technologies that protect space assets against evolving cyber threats. It is meant to let engineers assess cybersecurity monitoring, analysis and protection capabilities both on the ground and in orbit, so that the solutions can mature ahead of future European missions.

The spacecraft is a 3U cubesat, roughly three stacked ten-centimetre units, built by Alén Space with reprogrammable processing and a cybersecurity monitoring payload. Reprogrammable processing matters for a testbed: it lets researchers load and swap defensive tools after launch rather than freezing the design on the ground.

## Who built what

GMV ran the project through teams in Romania and Spain. The Spanish team built key ground segment elements, including Alén Space's mission control centre, which is based on GMV's FocusSuite software. Both teams also built a representative flatsat, a ground replica of the spacecraft's electronics, used to simulate and validate operations before and during flight.

Day-to-day operations now sit with ESA's ESEC team. ESOC's FOX facility in Germany provides S-band and X-band communications, along with radio-frequency sampling to detect interference, supported by ESA's Estrack ground-station network.

## Why it matters

Satellite cybersecurity has moved up the agenda as jamming, spoofing and intrusion concerns have grown, and a cheap cubesat is a low-risk place to try defenses before they go on expensive spacecraft. The Alén Space post does not give the orbit, launch date or launch vehicle, so those details remain unconfirmed here.
