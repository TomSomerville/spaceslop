NASA on November 23 put out a request for proposals for the first nuclear reactor designed to make electricity on the Moon, asking industry to design and test a small fission power system called Lunar Reactor-1, or LR-1.

The agency wants LR-1 to produce 20 kilowatts of electric power, which it equates to roughly 16 average U.S. households for a year. The target is a launch-ready system by 2030 that can operate on the surface for at least five years after deployment. Proposals are due December 21.

## Why a reactor

Solar power struggles on the Moon because a lunar night lasts about two weeks. A reactor delivers continuous output through darkness, which NASA says will support habitats, communications, rovers and science instruments under its Moon Base plans. The agency also framed the work as a stepping stone to power systems for Mars.

## Who is running it

NASA's Space Reactors Division leads the project together with the Department of Energy, and the reactor must meet DOE safety standards. The request reflects feedback from an August draft and a September industry day in Cleveland. LR-1 program manager Brian Giblin said in NASA's announcement that "a reliable source of safe and abundant energy will be vital to future exploration of the lunar and Martian surface."

## Context

The solicitation sits alongside smaller Moon Base power work such as the miniCAST solar and storage tower. Fission is the long pole in the surface power plan: solar arrays and batteries can supply early outposts, but anything resembling a permanent base will need steady, higher-capacity power. A 2030 launch-ready target is tight for a nuclear flight system, so schedule is the thing to watch once awards are made.

NASA's release did not state a contract value or the number of awards it expects.
