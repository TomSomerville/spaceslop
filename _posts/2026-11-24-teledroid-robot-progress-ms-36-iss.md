Russia's newest cargo ship to the International Space Station is carrying more than propellant and supplies. Roscosmos has said that Teledroid, an anthropomorphic robot meant to help cosmonauts with work outside the station, is due to arrive on Progress MS-36, which lifted off from Baikonur on Nov. 24 (covered in our earlier [launch report](/2026/11/24/progress-ms-36-launches-baikonur/)). Unite.AI headlined its coverage as the robot having launched on the ship; the detailed delivery plan below comes from Interfax's report of Roscosmos' statement, which gave the arrival as November.

## What it is

Teledroid is built by NPO Android Technology, with the experiment led by the TsNIIMash research institute, according to Interfax. It is designed to withstand vacuum, radiation and temperature swings, and cosmonauts operate it through an exoskeleton rather than a joystick. The control hardware went up first: the exoskeleton flew on Progress MS-35 on Sept. 16, 2026.

The robot can work in manual mode, automatically, or on voice commands. In a supervised mode a crew member assigns a task and the robot uses machine vision to find and pick up tools.

## The experiment

The plan has two stages. First, the robot is controlled inside the station, with tasks that include fetching objects from storage, simulating handing a tool to a cosmonaut, simulating tool use and putting objects back. In the second stage, cosmonauts Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina are to install the robot's external part on the Russian Nauka module during a spacewalk, after which they can control it from inside. Kikina trained on Earth as the robot's operator.

## Context

Teledroid follows FEDOR, the Skybot F-850 humanoid that Russia sent to the station in 2019 to test operator control and fine hand movements in microgravity. The new robot is aimed at a different job: taking over routine exterior tasks so crews spend less time on spacewalks.

Timing has slipped before, and the exterior installation depends on spacewalk scheduling that Roscosmos has not published. Progress MS-36 itself is not due to dock until Nov. 25 or 26, depending on the source, so the robot will not be unpacked for days.
