GomSpace, the Danish small-satellite manufacturer, has been awarded EUR 1.7 million (about SEK 19.3 million) as a consortium partner in SPIDER2, a European Defence Fund programme aimed at a future European constellation for space-based intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR). The company announced the award on 26 November.

SPIDER stands for Space-based Persistent ISR for Defence and Europe Reinforcement, and this is its second phase. GomSpace describes it as one of the largest space and defence initiatives funded under the European Defence Fund. The consortium is coordinated by Airbus Defence and Space SAS and includes more than 40 European space, defence and technology organisations. GomSpace joins at this phase through its Advanced Missions business unit.

## What GomSpace will do

The company's work package covers onboard signals-intelligence (SIGINT) algorithms and ground processing, plus an assessment of how small satellites can complement larger ISR platforms. The overall goal, as GomSpace frames it, is a constellation with faster revisit times, lower latency and better responsiveness for defence users, supporting sovereign European intelligence capability.

The project runs 30 months, with completion expected in mid-2029. That makes this a study-and-demonstration contract rather than a hardware order: the money funds algorithms, processing and architecture analysis, not satellites for launch.

## Why it matters

For GomSpace, the value is positioning. Edgar Milic, vice president of the Advanced Mission business unit, said in the release that "the future of defense and security increasingly depends on resilient, responsive and cost-efficient space assets." The company says the work strengthens its case as a supplier for future European space ISR programmes, should the constellation move beyond the research phase.

It also fits a pattern in GomSpace's recent European defence work. Earlier this year the company announced a share of a separate European Defence Agency research contract on very low Earth orbit military satellites. SPIDER2 is larger in scope and puts it alongside Airbus, which leads the consortium, on a programme that could shape how Europe buys ISR capacity as it tries to reduce dependence on non-European intelligence data.

The release does not say how the SPIDER2 funds are split among the 40-plus partners or what the follow-on procurement path looks like; those details will matter more than the €1.7 million figure.
