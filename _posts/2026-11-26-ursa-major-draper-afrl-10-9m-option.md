The Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) has exercised a $10.9 million option with Ursa Major, the Colorado propulsion company, to keep developing its Draper liquid rocket engine. The option follows two flights of the Affordable Rapid Missile Demonstrator, a test vehicle that uses Draper for propulsion, according to reports published on November 12 and 13.

## What the money covers

The option sits under a $28.6 million AFRL contract announced in May 2025, which tasked Ursa Major with maturing the engine and integrating it into a flight demonstrator. Interesting Engineering reports that the first demonstrator flight was announced in March 2026, about eight months after the contract began, and that the vehicle reached supersonic speed. A second flight roughly 45 days later widened the flight envelope and tested engine performance and control through different phases of flight. That outlet notes the flights were supersonic, not a publicly confirmed hypersonic flight.

Ursa Major describes Draper as a storable-propellant engine of about 4,000 pounds of thrust that burns hydrogen peroxide and kerosene in a closed catalyst cycle. Because the propellants can sit at room temperature for years, a vehicle could be ready to launch without loading or conditioning fuel first. The company also says the engine can throttle and restart multiple times. Those figures are the company's own claims.

"Draper is at the heart of our hypersonics work," Ursa Major president Jason Meredith said, according to ColoradoBiz, adding that AFRL and the Air Force have been partners from the start.

## Context

The company, founded in 2015 and based in Berthoud, Colorado, with manufacturing in Youngstown, Ohio, has built its business on propulsion for both space and defense. Its Hadley engine powers Stratolaunch's Talon-A2 hypersonic test vehicle; Interesting Engineering reports the company counted 10 successful Hadley flights as of April 2026. Ursa Major unveiled a Draper-powered medium-range missile called HAVOC in February 2026, though that is a design claim and not a fielded weapon. ColoradoBiz also lists a $137 million Navy missile contract for the company from October.

The option is a development milestone. Neither AFRL nor Ursa Major has disclosed a fielding schedule for any Draper-powered weapon, and the reports do not give the full term of the option.
