The University of Louisiana at Lafayette has finalized a deal that puts SpaceX in a former school building in Pecan Island, a few miles from the site where the company has said it wants to build what it calls the world's largest spaceport, WBRZ reported on Thursday.

## The terms

The 40-year lease runs through an affiliated entity, Ragin' Cajun Facilities Inc., with approval from the University of Louisiana System Board of Supervisors. SpaceX has committed to spend at least $10 million improving the roughly 39,000-square-foot building. The lease also contemplates a possible future sale of the property to SpaceX. WBRZ's report does not say what SpaceX intends to use the building for, and the university described the arrangement only as an "unexpected opportunity."

## The building

The school opened in 1964 and closed after Hurricane Rita in 2005. Brothers Peter and David John later turned it into a hunting lodge and donated it to the university in 2025 for wildlife and coastal erosion research, with the board naming it the Peter M. John and David M. John Wildlife & Coastal Preservation Center. Peter John said the family supports the new use, which he framed as a continuation of the property's role in education and discovery.

## Context

SpaceX announced in August a $100 billion development in Vermilion Parish that it describes as the world's largest spaceport, according to WBRZ. The project has drawn scrutiny since: our earlier coverage noted [records on the wharf road work](/2026/11/21/spacex-louisiana-spaceport-wharf-road-records/) and [a pause in the state coastal permit review](/2026/11/25/louisiana-pauses-spacex-coastal-permit-review/).

The source does not say what SpaceX plans to do with the building. The sale timeline and the use of the site are the things to watch once renovation begins.
