Spacedata, a Japanese startup that generates digital twins of cities and the Earth from satellite data, has taken an investment from Zenrin Future Partners, the corporate venture fund of Japanese map and spatial-data company Zenrin. Japanese tech outlet BRIDGE reported the deal on Nov. 13, citing a Nov. 12 announcement. Neither the amount nor the round type was disclosed.

Business outlet Sogyo Techo reported a Spacedata funding announcement on Nov. 19, also without an amount or investor list. The two reports may describe the same raise; the sources don't make that clear.

## What Spacedata does

Founded in 2017, Spacedata uses AI to turn satellite imagery into 3D models of real places, then runs simulations inside them. Its pitch is "make space intelligent", where "space" means both orbit and physical space in general. BRIDGE lists a wide spread of work: a free library of 3D city models called OpenEarth, crowd and social simulations populated by AI agents, defense simulations covering drones, hypersonics and electromagnetic effects, and a platform called RobotBrain that moves settings between simulated and real robots, from drones and humanoids to underwater vehicles and satellites.

The space-industry hooks are concrete. BRIDGE reports Spacedata built a digital twin of the International Space Station with JAXA and in November 2024 released Space Station OS, an open software stack for space stations. It has also worked with Japan's infrastructure ministry on the PLATEAU urban digital-twin program and on a UN disaster-resilience project that includes a digital twin of Tonga.

## Why a map maker

Zenrin owns some of Japan's most detailed national mapping and spatial data. BRIDGE says the two companies plan to look at combining their strengths to push 3D spatial information further and develop new spatial-data services. For Spacedata, a partner with ground-truth map data is an obvious complement to models built from orbit.

Sogyo Techo says the new money will go toward city-scale visualization, simulation and disaster resilience, applying the tools to construction and infrastructure sites and equipment inspection, and building large, fast simulation environments to train robots and other "physical AI" systems. BRIDGE puts the company's headcount at 207, including contract and part-time staff.
