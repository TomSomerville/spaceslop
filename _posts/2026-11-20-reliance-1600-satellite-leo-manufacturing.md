Reliance Industries is looking for manufacturing partners to build a planned 1,600-satellite low Earth orbit network, according to a Bloomberg report summarized by Business Today. The constellation is aimed mainly at India's connectivity and strategic needs.

The company is reportedly talking with Indian satellite makers, tech startups and industrial groups, and has offered to fund the infrastructure needed to scale up production. That is the real bottleneck: India currently builds fewer than 15 satellites a year, while the constellation would need roughly 200 a year.

Reliance is not planning to build everything at home. The plan combines domestic manufacturing with imports, including at least 400 satellites from the US. The company is reportedly discussing procurement with two US firms, evaluating European suppliers and ruling out Chinese ones.

## A slower timeline

A test mission is targeted for September 2027, with wider deployment from early 2028. Full completion is now aimed at 2035, pushed back from an earlier 2030 target because of manufacturing and launch constraints, per the report.

## The competition

The report frames the effort against Starlink, which it says has more than 10,000 satellites and at least 13.6 million customers, and brought in over $5 billion in connectivity revenue in the three months to September. Eutelsat's LEO business, including OneWeb, earned €70.9 million over the same period.

Sunil Bharti Mittal, whose Bharti Airtel has backed OneWeb since 2015, is on the other side of the same argument. OneWeb has about 650 satellites. Mittal told Bloomberg TV that "Countries can't rely on another country," the same sovereignty logic Reliance is using.

Starlink's own entry into India is still subject to regulatory and security scrutiny, which strengthens the case for a domestic option. Whether Reliance can lift India's satellite output more than tenfold in a few years is the open question; the 2035 date suggests the company is aware of it. All details here are from a single secondary report of Bloomberg's reporting and have not been confirmed by Reliance in the material reviewed.
