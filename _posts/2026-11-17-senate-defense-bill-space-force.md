Senate appropriators released their fiscal year 2027 defense bill on Tuesday, proposing $54.8 billion for the Space Force, according to SpacePolicyOnline. That is $4.3 billion below the $59.1 billion the administration requested, though the request also leans on a further $12 billion through a separate reconciliation bill, for a total of $71.1 billion. The service received $32 billion in fiscal 2026. The whole Pentagon bill comes to $1.072 trillion, essentially matching the request.

## Where the money goes

National Security Space Launch gets $7.1 billion, which the report ties to 54 launches. The committee also adds money in several areas: $2 billion more for space-based AMTI (airborne moving target indicator, tracking moving objects from orbit), $3.3 billion more for the Space Data Network, $520 million more for Next Generation Overhead Persistent Infrared Polar missile-warning satellites, and $88 million for commercial space-based intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance tasking. Smaller additions cover commercial sensing, in-orbit refueling, propulsion test infrastructure for commercial launch, non-federal spaceports and Protected Tactical SATCOM-Global.

Golden Dome missile defense receives $9.4 billion of the $17.1 billion requested. SpacePolicyOnline notes that none of the $69 billion in reconciliation money in this bill appears to go to the Space Force.

## Pushback on sole-source awards

The committee objects to non-competitive contracting. Two of the programs it boosts, AMTI ($4.16 billion) and the Space Data Network ($2.29 billion), were both awarded to SpaceX without competition. Section 8127 of the bill would limit sole-source and non-competitive awards, and the committee wants its added funds used to ensure multiple vendors participate.

The language is a signal that appropriators want more than one vendor on these programs, though it must still survive negotiation with the House.

## What's next

This is a committee bill, not law. The government is currently running under a continuing resolution that expires December 11, so the final shape of Space Force funding, including the gap between the Senate figure and the request, remains to be negotiated.
