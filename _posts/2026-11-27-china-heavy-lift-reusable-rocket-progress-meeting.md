China held a progress meeting in Beijing on November 23 for its heavy-lift and reusable launch vehicle programs, according to a post on the website of state contractor CASC. The meeting was convened by the State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defense (SASTIND), the regulator that oversees the country's space industry.

## Who was in the room

The CASC account says attendees included senior SASTIND leaders and the officials responsible for the work, leaders from central and state departments and relevant local government agencies, principal leaders of CASC and other enterprises and institutions, and officials from SASTIND headquarters and affiliated bodies.

## What was said

The meeting reviewed progress on both programs, answered requests and suggestions from participating units on the spot, and set priorities for the next phase. Officials said the programs have produced "phased results" that lay the groundwork for improving China's access to space and for applying reusable technology at scale.

The priorities listed are increasing payload capacity, cutting cost and raising launch frequency. Work is to concentrate on basic research, key technology breakthroughs and verification, and developing standards and shared experience. Quality and safety are to be built into every stage of development and testing, with stronger oversight. Existing deployment plans are to be carried out carefully and on schedule under what the account calls a new whole-nation system with clear accountability.

## Reading it

The CASC post is a summary in official language and gives no vehicle names, test dates or schedule changes. It does not say which rockets the meeting covered, so it should not be read as news of a specific flight or delay. What it does show is that the government is managing heavy-lift and reuse as top-level programs with direct regulator oversight, and that cost and launch rate are named targets alongside payload.
