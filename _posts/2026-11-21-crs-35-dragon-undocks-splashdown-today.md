SpaceX's CRS-35 cargo Dragon is on its way home. The uncrewed capsule undocked from the space-facing port of the International Space Station's Harmony module at about 12:05 p.m. EST on 20 November, according to NASA and Space.com, and is targeting a splashdown in the Pacific Ocean off California at roughly 4:55 p.m. EST (1:55 p.m. PST) on Saturday, 21 November.

The departure came a day later than first planned. As we [reported earlier](/2026/11/18/dragon-crs-35-undocking-weather-delay/), NASA and SpaceX stood down from a Wednesday undocking because of poor weather at the recovery sites. Space.com says the station was passing about 260 miles (418 km) above western China when Dragon pulled away, and that the departure webcast did not show the moment itself because it fell in a gap in high-bandwidth video coverage.

Neither NASA nor SpaceX plans to stream the reentry or splashdown. NASA says it will post updates on its space station blog instead.

## What's coming back

Dragon launched on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral on 13 October and arrived at the station on 15 October with more than 6,300 pounds of crew supplies and science. The trip home carries thousands of pounds of cargo, including research samples. NASA highlights a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease, frozen tumor organoids that could inform cancer treatment research, and frozen microbial samples for work on how antibiotic-resistant bacteria survive and evolve in space. A lighting system that spent more than three years on the station, tested for its potential to support astronaut sleep and well-being, is also aboard.

## Why it matters

Space.com notes that Dragon is the only cargo spacecraft currently flying that can bring material back to Earth. Cygnus, Progress and Japan's HTV-X are all expendable and burn up on reentry, so time-sensitive biological samples and hardware that researchers want to examine on the ground depend on Dragon's return flights, and on the weather cooperating at the splashdown zone.
