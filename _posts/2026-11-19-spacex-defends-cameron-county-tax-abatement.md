SpaceX has publicly pushed back on Cameron County's refusal to consider a tax break for its proposed $24 billion data center and gas-fired power plant near Starbase, arguing in a post on X that the arrangement would have been good for county finances.

The county commissioners voted 3–2 on November 17 against holding a public hearing on the proposal, as [we reported earlier](/2026/11/18/spacex-starbase-data-center-tax-break/). According to the RGV Business Journal, SpaceX responded on Wednesday that the deal was meant to give the county "more predictable revenue" while encouraging continued investment in the region. That phrase also appears, nearly word for word, in Tesla North's account of the statement.

## The company's argument

SpaceX's case rests on how Texas sets property tax rates. Under the state's no-new-revenue framework, rising property values generally let a local government cut its rate while collecting roughly the same money. SpaceX says that logic breaks down when the assets are expensive computing hardware, which loses value quickly. Large swings in taxable value, it contends, could move tax rates around and eventually cut revenue. Tesla North reports the proposal would have used payments in lieu of taxes to smooth that out.

SpaceX said it looks forward to more discussions with local jurisdictions. It did not say the project was cancelled. Neither outlet reproduced the full text of the post, and a separate report that the company would shift investment to other sites outside the county could not be confirmed from the pages we were able to read.

## What happens now

Judge Eddie Treviño Jr. and Commissioner Joey Lopez voted to advance the item. Lopez told KRGV he wanted commissioners to look at the financial figures before deciding either way, and that with the hearing vote failing there would be no second meeting. Reporting on the majority's reasoning is thin, and outlets differ on exactly which commissioners voted no, so treat individual names with caution.

The practical effect is that the abatement request is stalled at the county level. The city of Starbase, which our earlier article flagged as the next stop, remains the open question for a project of this size.
