JAXA showed reporters in Tokyo on Wednesday a four-legged test model of the rover it is building for LUPEX, the lunar polar mission it is pursuing with India. The mission aims to put the rover near the Moon's south pole in 2028.

The division of labor is straightforward: Japan supplies the rover and India develops the lander. The two countries agreed to the joint effort last year. Once on the surface, the rover is expected to look for potential water sources and drill at a site to collect samples.

## What the prototype has done

The model on display is a four-legged test article that will feed into the flight rover. Testing so far has covered climbing slopes using a single leg, driving continuously on two legs, and moving under vacuum conditions. Those tests target the mobility problem that matters most at the poles, where terrain is rough and the rover has to cope with slopes and loose soil.

## Why water

LUPEX project manager Takeshi Hoshino put the goal bluntly: "We ultimately want to use lunar water for rocket fuel." He also described producing fuel on the Moon for future missions as a key objective. That puts LUPEX in the same camp as other resource-prospecting efforts: before anyone builds a propellant plant, someone has to confirm how much water is there and in what form.

The mission remains a 2028 target, and the source does not give a specific launch date or rocket. For now the milestone is hardware: a rover that can move the way a polar mission needs it to, and an international split of responsibilities that has so far held together.
