SpaceX is scheduled to launch USSF-317, another classified payload for the U.S. Space Force, from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California on Thursday night. Liftoff is planned for 8:32 p.m. Pacific, which is 04:32 UTC on November 20, from Space Launch Complex 4 East, according to Spaceflight Now. This article was written before liftoff, so the outcome is not confirmed.

The rocket will head out on a south-easterly trajectory. SpaceX will fly booster B1100 on its 11th mission. The booster's earlier flights include NROL-105, USSF-385 and eight Starlink batches. About eight minutes after launch it is due to land on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You*. A successful landing would be the 235th on that vessel and the 679th Falcon booster landing overall, per Spaceflight Now.

## What is known about the payload

Very little. SpaceX will launch an undisclosed number of satellites, and the Space Force has not confirmed details of the payload. Spaceflight Now describes the flight as the latest in a series of "R" missions tasked by the Space Force and lists five predecessors: USSF-366 (R-1) on August 16, USSF-153 (R-2) on September 10, USSF-385 (R-3) on September 26, USSF-432 (R-4) on November 6 and USSF-627 (R-5) on November 12. After each of those launches, 23 satellites were catalogued, but the Space Force has not confirmed what they are or how they were deployed.

The pace is the notable part. Two of the five earlier flights came within the past two weeks, and Spaceflight Now says SpaceX is scheduled to fly at least two more Space Force missions before the end of the month. Launch-tracking listings describe the mission as possibly a batch of Starshield-based satellites, but that is speculation drawn from stage drop zones, not anything the government or SpaceX has said.

## Why it matters

The cadence shows how routine short-turnaround national security launches have become on Falcon 9, and how much of that work now happens with minimal public disclosure. Booster reuse is part of what makes it possible: B1100 is flying its second Space Force mission in a row of national-security work, having supported USSF-385 earlier this year.

Whether the satellites are a single program or several, the tally of catalogued objects will be the main public clue once the launch is done.
