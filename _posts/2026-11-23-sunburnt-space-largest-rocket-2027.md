Sunburnt Space, a Sydney-based rocket startup, said on 23 November that it is building its largest vehicle yet, with a first flight planned for the end of 2027, according to Space Connect.

The company describes the new rocket as many times the size of Pathfinder, the smaller vehicle it has already flown four times. No dimensions, name or launch site beyond "Australia" were given in the report, and the article does not mention funding.

## A rocket that comes back

The notable part is the mission profile. The vehicle is meant to carry more than a tonne of payload to altitudes between 140 km and 400 km and then return that payload to Earth. Customers could then inspect, fix and refly their hardware instead of writing it off, the same logic behind suborbital and reentry test services elsewhere in the industry. Sunburnt Space says it plans monthly flights.

The company is starting with satellite testing. "We're starting with satellite testing because that's where the pain is today," founder and CEO Brad Younger said. He also said that "getting to space and back should be routine."

## Context

The altitude range is worth noting. Flights to 140 km are suborbital, and 400 km is roughly the altitude of the International Space Station, but the report does not say whether the vehicle is intended to reach orbit. It describes a return-and-refly service, not a satellite launcher.

Sunburnt Space's earlier flights were far smaller. The company has been flying Pathfinder-class vehicles from sites in New South Wales, so a rocket that lifts over a tonne would be a large step up in size and complexity. The 2027 date is a company plan, and first flights of new rockets frequently slip.

Details such as launch site, pricing and customers have yet to be disclosed.
