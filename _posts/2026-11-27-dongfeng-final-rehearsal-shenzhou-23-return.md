China's Dongfeng landing site in the Inner Mongolian desert ran its final full-system rehearsal on Nov. 26, Xinhua reported, clearing the way for the return of the Shenzhou-23 crew from the Tiangong space station. The agency gave no landing date, saying only that the capsule will come home "in the near future."

Shenzhou-23 will bring back Chinese astronauts Zhang Zhiyuan and Li Jiaying together with Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreign astronaut to fly to Tiangong. Their handover to the Shenzhou-24 crew, which [launched on Nov. 25](/2026/11/25/shenzhou-24-launches-first-foreign-astronaut/), is what makes this return possible.

## What the drill covered

Xinhua said the exercise was run as a full-system, full-state, full-process simulation of aerial search and ground response, including checks of tracking and communications equipment. Five helicopters took off in a rectangular formation on receiving simulated landing-point coordinates, then covered the roughly 1,300 square kilometre landing zone. At the simulated touchdown spot, crews practised hydrazine detection (a check for leaked toxic propellant), set up a cordon and rehearsed opening the hatch.

The recovery team has been working on night operations, since the return is expected in the early hours. Site officials told Xinhua they refined the night search plans and improved lighting at the landing area. The medical teams have also stocked sleeping bags and blankets against the cold, and the air units logged 26 flight sessions that included dark-capsule flights and landings on dusty ground.

Shi Xundong, an official at the site, described the drill as comprehensive preparation to ensure the crew gets back safely. Another official, Guo Shixiong, said air, ground and perimeter units are ready for a fast, coordinated response.

## Why it matters

Landing is the one phase of a Chinese crew rotation that cannot be wholly rehearsed in orbit, and this return carries extra weight because of the first foreign passenger. China has been steadily opening Tiangong to international participation, and a clean recovery would close out that first mission on schedule.

The next move belongs to the station: the Shenzhou-23 crew will undock once the final handover checks are complete, and the exact landing time is typically announced by China's human spaceflight agency only shortly beforehand.
