SpaceX will not fly Ship 42 again. Elon Musk said the first Starship upper stage ever caught by the launch tower's arms will go on public display instead, according to Space.com.

Ship 42 flew on Starship's 15th test flight on 13 November and was caught at Starbase, Texas, about a day later; our [earlier report]({% post_url 2026-11-14-starship-ship-catch-flight-15 %}) covers the flight itself. Musk had said on X that the ship "could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment." On Wednesday, speaking virtually at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin, he took the opposite line, saying SpaceX would "not take any chances with the ship" given its historic status. Space.com says Musk was in China at the time as part of a US delegation.

## Where it ends up

Musk did not say where the ship will be displayed. Space.com's guess is Starbase, the company's South Texas production and launch site. That matters in a small way, because SpaceX plans to shift most Starship launches to a planned Louisiana site, where Space.com notes construction has not started.

## Why it matters

Reflying Ship 42 would have been the quickest proof that an upper stage survives orbital reentry and a tower catch well enough to go again. Skipping that leaves reuse of a caught ship undemonstrated for now, and puts the question on the next ship. Before Flight 15, SpaceX had caught the Super Heavy booster three times and reflown boosters twice. The ship is the harder half because it returns from orbital speed.

Musk framed the catch as a step toward what he called the "holy grail of rocketry," and said Starship "will be the first-ever fully reusable orbital space system." Space.com also notes his stated goal of about 10,000 Starship missions a year by 2030. A museum piece after one flight is a long way from that cadence, though a historic first is arguably worth more on a plinth than in a refurbishment queue.

The Space.com article does not give a schedule or name the next ship to attempt a catch. Space.com links a separate piece saying SpaceX will try its first double catch, booster and ship together, on the next test flight, but that was not discussed in the article itself and we haven't independently confirmed it.
