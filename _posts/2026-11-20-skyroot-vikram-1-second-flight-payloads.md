Skyroot Aerospace has named the partners for the second orbital flight of its Vikram-1 rocket: Indian satellite maker HEX20, German deployment specialist Exolaunch and Italian propulsion firm OhmSpace. The line-up was announced on Thursday at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, according to Siasat Daily.

## Who flies what

Skyroot supplies the rocket and its own 4U SCOPE satellite. HEX20, based in Thiruvananthapuram, brings a second 4U satellite, NILA III. Exolaunch provides the 8U deployment system that will release both spacecraft into orbit. OhmSpace contributes a high-temperature resistojet thruster, mounted on NILA III, which will be tested in space as an in-orbit technology demonstration.

HEX20's chief executive, Amal Chandran, told The Hindu that NILA III had originally been planned for a SpaceX Transporter rideshare mission, as relayed by Siasat. The switch to Vikram-1 is, in his words, an important step for India's growing private space industry. Exolaunch's chief revenue officer, Kier Fortier, said the teams went from first discussions to satellite integration in less than two months.

## Why it matters

Vikram-1 reached orbit on its first attempt in July, making Skyroot the first Indian private company to do so. A payload that was meant for a SpaceX rideshare moving to a domestic small launcher is the kind of customer pull a young launch company needs, and the speed of the integration suggests the interfaces are not a bottleneck.

The report does not give a launch date for the mission, and Skyroot has not been quoted on timing in the piece. Treat any date you see elsewhere as unconfirmed until the company publishes one.
