OrbitAID Aerospace, a Bengaluru-based startup, is preparing what it describes as an end-to-end satellite life-extension demonstration in low Earth orbit, targeted for the first quarter of 2027. The plan was detailed by Payload and Analytics India Magazine, and is back in the news this week through Indian press coverage.

The mission uses two 70 kg satellites, AayulSAT-2A and AayulSAT-2B, one acting as the chaser and the other as the target. After separation, the chaser is to track the target from a distance, switch to visual navigation, and use its sensors to inspect the target and build a 3D reconstruction. It then docks, transfers propellant, raises the target to a higher altitude and returns it to its original orbit.

## One flight, the whole sequence

OrbitAID's pitch is completeness. CEO Sakthi Kumar told Payload that most rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) demonstrations to date "have qualified a single piece of the sequence", while this flight is meant to prove the entire process. The company says it took the pair from concept design to launch readiness in 11 months, using an international supply chain.

The hardware is the company's own: a Standardised Interface for Docking and Refuelling (SIDRP), a docking mechanism called ARCAS, and autonomous rendezvous, proximity operations and docking software.

## What it is really for

The demo is a sales tool. OrbitAID wants to turn it into servicing contracts with geostationary orbit operators, where extending a satellite's life is worth more, and says the design is intended to scale to GEO. Payload reports that commercial discussions for a GEO life-extension mission are already underway. The company also lists inspection, active debris removal, refuelling and orbital fuel stations among its work.

Neither outlet reported funding amounts or a launch provider, and the Q1 2027 date is a target rather than a booked slot. Servicing demos have a long record of slipping, and the company would be taking on docking, fuel transfer and orbit raising in a single flight, with no margin for a failure in any one step. If it works, it would give India's young space sector a first in on-orbit servicing, a market where Northrop Grumman, Astroscale and Starfish Space are already active.
