Umbra, the Santa Barbara radar-imaging company, announced Freetail on November 23, a new family of multi-mission satellites built in the United States. The first model, Freetail 5 (FT-05), is a 200-kg-class spacecraft with a mesh antenna of roughly 20 square meters deployed. It is slated to be launch-ready in the first quarter of 2028 and commercially available by mid-2028, according to the company.

## What changes

Umbra says FT-05 carries about four times the radar power and twice the antenna area of its earlier satellites, per CEO David Langan. Imaging bandwidth reaches 4 GHz, and the satellite produces single-, dual- and quad-polarization products. The company claims hundreds of 25 cm images per satellite per day, resolution down to 5 cm, and hourly global revisit, which presumably depends on constellation size; Umbra has not said how many satellites that implies.

Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images with microwaves rather than light, so it works at night and through cloud. Umbra pitches coherent change detection at sub-centimeter scale, wide-area maritime search and geolocation of emitters among the uses.

## A platform, not just a satellite

The architecture is software-defined, meant to be updated on orbit, and can host third-party payloads such as sensors, communications gear or processors. Umbra lists more advanced modes: multistatic imaging, formation flying, single-pass 3D imagery, non-Earth imaging, direct-to-theater downlink and user-defined waveforms.

Manufacturing happens at Umbra's 50,000-square-foot facility in Goleta, California. The company says all flight software and 90 percent of components are its own, the highest share on any of its spacecraft, building on a bus refined over 14 missions. FT-05 is compatible with dedicated and rideshare launches from U.S. and international providers, and polar or mid-inclination orbits.

## Context

Umbra recently outlined plans for [seven more spacecraft](/2026/11/16/umbra-seven-more-spacecraft-launch-plan/), and Freetail looks like the hardware behind the next phase. Heavier, more capable satellites put it in a market where radar competitors are also moving up in size and power. No customers were named; Umbra describes the platform as designed for U.S. and allied operational and research missions. The 2028 date is two years out, so schedule is the main thing to watch.
