Russia launched a classified military satellite from the Plesetsk Cosmodrome on 7 November, using a Soyuz-2.1b with a Fregat upper stage from Pad 3 at Site 43. RussianSpaceWeb estimates liftoff at 16:22 Moscow time (1322 UTC) from orbital elements, since no official launch time was released.

Russia's Ministry of Defense announced a successful launch of a military payload, saying that the launch and delivery to orbit were nominal and that the satellite is under control of ground assets. The developer, ISS Reshetnev, reposted the announcement.

## What it is

The payload was not named, but RussianSpaceWeb identifies it as a Glonass-class navigation satellite. The US Space Force catalog lists it as GLONASS-K1 (Kosmos-2621), though RussianSpaceWeb says that name is presumed. Tracking data shows the satellite in an orbit of roughly 19,201 by 19,288 kilometers at 65.2 degrees inclination, with the Fregat nearby at about 19,153 by 19,442 kilometers. That is a typical medium-Earth orbit for Glonass.

This was the second Glonass launch from Plesetsk in 2026, following one on 17 September. Authorities in the Komi Republic and Tyumen Region issued drop-zone warnings for spent stages, consistent with this class of mission.

Glonass is Russia's counterpart to GPS, and replenishing it with newer K-series satellites is a long-running effort. The launch is a small data point for Russian military launch cadence, which the same outlet separately tracks in its coverage of pressure on Soyuz production.
