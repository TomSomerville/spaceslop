China launched three Yaogan-51 satellites, numbered 07, 08 and 09, on a Long March 2C from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center in Sichuan at 11:58 p.m. Beijing time on November 5 (15:58 UTC). State media said the spacecraft reached their planned orbits.

Xinhua counted the flight as the 675th mission of the Long March rocket family. The The Long March 2C was the vehicle used, flying from Xichang.

## What the satellites do

Official detail is thin, as usual for Yaogan ("remote sensing") launches. China News Service and Xinhua give the main applications as scientific experiments and electromagnetic environment detection, meaning measurement of radio and other electromagnetic energy at a location. Western analysts often read such wording as cover for military signals-intelligence or surveillance work, but the Chinese announcements do not say so and the sources used here do not either.

## Context

The sources read for this story did not publish orbit details or a rocket-specific flight count, and CASC has said little beyond the launch time and the satellites' broad purpose. Yaogan missions typically fly in batches of three, as this one did.
