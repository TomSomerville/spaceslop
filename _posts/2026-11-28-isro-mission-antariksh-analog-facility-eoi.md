ISRO's Human Space Flight Centre (HSFC) in Bengaluru is asking Indian industry to build a ground-based facility where astronauts can train and mission teams can rehearse before flying. The agency issued an expression of interest dated November 26 for what it calls Mission Antariksh, an Earth-based human spaceflight analog simulation facility. The details here come from a secondary summary of The Hindu's report; I could not locate the EoI document itself.

## What is being asked for

According to that summary, the facility would serve as a training ground for astronauts, a place to rehearse missions, and a platform for testing systems and validating procedures. It would include simulator modules for Gaganyaan, India's crew capsule, and for the planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station, plus a neutral buoyancy simulator for spacewalk practice, systems for space-specific engineering tasks, representative spacecraft and station modules, a mission control centre, and simulation hardware and software. Crew, medical, ground support and utility infrastructure are part of the scope.

The simulated activities include work inside modules, extravehicular activity, docking, robotic operations, crew coordination and contingency response. The plan calls for six analog mission campaigns in two operational phases, three per phase, each phase opening with a preparatory dry rehearsal. HSFC would monitor progress and supervise the campaigns through a committee.

## Context

The report does not give a submission deadline, contract value, selection timeline or facility size, and other outlets differ on contract length, so those details should be checked against the original EoI.

A neutral buoyancy tank in particular is a significant piece of infrastructure: it is the standard way agencies rehearse spacewalks on the ground.
