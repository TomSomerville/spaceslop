SpaceX has caught a Starship upper stage with the launch tower's arms, the first time the second stage of an orbital rocket has come back to its launch site. Ship 42 touched down on the "chopsticks" at Starbase, Texas, at 3:20 p.m. CST on 14 November (2120 UTC), about 22.5 hours after Flight 15 lifted off.

The other half of the stack did not go to plan. Booster 22, the Version 3 Super Heavy, was supposed to return to the tower about 6.5 minutes into the flight. Instead its guidance system judged it was out of position for a catch and sent it to a splashdown in the Gulf. Spaceflight Now reports the booster broke up before reaching the water. SpaceX commentator Dan Huot first attributed the diversion to an automated guidance abort and later corrected himself, saying that was not the reason the booster did not return. SpaceX is still investigating. Three earlier boosters have been caught, but no V3 booster has yet made it back, so a fully reusable orbital mission remains unclaimed.

## A busy day in orbit

Before the catch, Ship 42 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. SpacePolicyOnline notes these are too large for Falcon rockets. The ship then coasted through 14 orbits. SpaceX kept backup options to end the flight early with splashdowns off Hawaii or Chile, but did not need them. It reentered over the Pacific, crossed Mexico and the southwestern US at supersonic speed, then turned back over the ocean and lined up on the tower. The ship carried the words "Don't Panic" on its side.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman called the flight "almost magic", according to SpacePolicyOnline.

## What it means

Catching the ship matters because the upper stage is the harder half of the reusability puzzle: it comes back from orbital speed and must survive reentry intact enough to fly again. It also supports SpaceX's pitch for NASA's Moon landing contract. The Starship lunar lander is due to land astronauts starting in 2028 and needs in-orbit refueling with cryogenic propellant, which SpaceX has not yet demonstrated.

Open items include the booster investigation and the first Starship launch from Florida, which SpacePolicyOnline says could come this year. The FAA is also holding public scoping meetings on 17 to 19 November on higher Starship launch rates at Starbase.
