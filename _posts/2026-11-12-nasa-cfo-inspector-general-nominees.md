The Senate Commerce Committee held a hearing on 10 November on Joshua Shoemaker, nominated as NASA's chief financial officer, and Robert Steinau, nominated as inspector general. SpaceNews reports that senators asked few questions, and most of the attention in the just-over-an-hour session went to Danielle Thumann, nominated to the FCC. The committee plans to vote on the nominees on 18 November before they go to the full Senate.

NASA has had no Senate-confirmed CFO since January 2025. Greg Autry's spring 2025 nomination was returned without action at the end of that year. There has been no confirmed inspector general since Paul Martin left in late 2023. Along with the administrator and deputy administrator, these are the four NASA posts that need Senate confirmation.

Shoemaker is an accountant with more than 20 years in the private sector and Mississippi state government, and has been a senior adviser in NASA's Mission Support Directorate since August. He previously served as controller for the Republican National Committee. He pledged financial accountability, clearer views of program performance against budgets and schedules, transparency with Congress and modernized financial systems. He deferred questions on specific programs and on the proposed US Space Academy, saying the president will decide where it goes.

Steinau has worked in NASA's inspector general office since 2009 and has led it as its senior official since December 2024. He named three challenges: returning humans to the Moon, moving from the International Space Station to commercial stations, and cybersecurity. "NASA is moving with urgency, and it is imperative that accountability keep pace with ambition," he said, according to SpaceNews.

Both posts matter in a year when Congress is arguing over NASA's budget and agency programs are being reorganized.
