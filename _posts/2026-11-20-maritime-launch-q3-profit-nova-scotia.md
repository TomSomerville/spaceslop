Maritime Launch Services, the company building Spaceport Nova Scotia, reported third-quarter results on November 13. It posted revenue of about $5.57 million and net income of about $3.5 million, against a loss of roughly $5.7 million in the same quarter a year earlier. Revenue in the year-ago periods was nil.

The money is coming from real estate, not rockets. The release attributes the revenue to new long-term lease and launch-services agreements, chiefly a ten-year, $200 million launch pad lease with Canada's Department of National Defence, signed in March. Nine-month revenue was about $12.1 million and net income about $5.7 million.

## Customers and cash

A second anchor is Germany's Isar Aerospace, which signed a ten-year, US$112.5 million facilities agreement in July. According to the release, it calls for four payments of US$3.75 million in the first year, followed by a 30-month gap before payments resume. The company says it holds more than $29 million in cash, and CEO Stephen Matier said that "Demand for reliable launch capacity continues to grow rapidly."

The release says the company is in talks with several launch providers, and that interest exceeds the pads available in the current phase. Vehicles under discussion range from about 150 kg to more than 15,000 kg of payload capacity. Four pads are planned: one is operational, and three are being co-developed with customers across 2026 and 2027. Infrastructure for medium-class rockets is a stated priority and has been approved for construction by the Province of Nova Scotia.

## What is still missing

The first orbital launch from the site is targeted for 2028. Until then, the profit reflects lease accounting, with the DND lease counted in equal monthly slices, rather than launch demand being proven in flight. Operating expenses implied by the release are around $2.1 million for the quarter, which is derived from the reported figures rather than stated by the company.

The company began trading on the Toronto Stock Exchange this month and plans a special shareholder meeting in the first quarter of 2027 to approve a share consolidation. One discrepancy in the release: a milestones section cites August 2026 net income of $3.2 million, which does not match the Q3 figure and may refer to a different period.
