Tumbleweed, a microgravity-access startup split between Vienna and Delft, has raised a €6.9 million seed round led by PactVC. The company announced it on November 3, and coverage describes it as the largest spacetech seed round in Austria to date. Vanagon Ventures, early backer Prototype Capital, EWOR and a group of international investors and angels also took part.

## What it sells

Tumbleweed, founded in 2024, rents out modular laboratory "Pods" that ride on its free-flying Oasis orbital platform. The pitch is that a lab or manufacturer can run an experiment in microgravity without building a spacecraft. Pods are pre-certified and supply power and data links. Customers test their hardware at their own site, hand the Pod over for launch, and then monitor the experiment remotely from orbit.

The company's entry offer is priced at €15,000, covering the Pod, development support, launch and operations, with lead times of two to three months that it says it wants to shorten.

## Proof of concept

The first mission, Oasis Alpha, flew on SpaceX's Transporter-17 rideshare on July 7, 2026, with experiments for four customers, all first-time space users. Tumbleweed says it was fully booked a year before launch, paid for upfront, and built in nine months, which it calls about five times faster than the industry norm. According to the company, the flight showed that organisations with no space background could get an experiment into orbit in three to four months for roughly €30,000.

It reports a customer conversion rate of around 40 percent, with about two-thirds of customers arriving through inbound enquiries. Its customer base is shifting from universities and hospitals toward national research institutes, contract research organisations and industry. Research areas include materials science, protein synthesis, organoid development, immune ageing, cardiovascular deconditioning and wound healing.

## What's next

Three further missions are planned for 2027, and Tumbleweed expects more than ten customers across them, from medical research, biotechnology and agritech. In 2028 it plans Oasis Beta, which adds re-entry so customers can get physical samples back. Dawn Aerospace [is supplying the reentry propulsion](/2026/11/20/dawn-aerospace-tumbleweed-oasis-beta-propulsion/) for that vehicle.

Coverage does not say how the seed money will be split. CEO Julian Rothenbuchner, who says traditional funding routes were too slow for the pace he wanted, leads a team of 25, and more than a third of them have invested their own money in the company.
