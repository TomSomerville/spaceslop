Virgin Galactic revealed its first next-generation spaceship in finished livery on October 28, named it Apeiron, and the same day carried it from its factory in Mesa, Arizona, to Spaceport America in New Mexico.

The ship departed Phoenix-Mesa Gateway Airport at 10:29 a.m. MST mated to the carrier aircraft Eve and landed at 12:59 p.m. MDT. Michael Masucci and Dan Alix flew Eve, with flight test engineer Tori Crichton aboard. The name came from a public vote among a shortlist, with participants from more than 80 countries. CEO Michael Colglazier summed it up: "This morning, we gave Apeiron a name. This afternoon, Apeiron came home."

## What comes next

Arrival is not the end of testing. Engineers will finish avionics software integration and validation and prepare for flight tests, which include glide flights and rocket-powered flights. After a successful test program, Virgin Galactic expects to begin flying customers on Apeiron in early Q2 2027. The Albuquerque Journal reported that flights had previously been scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026.

The company is designing each next-generation ship to fly twice a week, with a planned life of more than 500 missions. A static test article and the second ship are being assembled at the factory, and the rocket motor assembly line is being prepared. The second ship is expected to begin commercial operations in late Q2 or early Q3 2027.

The twice-weekly cadence is the core of the business case: the older SpaceShipTwo fleet flew a handful of customer flights a year.
