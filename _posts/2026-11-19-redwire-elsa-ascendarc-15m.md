Redwire has won a contract worth approximately $15 million to supply solar array wings for a geostationary communications satellite being built by AscendArc, the company said on 19 November. The wings are Redwire's Extensible Low-Profile Solar Array, or ELSA, a rolled and extended array designed to stow compactly for launch.

Redwire's release says delivery is scheduled for the third quarter of fiscal 2026. That is an odd date to cite in a November 2026 announcement, and the company did not elaborate in the material we read, so treat the schedule as unclear until Redwire or AscendArc says more.

## What Redwire is claiming for ELSA

According to Redwire, ELSA delivers up to 50% more power by volume than traditional arrays. The company also says the design is built for volume production, keeps the stowed profile and mass low, and allows faster turnaround. It says more than 65 ELSA wings have now been delivered or are in development.

Mike Gold, president of Redwire Space, tied the product to production rates, saying it is "designed for volume production" so customers can field satellites and constellations faster. Those performance figures are Redwire's own and were not independently verified.

## Why it matters

Solar arrays are a quiet bottleneck for satellite builders: they are large, mechanically complex, and often on the critical path. Suppliers that can sell them as a repeatable product, rather than a one-off, are positioned to benefit as manufacturers push toward higher volumes. A $15 million award for a single GEO satellite's wings is also a reminder that power systems are a meaningful share of a spacecraft's cost.

The release does not disclose the satellite's size, launch plans or customer, so the wider context for the AscendArc program remains thin. Redwire has been active on the power side lately; earlier this month it was selected for a roughly $40 million NASA Moon Base power demonstration, covered in [our earlier article](/2026/11/10/redwire-40m-moon-base-power/).
