MaiaSpace announced on 20 October that it has finished a new test campaign in southern France on the systems that disconnect its launcher from ground equipment. The tests used a dedicated bench called SpaceDreams.

## What was measured

The campaign covered two things, according to the company. One was mechanical loads: the forces and moments passed to the launcher's interface when the connection is broken. The other was disconnection kinematics, meaning how the systems behave through the release sequence, including interface separation, relative movement and the ground equipment clearing the launcher.

These are the umbilical and connection systems that feed a rocket until liftoff and must let go cleanly without damaging it, so a poor result here can mean a scrub or worse. MaiaSpace describes the work as part of a progressive qualification approach that moves toward more representative and integrated configurations.

## What comes next

The company says its next step is to qualify the ground equipment "in an even more representative configuration" before full deployment at the Guiana Space Centre, the launch site in French Guiana. MaiaSpace's post gives no measured load values, timings or quantitative results, and quotes no one, so there is no way yet to judge margins.

The test is incremental news rather than a headline milestone, but it fits the company's pattern of publishing hardware progress while its schedule gets scrutiny. SpaceSlop reported on 12 November on a delay linked to the European Launcher Challenge; this campaign is separate from that story and shows ground-system work continuing alongside vehicle testing.
