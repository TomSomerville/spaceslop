Axiom Space has provided an update on its Axiom Resilient Compute platform, ARC, saying two units are operating in orbit and have confirmed communications with each other and with the ground. The 6 October release, datelined Antalya, Türkiye, was issued with Kepler Communications, whose optical data relay constellation carries the hardware.

## What is in orbit

According to Axiom, ARC 1 and ARC 2 launched earlier in 2026 as part of Kepler's constellation and were developed with funds from the Texas Space Commission. The platform is aimed at distributed workload orchestration in space, AI and machine learning processing, and post-quantum secure communications. The ARCs use 2.5 Gbps optical links that Axiom says are compatible with the Space Development Agency's Tranche 1 optical communications standards. Axiom and Kepler have worked together on optical intersatellite links since 2023, and Kepler, based in Toronto, says it has launched 33 satellites to date.

## The post-quantum angle

Separately, Axiom says it completed a ground demonstration in which a workload encrypted with post-quantum cryptography (PQC) was migrated successfully. The company ties this to a June 2026 Executive Order and OMB guidance that, it says, require US agencies to move high-value systems to PQC keys by the end of 2030 and to PQC digital signatures by the end of 2031, countering "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks, in which encrypted data is stolen today to be cracked by future quantum computers. Axiom says PQC is built in at both the silicon and software layers.

The next milestone is an on-orbit demonstration of ARC's PQC capability. Longer term, Axiom wants to scale ARC into a global, sovereign, PQC-capable orbital processing network, and it says the platform also suits remote industries like oil and gas, mining and maritime energy that face intermittent connectivity.

Jonathan Cirtain, Axiom's CEO and president, said "the solution isn't just more bandwidth", arguing for processing data near where it is captured. Caitlin Marsh, president of Kepler Communications U.S., said on-orbit compute is already in service alongside Kepler's relay network. The release gives no customers or revenue figures; what it does establish is that the hardware is up and talking, and that the encryption claim is still to be proven in space.
