Rocket Lab chief financial officer Adam Spice says Electron prices could rise sharply within the next six to nine months, driven by small-satellite operators who can no longer book far-out rides on SpaceX's Falcon 9 rideshare missions. He described the expected change as a "very strong uplift in pricing", according to a Stocktwits report on his comments.

## The rideshare squeeze

The argument rests on capacity at SpaceX. Per the report, SpaceX had reportedly stopped taking new Falcon 9 rideshare reservations by July, and customers said they could not book Transporter slots beyond late 2028 or early 2029. Spice expects operators to begin looking at alternatives in the second half of 2027 and called Electron their next-best option. This is management's forecast, not a price change that has been announced.

## What the numbers show

Rocket Lab's own recent figures are less tidy. In the third quarter it booked a record 54 launches across Electron, its HASTE suborbital variant and Neutron, but revenue per launch averaged $7.8 million, down from $10.1 million a year earlier. The nine-month average rose to $8.7 million from $8.2 million. The company attributed the swings to customer mix and mission complexity, and management said new orders are already priced higher.

Demand is not the obvious constraint. Japan's Synspective ordered 20 more Electron launches for 2028 to 2031 in September, taking its total to 47, the largest commercial Electron deal so far.

## Can the factory keep up?

Production is the question. Rocket Lab built about 17 Electrons in the first nine months of 2026, against a factory CEO Peter Beck said was designed for 52 rockets a year. Spice estimated that adding 50,000 to 75,000 square feet could reach roughly 100 Electrons a year within two years, though finding skilled technicians is a challenge. Beck put the cost of a second production line at tens of millions of dollars.

## Market reaction

The comments landed in a rough week for the stock. RKLB fell nearly 11% to $45.42 on Friday, its seventh straight daily decline, for a 26% weekly loss and its worst week on record. It is down 35% year to date, Stocktwits reported.

The pricing thesis is a familiar one for small launchers: SpaceX's cheap rideshare sets the market ceiling, and when that supply tightens, dedicated small rockets gain leverage. Whether Rocket Lab can turn that into higher average revenue per launch will show up in its next quarterly results.
