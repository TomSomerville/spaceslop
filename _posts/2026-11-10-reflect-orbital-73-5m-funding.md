Reflect Orbital, the Hawthorne, California startup that wants to bounce sunlight from orbit to chosen spots on Earth, announced $73.5 million in new funding on November 10. Total funding now exceeds $100 million, following its Series A in May 2025.

Existing investors Sequoia Capital, Lux Capital and Starship Ventures returned. New backers include Buckley Ventures, Convective Capital, The Uncommon Group and Salesforce Ventures, among others.

## Where the money goes

Reflect Orbital says the funds will pay for its first satellite launches, development of its orbital reflector technology, and commercial partnerships in energy and lighting. The pitch is sunlight delivered after dark, for example to boost solar farms at dusk, and light for search and rescue in disaster zones.

The company's inaugural demonstration mission, which it says already has FCC approval, is targeted for the second quarter of 2027 and would collect data on reflector deployment and performance in low Earth orbit. It has also filed an FCC application for a separate developmental satellite to test its spacecraft and communications payload; that remains subject to regulatory approval.

On launch, Reflect Orbital says it has secured multiple new 2027 opportunities on top of existing bookings, and recently bought two dedicated launches from India's Skyroot Aerospace.

## Production claims

The company describes a manufacturing cadence that is unusual for a startup at this stage: a large reflector quadrant every 3.5 days, a four-boom deployable structure each week, and a 12,000-part satellite every two months. Its reflector material is described as about 28 times thinner than a human hair and foldable to the size of a shoebox. These are the company's own figures.

CEO Ben Nowack said Reflect Orbital is "taking a deliberate, science-led approach to deployment." The company says its Impact Science team will soon publish research on how the technology interacts with the surrounding environment, a pointed nod to the concerns astronomers and ecologists have raised about artificial light from space.

Everything still hinges on the 2027 demonstration. Until reflectors are deployed and measured in orbit, the commercial case rests on projections.
