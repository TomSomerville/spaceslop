Pixxel, the hyperspectral Earth-observation company, has formed a US subsidiary, Pixxel Federal, to sell imaging, satellite mission systems and geospatial intelligence to civilian, defense and intelligence agencies. The company announced it on 29 October.

## Leadership and board

Allyson Jenkins, previously Pixxel's vice president of public sector, becomes CEO. She joined Pixxel in January 2025 and has 18 years in national security and geospatial intelligence, including nine at the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA). The board is stacked with Washington experience:

- Lt. Gen. Ray Palumbo, US Army (retired), who spent 34 years in uniform and last served as Director for Defense Intelligence.
- Patrick Antkowiak, a former chief strategy and technology officer at Northrop Grumman.
- Frank Avila, a former senior intelligence executive with roles at the CIA and NGA.

Pixxel founder and CEO Awais Ahmed said the subsidiary lets the company build a dedicated, long-term presence in what he called an important market. Jenkins said hyperspectral data gives government users "an information advantage."

## The pitch

Hyperspectral sensors identify materials by their spectral signatures, which Pixxel says can reveal what conventional imagery misses. The company lists camouflage detection and terrain characterization for defense and intelligence users, plus critical-mineral and rare-earth detection, environmental monitoring and agriculture.

Pixxel already has some US government footing: a NASA Commercial SmallSat Data Acquisition Program contract, and a Strategic Commercial Enhancements contract from the National Reconnaissance Office, announced in May, to evaluate and integrate commercial hyperspectral data. Pixxel Federal plans to pursue more federal contracts and open an office in the Washington, D.C. area.

## Why a US entity

Selling to US intelligence and defense customers is easier through a domestic subsidiary with cleared leadership, which is a common move for foreign-founded space companies. The step follows Pixxel's $100 million Series C, announced on 7 September and co-led by Temasek and Seraphim, which brought total funding to $195 million. Pixxel says the money is earmarked for global expansion across sensing, software, satellite systems and manufacturing.
