Nearly 650 employees at ArianeGroup's Bremen site in Germany took part in a 24-hour warning strike on 24 November, according to European Spaceflight. Bremen is where the upper stages for Ariane 6 are built.

The walkout was part of a wider action by the IG Metall union, which said almost 39,000 employees at 22 facilities across northern Germany took part in the strike. An earlier company update put the Bremen participants at almost the entire site workforce.

ArianeGroup said the strikes "have not impacted the delivery schedule of the Ariane 6 upper stages" and that production at Bremen had been adjusted. That matters because Ariane 6 has been flying a busy schedule, with an Ariane 64 having lifted off for Amazon Leo on 24 November and another mission, Metop-SG B1, pencilled in for 17 December.

## The dispute

IG Metall had asked for a 5% pay increase plus binding commitments from employers on investment and job security. Before the latest round of talks, employers had offered no permanent pay rise for the first 20 months of a new contract, followed by a 1.3% increase.

After 26 hours of negotiations in Mannheim, the union and the employers' associations reached an agreement on 26 November. It includes a 2% raise from May 2027 and a further 2.3% from April 2028, along with new provisions on industrial investment and job protection.

The report does not say whether ArianeGroup's Bremen workers will ratify the deal, and it gives no detail on how the production adjustment was made. With the settlement reached, the immediate risk to upper-stage supply looks small, but it is a reminder that Europe's launch cadence rests on a few single-site production lines.
