The NATO Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) is nearly tripling its commercial procurement over the next two years, and space is set to take a growing slice, according to Space Intel Report.

NCIA General Manager Dylan Browne told the NATO Edge 26 conference in Izmir, Turkiye, that satellite communications, geospatial imagery and space situational awareness are expected to account for 25 percent of the agency's total spending by 2030. The agency's commercial contracts currently run to about 1.3 billion euros ($1.42 billion) a year. Space-related spending is projected to rise above 300 million euros annually.

The headline figure is about $3 billion in industry procurements over the next two years, with satcom a large component. Space Intel Report's full article is paywalled past its opening paragraphs, so a breakdown of that total, specific contracts and vendors were not available to us. The piece is tagged with NATO Satcom Services 6th Generation (NSS6G), which suggests that program is part of the picture, but that link is an inference from the tags, not a stated detail.

## Why it matters

NATO has increasingly leaned on commercial providers for space services rather than owning every asset itself. A multi-year jump in NCIA's budget for satcom, imagery and space domain awareness is a demand signal for European and transatlantic operators alike, from geostationary and low-orbit communications providers to Earth observation and tracking companies.

The alliance has also been working on launch access. We covered the NATO Starlift launch initiative earlier this month; this procurement push sits on the services side of the same effort to rely on commercial space.
