Galactic Energy launched a Ceres-2 rocket from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 7:37 a.m. Beijing time on November 7, placing four satellites into a 535 km sun-synchronous orbit, CGTN reported. The payloads included the Guanlan experimental satellite and Yunyao-1 28; other coverage names Zhongbei Daxue-1 and Juntian-1 06A, though that detail comes from search summaries rather than the CGTN page.

Ceres-2 is the medium-lift upgrade of the company's Ceres-1 family. CGTN describes three solid stages in series with a liquid upper stage, a liftoff mass of about 100 tonnes and a payload capacity of 1.3 tonnes to a 500 km sun-synchronous orbit. It can launch from land or sea.

Flight history is described inconsistently. China Daily, as summarised by search results, calls this the second Ceres-2 mission after a January flight that failed; the CGTN page does not mention earlier flights. Treat the "return to flight" framing as reported but not confirmed here.

For Galactic Energy, a successful Ceres-2 matters because the solid-fuelled vehicle gives it a heavier rung above the small Ceres-1, competing for commercial constellation and Earth-observation payloads in China's growing private launch market.
