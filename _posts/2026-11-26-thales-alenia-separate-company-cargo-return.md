Thales Alenia Space intends to put its proposed space station cargo service into a separate company, according to European Spaceflight. The plan surfaced in a joint statement published on 13 November by the Italian unions FIM, FIOM and UILM after a meeting with the manufacturer's executive management.

If the company's proposal succeeds, it would bring in private investors and set up a special purpose vehicle (SPV), described as a separate legal entity that would handle development, financing and commercialisation of the service.

## The ESA contest

The service is Thales Alenia's entry in ESA's LEO Cargo Return Service effort. In May 2024 ESA awarded Thales Alenia and The Exploration Company contracts for the first phase, aiming at a European commercial cargo service to and from low Earth orbit, with a first demonstration targeted for 2028.

In early 2026 ESA revised the second phase and renamed it ALADDIN (Autonomous LEO Accelerated Demo Docking to ISS Node). Bidders can propose demonstrations to the International Space Station by mid-2029, or to a future commercial station by the end of 2031. Thales Alenia expects ESA to finish evaluating its ALADDIN proposal by the end of November.

Its rival is already ahead. On 10 September ESA awarded The Exploration Company a contract worth up to €760 million: €310 million for an ISS demonstration plus €450 million in options for further missions. ESA said at the time that it was continuing the tender with Thales Alenia to preserve competition in the European commercial cargo market.

## Why it matters

A dedicated SPV with outside capital would shift the cargo vehicle from a prime contractor's internal programme toward a commercially financed venture, the model ESA has been steering toward for station logistics. The details come from a union statement relayed by one outlet, and Thales Alenia has not been quoted in the material available, so the structure and timing remain unconfirmed until ESA decides.
