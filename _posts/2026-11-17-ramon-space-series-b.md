Ramon.Space has raised $124 million in a Series B round, the company announced on 17 November. Growth equity firm PSG led the round. Micron Technology came in as a strategic investor, alongside Senvest and Phoenix Financial. Existing backers StageOne, Deep Insight and Grove Ventures also took part.

Ramon.Space builds on-board computing systems designed to survive radiation, extreme temperatures and tight power budgets. Its pitch is that this hardware can be produced at enterprise scale, "into the millions of nodes", rather than as a handful of bespoke flight computers. The company names communications mega-constellation operators and Earth observation providers, commercial and government, as its customers, though the release names none of them and no products.

The company says the money will go toward ramping production to meet rising demand, speeding deliveries against a growing pipeline, getting next-generation systems to customers sooner and entering new markets.

## Why it matters

Computing in orbit is becoming a selling point for satellites, and for in-orbit data processing generally. CEO Avi Shabtai said: "We have proven that Earth-grade computing works in space, and we are delivering it today." Ronen Nir of PSG said processing and managing data in orbit is becoming essential to the space economy.

Micron's presence is worth noting. A memory maker taking a strategic stake suggests the supply chain for space-grade compute is being treated as a market of its own rather than a niche. The release gives no valuation, revenue or delivery figures, so how much of that demand is already contracted remains unclear.
