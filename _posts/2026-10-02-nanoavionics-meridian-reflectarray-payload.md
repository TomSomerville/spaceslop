Meridian Space and satellite builder Kongsberg NanoAvionics said on October 2 that the first in-orbit communications payload from Meridian has been activated. The First Customer Link (FCL) satellite launched on October 1 on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare from Vandenberg Space Force Base.

NanoAvionics reported establishing two-way communications on the first available ground station pass, with all subsystems, including Meridian's payload, powering on and responding. FCL flies on NanoAvionics' MP42 microsatellite bus, and the company says it has a 100% first-contact success rate across more than 60 customer satellites launched.

## The contract behind it

FCL is part of a €122.5 million (US$140 million) contract for NanoAvionics to build 280 satellites for Meridian. It is the first space deployment of Meridian's reflectarray antenna, a lightweight, electronically reconfigurable design that the companies say cuts satellite mass and power needs and so lowers the cost of a dedicated LEO network, following five years of development and qualification.

NanoAvionics CEO Atle Wollø said it took just over a year from kick-off to orbit, and that the next step is a constellation-optimised satellite built in a new serial manufacturing facility capable of producing hundreds of custom satellites a year. Meridian CEO Massimiliano Ladovaz described the goal as customers owning their networks instead of leasing capacity.

Meridian plans two offerings: Meridian Express, a standardised, rapidly deployable constellation aimed at commercial and defence users who need secure communications quickly, with satellite deliveries expected from 2027; and a larger customer-owned baseline architecture for high-capacity broadband.

An initial power-on and first contact is the easy part; the payload still has to prove link performance, which the release does not quantify. On the same launch, NanoAvionics also supplied power systems, battery packs and solar panels for an undisclosed constellation customer.
