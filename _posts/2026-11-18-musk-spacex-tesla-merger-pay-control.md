A tie-up between SpaceX and Tesla is still only under discussion, but a Reuters analysis of what it would mean for Elon Musk's compensation shows where the leverage sits: voting control would come quickly, while the big pay awards would not.

Musk said on a SpaceX earnings call in October that both companies' boards had formed special committees to discuss a potential merger, per Reuters. No deal has been announced, and Musk, Tesla and SpaceX did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment.

## Control first, money later

SpaceX granted Musk a pay plan before its summer IPO that is tied to market-capitalization goals, a Mars goal of one million people, and a target of 100 terawatts of compute per year for space data centers. Musk receives the voting power immediately but can't collect the shares' economic value until he hits the goals. Reuters says that gives him about 85% control of SpaceX, and Equilar values the shares at up to $719 billion at the top market-cap target.

Tesla's $1 trillion plan from 2025 is tied to market-cap and operational goals, and Musk has not hit any yet. Even if he met all of them, board documents put his Tesla voting power at about 29%.

Combined, Barclays estimates, Musk would own roughly 37% of the new company but hold 56% of the votes, because his SpaceX shares carry ten times the normal votes and Tesla has no equivalent share class.

## What he would collect

Reuters, citing six executive-compensation experts, found a merger would likely give Musk only a small slice of the Tesla package. Under Tesla's change-in-control terms he could receive market-cap-based awards without the operational goals, but Tesla's roughly $1.6 trillion value would need to reach $2 trillion for those to vest, worth about $20 billion in stock. Higher thresholds were seen as unlikely.

Experts quoted were skeptical of the plans themselves. Charles Elson of the University of Delaware said the pay package is a distraction and the real story is "complete control." Kevin Murphy of USC expects the board to keep the plans and adjust goals for the combined company, as SpaceX did after acquiring xAI, and called the Mars goal "pretty pie-in-the-sky." Four experts said SpaceX's board could likely amend the plan however it wishes.

Much of this rests on expert opinion and third-party estimates from Barclays and Equilar at current company sizes, so the figures could shift.
