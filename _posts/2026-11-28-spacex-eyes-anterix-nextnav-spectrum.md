SpaceX is reportedly interested in buying spectrum from two US companies, Anterix and NextNav, as it works to turn Starlink Mobile into a full wireless carrier. Bloomberg reported it on 27 November; we read the report through a summary published by Oninvest, not Bloomberg's original, and there is no confirmation from SpaceX. Anterix shares rose 10.6 percent that day and NextNav shares rose 14.5 percent.

## What is on the table

Anterix is the largest US holder of 900 MHz spectrum licences, per Bloomberg. Utilities use about 15 percent of it, leaving roughly 85 percent open for new deals. Management says the remainder could triple in value with the right buyer and could be sold as a single block. Chris Guttman-McCabe, its head of corporate development, told Bloomberg: "You can assume we're talking to all the right parties."

NextNav builds geolocation and navigation systems that use the lower 900 MHz band. SpaceX has already asked the FCC to include NextNav's spectrum in its study of direct satellite-to-smartphone service. Industry groups oppose NextNav's own proposal to reallocate that band.

## Risks

The deal is far from done. The FCC could decline to authorise satellite use of Anterix's spectrum, which would cut its value to SpaceX. Anterix also faces long engineering work to clear existing users from narrow, local blocks, and SpaceX could choose NextNav instead. In August SpaceX urged the FCC to allow satellite communications in the same band, and in February the FCC cleared Anterix to use wider channels there.

## Context

The report fits a pattern. Bloomberg noted that SpaceX agreed to a roughly $17 billion deal with EchoStar in September 2025 and, last month, to buy low-band spectrum from Grain Management for about $8 billion. It also said SpaceX has explored access to about 80,000 US cell sites. Analysts quoted were upbeat. Clear Street's Greg Pendy said, "They definitely want spectrum in that band."

Neither price nor any signed agreement has been reported. For the space industry, the notable part is who is buying: a launch company is now assembling terrestrial spectrum holdings to compete with AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile.
