NASA is reopening the question of how to get Perseverance's rock samples back from Mars. Program executive George Tahu told a virtual town hall for the planetary science community, according to SpacePolicyOnline, that a Perseverance Sample Retrieval Architecture (PSRA) strategy team will start in January and spend about a year studying alternatives.

The agency ended the original Mars Sample Return program last year. NASA and ESA had planned a lander to receive samples, a rocket to put them in Mars orbit and an ESA spacecraft to ferry them home, but costs climbed to about $11 billion. Outgoing Administrator Bill Nelson deferred the decision in January 2025, and the FY2026 budget request proposed cancellation. Congress gave $110 million for Mars Future Missions without specifically funding the sample program.

The new team is internal. A NASA spokesperson said it will include three year-long detail assignments for civil-servant engineers and scientists, and its job is an impartial recommendation on retrieving and safely returning the samples. It will review lessons from past efforts, assess what NASA and industry can do now, test whether constraints can be dropped, and consider new acquisition methods. The stated goal is "a high-level architecture approach and recommendations by the end of next year."

Administrator Jared Isaacman has said that returning the samples would be among the most important scientific achievements in history, SpacePolicyOnline notes. The open question is how to do it affordably. Meanwhile China plans to launch Tianwen-3 in 2028 and return samples in 2031.
