SpaceX is scheduled to launch USSF-266 for the U.S. Space Force on Tuesday, with the window opening at 7:55 a.m. PST (1555 UTC) from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. The payload is classified, and the Space Force has not described it.

The rocket is a Falcon 9 booster, B1081, flying for the 29th time. Its earlier missions include NASA's Crew-7, PACE and TRACERS. After stage separation it is due to land on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You* in the Pacific, less than 8.5 minutes after liftoff. According to Spaceflight Now, a successful touchdown would be the 236th landing on that vessel and the 681st booster landing overall.

## What the mission may be

USSF-266 is the third mission in a series carrying the "TH" designation. The first two, USSF-259 (TH-1) on September 16 and USSF-481 (TH-2) on October 15, each added 17 satellites to the Space Force's public orbital tracking catalog. That suggests USSF-266 may also deploy a batch of satellites, though that is inference rather than anything the Space Force has confirmed.

Spaceflight Now reports the mission is likely part of a $1.6 billion task order for 18 Falcon 9 launches in the Space Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio. That order was awarded in late July under the National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane 1 contract, and SpaceX is expected to complete all 18 launches by the end of 2027.

## A busy month for national security launches

USSF-266 is SpaceX's fifth government launch this month: four for the Space Force and one for the National Reconnaissance Office. Recent examples on the site include [USSF-317](/2026/11/21/spacex-ussf-317-launched/) and [NROL-136](/2026/11/22/nrol-136-launch-vandenberg-pad-record/). The cadence matters more than usual given that United Launch Alliance's Vulcan is sidelined after its [failure on the Amazon Leo launch](/2026/11/15/vulcan-fails-amazon-leo/), which leaves SpaceX carrying most of the load for Pentagon payloads in the near term.
