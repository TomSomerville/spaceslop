South Korea's space agency wants the country to be able to launch more than 20 times a year by the mid-2030s, a sharp step up from a national launch rate that today is a rocket or two annually. The target sits in a space transport strategy discussed on Monday at the 11th working-level committee on space development promotion, held in Sejong and led by Korea AeroSpace Administration (KASA) chief Oh Tae-seok, according to DigitalToday's English edition.

## The plan

The stated goal is for Korea to independently serve most of its major domestic satellite launch demand, including a planned Korean low Earth orbit communications network. To get there, the strategy leans on three families of vehicles: the government-developed Nuri rocket, next-generation launchers, and private-sector rockets.

For Nuri, the agency wants to build an institutional foundation and look for extra launch demand, on the theory that regular flights improve reliability and mission performance. It also plans to develop reusable next-generation vehicles aimed at low-cost, high-frequency transport, support demonstrations by private small and mid-sized launch companies, develop solid-fuel launchers, and expand launch infrastructure.

Note the caveat: the strategy and other committee items still need deliberation and approval by the National Space Committee before they are final. The 20-launch figure is therefore a proposal, not yet national policy. Earlier Korean reports have cited lower cadences, around 10 launches a year for reusable vehicles, so the number is worth watching as it moves through approval.

## Chollian 6

The committee did approve one concrete item: the development plan for Chollian 6, a geostationary satellite to succeed Chollian 2B, launched in 2020. It is being developed jointly by KASA and the ministries of climate, energy and environment and of oceans and fisheries, to track air pollutants such as fine dust and ozone as well as marine hazards including red tides and oil spills. It would be the first Korean satellite to carry a domestically produced geostationary environment and ocean payload.

Chollian 6 passed a preliminary feasibility study in August 2026. Development starts in 2027, with a launch targeted for 2033.

## Why it matters

Korea has a working medium-lift rocket in Nuri but no high-cadence launch industry. A 20-flight goal implies private launchers and a reusable vehicle doing most of the work, plus more pad capacity; the choice of a second launch site, which Korean reports say is due in December, is part of the same picture. Oh said space is an essential resource for national survival, touching communications, navigation, the economy and security, and said he hopes ministries and the private sector will cooperate to expand transport capability.
