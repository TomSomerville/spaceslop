Planet Labs and Stoke Space have signed a multi-launch agreement under which Planet's satellites will fly on Stoke's Nova rocket, the companies announced on 9 November. The deal gives Planet primary payload rights, which means it can choose launch dates and target orbital planes rather than sharing a ride and accepting someone else's schedule. The release doesn't state how many launches are covered or give a price.

## Where Nova stands

Missions will launch from Space Launch Complex 14 at Cape Canaveral, the pad that sent John Glenn into orbit in 1962. According to the release, Nova Pathfinder orbital flight demonstrations begin in 2027. The Nova Block 2 version is designed to carry more than 15 metric tons to low Earth orbit in a fully reusable configuration, and Stoke also plans a second launchpad and recovery infrastructure at SLC-14.

That timeline is the main caveat. Nova hasn't reached orbit, and the agreement is a bet on a vehicle still in development. Planet's own release says as much in its forward-looking statements, listing delays or technical challenges with third-party launch vehicles among the risks.

## Why Planet wants it

Planet says the agreement supports its long-term launch strategy for maintaining and expanding its constellation, and that it works with a diversified set of launch providers in the US and abroad. For an operator that flies many small satellites on a steady replacement cycle, dedicated or primary-payload access is valuable: rideshare missions are cheap but fix the orbit and the date.

Will Marshall, Planet's co-founder and CEO, said, "We need launch partners who move as quickly as we do." Andy Lapsa, Stoke's co-founder and CEO, said that providing dependable, customer-driven launch services is "at the core of what we are building at Stoke."

The announcement came on the day Planet's chief space officer, James Mason, was due to speak on a global space governance panel at the Reuters Space & Satellite Summit, where the release said he would discuss the company's multi-provider launch strategy. For Stoke, the deal is a named commercial customer ahead of first flight, which tends to matter when a new rocket is courting financing and further bookings.
