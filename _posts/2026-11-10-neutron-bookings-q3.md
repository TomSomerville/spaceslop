Rocket Lab says it signed eight Neutron launch contracts worth more than $515 million in the third quarter, all for a rocket that has not yet flown. Via Satellite reports the new deals lift Neutron's backlog to 16 missions, and CEO Peter Beck says the first 16 slots are booked, with the next open ones not until the 2030s.

The customers are ICEYE (three launches), Muon Space (two), Starcloud (one), Kepler Communications (one) and the US Space Force, which signed for a dedicated launch supporting its Space-Based Airborne Moving Target Indicator program. Rocket Lab is holding some capacity for its own space services missions.

## Price and schedule

The base price is now $70 million per launch, up from a previously reported target of $50 million. Beck said the company chose to avoid discounted early launches: "we set out to deliver a rocket into an environment with extreme demand at full price or higher." He also said "Launch is extremely constrained and that is exactly the situation we predicted."

The debut has slipped to 2027, following work on a first-stage tank issue that surfaced earlier this year. Separately, the Defense Department's Office of Strategic Capital announced a $150 million conditional loan commitment to Rocket Lab to raise Neutron's flight rate, upgrade manufacturing and build reusability infrastructure.

## The wider quarter

Group revenue hit a record $268 million, up 73% year on year, with net loss of $79.7 million against $18 million a year earlier. Space Systems made up $214.8 million of revenue and Launch $53.1 million. Total backlog is about $3.6 billion, up 52% from the end of Q2, and the combined launch manifest stands at 117 missions across Electron, Neutron and HASTE.

The pricing power is the signal: with Vulcan down and other medium-lift rockets late, customers are paying more to hold a place in line. That also means Neutron now carries a long queue of commitments to a vehicle with no flight history.
