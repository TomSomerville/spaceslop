Reaction Dynamics, the Quebec launch startup, unveiled Borealis on October 20: a medium-lift rocket designed to put 6 tonnes into low Earth orbit, with a first launch targeted for 2031.

Borealis is the second vehicle in a two-rocket roadmap. The first, Aurora, is the technological foundation, with a first orbital flight from Canadian soil targeted for the fourth quarter of 2028.

## Storable propellants

The company is moving to liquid propulsion using storable propellants, catalytic ignition and a scalable engine architecture. Storable propellants stay liquid at ambient temperature and need no cryogenic cooling, which Reaction Dynamics says could simplify ground infrastructure and allow flexible operations, including in northern regions. A campaign of 50 tests included 21 liquid-fueled engine hot fires, on top of more than 350 hot fires from earlier programs. Orbital-scale engine hardware is being built near Montreal, with testing due to begin in November 2026.

The release gives no engine thrust, vehicle dimensions, stage count or specific propellants.

## The business case

Since its 2017 founding Reaction Dynamics has secured about CAD $50 million in private investment and public funding commitments. It plans to raise another CAD $350 million in private investment through 2031. It has 68 employees and aims for more than 350, with about 375,000 square feet of manufacturing and integration space in Quebec.

The company eventually targets 24 Borealis missions per year and cites a commercial pipeline of more than US$5 billion backed by at least 10 agreements and letters of intent; it notes the pipeline includes non-binding commitments. CEO Bachar Elzein said, "We founded Reaction Dynamics because we believe Canada should be able to reach orbit on its own terms."

A 2031 date and a CAD $350 million raise are ambitious for a company at this stage, and Borealis is as yet a plan, not hardware. The next evidence will be the November engine tests and the Aurora flight schedule.
