The Korea Aerospace Administration (KASA) designated four technologies as "space new technologies" on November 25, its third round of the program, and for the first time included a space biotechnology entry.

The program, which gives domestic technologies a route to public procurement and promotion, designated five in January and eight in June, so the total is now 17. This round drew nine applications: seven satellite, one launch vehicle and one in a new "other types" category, which covers space medicine and space semiconductors, devices and materials. Four passed document and interview review, on-site verification and a final review.

## The four designations

- **Edelway**, satellites: an integrated unit for ultra-small synthetic aperture radar satellites, combining broadband waveform generation, received-signal processing and transmit/receive timing control in one device to cut size and weight.
- **IOPS**, satellites: an AI platform that detects anomalies and analyses the health of multiple types of satellite, designed to work in environments isolated from the external internet.
- **Perigee Aerospace**, launch vehicles: a cryogenic composite propellant tank that uses carbon fibre without a metal liner and is bonded with adhesive. The report says it is about 30 percent lighter than a conventional aluminium tank.
- **SpaceMedic**, other types: an unmanned, automated "space micro-physiological system" payload, verified on the Nuri rocket, that handles bio 3D printing, cell culture and real-time observation in orbit. It is the first space biology designation.

## What follows

KASA says it will support commercialisation through promotion, space-environment testing support and links to public procurement. Dailian paraphrases KASA Administrator Oh Tae-seok as saying the round shows the agency is finding convergence technologies such as space biology, beyond core satellite and launch work, and that priority public purchasing and innovative-product designation should help them reach the market.

The designations are not funding awards, but for small Korean firms a government stamp can matter when selling to agencies that are otherwise cautious about unproven hardware.
