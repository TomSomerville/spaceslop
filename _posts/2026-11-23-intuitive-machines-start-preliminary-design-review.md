Intuitive Machines said on November 23 that it has completed the preliminary design review (PDR) for START, the Stirling Technology spAce Research experimenT, a small nuclear-style power system meant to keep hardware alive through the lunar night.

START is being developed with the Air Force Research Laboratory's Space Operations via Nuclear Enhanced Technology (SONET) team, and the review is part of the $8.2 million AFRL contract extension the company announced earlier. The goal is to mature compact nuclear power technology toward flight readiness.

## What START will do

A lunar night lasts about 14 Earth days. Without sunlight, solar-and-battery spacecraft face a long, extremely cold stretch, which is why most landers have so far been short-lived once the Sun sets. START targets that gap by demonstrating Stirling power conversion, a heat engine that turns heat from a radioisotope source into electricity.

The demonstration will not use a real radioisotope. According to the release, START is slated to fly to the International Space Station through the ISS National Laboratory in early 2028, where the station's electrical power will stand in for the radioisotope heat source. That lets the company test the conversion hardware in the space environment without launching nuclear material.

Co-founder and CTO Tim Crain said in the release: "Surviving the lunar night is a fundamental infrastructure challenge we have to solve."

## Context

A PDR is an early engineering gate, well short of flight hardware, so the 2028 date is a plan rather than a commitment. The company describes itself as a space infrastructure company working across Earth orbit, cislunar space and deep space, and nuclear heat and power fits the sustained-operations pitch behind its lunar work.

The announcement comes as NASA is also courting industry for lunar surface power and infrastructure. Intuitive Machines is one of several companies chasing that market, and a flight-tested Stirling converter would be a credible building block for longer-lived Moon missions.
