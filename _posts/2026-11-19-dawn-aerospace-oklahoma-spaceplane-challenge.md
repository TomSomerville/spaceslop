Dawn Aerospace and the Oklahoma Space Industry Development Authority (OSIDA) on Thursday named the seven winners of their Suborbital Spaceplane Challenge. Together the teams will share 25 flights on Dawn's Aurora spaceplane, with each winner getting between three and five dedicated flights. Judges picked them from 12 finalist submissions.

## What flies

Aurora is an uncrewed, rocket-powered spaceplane that takes off from a conventional runway, climbs to the edge of space and glides back to land. According to Dawn, it can deliver up to 127 seconds of microgravity depending on payload mass, and can simulate lunar gravity (0.17 g) and Martian gravity (0.38 g) by controlling its rotation. The company describes boost-glide profiles up to Mach 3.7 and says flights are designed for twice-daily operations with a four-hour turnaround. Payloads are limited to 15 kg in a 250 mm cube, and teams get them back within minutes of landing.

## The winners

- **AstroTwitch** (Oklahoma State University, with ETH Zurich): muscle tissue response to microgravity.
- **Carbon-Carbon Leading Edge** (University of Tulsa): thermal protection materials for high-speed flight.
- **INFLATE** (Oklahoma State University and the Oklahoma Aerospace Institute for Research & Education): inflatable structures in microgravity.
- **Low-Cost Star Tracker Array** (University of Oklahoma, with the University of Colorado Colorado Springs and SEDS OU): a star tracker test above most of the atmosphere.
- **MitoTRACER** (University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center, with the University of Pittsburgh and Washington University School of Medicine): mitochondrial transfer between cells under space-related stress.
- **Modular Tank PMD Test Bed** (Oklahoma Baptist University, with Quantum Space): fuel behavior in tanks under microgravity, relevant to refueling and propulsion. PMD stands for propellant management device, the hardware that keeps liquid where the engine can use it.
- **T-GRAV** (Langston University): human T-cell behavior in reduced gravity.

## Why it matters

The flights will launch from Infinity One Oklahoma Spaceport in western Oklahoma, on the former Clinton-Sherman Air Force Base. Dawn says construction there is underway and that commercial flights are planned to begin in late 2027, so the winning experiments are effectively anchor customers for a site that has yet to host its first flight.

Grayson Ardies of the Oklahoma Department of Aerospace and Aeronautics and OSIDA said the Challenge "puts that investment to work for our universities." Dawn CEO Stefan Powell said of the research use case, "That is exactly what Aurora is built for."

The pitch is a cheap, frequent alternative to sounding rockets and parabolic aircraft for small payloads, with the runway-to-runway turnaround of an aircraft. That pitch is still unproven at the flight rates quoted: the numbers above are Dawn's design targets, not demonstrated performance, and the announcement gives no schedule for individual flights before the late-2027 start of commercial operations.
