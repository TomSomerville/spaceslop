The Expedition 75 crew spent the week loading a SpaceX cargo Dragon for its trip home, according to NASA's station blog. Dragon delivered over 6,300 pounds of science, supplies and hardware on 15 October and is nearing the end of its stay. NASA has not published a departure date. The capsule will splash down in the Pacific and be recovered by NASA and SpaceX teams.

NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Watkins began packing payloads on 12 November, and Menon and the Canadian Space Agency's Joshua Kutryk later rearranged return cargo to balance its mass and center of gravity. Items headed to Earth include samples such as bacterial DNA and 3D-printed blood vessels, plus lighting hardware that recreates natural daylight patterns after more than three years of testing. Final packing of preserved samples from portable freezers and incubators happens the day before departure.

Spacesuit upkeep followed a busy few weeks. Luke Delaney cleaned cooling and ventilation systems in two suits in the Quest airlock. Menon and Kutryk wore those suits on 29 October to install a roll-out solar array, and a spacewalk on 5 November installed the final roll-out array, per the blog.

Research continued around the cargo work. Delaney harvested thale cress grown in Kibo's Multi-use Variable-g Platform and preserved samples in the Life Science Glovebox. Roscosmos commander Pyotr Dubrov studied making high-purity semiconductor crystals in microgravity, and he and Anna Kikina tested AI tools for transcribing crew audio reports for ground controllers.
