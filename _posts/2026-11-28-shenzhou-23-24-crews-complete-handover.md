The Shenzhou-23 and Shenzhou-24 crews held their in-orbit handover ceremony on Nov. 28 Beijing time, formally passing control of the Tiangong space station to the newcomers, the China Manned Space Agency said on its news page.

The agency described the ceremony as a transfer of the station's "keys." It gave no exact time for the event.

## Who stays and who goes

Shenzhou-24 commander Tang Hongbo and crewmate Wu Yi now run the station, [after launching on Nov. 25](/2026/11/25/shenzhou-24-launches-first-foreign-astronaut/). Zhu Yangzhu, who arrived with the Shenzhou-23 crew, stays on board as part of the Shenzhou-24 crew for what the agency calls a one-year residency experiment. That is longer than the roughly six-month rotations China has used so far.

Heading home on Shenzhou-23 are Zhang Zhiyuan, who will command the return flight, Li Jiaying, and Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreign astronaut to fly to Tiangong. The agency said all three have finished their assigned tasks and will land at the Dongfeng site in Inner Mongolia "in the near future," without naming a date. They will bring back items from the station that the agency called "secret gifts."

## What's next

The handover was the last major step before departure. The landing site [finished its final full-system rehearsal](/2026/11/27/dongfeng-final-rehearsal-shenzhou-23-return/) on Nov. 26, and the agency said the site and supporting systems are preparing for the crew's return.

The same news page also noted that Pakistan's prime minister and army chief spoke by phone with Ali aboard the station on Nov. 28. The page gave no further detail on the call, and this report has not independently confirmed its content.

Undocking and landing are the remaining milestones. China has not announced timing, and the agency's wording suggests an announcement may come only shortly before the capsule leaves the station.
