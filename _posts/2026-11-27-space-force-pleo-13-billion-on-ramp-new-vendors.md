Space Systems Command, the Space Force's acquisition arm, posted a special notice on 24 November saying it will take on new vendors for its Proliferated Low Earth Orbit Satellite-Based Services (PLEO SBS) contract, according to Venture Atlas. The 3 billion ordering ceiling does not change; new entrants would compete for the capacity left under the existing ceiling rather than a fresh award.

The notice is a 30-day warning, not a request for proposals or a purchase commitment. Reporting on it says a formal solicitation is expected on SAM.gov after that period, with proposals accepted through 17 July 2028. Existing prime contractors are reportedly not eligible to apply. The GovConWire write-up of the notice could not be retrieved in full for this article, so details beyond the search summary rest on Venture Atlas.

## Why it matters

PLEO SBS is the vehicle the Pentagon uses to buy commercial low Earth orbit communications. Venture Atlas notes that DISA awarded seats on it in July 2023, when the ceiling was $900 million, to 16 companies including SpaceX and OneWeb Technologies. The ceiling was later raised to $13 billion; a Space Force official, quoted in a search summary, said the programme was going to hit the old ceiling in 2025.

An on-ramp gives newer constellation operators a way into government demand without a separate competition, and it keeps the contract as a single ordering channel as the military's reliance on commercial satellite capacity grows. What it does not do is add money: every dollar to new vendors comes out of the same $13 billion pool.
