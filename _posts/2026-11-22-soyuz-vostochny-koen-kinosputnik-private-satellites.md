A Soyuz-2.1b rocket launched from the Vostochny Cosmodrome on November 14 at 4:16 a.m. Moscow time (01:16 GMT), carrying the privately built KOEN and Kinosputnik spacecraft. Roscosmos described the launch as successful, according to TASS.

KOEN was built by MT-LAB and Kinosputnik by Sputnik Group. Roscosmos called it the first launch of private satellites subsidised under the Cosmos national project, and the first time private spacecraft made up the main payload of a Russian launch. The TASS report does not say how large the subsidies were, and did not name the launch pad or give orbital parameters. Launch databases list the mission as a Fregat-M upper stage flight to a sun-synchronous orbit with a handful of smaller secondary payloads, though that detail was not confirmed in the Roscosmos statement as reported.

## Why it matters

Russia's launch manifest has long been dominated by state and military payloads, with commercial work small by comparison. A flight where private Earth-observation satellites are the headline cargo, paid for in part through a national programme, is a signal that Moscow wants a domestic commercial remote-sensing sector. How competitive that sector can be, given sanctions and the loss of most Western customers, is a separate question this launch doesn't answer.

The Soyuz-2.1b, built by the Progress Rocket and Space Center, remains Russia's workhorse medium-lift vehicle. Vostochny, in the Far East, is the country's newest launch site and handles a growing share of its civil launches.
