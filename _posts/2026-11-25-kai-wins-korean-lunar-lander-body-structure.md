Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI) has won the job of building the skeleton of South Korea's first domestically developed Moon lander. According to Korean press reports based on a KAI announcement on November 25, the company signed a contract worth about 15 billion won with the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI) on November 23.

KAI will develop and deliver four lander body structures by December 2030: two for ground testing and two for flight verification. The structure is the part that carries the launch vibration and landing shock and holds the payloads and key equipment in place, which is why Korean outlets describe it as the lander's "bone structure."

## Part of the lunar Phase 2 program

The contract sits under the second phase of Korea's lunar exploration program, run by the Korea AeroSpace Administration. Phase 1 produced the Danuri orbiter, launched in 2022. Phase 2 aims for an independently built lander that demonstrates a soft landing on the Moon and carries science and technology payloads, with launch targeted for 2032.

KAI worked on Danuri's structure, domestically produced electronics, and assembly and testing. In November 2025 it joined development of a qualification model of the lander's landing gear. The new award widens its role from the legs to the whole body. The reports also cite KAI's thermal vacuum chamber, a 4-ton-class facility built in 2025, which they describe as the largest private test infrastructure of its kind in Korea.

The lander is being split across several contractors. Separate reporting from late 2025 had Hanwha Aerospace taking a roughly 103.3 billion won propulsion contract covering the landing engine and attitude thrusters, so the structure now has a supplier alongside it.

A KAI official said it was meaningful to join the lander program after Danuri, and pledged to use its satellite and probe experience to help Korea secure independent lunar exploration capability.
