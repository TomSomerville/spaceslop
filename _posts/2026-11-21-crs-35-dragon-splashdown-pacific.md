The uncrewed SpaceX Dragon that spent five weeks at the International Space Station is back. NASA says the capsule splashed down in the Pacific Ocean near Los Angeles at 2:03 p.m. PST on 21 November, completing the agency's 35th SpaceX commercial resupply services mission.

That is a little ahead of the roughly 1:55 p.m. PST target we [reported this morning](/2026/11/21/crs-35-dragon-undocks-splashdown-today/), after a weather delay pushed the undocking back a day. Dragon left the station at 11:05 a.m. CST on 20 November, per NASA's blog.

## The mission in brief

Dragon launched on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral and reached the station on 15 October, two days later. NASA says it delivered more than 6,300 pounds of science and supplies for the Expedition 75 crew, including a pair of roll-out solar arrays meant to supplement the station's power system.

## What came home

Dragon is still the only operational cargo vehicle that can return material to Earth intact, so the manifest matters. NASA lists:

- a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease;
- frozen tumor organoids treated aboard the station, which researchers hope will point to better cancer treatments and more personalized medicine;
- frozen microbial samples for studying how antibiotic-resistant bacteria survive and evolve in space;
- a lighting system that spent more than three years on the station, where it was tested for its potential to support astronauts' sleep and well-being on long missions.

NASA's post does not cover recovery operations or the next steps for the samples, and it includes no quotes from NASA or SpaceX officials. Neither agency streamed the reentry, so the blog post is the primary public record of the landing.
