Canada's Department of National Defence (DND) has signed a $20 million, 10-year Facility Access Agreement with NordSpace for priority use of a dedicated launch pad at the Atlantic Spaceport Complex in St. Lawrence, Newfoundland and Labrador. Defence Minister David McGuinty announced it on October 21 at the Space Bound Conference in Ottawa, according to NordSpace's press release.

The $20 million is a base fee for keeping facilities, systems and standby readiness in place. Individual launches would be contracted separately, so this is access to infrastructure, not an order for rockets.

## Standby launch

NordSpace says the agreement is meant to maintain and harden the site for secure, responsive launch. It plans to go further by keeping a launch-ready Tundra vehicle and several Aquila spacecraft buses on standby there, with a stated target of getting a payload from call-up to on-orbit operation in 24 to 72 hours depending on mission scope. That is a goal rather than a demonstrated capability. NordSpace is working with 3 Canadian Space Division to define requirements and mission profiles.

McGuinty said "Canada's sovereignty doesn't stop at the edge of our atmosphere." NordSpace CEO Rahul Goel said a sovereign spacefaring Canada "must be able to launch payloads into orbit from its own soil, on its own rocket."

## Where the pieces stand

The release says Phase 2 construction at the spaceport is targeted for completion by spring 2027. NordSpace's first satellite, Terra Nova, launched earlier in October and is operating in orbit. The Taiga suborbital vehicle is awaiting its first flight pending regulatory approval, and will later evolve into the VERTEX test platform. The company is also working with Transport Canada on launch approvals under the new Commercial Space Launch Act.

Canada has no orbital launch capability today, so a decade-long defence commitment gives NordSpace revenue visibility and a government anchor customer well before the Tundra has flown. Note that the source is a company-issued release.
