The U.S. Space Force is sounding out industry on a small fleet of spacecraft that would operate beyond geosynchronous orbit, in the space between Earth and the Moon. A request for information, posted on SAM.gov on Nov. 2 under the name Cislunar Space System, asks what vendors could deliver on a fast, low-risk schedule, according to Air & Space Forces Magazine.

## What the notice describes

The service is looking at up to five satellites, and possibly up to five separate contracts. Those spacecraft would be "seed vehicles" that inform the acquisition strategy for a possible proliferated cislunar constellation, the notice says. Officials are particularly interested in space domain awareness, positioning and navigation, and communications. The Space Force also wants propulsion for station-keeping and orbital transfers, plus onboard AI and compute for long stretches of autonomous operation.

Per SatNews, the timeline runs to launch readiness by late 2029 and full on-orbit operations by 2031, with up to five firm-fixed-price preliminary design study contracts as early as early 2027. SatNews gives a response deadline of Nov. 24. The buses would need to be commercial or near-commercial, with flight heritage in the past three years or qualification within 24 months, and would have to run software able to hold station and recover from faults for at least 72 hours without ground contact.

## Why it matters

Maj. Gen. Stephen Purdy, who oversees advanced programs, framed the effort against China's lunar plans, saying "They're not planning for a one-off demo." He described the approach as speed-first: "This isn't a four- or five-year science project. This is about getting capability out soon."

For launch providers and spacecraft makers, a defense customer for cislunar hardware would be a new market alongside NASA's Artemis and commercial lunar delivery work. The notice is only a request for information, not a solicitation, so no money is committed and the scope could change. The sources differ on some details, and neither article specifies payload mass, power or design life, so those figures should be treated as unconfirmed here.
