Rakuten AST, the new direct-to-phone satellite company formed by Rakuten Mobile and AST SpaceMobile, is in talks with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) over satellite production and future domestic launches, according to Japanese business coverage of the company's November 9 briefing.

## What was announced

Rakuten AST was announced on November 6. Rakuten Mobile holds 51% and AST's Japanese entity 49%, per ITmedia. The venture plans to receive up to 148 billion yen over three years under the J-LEO framework, a low-Earth-orbit communications project run through Japan's communications ministry. The aim is direct-to-device service, meaning ordinary smartphones connecting to satellites, with an emphasis on disaster resilience and on not depending on one foreign provider such as Starlink.

ITmedia's account says the planned constellation sits at about 690 km and 43 degrees inclination, and that service is targeted for March 2029. It adds that the company is discussing mass production of satellites with MHI. Toyo Keizai's headline says talks with MHI also cover domestic launches. Neither source I could read gave a launch schedule or contract, so this is discussion, not a deal.

## How many satellites?

The numbers do not line up. Toyo Keizai's headline refers to a 136-satellite, Japan-dedicated network; the body is paywalled and I could not confirm the breakdown. ITmedia, by contrast, describes coverage needs of 70 or more satellites with the current antenna, falling to about 40 to 50 with the larger next-generation antenna, which it puts at roughly 223 square metres against 6.2 square metres on current satellites. ITmedia's text was partly garbled in my fetch, so treat its figures as approximate. The two may describe different scopes, such as a Japan-only subset of a wider fleet, but that is my speculation, not something either article says.

## Why it matters

Starlink is the obvious benchmark, and Japan's government is putting public money behind a domestic alternative. The MHI talks matter because Japan's H3 rocket is the natural home-market launcher for such a fleet, though the agency and MHI have not commented in anything I read. Toyo Keizai frames the constellation race as having moved into a phase where deployment ability, not announcements, decides winners.
