SWISSto12, the Swiss maker of small geostationary satellites and radio-frequency hardware, has picked the German Aerospace Center (DLR) as its primary supplier of flight dynamics software for upcoming HummingSat missions. The company announced the deal on 18 November.

The work will be done by DLR's German Space Operations Center (GSOC) in Oberpfaffenhofen, near Munich. According to the release, the software covers orbit determination and propagation, manoeuvre planning and calibration, orbit events, antenna pointing and long-term mission analysis. SWISSto12 says the aim is to standardise flight dynamics across its programmes, using a European software package built into its own ground segment.

## Why it matters

Flight dynamics is the unglamorous maths that keeps a satellite where it is supposed to be. HummingSat is a compact GEO platform, and the release says the software is tuned for platforms that use electric orbit raising, electric station-keeping and combined orbit and attitude control. Electric propulsion makes the trajectory problem more involved than the chemical-thruster case, because burns are long and spread over weeks or months.

The contract builds on an existing relationship. GSOC already supports SWISSto12's mission analysis across several programmes, and DLR institute director Felix Huber said in the release that GSOC "has supported SWISSto12 since the early phases of the HummingSat missions." SWISSto12 CEO Emile de Rijk described the tie-up as bringing together "two highly complementary capabilities."

## Context

HummingSat is an ESA Partnership Project. SWISSto12 says it is currently delivering seven HummingSat GEO platforms to operators worldwide, and it also supplies payloads for several LEO constellations. The release calls the missions "upcoming" but does not give launch dates, customers' names or a contract value, so there is no way to tell from this announcement when the first of the software's real-world uses will come.

For SWISSto12, the practical effect is a standard European toolchain for operations rather than a bespoke setup per customer. For DLR, it is another external customer for its flight dynamics group, which also supports satellite operators beyond the agency's own missions.
