Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI), South Korea's main aircraft and satellite manufacturer, has agreed to invest 1.5 trillion won (roughly $1 billion) in South Gyeongsang Province over five years, from 2027 through 2031. The agreement was signed on November 23 at the provincial government headquarters.

The signatories were the provincial government, the trade and industry ministry, the cities of Jinju and Sacheon, and KAI. Per the Seoul Economic Daily, the money is earmarked for next-generation civil aircraft technology, localization of high-value aviation components, satellite systems and infrastructure, and components for next-generation combat systems. The text of the agreement has not been released, and reporting does not say how the total divides across those areas or how much goes to satellites specifically.

## Who does what

KAI will draw up its own investment plan. The ministry will back the project and review regulatory changes, while the province and the two cities will help secure sites and speed permits. KAI also plans to build up local suppliers and create jobs through research partnerships and specialist training.

Governor Park Wan-su called the deal a turning point for the aerospace industry in Gyeongnam and in South Korea, and said he expects KAI to spend on R&D as well as facilities, including aircraft engines and satellite systems. He added that what matters is carrying it out quickly.

## Why it matters

Sacheon is where the Korea AeroSpace Administration and KAI's headquarters sit, and the province is trying to build an aerospace complex city around them. Park has called for a special law on that complex this year. The KAI deal follows a 312 trillion won advanced-industry plan for the Yeongnam region announced in July, and talks with Hanwha Aerospace, Hanwha Ocean and Hyundai Wia on similar plans are continuing.

Seoulz reports a parallel trade-finance arrangement the same day: Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai Rotem and KAI, with three banks, will create a 58.4 billion won fund that backs 820 billion won in preferential guarantees for partner companies. The trade ministry puts the combined package at 1.5 trillion won of investment plus 820 billion won of financial support.

This is a regional industrial-policy deal rather than a space contract, and the satellite share is unquantified. Still, it shows how Seoul is trying to concentrate its space and defense supply chain around the new space agency's home base.
