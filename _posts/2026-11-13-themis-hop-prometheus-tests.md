Europe's Themis reusable-rocket demonstrator is still waiting for its first hop, and ESA says recent engine tests do not change that. European Spaceflight reports that ESA and ArianeGroup confirmed hot-fire tests of the Prometheus engine on 29 October at DLR's P5 bench in Lampoldshausen were "conducted independently of Themis flight preparations", in ArianeGroup's words. ArianeGroup announced the completion on 6 November.

One aim of the tests was to validate a new combustion chamber made with Cold Gas Spray additive manufacturing, intended to build engines faster and cheaper. ArianeGroup is preparing Prometheus Mark 2 for a later campaign. It would power Themis 1E, a variant with upgraded structures and tanks for longer, higher flights. An ESA spokesperson said the operating profile for Themis's first flight has already been tested with the same engine configuration as the demonstrator's.

The schedule has slipped repeatedly. In January ESA officials said they were waiting for spring to fly from Esrange in Sweden, and Director General Josef Aschbacher had aimed at the first quarter. Neither happened. Géraldine Naja, ESA's new Director of Space Transportation, said at a 22 October press conference that she was unsure the first hop would happen this year. European Spaceflight reports ArianeGroup and Esrange operator SSC Space are expected to decide by the end of November whether to try for a 2026 flight, with winter snow threatening to halt testing.
