Space One, the Tokyo-based private launch company, has put a date on the fourth flight of its Kairos solid-fuel rocket: Friday, January 22, 2027, in a 15:00 to 15:20 window local time, from its own Spaceport Kii in Wakayama Prefecture. The announcement came in a short press release on the company's website on November 24.

If weather, hardware or range issues get in the way, the company has set a backup period from January 23 through March 31, 2027. The release says the exact liftoff time may be reset within the daily window.

## What the release does not say

The notice is a scheduling document, and it is thin on substance. It does not name payloads, describe changes to the vehicle, or give a cause for the previous failure. Those details may come in separate material from the company, but they were not in the release.

The track record explains why that matters. Kairos has flown three times without reaching orbit. The first launch in March 2024 ended about five seconds after liftoff, when the flight termination system detected lower-than-expected velocity and thrust. The second flight in December 2024 failed after a longer flight. The third, in March 2026, was terminated in flight, and five small satellites aboard were lost, according to Space.com and the company's own earlier notices.

## Why it matters

Space One is Japan's attempt at a commercial small-launch company, backed by a group of major Japanese corporations, and it operates the country's first privately owned launch site. Japan has been pushing to build a domestic commercial launch industry alongside the H3, and Kairos is the furthest-along private candidate. A successful flight 4 would be the first orbital success for a Japanese private rocket.

The roughly two-month gap between the announcement and the target date is a fairly normal runway for a launch campaign with public hazard-area notices, which the release says are attached as separate documents. The company also notes there is no public viewing area at the site and that traffic controls around it are under consideration.

Until Space One publishes the findings from the third flight and the manifest for the fourth, the January date is best read as a target. Three failures in three attempts means the more interesting information will be what changed.
