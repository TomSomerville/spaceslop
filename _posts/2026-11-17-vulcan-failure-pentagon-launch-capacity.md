ULA's Vulcan failure on 15 November is now a national security story as well as a commercial one. [Our earlier report](/2026/11/15/vulcan-fails-amazon-leo/) covered the loss of 40 Amazon Leo satellites; Payload's follow-up looks at what it means for the Pentagon, which depends on Vulcan for some of its hardest missions.

## What is known

ULA said the mission was lost about ten minutes into flight, but that trouble began just after liftoff. Per Payload, booster performance problems led to stage separation while the vehicle was too slow to reach orbit. ULA says its solid rocket boosters performed nominally. The FAA has opened a mishap investigation. ULA wrote on X that "Today, we did not meet that standard", referring to its mission of providing "assured access to space for our nation."

The flight was Vulcan's return after a February launch that suffered a solid rocket booster issue, though it still delivered its Space Force payloads. That anomaly led to a nine-month grounding while the Space Force investigated, and in October the service reassigned six national-security launches from Vulcan to SpaceX.

## Why the Pentagon cares

The National Security Space Launch program splits work into two lanes. Lane 1 covers less complex missions that tolerate more risk and has gone to newer providers including Blue Origin, Rocket Lab, Stoke Space and Relativity. Lane 2 holds the most demanding "no-fail" missions. So far only SpaceX, with Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy, and ULA, with Vulcan, have flown them. Blue Origin's New Glenn is eligible but has not.

Payload notes that repeated Vulcan groundings show how thin the redundancy is, a point sharpened by Elon Musk's stated intention to eventually retire the Falcon family in favour of Starship. Analysts worry the failure strains already limited capacity for government and commercial customers. One investor, Lewis Alun Jones of Generation Space, argued it could push the Defense Department to lean on SpaceX to keep flying Falcon 9: "It puts more pressure on the DoD to put pressure on SpaceX."

Payload does not say which Space Force missions, if any, were affected by this flight, and no cause has been announced.
