Hyundai Rotem, the South Korean defense and rail manufacturer, has completed a second test building at its Aerospace Propulsion Test Center in Seosan, South Chungcheong Province. The company held the completion ceremony on November 17 and announced it the next day, according to Segye Ilbo. The new building is due to start full operation in 2027.

## What the building is for

The Seosan site was built in 1998 on roughly 23,140 square meters. Hyundai Rotem describes it as the only facility in Korea equipped to test both hypersonic and supersonic engines, and the report says it previously supported test work on the KSR-III liquid rocket engine and the propulsion system for the Nuri launch vehicle.

The second building works alongside the first. It houses combustion test equipment, rooms for performance testing of individual components, and upgraded safety systems. Its planned workload covers three areas:

- **Methane engines for reusable launchers**, in 35-tonne and 10-tonne thrust classes. Hyundai Rotem says it first ran a domestic methane engine combustion test in 2006 and is aiming for what it calls Korea's first commercial methane engine. Methane burns with less soot than kerosene, which is why it is favored for engines meant to be reused.
- **Hypersonic engines**, specifically a solid-fuel scramjet and a dual-mode ramjet/scramjet that switches mode with flight speed.
- **Major components for upper-stage engines** that place satellites in orbit.

## Contracts behind it

The report ties the expansion to two programs. Hyundai Rotem designed and built a scramjet combustor assembly for the HyCore hypersonic vehicle project run with the Agency for Defense Development, and the article says the vehicle exceeded its target speed. The company also won a contract last year from the Defense Technology Promotion Research Institute to develop methane engine technology for reusable launch vehicles.

CEO Lee Yong-bae said the center represents more than 30 years of work indigenizing guided-weapon and launch-vehicle engines, and that he expects the new building to play a central role in Korea's defense self-reliance and its ambitions as a space power. A company official called the expansion a strategic foothold for securing independent propulsion technology.

## Why it matters

Test capacity is a real bottleneck for new engine programs, and Korea's reusable-launch ambitions currently run through a handful of facilities. Adding a second building ahead of 2027 suggests Hyundai Rotem expects methane engine hardware to be ready for sustained firing within the year. The article gives no first-firing date and no flight schedule for the engines, so the timeline beyond the building itself remains unstated.
