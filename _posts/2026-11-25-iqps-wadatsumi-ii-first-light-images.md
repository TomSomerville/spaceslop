Japanese radar imaging company iQPS (QPS Research Institute) has released the first images from its newest satellite, QPS-SAR20, nicknamed WADATSUMI-II. The company said on November 25 that the satellite began observations on November 19, after Rocket Lab's Electron launched it in early November. Our earlier article covers [that launch](/2026/11/22/electron-sea-god-surveys-iqps-wadatsumi-ii/).

## What the images show

iQPS published seven first-light images taken between November 19 and 24. In its high-definition spotlight mode the satellite delivers 46 cm azimuth resolution; range resolution varied from 36 to 69 cm across the set. In normal stripmap mode the resolution is 1.8 m.

The locations were chosen to show off synthetic aperture radar, which images by radar echo rather than reflected sunlight. They include Yakushima in Kagoshima under cloud and light rain, Rocket Lab's Launch Complex 1 on New Zealand's Mahia Peninsula at night, a 7 km view of Venice with its islands, bridges and boats, Matsuyama in Ehime in the rain, the Mayon volcano in the Philippines, central Sydney with its harbour and ships, and Mahia Beach in clear weather.

iQPS says the cloudy, rainy and night-time shots illustrate the all-weather, day-and-night capability it is selling for disaster response, infrastructure monitoring and environmental observation. Image processing was supplied by Altek Technology of Taiwan.

## Industry context

The satellite was built with more than 25 partner companies, mainly in northern Kyushu, which is part of iQPS's pitch as a regional manufacturing chain. The company is racing to fill out a constellation of small SAR satellites so it can revisit any spot on Earth frequently, the same strategy pursued by Synspective, ICEYE and Capella. First-light images are a company-chosen showcase, so the real test is how consistently the constellation delivers that quality to paying customers.
