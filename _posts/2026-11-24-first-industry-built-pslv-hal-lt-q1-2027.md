India's first fully industry-built Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle is finished and waiting for a launch slot. ISRO Chairman V Narayanan told the fifth India International Space Conclave on Nov. 24 that the rocket, built by the HAL-L&T consortium, will make its maiden flight in the first quarter of 2027. "The first launch is expected next year in the first quarter," he said, according to The Tribune.

Under a September 2022 contract with NewSpace India Limited, ISRO's commercial arm, the consortium is to build five PSLV-XL rockets end to end. Until now, industry supplied components while ISRO integrated and launched the vehicle. Handing over whole-rocket production is meant to free ISRO to concentrate on newer programs.

## Wider remarks

Narayanan used the conclave, organised by the Indian Space Association, IN-SPACe and ISRO, to push for more private participation. He said India's space startup base has grown to about 440 companies and that 70 to 80 percent of launch vehicle and satellite mission budgets go to domestic industry. He named large optics, raw materials, semiconductors, processors and robotics as gaps: India cannot yet make 2 to 3 meter optics for geostationary platforms, and a single optical payload can take about 1.5 years to build.

On navigation, he said ISRO plans three more NavIC satellites over the next 1.5 years to rebuild the regional constellation. On Gaganyaan, the crewed program, he said preparations are in the final phase, with the orbital module still being worked on, and that launch would come at the earliest opportunity. Our earlier piece covers the uncrewed G1 test flight planned for December.

The PSLV date is a target stated by the agency head, not a scheduled launch, and ISRO has not published a firm day.
