Space Cargo Unlimited has finally shown off REV1, the reusable reentry vehicle it first announced nearly four years ago, and attached a new date to it. The startup unveiled the design on Nov. 17 at Space Tech Expo in Bremen, Germany, and told Aviation Week it aims to deploy REV1 and its supporting elements in 2030.

REV1 is co-developed with Thales Alenia Space. Aviation Week reports a mass of 3.7 tonnes, a payload of just over a tonne, a continuous power supply of 2.5 kilowatts and a design life of about 20 flights. Interesting Engineering adds that missions could run up to six months in orbit. The payload is roughly ten times what the company's current BentoBox platform carries.

## Two halves, one docking problem

The notable design choice is a split architecture. REV1 is a reentry capsule paired with a separate orbital power module. Only the capsule comes home; the power module stays up. Aviation Week notes that splitting the vehicle saves mass and launch demand, but it means the two pieces have to dock on orbit, and the company has not yet chosen suppliers for the docking hardware. When the capsule is away, the orbital module is meant to act as a node in a low Earth orbit communications network and provide high-bandwidth links to REV1.

The company also pitches features aimed at pharma and biotech customers: late loading of time-sensitive cargo such as biological samples, vibration and load damping for launch and reentry, live sensor data and edge computing so customers can watch experiments run, and certified chain-of-custody handling for biological payloads. Aviation Week reports the firm is in talks with more than 150 potential customers, many in life sciences.

"With BentoBox, we are developing the space factory that can fly across a fleet of vehicles," CEO Nicolas Gaume said.

## The road to 2030

That fleet strategy is how the company plans to earn revenue before REV1 exists. Its BentoBox microgravity platform is due to fly next year on ATMOS Space Cargo's Phoenix 2 capsule, a mission that slipped from 2026 to 2027. A one-tonne BentoBox is booked on SpaceX's Starfall reentry vehicle no earlier than 2028. Interesting Engineering reports the company booked at least 10 missions on various reentry vehicles this year, and SpaceSlop covered its [MaiaSpace launch booking](/2026/11/18/space-cargo-unlimited-maiaspace-launch/) last week.

REV1's timeline has drifted before: earlier reporting targeted a 2025 launch. Until 2030, the company is effectively a customer of other people's capsules, including ATMOS and SpaceX, some of which are chasing the same in-space manufacturing market it hopes to serve with its own vehicle.
