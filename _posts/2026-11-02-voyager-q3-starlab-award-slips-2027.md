Voyager Technologies, the lead partner in the Starlab commercial space station, reported third-quarter results on November 2 with record bookings and backlog, and a message investors may care more about: NASA's award for the next phase of its commercial station program has slipped from this year to spring 2027.

## The numbers

Revenue was $88.7 million, up 124% year over year, though about $31 million of that came from Astrobotic, which Voyager acquired this year. Excluding it, revenue was roughly $58 million. Bookings reached $116 million, mostly organic, and backlog rose to $742 million from $336 million, including $347 million from Astrobotic. Defense awards totaled $62 million, and the company cited a new partnership with Anduril building on $60 million of existing joint contracts. Initial production units from its Pueblo defense complex are expected in early 2027.

The loss widened. Adjusted EBITDA was a $44.9 million loss, against $38 million in Q2, and the GAAP loss was $55.7 million. Gross margin was 11.5%, short of the roughly 17% management had guided. Voyager cut its full-year margin target to low double digits from mid-teens, and lowered its Q4 outlook. It raised the low end of 2026 revenue guidance by $10 million to $285 million to $305 million, and reaffirmed positive EBITDA exiting 2027 and positive free cash flow in 2028.

## Starlab and NASA

Signed Starlab reservations reached about $700 million, up from $500 million the previous quarter. But NASA has issued its Commercial LEO Destinations Phase 2 request for proposals, and management said the award is expected in spring 2027, later than previously hoped. Voyager also gave no update on a defense contract it had expected by year-end, and postponed its December investor day because of the timing of Griffin-1, the Astrobotic lunar lander.

Management gave a 2027 framework of $150 million to $200 million in Astrobotic revenue, after a second-half contribution of $40 million to $50 million.

The figures here come from a third-party earnings summary, not Voyager's own release, which we did not read. Cash is the pressure point: third-quarter free cash flow was negative $47.8 million, and liquidity of $815.7 million sits against $841.1 million of convertible notes.
