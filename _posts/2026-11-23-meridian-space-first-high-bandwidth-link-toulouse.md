Meridian Space says its First Customer Link (FCL) demonstration satellite has established a high-bandwidth connection with a ground segment in Toulouse, France, validating the company's reflectarray antenna in orbit, Satellite Today reported on November 16.

The link follows the payload's activation in early October, which we covered in [an earlier article](/2026/10/02/nanoavionics-meridian-reflectarray-payload/). FCL launched on October 1 aboard a SpaceX Transporter-18 rideshare. Meridian built the communications payload; Kongsberg NanoAvionics supplied the satellite bus. Meridian's earlier announcements did not include the link's speed, duration or latency, and the Satellite Today report does not either.

## What the antenna does

A reflectarray is a flat antenna that steers a signal electronically rather than with a dish. Meridian says earlier reconfigurable reflectarrays were fixed installations ill-suited to telecom, while its design uses far less power than other software-defined satellites and fits on a smaller platform. The company puts the work at about five years of development.

CEO Massimiliano Ladovaz, who joined in July 2025 after stints at OneWeb, Eutelsat, SES and Inmarsat, told the outlet the antenna has now been proven both on the ground and in orbit. The pitch is cheaper low Earth orbit constellations that governments and regional operators could own rather than lease.

## What comes next

Meridian plans a testing phase with early customers, including governments, regional operators and telecoms, covering fixed and mobile use and possibly defence waveforms. It intends to scale production in Long Beach, California, and expand in Europe, and is working on a "flat satellite design" that would fit more spacecraft into a single launch.

The company is a spin-out of SpinLaunch, which is being separated into an independent business. It met its goal of a first launch in 2026, set when it announced the constellation in April 2025. The October announcement tied FCL to a €122.5 million contract for NanoAvionics to build 280 satellites, with Meridian Express deliveries expected from 2027. Whether the demonstration translates into customer contracts is the next thing to watch.
