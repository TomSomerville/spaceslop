Airbus has agreed to sell its US satellite manufacturing arm to Cerberus Capital Management, the private equity firm that already owns Stratolaunch. The deal was announced on 24 November. Financial terms were not disclosed, and the parties expect to close by the end of 2026.

The business is Airbus U.S. Space Systems, the satellite production operation of Airbus U.S. Space & Defense at Merritt Island, Florida, near NASA's Kennedy Space Center. Air Data News reports the factory covers roughly 200,000 square feet and has built more than 650 satellites that have gone to orbit.

## What Cerberus says it will do

Cerberus intends to turn the unit into an "independent, US-controlled satellite manufacturer," according to the report, and plans to invest in engineering, product development and domestic suppliers. The firm manages about $74 billion in assets and set up a fund in 2022 aimed at industries critical to US national security. Its aerospace portfolio already includes Stratolaunch, which it bought in 2019, and Vivace International, acquired in February 2026. Retired Gen. John W. Raymond, the first Chief of Space Operations of the US Space Force, is a Cerberus senior managing director and is involved in the deal.

## Why Airbus is letting go

The Florida plant traces back to a 2016 joint venture with OneWeb, built to mass-produce small satellites. Airbus bought out the remaining 50 percent from Eutelsat OneWeb in January 2024. The Financial Times reported in August that Airbus had opened a strategic review of the US operation, and the sale follows from that.

Airbus has said the sale does not mean it is leaving satellite manufacturing. The review ran separately from Project Bromo, the planned combination of Airbus's space activities with those of Leonardo and Thales in Europe. The practical effect is a cleaner split: the European space business consolidates in one venture, while the US factory moves to an owner pitching itself as a domestic, US-controlled supplier, a label that matters for national security work.
