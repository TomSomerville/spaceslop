Canadensys Aerospace, the Toronto-area company best known for its lunar rover work, has landed its first U.S. Army order for RAPTOR, an electric six-wheeled off-road vehicle derived from its Moon-mobility technology, SpaceQ reported on November 26.

RAPTOR carries a sensor suite and can be driven remotely or run autonomously. It has a gross vehicle weight rating of three tons, roughly 2,700 kg, and is aimed at customers who need a heavy electric vehicle that can cope with rough terrain. Neither the number of vehicles nor the value of the Army order was disclosed.

## A product line built on lunar know-how

The Army deal is not the company's first RAPTOR sale. CEO Christian Sallaberger said Canadensys has already sold the vehicle to several U.S. customers, and in October 2024 it shipped the last vehicle from an order for a European defence customer. Sallaberger said the company is "proud that technologies we developed for Moon missions are being used" to support North American security and sovereignty goals.

The pattern is a familiar one for small space hardware firms: lunar rovers are a long-lead, government-dependent business, so a terrestrial derivative with a nearer-term buyer helps keep the engineering team funded.

## Lunar context

The timing matters because Canada's own lunar rover plans have been in flux. The Canadian Space Agency ended its Lunar Rover Mission in March; that small rover had been planned to reach the Moon's south pole in 2029. Canadensys remains one of three companies studying the agency's larger Lunar Utility Vehicle, according to SpaceQ.

For Canadensys, a defence customer for a vehicle derived from its lunar work offers revenue while the Canadian lunar programme is reshaped. Details of what the Army intends to do with the vehicle were not given in the report.
