The EU Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA) has published the fourth part of its 2026 market report series, and for the first time it covers secure satellite communications and space situational awareness (SSA), the tracking of objects and collision risk in orbit.

## Secure satcom

EUSPA forecasts that revenue from secure satellite communications data services sold to EU users will grow from just over €200 million in 2025 to nearly €1.2 billion by 2040. Maritime surveillance is the biggest source of demand today, but the agency expects law enforcement, civil protection and force deployment to overtake it by 2040.

The report also estimates that "communications on the move", meaning links to ships, aircraft and vehicles, will account for about 25% of fixed satellite service capacity demand between 2025 and 2040, including its use in emergency management.

On the supply side, EUSPA points to GOVSATCOM, the EU's pooled government capacity hub. It draws on eight satellites operated by five member states. Cyprus made the first operational use of the service in March 2026. EUSPA also says it will manage government services on IRIS², the planned constellation of around 350 satellites.

## Collision avoidance

For SSA, the report summarizes the EU Space Surveillance and Tracking (EU SST) service between March 2025 and February 2026. In that period it analyzed about 30,000 close approaches, more than 4,000 of them rated high-risk, involving over 640 spacecraft from more than 105 organizations. Its front desk delivered over 270,000 products and added 314 registered users and 113 registered satellites.

## Navigation security

The agency also highlights Galileo's OSNMA feature, which lets receivers verify that navigation signals are authentic and so helps spot certain spoofing attacks.

These are agency forecasts rather than market observations, and the long horizon to 2040 means they say more about EUSPA's expectations for government demand than about near-term sales. Even so, the numbers sit alongside the bloc's recent push for sovereign connectivity through IRIS² and national military constellations.
