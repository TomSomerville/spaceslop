Blue Origin is reportedly now aiming to fly New Glenn again on February 20, 2027, about a month later than the January 2027 target given in early November. The report comes from Advanced Television, which cites unnamed reports and does not name an outlet. Blue Origin has not announced the date, and the article itself describes the delay as unconfirmed.

The vehicle is expected to carry AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird satellites 17, 18 and 19, according to the same report, though it says that payload is only "likely". No mass or configuration details were given.

## How it got here

New Glenn's recent history explains the caution. The article recounts that the NG-3 mission launched on April 19, suffered a mishap and was de-orbited. On May 28 the NG-4 vehicle exploded during a static fire at Launch Complex 36 in Cape Canaveral, destroying the rocket and badly damaging Blue Origin's only operational New Glenn pad. On August 5 the cause was confirmed as one of the rocket's BE-4 engines.

The November 15 loss of 40 Amazon Leo satellites on a ULA launch added to worries about the BE-4, which also powers Vulcan, though the report says a preliminary investigation eased those concerns.

Earlier in the month Blue Origin moved its return to flight from December to January 2027, as covered in our [earlier article on GS3 and Northstar](/2026/11/05/blue-origin-new-glenn-gs3-northstar.html). A February date would be a second slip in roughly three weeks, if it holds.

## Why it matters

The delay lands hardest on customers waiting for a ride. The report points to AST SpaceMobile's near-term deployment plans as the bigger concern, but does not say how the schedule would change. Until Blue Origin confirms a date, treat February 20 as a working target from a single source rather than a settled schedule.
