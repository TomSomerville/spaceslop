SpaceX and Tesla have picked five directors to guide the committees negotiating a possible merger, the Financial Times reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters, relaying the story, said it could not immediately verify it, and neither company had responded to requests for comment.

According to the FT account, three of the directors come from Tesla: Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung. Two come from SpaceX: Roelof Botha and Randy Glein. They sit on the special committees of independent directors that Musk said in October had been formed at both companies, with Musk himself recused because he is conflicted.

## Advisers and structure

The FT reports that advisers are refining the financial models that will anchor each side's negotiating position in an all-stock deal. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs are said to be advising SpaceX, and Evercore is said to be advising Tesla. The bankers are also reportedly looking at where the two businesses overlap.

## Hurdles

The FT flags steep regulatory obstacles, tied to SpaceX's US defense contracts on one side and Tesla's dependence on China on the other. SpaceX's national-security work, from launch contracts to Starshield, is exactly the sort of business that makes a merger with a company exposed to Chinese operations awkward.

Bloomberg reported earlier this month that an announcement could come as early as December, according to Finimize's summary. No merger agreement has been signed, and the FT's sourcing is anonymous, so the timeline and the committee line-up should be treated as unconfirmed.

For the space industry the interesting question is what a combined company does with SpaceX's launch and Starship business, and how its defense customers react.
