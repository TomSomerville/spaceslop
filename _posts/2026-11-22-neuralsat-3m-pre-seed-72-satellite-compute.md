NeuralSat GmbH, a German startup founded earlier this year, has raised €2.9 million (about $3.2 million) in pre-seed funding to develop a 72-satellite network for relaying and processing data in orbit. The round was announced on Thursday, November 13.

Capnamic and Bayern Kapital co-led the round. Other participants were ZOHO.VC, Robin Capital, BAB (the development bank of Bremen and Bremerhaven), Mission E and KIEMIS Holding. The company is led by CEO Mohd Bilal.

## What it plans to build

The concept combines two fashionable ideas: space-based data relay and orbital computing. NeuralSat wants operators to be able to offload both communications and processing onto its constellation, reaching up to 50 Gbps through optical and high-frequency radio links. Target uses include real-time edge processing, hosted workloads for commercial and defence customers, Earth observation and signals intelligence. Bilal said the company intends to "transform how intelligence is connected and delivered in space."

## Timeline

The new money will go toward a first in-orbit demonstration node, targeted for October 2027. Commercial operational satellites would follow after that. A 72-satellite constellation is a large ambition for a pre-seed company, and the funding announced covers only the early demonstration phase; the reporting does not say how the full network would be financed or launched.

The startup joins a growing group of European companies pitching orbital data infrastructure as demand rises for processing satellite data closer to where it is collected.
