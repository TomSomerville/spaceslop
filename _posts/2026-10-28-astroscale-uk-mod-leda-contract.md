Astroscale's UK subsidiary has been awarded a £15 million contract by the UK Ministry of Defence to act as prime contractor for LEDA, a new defence space programme. The company announced the award on 28 October from its base at Harwell, Oxfordshire.

## What LEDA is

Under the contract Astroscale UK will manufacture, launch and operate the LEDA spacecraft. Astroscale says the mission will feed the UK's understanding of orbital warfare, including how such spacecraft could counter rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) threats, that is, another satellite manoeuvring close to a UK asset. BAE Systems and Open Cosmos are named as key partners.

The award lands against a shifting policy backdrop. Astroscale points to the recently published UK Space Strategy, which puts defence and national security at the centre, and to the Defence Investment Plan, which allocates £3.2 billion to space through 2030.

## Why Astroscale

The company's pitch rests on its servicing record. ELSA-d demonstrated rendezvous and magnetic capture of a client spacecraft, and in 2024 ADRAS-J approached and inspected a non-cooperative rocket body from under 15 metres. Astroscale says its UK team built that mission's ground segment and led flight dynamics operations. Upcoming work includes ELSA-M, billed as the first active debris removal mission involving a commercial satellite, and Orpheus, aimed at sovereign UK defence capability.

Nick Shave, Astroscale's UK managing director, said the aim is to be "the trusted partner for Space Control". Rear Admiral Tony Williams of the National Armaments Director Group (NADG) said "control of space is not about seeking conflict in space", framing the capabilities as protection for the economic, societal and military uses of space.

The contract is small beside the headline defence budget, but it is a tangible step in moving a company known for debris removal toward defence customers, a path Astroscale says it expects to carry into export markets as well.
