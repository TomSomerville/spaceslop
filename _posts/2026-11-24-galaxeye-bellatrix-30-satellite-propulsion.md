Indian Earth-observation startup GalaxEye has awarded Bellatrix Aerospace a multi-year contract to supply propulsion for its next 30 Drishti Gen-II satellites, Inc42 reports. The deal was announced at the fifth India International Space Conclave, organised by the Indian Space Association. The value was not disclosed.

## What Bellatrix will deliver

Bellatrix will design, build, test and deliver its Arka Hall-effect electric propulsion systems, starting with units for the first two satellites and scaling up to the full set. It will be the program's primary propulsion supplier. The thrusters will move satellites from their drop-off orbits to assigned slots, hold constellation spacing, dodge debris and deorbit spacecraft at end of life.

The satellites form GalaxEye's OptoSAR constellation, which the company aims to have deployed by 2030. The two companies describe the order as the largest private-sector space order in India by satellite count, a claim we have not independently verified.

## Context

GalaxEye's first satellite launched in May on a SpaceX Falcon 9 under Mission Drishti. In July the company said it had lost contact with the spacecraft after an anomaly it attributed to a geomagnetic solar storm. The Gen-II order is therefore a statement of intent after a rough first flight. GalaxEye is also in talks with other Indian companies for power, control and communications subsystems, and cofounder Pranit Mehta said, "The Indian private ecosystem is here and now," urging Indian firms to partner with each other rather than lean only on government.

Bellatrix, for its part, flew its propulsion technology on ISRO's PSLV-C58 POEM-3 mission in January 2024, and raised $20 million in a pre-Series B round led by Cactus Partners in March 2026, per Inc42.
