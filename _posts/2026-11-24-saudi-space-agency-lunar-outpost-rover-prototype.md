Lunar Outpost, the Colorado rover company, said on November 24 that the Saudi Space Agency (SSA) has selected it to support joint development of an engineering prototype of lunar rover technologies. The announcement came from the company; the SSA's position was not part of the release.

According to Lunar Outpost, the work covers concept definition, preliminary engineering, design and systems integration. Some activities will take place in Saudi Arabia. The prototype is meant to test technical concepts and help the kingdom build skills in robotic mobility, systems engineering and integration. SSA engineers will take part directly in development and testing alongside Lunar Outpost specialists.

Lunar Outpost founder and CEO Justin Cyrus said that "The Saudi Space Agency has established a clear technical framework to drive this collaboration." He added that the company looks forward to "contributing our proven expertise in advanced off-planet robotics."

The release gave no contract value, schedule, prototype name or milestones, so it is unclear how large the effort is or how it relates to any flight plans.

## Why it matters

For the SSA, the deal is a route to hands-on lunar hardware experience through an international partner rather than building a rover capability from scratch. For Lunar Outpost, it is a foreign government customer on top of its American work. The company describes itself as having operated the first commercial rover on the Moon and as leading NASA's Lunar Terrain Vehicle program, and says it has ten missions planned before 2030.

That NASA program is where the company's profile is highest. Last week it reported clearing NASA's preliminary design review for its Pegasus vehicle; see [our earlier coverage](/2026/11/19/lunar-outpost-pegasus-ltv-preliminary-design-review/).
