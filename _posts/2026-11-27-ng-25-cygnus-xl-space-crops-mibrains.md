NASA has detailed the science riding on Northrop Grumman's 25th commercial resupply mission to the International Space Station, NG-25, which is targeted to launch Sunday, 29 November, on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida.

The spacecraft is a Cygnus XL, named for Apollo 13 commander Jim Lovell, carrying about 11,000 pounds of science equipment and supplies, according to TeslaNorth's account of the mission briefing. Public launch schedules list the liftoff for around 15:47 UTC, with a backup the following day; those times are provisional.

## Crops and brain models

Two NASA investigations are highlighted. The first is a prototype growth chamber, described by NASA as a first-of-its-kind space crop production system, which will validate its water delivery and volume optimization technology for growing crops in microgravity. Sources summarising the release name the hardware Ohalo III, but the NASA text relayed by the NCIJ Network does not, so treat the name as secondary reporting.

The second is a precision-health investigation that will test a miniature fluid-handling system meant to keep lab-grown human brain models, known as miBrains, alive. It follows an earlier investigation that sent miBrains to the station.

Both are the sort of work that matters for long-duration exploration: food production and biological research that cannot depend on frequent resupply.

## More Falcon 9 flights for Cygnus

At a NASA briefing on 23 November, Northrop Grumman's Dave Schiller said: "We do have one additional Falcon 9 on the manifest for Cygnus." No date or mission number was given, and Northrop has not said when its own Antares 330 rocket will take over Cygnus launches, TeslaNorth reported.

The mission follows earlier coverage on this site of [Cygnus XL's role in robotic welding work at the station](/2026/11/23/nasa-joins-robotic-welding-iss-cygnus-xl/).
