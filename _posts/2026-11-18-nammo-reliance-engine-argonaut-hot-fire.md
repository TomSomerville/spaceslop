Nammo UK has completed the hot-fire test campaign for the first prototype of Reliance, the main landing engine for the European Space Agency's Argonaut lunar lander. The tests were run at the company's Westcott Venture Park site in Buckinghamshire, England, according to Aerospace Testing International.

Reliance is a 6 kN bipropellant engine burning monomethylhydrazine (MMH) with mixed oxides of nitrogen (MON). In testing it throttled between 50% and 100% thrust while holding combustion efficiency and stability. Nammo says it is the first electrically pump-fed, regeneratively cooled MMH/MON in-space engine to show deep throttling in hot-fire tests. The throttling comes from a pintle injector, electrically driven centrifugal pumps and an integrated controller. Deep throttling matters for a lander because it lets the vehicle adjust thrust smoothly during a precision descent.

The article does not say how many firings were run or for how long, so the scale of the campaign is not public from this report. The programme has also passed its preliminary design review.

## What comes next

Nammo is now preparing an engineering model build and test campaign scheduled for 2027, with further hot-fire work to validate the engine's control logic. The company plans to finish qualification and bring Reliance into service within three years.

Robert Selby, vice president at Nammo Space, called the campaign "a major development milestone for both the program and European lunar exploration." Ludovic Duvet, Argonaut team leader at ESA, said "Reliance is a key technology step for Europe."

## Argonaut context

Thales Alenia Space leads Argonaut, Europe's first lunar lander, which is meant to give the continent independent access to the Moon and to support NASA's Artemis programme. It is designed to carry up to about 1,500 kg of cargo, infrastructure and science payloads to the surface. ESA is targeting a first mission around 2030 on an Ariane 6, with landing accuracy improving from roughly 250 m on the first flight to about 50 m by the third, and further missions every two to three years.

Reliance builds on Nammo's LEROS family, whose LEROS 4 engine flew on Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost landing in March 2025.
