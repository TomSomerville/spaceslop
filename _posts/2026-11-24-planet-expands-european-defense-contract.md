Planet Labs said on November 24 that a European defense and intelligence customer has expanded an agreement first signed in June. The original deal was described as seven-figure and one year long. Planet did not name the customer, the value of the expansion or its term.

Under the expanded contract the customer will use PlanetScope Monitoring, Planet's daily imaging service, together with high-resolution tasking, where satellites are directed to image specific locations. Planet lists the aims as better maritime and land-use awareness and protecting critical infrastructure from emerging threats.

Jon Powers, Planet's vice president of global defense and intelligence, said that "allied defense forces require immediate, high-cadence satellite data integrated directly into their operational systems."

Planet has been announcing a run of European defense wins, and this one fits the pattern: governments there are buying commercial imagery for routine monitoring rather than relying only on national satellites. The wording of the release leaves open how this agreement relates to other European deals the company has disclosed, and secondary coverage was not consistent on the timeline, so treat the June origin as the company's own account.
