China launched Shenzhou-24 at 08:18 Beijing time on Wednesday (00:18 UTC, 7:18 p.m. EST Tuesday) from Launch Site 91 at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, carrying the first foreign national ever to fly on a Chinese crewed mission: Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali.

China in Space reports that the Long March 2F/G reached orbit after about ten minutes and the spacecraft deployed its solar arrays. Docking with the Tianhe core module's forward port was planned roughly three and a half hours after liftoff; neither source we read confirmed the docking at the time of writing.

## The crew

Tang Hongbo commands the mission on his third flight, after Shenzhou-12 in 2021 and Shenzhou-17 in 2024, which SpacePolicyOnline says makes him the first taikonaut to visit Tiangong three times. Wu Yi is a first-time flyer. Ali flies as a payload specialist and will stay about a week, carrying experiments from Pakistani researchers.

China and Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO agreed in February 2025 to fly a Pakistani astronaut. Ali and fellow pilot Khurram Daud were selected in February 2026 and began training in China in April, according to SpacePolicyOnline. Daud did not fly. Beijing announced the actual flight assignment only the day before launch.

## Handover and return

Tang and Wu will take over from the Shenzhou-23 crew, who will greet the newcomers and hand over science duties over about a week. Ali returns to Earth with Shenzhou-23's Zhang Zhiyuan and Li Jiaying. Zhu Yangzhu, the third Shenzhou-23 member, stays aboard; SpacePolicyOnline describes his stay as China's first year-long mission. China in Space gives the Shenzhou-24 crew a planned six-month tour.

By China in Space's tally, this was the 24th Shenzhou flight, the 24th Long March 2F/G launch, the 680th Long March of any type and China's 84th launch of 2026.

## Why it matters

Tiangong has so far been a purely Chinese outpost. Putting a foreigner aboard a Chinese crew vehicle, rather than a Chinese-built experiment on a foreign one, is a statement of how Beijing intends to pitch the station as the ISS approaches retirement. It is also a cheap way for Pakistan to get an astronaut without developing a crew vehicle.
