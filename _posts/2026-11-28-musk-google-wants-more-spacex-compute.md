Elon Musk said on X on November 28 that Google wants to rent more computing capacity from SpaceX. No new agreement, price or timeline has been announced, so for now this is Musk's description of customer interest rather than a signed deal.

His argument, as reported by Crypto Briefing, is that power rather than chips is the main constraint on AI companies. SpaceX builds its own power generation, he said, which lets it bring capacity online faster than rivals that depend on the grid. He named Google and Anthropic as companies leasing compute from SpaceX because of that bottleneck.

## The existing deal

Google already has a large lease. Under an arrangement disclosed on June 5, it pays SpaceX $920 million a month from October 2026 through June 2029 for about 110,000 Nvidia GPUs plus the CPUs, memory and other hardware needed to run them, according to CNBC. That is roughly $30 billion over the term. Google described it as "bridge capacity" for surging demand for its Gemini Enterprise agent platform.

The contract has exit clauses. If SpaceX had missed the GPU delivery commitment by September 30, Google could have walked away or taken fewer GPUs at a reduced fee, and after 2026 either side can terminate on 90 days' notice, CNBC reported.

## Why a space site cares

The capacity sits in data centers built for xAI, which SpaceX merged with in February. Anthropic agreed in May to use all of the compute at the Colossus 1 site in Memphis. The ground-based leases give SpaceX AI revenue now, while its pitch for orbital data centers is still years out. The FCC authorized up to one million "Starmind" satellites this week, and SpaceX has said it expects to begin deploying orbital compute satellites as early as 2028.
