Nara Space Technology, a South Korean small-satellite manufacturer, has been selected as the preferred negotiator to build the K-waterSAT water-resources microsatellite constellation for Korea Water Resources Corporation (K-water), according to a regulatory filing reported by Seoulz.

## What the filing says

The selection was dated October 26. Nara Space's offered bid is 10,112,272,727 won, excluding value-added tax. That equals 76.39 percent of the company's 2025 year-end separate sales of 13.24 billion won, which is why the deal had to be disclosed: for a company of this size, a single contract is worth most of a year's revenue.

K-water is listed as both the demanding and the ordering institution. The disclosed delivery deadline is 1,200 days from the contract date, or a little over three years.

Preferred-negotiator status is not a signed contract. Nara Space said the final signing and the final amount will be settled in further negotiations, and that the disclosure may change as that process goes on. The Seoulz article does not say how many satellites the constellation will include or give details of the payloads beyond the water-resources focus. A related headline on the same page, dated November 25, refers to a contract for four optical microsatellites for water-resource monitoring with launches planned by 2029, but this run could not read that article, so those details are unconfirmed here.

## Why it matters

Korea is pushing hard to grow a domestic commercial space sector, and state-owned bodies buying dedicated Earth-observation satellites for their own missions are one of the more reliable sources of orders for startups. For Nara Space of this size would be a significant addition to its order book.

The next thing to watch is the signed contract and its final value, which Nara Space would be expected to disclose once negotiations conclude.
