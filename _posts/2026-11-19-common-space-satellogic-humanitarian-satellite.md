Common Space, a nonprofit building a community-governed Earth-observation constellation, has selected Satellogic as spacecraft partner for its first high-resolution humanitarian satellite. Satellogic announced the award on November 19 on its own news page.

Satellogic will build a NewSat Mark V satellite and handle the launch, ground segment, mission operations and lifetime support. The target is a launch in July 2027 on a SpaceX rideshare mission. The announcement gives no resolution, orbit or sensor figures beyond "high-resolution," and no contract value.

## The model

Common Space calls its approach a "club-good model": imagery is collected for humanitarian and public-interest needs, openly licensed, and tasked according to community input rather than a commercial customer's priorities. Satellogic says more than 60 organizations sent letters of support, including the World Food Programme, Doctors Without Borders, Global Fishing Watch, Save the Children and Direct Relief.

Common Space is raising philanthropic capital for a six-satellite constellation that would give daily high-resolution coverage of priority areas worldwide by 2030. Private-sector organizations can join through paid membership meant to sustain the infrastructure. No funding total was disclosed. Satellogic says Common Space picked it through a competitive request for proposals that weighed technical fit, community needs, mission alignment, cost and path to orbit.

## Context

The announcement comes from Satellogic itself, so the account of the selection process is the company's. The news is mostly a funding and delivery test: a single satellite is a start, but the six-satellite constellation depends on philanthropic money that has yet to be announced. For Satellogic, it is another small-satellite contract beyond its defense and sovereign customers.
