SpaceX's push into ground-based computing now has an Arizona front. The company has confirmed it is behind a proposed data center and battery storage campus in Cochise County, and Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes has told county officials she wants the process slowed down.

According to KJZZ, the proposal covers about 750 acres and would draw 6,000 megawatts of power at full build-out. The applicant of record is Tombstone Resources LLC, which the station reports will buy another 348 acres from Southline Transmission LLC to complete the site. If approved, it would be SpaceX's first facility in Arizona. SpaceX held a public meeting at Cochise Elementary School in early November.

Arizona's Family reports SpaceX puts the investment at $50 billion and describes the site, near the Willcox Playa, as the largest data center in the state. SpaceX says it would create more than 5,000 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent ones, and its application says it would spend more than $3 billion on power grid upgrades. The company also claims the project would lower neighbors' electricity bills and property taxes. Those are SpaceX's claims; the outlet's article gives no independent figures for power or water use.

## The attorney general steps in

Mayes wrote to the county supervisors saying "more time is needed to adequately study grid and water impacts," KJZZ reports, and the station says she asked them to delay their vote. She told Arizona's Family she has concerns and that her office is taking a closer look, questioning "whether that would destabilize our grid in the southwest and maybe even potentially in the West."

The first formal step is a Cochise County Planning and Zoning hearing scheduled for Dec. 9. Arizona's Family said it had not heard back from the planning department or the three supervisors at publication.

Local opposition is organizing. Tara Clayton of Rural Arizona Engagement, whose group helped defeat a large data center project in Pinal County over the summer, cited water supply, wildlife and chemical use as concerns. Yale energy economist Ken Gillingham said SpaceX "has acquired a reputation for not being the most environmentally oriented of the large hyperscalers" and noted the company has used diesel generators at some facilities.

## Why it matters

The Arizona plan lands alongside SpaceX's roughly $24 billion data center and natural gas project at Starbase in Texas, which has run into its own local pushback over a tax abatement. KJZZ also reports that Mississippi environmental regulators found SpaceX began construction on a data center there without necessary permits; that detail comes from the station and was not independently confirmed here.

For a launch company, a multi-gigawatt terrestrial computing build-out is a big pivot of capital and political attention. Each site brings the kind of permitting, water and grid fights that rocket pads rarely face, and the Arizona hearing is an early test of how receptive rural counties will be.
