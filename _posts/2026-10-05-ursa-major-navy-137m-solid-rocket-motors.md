Ursa Major, the Colorado propulsion company better known to space watchers for liquid engines, has taken a large step into tactical missile propulsion. The U.S. Navy awarded it two contracts worth a combined $137 million to develop and qualify solid rocket motors, according to the company's release dated October 5, 2026.

## Two motors, two missiles

The larger piece is $91 million for the motor of the Compact Air-to-Air Missile (CAAM), a weapon meant for current and next-generation aircraft. Ursa Major says it is designing a compact, low-cost 7-inch motor that uses a Highly Loaded Grain, a propellant-packing approach intended to hit performance targets inside a tight volume. The company says the manufacturing methods used for development are the ones it plans to carry into full production.

The other $46 million goes to the Naval Modular Missile (NMM), a medium-range variant. Here Ursa Major will supply a 10-inch motor designed for high-rate production and for compatibility with existing Navy shipboard launch systems. The funding flows through the Office of the Under Secretary of War.

The company says the work covers developing and qualifying the motors, using its Lynx manufacturing approach, expanding energetics production capacity and applying additive manufacturing.

## Why it matters

Solid rocket motors have been a chokepoint for missile makers for years, and Ursa Major is pitching itself as a way through it. "Production is the bottleneck in missile programs right now, and that's the part we've been building for," said Jason Meredith, president of Solid Missile Systems at Ursa Major.

The award builds on earlier Navy work. In July 2026 the Navy gave the company $10 million to take its MK 104 design, for the Standard Missile family, through critical design review and static-fire testing. In February 2026 Ursa Major completed a $25 million cost-share Solid Rocket Motor Manufacturing Pathfinder Program with the Navy and the Office of Strategic Capital, which included designing, building and static-firing a 10-inch Highly Loaded Grain motor and developing a proprietary propellant. That work involved Naval Air Warfare Center Weapons Division at China Lake and Naval Surface Warfare Center Indian Head Division.

Ursa Major is headquartered in Berthoud, Colorado, with additive manufacturing operations in Youngstown, Ohio. The release gives no delivery schedule or production quantities, so how fast the company can turn development contracts into volume remains the open question. For a firm that built its name on engines for launchers and hypersonic vehicles, defense solids are fast becoming a larger share of the business.
