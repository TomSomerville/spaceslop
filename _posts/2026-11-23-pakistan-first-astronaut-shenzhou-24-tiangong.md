China is preparing to carry the first foreign astronaut to its Tiangong space station, and Pakistan's planning minister is in the country for the send-off.

Arab News Pakistan reports that Ahsan Iqbal arrived in China to attend a departure ceremony set for Monday, Nov. 23, for the Shenzhou-24 mission, and is due to visit the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center to meet the two Pakistani astronaut candidates. Shenzhou-24 will launch on a Long March 2F. A launch rehearsal has been completed and the spacecraft was moved to the launch area on Nov. 17, the report says.

## Who is flying

Pakistan shortlisted two pilots, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud. Arab News says one will fly as a payload specialist and the other will serve as backup. Which one is flying has not been confirmed in the sources reviewed, and Chinese authorities have not announced a launch date. Reports expect about a week aboard Tiangong, running scientific experiments, before the astronaut returns on Shenzhou-23.

The flight follows an agreement signed by the two countries in February 2025 to train Pakistani astronauts.

## Why it matters

Tiangong has so far hosted only Chinese crews. Seating a foreign national is a notable step for China's effort to position its station as an international alternative to the ISS, and it gives Pakistan a place in human spaceflight without building its own capability. The short-duration, payload-specialist format also keeps the logistics modest: no change to the station's crew rotation is described in the reports.
