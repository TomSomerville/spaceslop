NASA has selected Astranis for the initial phase of NEXUS, a commercial Ka-band relay initiative, the company announced on 6 November. The award is firm fixed price and has a maximum potential value of $264.3 million, according to Astranis.

NEXUS stands for Network Extension for User Continuity and Sustainability. Its purpose is to keep existing missions connected as NASA retires its Tracking and Data Relay Satellite (TDRS) fleet in geosynchronous orbit. The aim, per the release, is backward-compatible Ka-band relay service that supports current users for at least 15 years.

## Structure of the deal

The initial phase runs from 4 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, with follow-on phases through 2029. The scope covers space and ground systems, launch and deployment, an on-orbit demonstration, and a later operational service phase. Astranis says the work is run through NASA's Communications Services Project within the Space Communications and Navigation division, under a Nonreimbursable Space Act Agreement, the kind of arrangement NASA uses to partner with industry rather than buy hardware outright.

The "maximum potential value" wording is worth noting: the figure presumably covers all phases, and only the initial phase is now underway. Later phases depend on the early work.

## Why it matters

TDRS has long been the relay backbone that lets spacecraft in low Earth orbit, including the ISS, talk to the ground without a ground station in view. Replacing it with a commercial service is part of NASA's broader move to buy communications as a service rather than own the satellites. For Astranis, which builds small geostationary satellites, it is a government anchor customer for a business that has mostly sold to commercial operators.

John Gedmark, the company's CEO and cofounder, said keeping NASA's most important missions connected takes infrastructure that is "resilient, proven, and built to last," and that NEXUS "puts our production capacity to work for NASA." Astranis says it has raised more than $1.2 billion and reports a commercial contract backlog above $1 billion.

The release doesn't say how many satellites NEXUS will use or when the demonstration will launch.
