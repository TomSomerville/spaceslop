Northwood Space, the ground-station startup, says it has held full-duplex contacts with a government satellite in geosynchronous orbit (GEO) using its own ground antenna. Via Satellite reported the milestone on November 25. The demonstration took place in September.

Full-duplex means one antenna transmits commands to a satellite and receives data from it at the same time, over the same link. Northwood said the link carried telemetry, tracking and control traffic, and it used the company's Portal phased array. The company's own wording was that a single aperture doing simultaneous transmit and receive is hard to pull off. Northwood had previously shown full-duplex communications with a satellite in low Earth orbit.

## The Space Force angle

The work was a prototype contract with Space Systems Command's Program Acquisition Executive for Battle Management Command, Control, Communications and Space Intelligence. The link ran through the Space Force's Joint Antenna Marketplace (JAM), a system through which government operators can tap commercial antenna capacity. Northwood said it was the first time JAM had integrated and used a commercial phased array.

Northwood described the GEO demonstration as its final prototype milestone, reached within a year of the award. According to the company, finishing it lets program offices negotiate production agreements without writing a new contract under the Federal Acquisition Regulation. That shortcut is the practical prize: it lowers the paperwork barrier to buying more Northwood capacity.

## Context

Northwood is building a network of ground stations meant to give satellite operators dependable access without having to overbuild their own infrastructure. The company said JAM is one route to that network but not the only one. We covered its [$375 million Series C](/2026/11/02/northwood-375m-series-c/) earlier this month.

The report does not name the satellite or its operator, the orbital slot, or where the antenna sits, and it gives no performance figures beyond the timeline. The claims here are Northwood's own.
