Open Cosmos has signed up Greek company EMTECH Space to handle a specialist piece of testing on NanoMagSat, the three-satellite European Space Agency mission it is building to map Earth's magnetic field. Open Cosmos announced the partnership on Nov. 23.

EMTECH Space has delivered and installed its EOS Validation Facility at Open Cosmos' manufacturing site in Harwell, in the UK, where it is already in use on NanoMagSat hardware. The facility measures the magnetic fields a spacecraft generates on its own. For a mission whose job is to measure faint natural magnetic signals, the satellite's own electronics are the enemy: stray fields from wiring, motors and power systems can swamp the science. Characterising and controlling that "magnetic cleanliness" before launch is a basic requirement.

"This is a critical step in validating the satellites' performance," said Athanasios Kotsaras, EMTECH Space's chief technology officer for electromagnetics.

## Where the kit came from

EMTECH originally built the facility for Hellenic Space Dawn, a mission under Greece's national small-satellite programme, and now sells it as an off-the-shelf product. That programme is backed by the Greek Ministry of Digital Governance, ESA and the Hellenic Space Center, with funding from the EU's Recovery and Resilience Facility. Open Cosmos has a Greek arm, Open Cosmos Aegean, which grew out of that work; its managing director, Maria Kalama, framed the deal as another way of creating opportunities for Greek partners.

## The mission

NanoMagSat is one of ESA's fast-track Scout Earth-observation science missions, a line meant to fly small, cheaper missions quickly. Its three satellites will study the geomagnetic field and the ionosphere, with applications in space-weather monitoring and navigation. The first launch is planned for 2027, according to Open Cosmos. The release did not give a value for the EMTECH contract.
