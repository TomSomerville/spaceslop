Marble Imaging, a Bremen-based Earth-observation startup, has raised an additional €6 million in a seed extension led by Cherry Ventures. The money goes toward what the company describes as Germany's first commercial constellation of very-high-resolution optical satellites.

The extension follows an initial €5.3 million seed round led by High-Tech Gründerfonds in December 2025, according to Vestbee. That brings Marble's total seed funding to about €11.3 million. Other backers in the first round included BBM, Lightfield Equity, Oslo Venture Company, Sentris Capital and SpaceFounders.

## The hardware

Marble's first spacecraft, Marble Sat-1, is fully funded and has completed pre-flight testing, Tech.eu reports. It is designed to capture imagery at up to 0.7 metre resolution in visible and near-infrared bands, and to collect shortwave infrared data as well. Launch is scheduled for early 2027; Vestbee puts it at February 2027.

The longer-term goal is a constellation of 30 satellites by 2030. The company is also building an AI-based analytics layer on top of the imagery, aimed at defence, civil security and critical infrastructure customers, plus uses such as disaster management and climate security. Neither source names specific customers.

## Why it matters

Europe's push for sovereign space capabilities is the pitch here. Cherry Ventures founding partner Filip Dames said in the announcement: "Europe needs its own eyes in space, and Marble Imaging is building them." CEO Robert Hook, who founded the company in August 2023 with Alexander Epp, Dr Gopika Suresh and Dr Raul Scarlat, framed the product around fast, usable data: "To make better decisions, data access must be immediate and actionable."

Sub-metre optical imagery is a crowded market, with established commercial operators in the US and Europe. A seed-stage company going from one satellite to thirty will need substantially more capital than it has raised so far, and the 2030 target should be read as an ambition rather than a funded plan. For now the near-term test is simpler: getting Marble Sat-1 to orbit and producing imagery at the advertised resolution.
