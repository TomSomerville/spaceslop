JAXA, France's CNES and Germany's DLR are working toward postponing the first flight of the CALLISTO reusable rocket demonstrator to early Japanese fiscal year 2028, according to a report on JAXA's briefing to a Japanese education ministry subcommittee on 26 November. The report says final coordination with each country's organisations is still under way, so the date is not yet locked.

The flight had been planned for the end of FY2026 through FY2027. JAXA said the team found it had to confirm that some primary-structure subsystems carry enough design margin for the conditions of reuse, including descending engine-first. The review led to extra design work, analysis and testing across the vehicle and its interfaces, and affected material procurement and manufacturing.

## What CALLISTO is

The vehicle is about 13.5 metres long and 1.1 metres across, with a fully fuelled mass of around 4.2 tonnes. Flights from the Guiana Space Centre are expected to reach roughly 15 km and about Mach 1, with an in-flight relight of the liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen engine, deceleration and a vertical landing. JAXA supplies the propulsion system, the liquid oxygen tank and the aft fuselage; DLR provides the hydrogen tank, nose section and landing gear; CNES handles ground facilities.

The flight liquid oxygen tank has been delivered to the Tsukuba Space Center and the engine turbopump has been tested on its own, the report says. This is not the first slip: the start date moved from FY2024 to FY2025 and then FY2026 in earlier revisions, which added thermal protection and structural reinforcement.

## Context

CALLISTO's smaller predecessor, RV-X, flew at the Noshiro Rocket Testing Center on 11 July 2026, rising 10.6 metres in a flight of 39.7 seconds. JAXA found no reusability problem and has ended that series, with the same engine type to be used on CALLISTO. The agency says it intends to use the programme to assess how reuse could cut launch costs and raise cadence, and to support commercial development through joint research and technology transfer.
