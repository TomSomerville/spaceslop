South Korea's space agency has finished a dry run of how the stages of its Nuri rocket will travel to the launch site, a logistics change that takes effect with the sixth flight planned for next year.

The Korea AeroSpace Administration (KASA) and the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI) ran the check from November 24 to 26 together with Hanwha Aerospace, Nuri's system integrator, according to Korean reports published on November 26. Instead of real hardware, the team used mock-ups of the first, second and third stages.

## What changes

For Nuri's first five launches, stages were assembled and then integrated at the Naro Space Center in Goheung. From the sixth launch, each stage will be built and assembled at Hanwha's new plant in Suncheon, then shipped to Naro for final integration and launch. Hanwha recently completed that plant; [our earlier coverage]({% post_url 2026-11-23-hanwha-aerospace-suncheon-launch-vehicle-plant %}) has the details.

## The rehearsal

The mock-ups were mounted on dedicated transport fixtures and moved by self-propelled modular transporters, the low platform vehicles used for oversize loads, between the Suncheon facility and Yulchon port. They were then loaded on a barge for a sea leg from Yulchon toward the waters off Dolsan Island, about 80 km round trip. The real stages will make a sea passage of roughly 121 km from Yulchon to Naro.

Hanwha led the land and sea movement, while KASA and KARI observed. KARI advised on securing the stages, vehicle choice and routing, drawing on its experience moving the first stage of the earlier Naro rocket, which arrived from Russia.

## What officials said

KASA Administrator Oh Tae-seok said the agency would manage the whole transport process closely so that preparations for next year's sixth launch stay on track. KARI President Lee Sang-cheol said moving a launch vehicle safely depends on technology built up over years of development and operation, and pledged close cooperation with Hanwha. Both quotes are translated from Korean.

## Why it matters

Shifting assembly to an industrial site is part of Korea's move from a state-run build to a private, industry-led production model. The trade-off is a new step in the chain: a rocket stage now has to survive a road and sea journey before it ever reaches the pad. The reports give no launch date for the sixth flight beyond "next year", and the agencies said the results of the check will be applied to moving the actual stages and to settling the new procedure.
