Europe's next Ariane 6 will carry a weather satellite. Arianespace has set December 17, 2026 for mission VA272, which will launch Metop-SGB1 from the Guiana Space Centre in French Guiana. Liftoff is planned for 21:36 local time, which is 00:36 UTC on December 18, according to the launch announcement published by ArianeGroup.

## The mission

Metop-SGB1 is the second satellite of the EUMETSAT Polar System Second Generation. Airbus Defence and Space built it under contract with the European Space Agency, and EUMETSAT will operate it. It carries five instruments that complement those flown on Metop-SGA1, which Ariane 6 launched in August 2025. Together the two spacecraft are meant to deliver observations of winds, precipitation, soil moisture, ice clouds and sea ice extent, supporting forecasting, extreme-weather prediction and climate monitoring.

Ariane 6 will fly in its two-booster Ariane 62 configuration. The flight from liftoff to satellite separation is planned to last about one hour and five minutes, ending in a sun-synchronous polar orbit at roughly 800 km.

## By the numbers

VA272 will be the 11th Ariane 6 flight and the seventh in the Ariane 62 configuration. It will be the fourth Ariane 6 mission to sun-synchronous orbit. For Arianespace it is the 364th launch and the sixth of 2026.

The announcement did not state the satellite's mass.

## Why it matters

The December flight is a quieter assignment than the recent Ariane 64 launches of Amazon Leo satellites, which have dominated the rocket's 2026 manifest. Metop-SGB1 is an institutional European payload, the kind of mission Ariane 6 was built to guarantee Europe independent access to space for. Weather satellites in polar orbit feed numerical forecast models, so schedule slips matter to meteorological agencies more than the launch calendar alone suggests.

The date is the provider's plan. Earlier this week an Ariane 6 flight was scrubbed for a ground fault before flying, so December 17 remains subject to the usual launch-campaign risks.
