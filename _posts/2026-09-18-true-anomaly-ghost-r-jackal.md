True Anomaly has been selected by the Defense Innovation Unit, which True Anomaly's release styles as part of the Department of War, to deliver its Jackal autonomous orbital vehicle for GHOST-R, short for Geosynchronous High-Resolution Optical Space-Based Tactical Reconnaissance. The company announced the selection on 18 September.

## A step toward RG-XX

According to True Anomaly, GHOST-R is a stepping stone to RG-XX, the U.S. Space Force's program of record for a persistent, maneuverable, proliferated constellation in geosynchronous orbit (GEO) for space domain awareness, meaning keeping track of what other satellites are doing up there. The company argues that GEO is becoming contested and that today's architecture leans on a small number of exquisite, expensive satellites. A proliferated layer of lower-cost platforms, it says, would remove single points of failure and raise operational tempo.

On GHOST-R, True Anomaly will operate Jackal initially together with Space Force mission operators. After that, the vehicle becomes a government-owned, government-operated asset.

## The hardware

True Anomaly describes Jackal as a single vehicle meant to watch dozens of satellites, investigate anomalies on friendly spacecraft, and track high-value targets across multi-year missions. The GEO version, per the release, has more payload capacity, radiation-tolerant parts, more redundancy, an alternative positioning, navigation and timing source, and secure communications. It also carries high-thrust, high-delta-V propulsion with six-degree-of-freedom control and the ability to burn in place, plus a rendezvous and proximity operations sensor suite for detecting, tracking and imaging other objects.

The company's Mosaic command-and-control software, which it describes as multi-vehicle and multi-domain, will support mission planning, analysis and operator training on GHOST-R, and will inform C2 software for a full constellation.

CEO and co-founder Even Rogers said Jackal and Mosaic were "purpose-built for this mission, delivering superior maneuverability, sensing, and autonomy" at a price that allows scaling.

The post gives no contract value, number of vehicles or launch date, so the size of the award is unknown. Jackal's earlier outing on the Space Force's VICTUS HAZE mission is covered separately on the company's newsroom.
