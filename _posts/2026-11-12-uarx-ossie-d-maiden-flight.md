UARX Space, based in Nigrán, Spain, has flown the first OSSIE-D, an orbital transfer vehicle (OTV) it says was designed and built in Spain, with about 75% of its components developed in-house. It launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 and was released using UARX's own separation system, SAU&RON 24. The company calls it a world first for one firm to fly both an OTV and the separation system that deploys it.

This is a press release republished by SpaceNews, so the performance claims are unverified, and it gives few mission details beyond deployment and power operations.

Supplier DHV Technology designed, qualified and built the vehicle's full electrical power subsystem: solar arrays, a power conditioning and distribution unit, main battery modules and a solar array drive assembly. Initial telemetry reportedly shows power generation, distribution and storage working as expected. DHV is also using the flight to test new interconnection methods, solar panel deployment and hinge mechanisms and advanced photovoltaic technology.

UARX CTO and co-founder Andrés Villa called it "a milestone for the Spanish aerospace industry". DHV managing director Vicente Díaz said seeing the subsystem "operating as expected in orbit is an important milestone for our team."

OTVs, small vehicles that move satellites between orbits after a rideshare drop-off, are a crowded niche. A Spanish entrant matters mostly as a sign of national capability building, and the next test is whether it can deliver customer payloads.
