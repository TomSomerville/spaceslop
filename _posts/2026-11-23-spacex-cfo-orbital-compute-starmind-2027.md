SpaceX chief financial officer Bret Johnsen gave the clearest public schedule yet for the company's plan to put AI computing in orbit, speaking at the TomorrowX Summit on 17 November.

According to a Stocktwits report on the talk, the first dedicated AI data-center satellites, which SpaceX calls Starmind, are due to begin operating in 2027. The constellation would grow in 2028, with gigawatt-scale capacity expected in 2029. Johnsen said orbital computing should undercut terrestrial alternatives on cost once reflight of the Starship second stage becomes routine. He called the recent recovery of a Starship second stage a critical milestone for that case.

## Funding the buildout

Johnsen also addressed how SpaceX intends to pay for the broader compute expansion. He said a large portion "will be just funded by the business itself", helped by tighter handling of customer deposits and supplier payments, stronger operating cash flow and existing debt facilities. He said payback on current deals is often under a year, so the company only has to bridge a gap of a few months.

Bloomberg reported separately that SpaceX could sell around $4 billion of bonds this week, part of a $40 billion financing plan for its AI computing business announced in October to buy Nvidia Vera Rubin chips. That figure comes from Stocktwits' summary of the Bloomberg report; SpaceX has not been quoted confirming the bond sale.

## Why it matters

The orbital-compute pitch rests on launch cost, and launch cost rests on Starship. Reusing the upper stage is the step that has eluded every orbital rocket so far, which is why Johnsen tied the economics to it. If reflight stays hard, the cost advantage he describes does not materialize, and the ground-based buildout he is funding in parallel carries the business.
