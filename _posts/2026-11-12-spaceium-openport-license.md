Spaceium, a startup working on in-orbit refueling, is opening its OpenPort technology to anyone who wants to build it. Payload reports that any company can license and build the hardware for $1. CEO and cofounder Ashi Dissanayake told the outlet Spaceium is "not here to sell a refueling interface and make money." The plan is to earn revenue selling fuel to spacecraft.

OpenPort is androgynous, meaning fuel can flow either way through the same interface, so one port can act as both pump and fill point. That matters for Spaceium's planned network of depots, which could refuel themselves and then top up other spacecraft. The port is fuel-agnostic. It also replaces the fill port used on the ground before launch, so it adds no new hardware to a spacecraft. CTO and cofounder Reza Fetanat said the design responded to customer requests.

A competitor already has a similar offer: Orbit Fab made its RAFTI port available under an open license in October 2021, according to Payload.

Spaceium has some flight heritage. A mission on SpaceX's Transporter-15 rideshare this month tested a robotic arm the company built to help service its depots. It plans to send OpenPort to the International Space Station in the first half of 2027 to demonstrate xenon transfer to a spacecraft docked outside the station.

The pitch is familiar but still unproven at scale: a satellite is normally finished when its propellant runs out, even if everything else works, and refueling could extend lives and ease the rationing of maneuvers. Whichever interface operators standardize on will shape that market.
