ESA has signed European Launcher Challenge contracts with three of the four companies it picked, and the fourth, MaiaSpace, is still waiting. According to European Spaceflight, the reason is the way MaiaSpace asked to be funded, not the size of its award.

The challenge has two funding components. Component A subsidises launch service contracts, and Component B co-funds development and demonstration of upgraded launch vehicles. At ESA's 2025 Ministerial Council in Bremen, member states committed €902.16 million in total. MaiaSpace's share was €183.4 million, all of it under Component A. PLD Space, Isar Aerospace and Rocket Factory Augsburg took mostly Component B.

On 27 August ESA announced contracts worth €543.6 million combined with those three: €197.8 million for Isar, €186.9 million for RFA and €158.9 million for PLD Space. It said the MaiaSpace process was "nearing completion and expected to resume in the coming weeks."

ESA's Director of Space Transportation, Géraldine Naja, said at a 22 October media briefing that MaiaSpace was the only challenger to seek support through launch service contracts, which made negotiations "a little bit more sophisticated". She called signing a "question of days". Three weeks after that briefing, European Spaceflight reports, ESA had not announced a signing.

The delay is a small one, but the challenge is Europe's main attempt to build a competitive, commercially minded launch sector, and one of four slots is still unfilled. MaiaSpace is a subsidiary of ArianeGroup.
