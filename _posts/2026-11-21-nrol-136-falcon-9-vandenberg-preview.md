SpaceX is preparing to fly NROL-136, a batch of satellites for the National Reconnaissance Office, from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. According to Spaceflight Now, the launch window opens at 4:16 a.m. Pacific on Sunday, November 22 (7:16 a.m. Eastern, 1216 UTC), from Space Launch Complex 4E.

## The mission

NROL-136 is the fourth launch this year for the NRO's "proliferated architecture," a constellation of intelligence-gathering satellites in low Earth orbit, and the 15th overall in the program, Spaceflight Now reports. The number of satellites aboard has not been disclosed, which is typical for NRO missions.

The proliferated architecture is built around many small, relatively inexpensive satellites rather than a few large, exquisite ones. The idea is that a larger fleet is harder to disable and can revisit targets more often. Launch cadence is what makes it work, and that suits a rocket that flies as often as Falcon 9.

## The booster

The first stage is B1097, flying for the 14th time. After stage separation it is slated to return to Landing Zone 4, a pad next to the launch site, about seven and a half minutes after liftoff. Spaceflight Now notes that a successful touchdown would be the 38th landing at LZ-4 and the 680th Falcon booster landing overall.

## Context

The launch follows closely behind USSF-317, a separate classified Space Force mission that flew from Vandenberg late on November 20 Pacific time, which we covered [here](/). Two national-security launches from the same California range within about a day is a reminder of how routine this work has become for SpaceX's West Coast pad.

The number of satellites on this flight has not been disclosed, and the launch date and time could still move.
