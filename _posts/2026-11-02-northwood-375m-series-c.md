Northwood Space announced a $375 million Series C on November 2, valuing the El Segundo, California ground-segment company at $2 billion post-money. Andreessen Horowitz and DFJ Growth co-led the round, according to SatNews' report on the announcement. Northwood's own news page did not list the release when SpaceSlop checked, so this account relies on that trade-press coverage.

## What Northwood sells

Northwood's pitch is that satellite operators should not have to build their own dishes. It sells capacity on a shared network of ground stations using flat-panel phased-array antennas, with cloud scheduling and automated data routing. SatNews says the service covers satellites in low, medium and geostationary orbit. Co-founder and CEO Bridgit Mendler leads the company.

The new money is meant to scale antenna manufacturing, raise output of its Portal phased-array terminals, expand the shared network, and buy sites to extend coverage internationally. SatNews did not give a current or planned station count, and Northwood did not disclose how revenue splits between government and commercial customers. The customers are described as commercial constellation operators and defense users.

## A fast climb

The round follows a $100 million Series B about nine months earlier, along with a $49.8 million U.S. Space Force Space Systems Command contract to modernize the Satellite Control Network, the government's existing set of tracking antennas. In effect, the valuation has risen quickly as investors bet that ground access, rather than launch or satellite hardware, is becoming a bottleneck as constellations multiply.

The competitive context is the established ground-station-as-a-service market, where operators rent time on antennas run by others. Northwood is differentiating on electronically steered panels that can be manufactured in volume rather than large steerable dishes that take months to install.

What to watch is deployment: how many sites come online, and how quickly the manufacturing spend translates into capacity for customers.
