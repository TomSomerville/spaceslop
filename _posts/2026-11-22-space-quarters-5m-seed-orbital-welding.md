Space Quarters, a Tokyo startup spun out of Tohoku University in 2022, has closed a $5 million seed round to develop robots that weld large structures together in orbit and on the Moon. Global Brain led the round through its Flagship Fund IX, according to Dealroom's report dated November 13.

Other backers include the Tokyu Construction-GB Innovation Fund, the ANA Future Frontier Fund and the Canon Marketing Japan MIRAI Fund, along with additional Japanese investors.

## What it is building

The company pairs electron-beam welding with robotic arms. The pitch is that anything launched today has to be fully assembled on the ground and then squeezed into a fairing, which caps the size of habitats, antennas and other structures. Welding parts together after launch would sidestep that limit.

Space Quarters says it has made progress on the ground. In March 2026 it demonstrated stable welding of both metal and lunar regolith simulant under simulated microgravity aboard an aircraft. In June its DAIQ robotic system assembled and welded components in a ground test.

The new money is earmarked for hiring engineers, upgrading test facilities and preparing on-orbit and lunar demonstration missions planned for 2027 to 2028.

## Caveats

In-space construction remains early-stage, and none of this has been proven in orbit. Aircraft and ground tests are a long way from commercial deployment, and the source does not name the founders or include comments from the company. Dealroom notes a related effort in Europe: ArcSpace raised a €2 million seed round in August 2026 for an electron-beam welding demonstration planned for 2027.

The company joins a growing field of startups betting that in-space assembly and manufacturing will matter as station modules, large antennas and lunar infrastructure outgrow what a single rocket can carry. Whether welding in vacuum beats bolted or self-assembling designs will depend on the 2027-2028 demonstrations.
