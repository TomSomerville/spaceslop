Europe's next Amazon Leo launch is off for Monday. Arianespace halted the countdown for mission VA271, which Amazon calls LE-04, on Nov. 23 after what the company described as a technical event affecting the launcher's ground support systems. The next opportunity is no earlier than Tuesday, Nov. 24, and no new launch time has been set.

According to Arianespace's update, the sequence was stopped before the mobile gantry was rolled back and before launcher system operations began. In other words, the problem surfaced early in the terminal flow, while the Ariane 64 was still sheltered inside its service structure. The company said the rocket and its 36 Amazon Leo satellites are in a safe and stable condition, and that technical teams are assessing what went wrong. France-Guyane reported the same and noted that no further detail on the cause had been given.

Neither Arianespace nor local press has identified the faulty ground component, and nothing in the material available so far points to a problem on the vehicle itself.

## Why the slip matters

This would be the tenth Ariane 6 flight and the fourth for Amazon Leo, flown in the heavy Ariane 64 configuration with four solid boosters. If it succeeds, Arianespace's running total of Amazon Leo satellites in orbit would reach 136, France-Guyane reported. The flight sits inside a 24-launch agreement between the two companies, to which Amazon added six more Ariane 64 missions in September 2026.

The timing is awkward for Amazon. Eight days ago a ULA Vulcan carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites failed in flight, and SpaceSlop's [preview of this mission](/2026/11/21/ariane-64-amazon-leo-le-04-monday/) noted how much of the constellation's near-term deployment now leans on Europe's heavy launcher. A one-day ground scrub is routine by launch-industry standards, but with Vulcan grounded, each Ariane 6 slip pushes Amazon's buildout a little further right.

## What's next

Arianespace said it will publish further updates once the assessment is done. Launch listings currently carry a provisional Nov. 24 attempt at 11:09 UTC, the same time of day as Monday's window, though Arianespace itself has not confirmed a time. Whether Tuesday holds depends on how quickly the ground-system issue can be traced and cleared.
