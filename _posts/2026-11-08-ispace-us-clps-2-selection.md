ispace technologies U.S., the Colorado-based American arm of Japan's ispace, has been selected for NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services 2 (CLPS 2) contract. The company announced the selection, dated 7 November, in a release published on 8 November.

## What it means

CLPS 2 is an indefinite delivery, indefinite quantity (IDIQ) contract, the successor to the CLPS 1 vehicle under which NASA has bought robotic lunar deliveries since 2018. It runs for a 10-year base ordering period plus a five-year option, with a not-to-exceed ceiling of $10 billion. That figure is a cap, not money committed: being on the contract does not guarantee a mission, and ispace-U.S. will have to win each task order in competition.

What changes is its status. According to ispace, the selection makes the company eligible to compete for NASA task orders as a prime contractor, offering end-to-end payload transport and lunar surface operations with its ULTRA lander and future designs. Under the first CLPS contract the company worked on NASA lunar deliveries as a subcontractor.

## The company's pitch

ispace-U.S. is headquartered in Denver, where it handles spacecraft development, assembly, integration and test, and mission operations. It says it has invested more than $200 million in lunar mission development and supporting capabilities, and that ULTRA draws on lander development, testing and flight experience from ispace's Japan operations, which have flown two lunar missions.

Elizabeth Kryst, CEO of ispace-U.S., said CLPS 2 "gives us the opportunity to compete in leading those missions for NASA's lunar science and Moon Base objectives." Bretton Alexander, the firm's executive vice president of business development, said ULTRA offers NASA "reliable and affordable access to any location on the lunar surface for science and technology demonstration missions."

The release frames the selection around NASA's Moon Base Program, and includes a photo of Kryst with Carlos García-Galán, the program's manager. The practical test comes with the first CLPS 2 task orders, where ispace-U.S. will be up against the other companies on the contract.
