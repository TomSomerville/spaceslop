Rocket Lab's Electron flew its 101st mission on Friday, lifting off from Launch Complex 1 on New Zealand's Mahia Peninsula at 1:15 p.m. local time (00:15 UTC) and delivering a StriX radar satellite for Japan's Synspective to a 569 km low Earth orbit. Rocket Lab calls the mission a success.

The flight, named "Night Owls Never Sleep," was the company's 22nd launch of 2026. It carried a single synthetic aperture radar satellite, the 14th StriX to fly. SAR spacecraft image the ground through cloud and in darkness, which is the reason Synspective builds its constellation around them. We [previewed the launch on Thursday](/2026/11/26/electron-night-owls-never-sleep-strix-14/) when the window opened.

## A single-customer cadence

Rocket Lab says it has been the sole launch provider for every StriX satellite so far, with a 100% mission success rate for Synspective. The relationship has grown into the largest on Electron's books: Synspective signed a 20-launch deal at the end of September, which Rocket Lab describes as the largest commercial contract in Electron's history. Synspective now has 47 missions booked in total, with 33 still to fly through 2031.

That backlog is a big part of why Electron's cadence looks the way it does. A reliable stream of one-satellite-per-launch missions for a single repeat customer gives a small-lift rocket predictable revenue and a high flight rate, while each launch carries a full vehicle's worth of cost for one payload.

## Caveats on the paperwork

Rocket Lab's own release carries an inconsistency: its body text dates the launch "27 September 2026," while the dateline and every other account place it on Nov. 27. We have treated November as correct. The release also does not say whether the launch count of 22 sets a record for Electron in a calendar year; one aggregator describes it that way, but we could not confirm it from Rocket Lab's text. Rocket Lab's page was not directly reachable for this report, so details come from a syndicated copy of the release and coverage by MLQ.ai and Stuff.

The release did not name the next Electron mission.
