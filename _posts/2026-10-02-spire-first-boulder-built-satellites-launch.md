Spire Global says the first three satellites built at its Boulder, Colorado manufacturing facility are in orbit. The company announced on October 2, 2026 that the spacecraft launched on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission.

## A U.S. production line

The Boulder site handles development, manufacturing, integration and testing in one vertically integrated facility on U.S. soil. Spire says that supports supply-chain resilience and security for U.S. space assets, and it is aiming the site at U.S. national security and defense customers. The company has long built satellites in the U.K. and Europe; combined with that production, it cites total capacity of 300 to 400 satellites per year.

The release describes the launch as a milestone rather than giving details of the three spacecraft themselves, such as their mission, sensors or customers, and it does not say how many satellites Boulder is expected to produce in a given year.

"The successful deployment of the first satellites built at our Boulder facility marks an important milestone," CEO Theresa Condor said in the release.

## Why it matters

Defense buyers increasingly want space hardware made domestically, and a satellite operator with a U.S. production line can bid for work that foreign-built spacecraft cannot easily win. Spire sells weather and maritime data and builds satellites for others, and a domestic factory broadens what it can offer to government customers.

Transporter-18 also carried Starfish Space's first full-scale Otter, a mission [SpaceSlop covered at the time](/2026/10/01/starfish-otter-launch-nasa-sspicy/). SpaceX's rideshare flights have become the default route to orbit for smallsat makers, which is part of why companies like Spire can plan production around a regular launch cadence.

Spire's recent news flow also includes a run of NOAA and EUMETSAT weather-data contracts listed on its press page, but this article relies on the October 2 release alone. What remains to be seen is whether the Boulder line becomes a significant revenue source or stays a capability that Spire points to when courting defense customers.
