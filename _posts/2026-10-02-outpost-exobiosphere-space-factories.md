Outpost Technologies has found its first announced customer for Space Factories, the orbital manufacturing service it introduced in late September. Space biotech company Exobiosphere will fly its Orbital High-Throughput System, an automated laboratory for disease research, drug screening and sample return, as the first customer module on the platform, Outpost said on October 2, 2026.

## How Space Factories is meant to work

Space Factories is a reusable platform for making and researching things in orbit and bringing them back. Outpost describes shared infrastructure with standard power, thermal, data and safety interfaces, so that only the innermost manufacturing module has to be customized for each customer. The idea is to spare each customer from building a whole spacecraft around a small experiment.

Exobiosphere's module, known as OHTS, will ride on Outpost's CarryAll spacecraft. Outpost says CarryAll can return between 200 kilograms and 10 metric tons from orbit with precision landing, in a pressurized environment compatible with the International Space Station.

"Their OHTS is a perfect first module," said Jason Dunn, Outpost's co-founder and CEO. Exobiosphere, led by co-founder and CEO Kyle Acierno, describes its mission as finding "Cures from Space." Outpost president Amir Blachman is also quoted in the release.

## Timeline and context

The release gives a target of 2028 for Outpost's next orbital mission, so this is a booking for a flight that is still some way off. Outpost also points to the ISS's scheduled decommissioning in 2030 as a driver for commercial return logistics: once the station goes, the ability to bring samples and products back from orbit has to come from private vehicles.

That puts Outpost in a small but growing field of companies trying to make return-from-orbit a routine service. Varda Space Industries has flown capsules back to Earth for pharmaceutical and materials work, and SpaceSlop has covered its recent landings. Outpost's pitch differs in offering a shared, modular platform and a larger pressurized return vehicle rather than a dedicated capsule per customer.

The release does not give a price, contract value or launch provider. Exobiosphere is the first customer Outpost has named, not necessarily the first it has signed, and details of the OHTS module's mass and the mission profile are still to come.
