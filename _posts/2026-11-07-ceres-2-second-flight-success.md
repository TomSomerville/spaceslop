Galactic Energy's Ceres-2 reached orbit on its second attempt, lifting off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China at 7:37 a.m. on Saturday 7 November (Beijing time) and placing four satellites into a 535-kilometre sun-synchronous orbit. The flight, designated Ceres-2 Y3, followed a January debut that failed.

## The rocket and the recovery

Ceres-2 is a solid-propellant vehicle with three solid-fuel core stages and a liquid-fuelled upper stage, weighing about 100 tonnes. China Daily's report, which draws on a company news release, gives its capacity as up to 1.6 tonnes to a 500-kilometre low Earth orbit or 1.3 tonnes to a 500-kilometre sun-synchronous orbit.

The first Ceres-2 mission in January was meant to carry six commercial satellites and two experimental payloads. It failed, with the report attributing that to "technical malfunctions" without elaborating. Galactic Energy has not detailed the cause in the material reviewed here, so this article cannot say what was fixed.

## Payloads

The four satellites will collect meteorological data and test new technologies. The report does not name them or their operators.

## Company context

Galactic Energy is a Beijing-based private rocket maker. It operates three vehicles: the small Ceres-1, Ceres-2 and Pallas-1. According to the report, it has completed 27 missions and launched 94 commercial satellites in total.

The comparison to peers is instructive. Ceres-2 sits in a growing group of Chinese commercial medium-class rockets, and a clean second flight matters because a launcher with one failure on record needs to show repeatability before customers will book it. A single success does not establish reliability, and the company has not announced a next flight in the sources read for this article. The report is secondary, based on the company's release and a Xinhua photo, and the date reflects Beijing time; in UTC the launch was late on 6 November.
