CAS Space has sent its second sea-launch Kinetica-1 rocket out of its Guangzhou factory, according to an announcement dated November 18, reported by China in Space. The move comes before the first sea-launch version of the solid-fuel rocket has actually flown.

The rocket is bound for the Haiyang Oriental Spaceport in Shandong province. There it will be checked out with two customer satellites and enclosed in a 2.65-meter fairing, then loaded onto the launch vessel *Dongfang Hangtiangang*. The plan is to fire it from the Yellow Sea off Shandong into sun-synchronous orbit. No launch date has been announced.

## The first one is still waiting

The inaugural sea-launch Kinetica-1 reached the spaceport in August and completed several days of rehearsals aboard the vessel that month. It is meant to carry three satellites to sun-synchronous orbit from waters off Jiangmen, in Guangdong. Targets of September and then October both slipped.

China in Space reports that the delays reportedly trace to a planned Jielong-3 mission, which may have had payload or hardware problems. The same ship has also been reconfigured repeatedly since August for Jielong-3 and Kinetica-1 rehearsals, a Gravity-1 launch in September and a Ceres-1S launch in October, sailing much of the Chinese coast in the process. Hazard notices now suggest preparations for the first sea launch have resumed, with a target near the end of November. If that flight succeeds, CAS Space could move quickly to load the second vehicle.

## Why bother with the sea

Kinetica-1 is hardly unproven: it has flown successfully 16 times, per the report. CAS Space is pursuing sea launches to reach orbits that are either impossible from land or costly to reach, accepting reduced payload capacity to the target orbit as the trade-off.

The episode also shows how thin China's sea-launch infrastructure is. A single vessel is serving several rocket families from different companies, and its schedule has been the bottleneck as much as any rocket. Shipping a second vehicle early suggests CAS Space expects the first to go soon, but until it does, the second is a statement of intent rather than a launch.

Separately, the upcoming launch list for early December includes a land-based Kinetica 1 with an unspecified payload. That is a different mission from the sea-launch vehicles described here.
