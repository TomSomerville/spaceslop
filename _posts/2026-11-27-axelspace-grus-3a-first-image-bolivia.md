Japanese Earth-observation company Axelspace has released a picture taken by its GRUS-3A microsatellite, the first public image from the new satellite, while saying the spacecraft is still being verified ahead of commercial operations.

According to Axelspace's news post, dated November 27, the image was captured on November 18 (Japan time) over Caracollo in Bolivia. The company describes it as test data, used to check and tune the satellite's functions and performance rather than as a product image. The post does not give the image's resolution.

## Still in initial operations

GRUS-3A is currently in what Axelspace calls initial operations. In this phase the company is working through the whole chain from image planning to ground processing and quality assessment. The stated goal is to begin commercial operations in February 2027.

The more interesting detail is a caveat. Axelspace says there has been a high-speed communication anomaly on other GRUS-3 satellites, and as a precaution it is limiting the electrical current going to the power amplifiers on GRUS-3A, GRUS-3C and GRUS-3D. The post does not describe the anomaly's cause, which satellites are affected beyond that, or what the current limit does to downlink performance. A restricted power amplifier would be expected to constrain how quickly data can be sent to the ground, but Axelspace does not say so, and that remains an inference.

## Why it matters

Axelspace sells imagery and constellation services from small optical satellites, and the GRUS-3 generation is the next step in building out that fleet. A February start for commercial service leaves roughly three months to resolve the communications issue or to show customers that the throttled amplifiers are good enough. Earth-observation startups live on revisit rate and data volume, so any limit on downlink capacity matters to the business case.

The company has not announced a fix, and nothing in the post suggests the satellites are otherwise unhealthy.
