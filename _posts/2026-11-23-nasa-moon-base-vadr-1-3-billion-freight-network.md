NASA's Moon Base program is buying transportation before it knows exactly what will ride on it. Six task orders worth about $1.3 billion combined, placed under the agency's 10-year Venture-Class Acquisition of Dedicated and Rideshare (VADR) contract, cover launches and orbital transfer vehicles (OTVs) that NASA wants on standby for lunar south pole work. NASA announced the awards on Nov. 7; Spaceflight Now published a detailed breakdown of the six missions on Nov. 23.

Moon Base manager Carlos García-Galán described the approach as "flipping the formula": buy the rockets and transport first, then match them to payloads and landers while NASA keeps oversight. Transportation manager Mark Wiese called for an "intergalactic freight network" to move the mass needed on the surface, and said NASA is working with the Commercial Access to Space program to avoid paying for unused capacity.

## The six missions

The awards went to Arrow Science and Technology, Blue Origin and Impulse Space, with launches on Blue Origin's New Glenn, Relativity Space's Terran R and SpaceX's Falcon 9. According to Spaceflight Now, the orders break down as follows:

- **CAPSTONE 02**, a $185 million award: two satellites on a Falcon 9 with Quantum Space's Ranger OTV, launching in 2027 to 2028 to test cislunar navigation and communications.
- **Communications satellites for Intuitive Machines**: a Falcon 9 mission led by Impulse Space, possibly using its Mira OTV, carrying several Altus satellites on one ride.
- **MoonFall drones**: a New Glenn mission using Firefly Aerospace's Elytra OTV to deliver three JPL hopper drones near the Moon's surface in 2028.
- **Two Blue Ring missions**: both on New Glenn, one a communications demonstration, with details still to be set.
- **A Terran R demonstration**: Impulse Space leads, using its Helios kick stage and a lunar lander to test heavier cargo delivery. NASA called it "high risk, high reward" and has not picked a payload.

## Why it matters

The risk is concentrated in hardware that has yet to prove itself. Spaceflight Now notes that none of the OTVs involved (Blue Ring, Elytra, Mira, Helios, Ranger) has flown in space, and Terran R has not launched, with its debut expected in 2027. New Glenn is central to three of the six orders, which makes its flight cadence a schedule dependency for Moon Base.

The orders sit alongside the rest of NASA's Nov. 7 package: eight companies selected for the CLPS 2 lander contract, a Blue Origin contract to deliver the PROMISE rover, and a separate task order for SpaceX to use Starship to deliver communications satellites to cislunar space in 2028. Phase One of Moon Base runs through 2029 and is focused on technology demonstrations.
