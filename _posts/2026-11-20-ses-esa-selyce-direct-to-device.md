SES and the European Space Agency signed a partnership on November 17 to develop European direct-to-device (D2D) satellite technology under a project called SELYCE. The signing took place at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, Germany, with ESA's Geraldine Naja and SES Chief Product and Innovation Officer Xavier Bertran putting pen to paper, according to New Space Economy.

D2D means connecting ordinary consumer devices straight to satellites, the market Starlink and others are chasing in the US. SELYCE is Europe's attempt to build its own capability rather than depend on outside systems.

## Scope and backing

SES says the work covers development, integration and validation of building blocks for standards-based 5G non-terrestrial networks, assembled and tested as an end-to-end system for future commercial services. Initial applications named are vehicle connectivity, messaging and emergency voice outside mobile coverage, and communications during disasters.

The approach is phased, using a high-fidelity test environment to prepare technologies for later orbital demonstrations and then commercial deployment. No dates beyond the signing were given.

The Luxembourg Space Agency is the principal public funder, with the Belgian Science Policy Office and the UK Space Agency also participating. No funding amounts were disclosed. SES leads the industrial effort alongside Amphinicy Luxembourg, the University of Luxembourg and Redu Space Services, SES's wholly owned Belgian affiliate, and it expects more European participants to join.

## Why it matters

The announcement is a development-stage commitment, not a constellation: nothing here puts hardware in orbit yet. But it signals that ESA's commercialisation directorate sees D2D as a strategic area, and it gives SES, which has a separate D2D tie-up with Elveo Mobile, a publicly backed testbed to work from.
