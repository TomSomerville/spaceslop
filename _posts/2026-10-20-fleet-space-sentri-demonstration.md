Fleet Space Technologies has led a consortium through an end-to-end demonstration of SENTRI, a project that connects widely distributed sensors directly to Australian satellites and turns their detections into decision-ready information for remote or disconnected areas.

The demonstration took place on 23 September at Fleet's Adelaide headquarters. Sensor nodes detected and analysed a simulated hazard, and the result travelled over a live satellite link to the Defence Science and Technology Group's cloud-based OCHRE platform. The architecture combines commercial sensors, intelligence at the edge, an Australian-developed waveform, Fleet's satellites and OCHRE.

Fleet's co-founder and chief exploration officer Matt Pearson said the team had "shown that small, low-cost sensors can report straight to an Australian satellite", and that the sensor can change with the mission while the satellite link, the intelligence and the operating picture stay the same. Use cases named include emergency management, biosecurity, critical infrastructure monitoring and defence.

The project is backed by AU$1.6 million from the South Australian Government through the Defence Innovation Partnership. Partners are Safety From Space, the Australasian Space Innovation Institute, Insight Via Artificial Intelligence, Swordfish Computing, Adelaide University and DSTG.

Fleet notes that Centauri-9, built and tested at its Adelaide facility, launched in July 2026. The consortium is now looking for partners for an operationally representative field trial. The release does not say when that trial might happen.
