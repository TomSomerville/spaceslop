Southern Launch has signed ElevationSpace, a Japanese company building services to return advanced materials and other payloads from orbit, to bring its AOBA-2 capsule back at the Koonibba Test Range in South Australia. The agreement was signed in Tokyo, and the company says it is its first with a Japanese re-entry customer.

AOBA-2 was developed with Tohoku University and JAXA. Southern Launch says it will re-enter the atmosphere at more than Mach 25 before descending under parachute into the Australian outback. Southern Launch will lead delivery: Australian approvals and return authorisations, tracking and communications, weather monitoring, range operations and capsule recovery.

ElevationSpace's first satellite, AOBA-1, is being recovered at sea in Japan, so Koonibba gives the company an overland alternative. ElevationSpace's Ryohei Kobayashi said that securing an overland site in Australia lets it offer customers multiple recovery options. Southern Launch CEO Lloyd Damp said launching technology into orbit is "only part of the opportunity space provides."

The announcement gives no launch or return date for AOBA-2.

The signing fits a pattern at Koonibba, which has been handling returns of Varda Space Industries capsules. SpaceSlop has covered the most recent one, W-9, in a separate article. Austrade's Anthony Weymouth also welcomed the growing commercial relationship between Australia's space sector and Japanese partners.
