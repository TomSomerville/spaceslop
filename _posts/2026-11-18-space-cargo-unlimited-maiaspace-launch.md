Space Cargo Unlimited, the European in-space manufacturing company, has purchased launch capacity from MaiaSpace for its first commercial mission. The two companies announced the launch service agreement on 18 November in Paris.

Neither side has published a launch date, a price or details of the orbit, so the announcement works as a statement of intent rather than a schedule. MaiaSpace says the deal lays "the foundation for a long-term partnership" aimed at continuous orbital access for an in-space manufacturing fleet.

## What Maia offers

MaiaSpace describes Maia as Europe's first partially reusable launch vehicle. It has a 3.5-meter fairing and can lift up to 4 tons to low Earth orbit, depending on the version, the inclination and whether the optional Colibri kick stage is used. Launches would fly from the Guiana Space Center.

Space Cargo Unlimited's offer centers on BentoBox, a platform meant to support customer research and production in orbit, working with a vehicle partner. In the announcement the company's co-founder and CEO, Nicolas Gaume, said customers "need more than a route to orbit." MaiaSpace CEO Yohann Leroy said his company was "thrilled to welcome Space Cargo Unlimited as a new customer."

## Why it matters

In-space manufacturing companies depend on cheap, frequent rides to orbit, and Europe has few options of its own for small and mid-sized payloads. A commissioned launch gives MaiaSpace another named customer ahead of its debut, and gives Space Cargo Unlimited a European launch option for a fleet it hopes to fly repeatedly.

The usual caveats apply. The release does not say which Maia version would carry the mission or how the payload would return to Earth if it is meant to come back, and the rocket itself has yet to reach orbit. Until a date is attached, the agreement shows commercial interest more than a firm manifest slot.
