The Ariane 6 launch of 24 November left more behind in French Guiana than a successful payload deployment. Local and French outlets report that the liftoff set off a vegetation fire on the Ariane launch zone of the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, which burned roughly five hectares before it was brought under control.

According to Péyi Guyane, the rocket lifted off at 8:14 local time on Tuesday, and the fire followed the launch. Drought had left the surrounding vegetation dry, and wind pushed the flames across the ground. Firefighters said on X that the blaze threatened a tank of propane and liquid hydrogen, a detail repeated by Presse-citron. Crews from the Kourou fire station, part of the Paris Fire Brigade (BSPP), which provides fire safety at the spaceport, put it out. No injuries were reported.

The mission itself was not affected. Ariane 6, on its tenth flight, placed 36 Amazon Leo satellites in orbit, as covered in our [launch report](/2026/11/24/ariane-64-lifts-off-amazon-leo-le-04/).

## An expected side effect

Presse-citron reports that the firefighters describe this kind of fire as anticipated: engine heat and flame at liftoff can ignite grass in the savanna around the pad, and the risk is treated as managed. That framing matches the incident's outcome, but a fire that comes near cryogenic and flammable storage is the sort of event a launch site is built to avoid, and the dry conditions made it more likely to spread.

I found no statement from Arianespace or CNES about the fire in the sources I read, so the account here rests on the firefighters' public posts as relayed by the two outlets. The five-hectare figure is the one both outlets give.

With Ariane 6 now one of only two vehicles flying Amazon Leo hardware while others are grounded, any launch-site disruption matters beyond the local fire. Nothing reported so far suggests this one will affect the schedule; the next Ariane 6 flight, Metop-SG B1, is planned for 17 December.
