Firefly Aerospace has signed QASCOM as a commercial customer for its Elytra orbital vehicle, giving the Italian navigation specialist a six-month stay in lunar orbit starting with Blue Ghost Mission 3. Firefly announced the deal on Nov. 17.

QASCOM's payload is called COLLINS, short for Cislunar Orbiting L-band and LunaNet Integrated Navigation System. It will ride on Elytra Dark, the Elytra variant that stays in orbit while Firefly's Blue Ghost lander goes down to the surface. COLLINS is meant to show high-accuracy navigation using GNSS signals (the same family of signals that GPS belongs to) and future LunaNet positioning, navigation and timing services, including ESA's Moonlight navigation services. It will run for six months, with an option to extend.

## A commercial deal, not a CLPS one

The notable part is the contracting. QASCOM's earlier lunar payload, LuGRE, flew on Blue Ghost Mission 1 as a joint Italian Space Agency (ASI) and NASA instrument under a NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) contract. It was the first payload to track GNSS signals on the Moon's surface. COLLINS is different: Firefly says it is a commercial contract with the company directly, part of a public-private co-investment between ASI and QASCOM.

For Firefly, that is the point of Elytra. The company has been pitching the vehicle as more than a taxi that drops a lander and leaves. On Mission 3, Elytra will first deploy Blue Ghost into lunar orbit. The lander then goes to the Gruithuisen Domes for roughly 14 days of surface operations, while Elytra relays communications and runs COLLINS. Afterward the vehicle remains in lunar orbit for five years to support Firefly's own Ocula lunar imaging service.

## Timing

Mission 3 is targeted for no earlier than 2028 and will carry eight payloads in total, six of them through NASA's CLPS initiative. That puts the first revenue from this customer a long way off, and the date is a target rather than a commitment.

"Elytra's dual spacecraft setup puts us in a unique position to enable a wider scope of repeatable, reliable operations" on and around the Moon, Firefly CEO Jason Kim said. QASCOM CEO Oscar Pozzobon called the deployment "a strategic step in validating ASI's vision for next-generation lunar PNT services."

Firefly's release names no other outside Elytra customers. A lunar navigation demonstration is a modest first test of whether commercial hosted payloads in lunar orbit can add up to a business.
