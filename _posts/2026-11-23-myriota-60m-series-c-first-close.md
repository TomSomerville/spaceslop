Myriota, the Adelaide satellite-IoT company, has secured $60 million as the first close of a Series C round, Startup Daily reported on Nov. 23. The National Reconstruction Fund Corporation (NRFC) and Main Sequence Ventures co-led, with superannuation fund Hostplus participating and South Australian investment manager Funds SA joining the cap table. The NRFC's own cheque was not disclosed. The remainder of the round is expected to close in early 2027, and the raise took about 100 days, according to the report.

The new money pushes Myriota's total funding past $160 million. The company was spun out of the University of South Australia in 2015 by Alex Grant and David Haley. Earlier rounds include a $15 million Series A in 2018, which drew Boeing's HorizonX, and a $28 million Series B in 2020. The NRFC had also put $25 million into a $50 million raise two years ago.

## What Myriota sells

Myriota builds low-power transmitters that send small packets of data to its satellites, a niche suited to things that sit in the field for years and have no mains power. It started with farmers monitoring remote water tanks and now tracks industrial fuel tanks in North America and Latin America, with Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia Pacific named as next markets. Recent products include HyperPulse, a 5G service that blends satellite and terrestrial connectivity, and AssetHawk, a long-life tracker for equipment such as trailers.

On the space side, Myriota launched 16 satellites this year, with more launches booked for 2027 and 2028. The company plans a larger constellation to add capacity and redundancy. CEO Ben Cade said the company "is building the infrastructure to connect the physical world at unprecedented scale."

## Context

Myriota was also part of the group of South Australian space companies behind the state-backed Kanyini satellite, built at Lot Fourteen in Adelaide. The round is another sign that government-backed capital is increasingly filling the gap for Australian space hardware companies, a trend the NRFC's repeat participation illustrates. Financial terms such as valuation were not reported.
