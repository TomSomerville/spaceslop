NASA on Wednesday announced the AMPERE Challenge, a prize competition for US companies to build prototype mid-power Hall effect thrusters, a type of electric propulsion that uses electric fields to accelerate ionized propellant. It is the first contest in the agency's Tech Maturation Prize series.

The prize pool is $500,000, split between two winning teams at up to $250,000 each. The money is only part of the offer. Teams that advance get hands-on work with NASA staff and the chance to take no-cost licenses to NASA-developed Hall thruster technology that runs on krypton, and NASA will test the finished prototypes at its Glenn Research Center at no cost to the teams.

## How it works

The challenge runs in three phases. In Phase 1, teams submit a concept design, a manufacturing plan, and projections for cost and performance. NASA may select two teams to move forward. In Phase 2, those teams build prototypes. In Phase 3, NASA tests them at Glenn.

Registration closes January 27, 2027, at 5 p.m. EST. NASA is holding a virtual industry day on Thursday, November 19. Full rules and eligibility requirements are on the challenge website, ampere.nasatechmaturation.org. NASA's announcement says only that the challenge is for US companies.

Glenn and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory manage the project, which is funded by NASA's RTMD. Yet2 administers the contest under a NASA contract.

## Why it matters

NASA's release does not name a specific mission or customer for the hardware. It frames the aim as speeding development for future NASA and commercial missions.

AMPERE project co-owner Jason Frieman of Glenn said the approach is meant to "remove key barriers and facilitate commercialization." He said NASA hopes participants will "demonstrate their capabilities to NASA and other government and commercial stakeholders."

The krypton licensing is a notable detail. Handing out NASA's design work for free lowers the cost of entry for companies that lack their own test infrastructure. NASA does not say how many companies it expects to enter. Two winners from a prize this size means most of the value for entrants will be the testing access and licenses, not the cash.
