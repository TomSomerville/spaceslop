A Long March 8A rocket lifted off from Commercial Launch Pad 1 at the Wenchang Commercial Space Launch Site in Hainan on 20 November at 11:59 UTC (19:59 local time), carrying the 30th group of satellites for China's Guowang constellation. According to China in Space, the launch went ahead in rain.

The payload was nine satellites built by the Innovation Academy for Microsatellites of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Each uses a small-satellite platform of roughly 695 kilograms with electric propulsion and two solar panels. Guowang is operated by China Satellite Network Group, a state-owned enterprise, and is Beijing's answer to Starlink and Amazon Leo.

## Cadence is the story

The flight was the 13th Long March 8A overall, and the variant has now flown five times in five months, according to China in Space. It was the 10th Long March 8 series mission of 2026 and China's 83rd launch of the year. The same report says the rocket now sits on the pad for only about two and a half days before liftoff, helped by streamlined pre-flight processes at Hainan International Commercial Aerospace Launch Co, the site operator. The stated goal is to fly the Long March 8 family 30 times a year.

That kind of turnaround matters because Guowang's size is limited by launch supply as much as by satellite production. The constellation is planned in the thousands of satellites, and every group flown on a medium-lift rocket such as the 8A adds capacity without tying up the larger Long March vehicles.

## How big is it now?

Counts differ. The main text of the China in Space report puts 249 Guowang satellites in orbit, either heading to or already in their operational orbits. A footnote cites the manufacturer as saying 420 are in space. The two figures conflict and the article does not reconcile them, so treat either with caution until an official tally appears.

China in Space also notes that domestic usage trials and agreements are under way ahead of a planned general rollout inside China. No service start date was given in the material we read.

CASC, the state contractor behind the Long March family, announced the launch on its own newsroom as a successful low-orbit internet satellite launch, in line with the details above.
