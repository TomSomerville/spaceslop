GomSpace, the Danish small satellite and subsystem maker, announced on November 19 a contract worth €8 million (SEK 89.1 million) for the second phase of a lunar mission. The customer is described only as "a Leading North American Space Company".

According to GomSpace, the mission involves two satellites that will orbit the Moon to prospect for resources in support of future lunar exploration. This phase moves the work toward full operational capability in lunar orbit and runs through end-of-mission operations around the Moon. Delivery is planned for late 2027. Upcoming milestones listed are a Critical Design Review, payload integration in the United States, and launch activities in the United States.

## Follow-on to a January design award

The deal builds on a first-phase contract the company announced on January 9, 2026, worth €2.9 million. That earlier award, signed by GomSpace's Luxembourg unit, covered the initial design of two spacecraft, with design work scheduled for the first half of 2026. The first release said that finishing the design stage was expected to lead to hardware manufacturing and integration milestones; the new contract is that follow-through, at nearly three times the value.

GomSpace says it will lean on its experience with ESA's Hera mission, including the Juventas CubeSat, to build high-reliability systems for deep-space work. Edgar Milic, the company's VP Advanced Mission, said in the announcement that the contract "marks an important milestone for GomSpace." The company did not say who the customer is, which launch vehicle is planned, or when the satellites would fly.

## Why it matters

For a company that earlier this month set a target of SEK 1.6 billion in revenue by 2030, a multi-million-euro deep-space order is the kind of work it wants more of: higher-value missions beyond its roots in standardized cubesat buses. It also fits the broader pattern of small spacecraft makers picking up lunar-orbit work as commercial and government programs push toward the Moon, with the actual customers often kept confidential until later.

Because the customer is anonymous, the claim that the mission is aimed at resource prospecting rests on GomSpace's own description and could not be checked against another party. The January announcement the company links to described the mission in terms of precise lunar orbit insertion and station-keeping rather than resource work.
