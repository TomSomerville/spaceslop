Sagittarius Logistics, a Colorado startup founded last year, has closed a $1.2 million pre-seed round to start work on a reusable launch system designed to fly from inland sites. Payload lists the investors as Nova Threshold, Felicis Scout Fund, Ingenuity VC and Space Venture Studios.

The team comes from Masten Space Systems, which went bankrupt in 2022 after overrunning its budget on a lunar lander. Founder David Masten leads the company with CEO Jonathan Slavik, COO Jenna Edwards and chief of production Daniel Sinkel.

The thesis is that launch demand will keep outpacing supply. Slavik argues that controlling its own inland sites lets Sagittarius book launches faster and avoid the fight for Cape slots. The vehicles are designed to abort mid-flight without endangering populated areas, with both stages able to return to land. That, he says, could support overland flight under stricter safety requirements, using methods the company says are protected by IP.

The roadmap is long and expensive:

- Yellowstone, a suborbital pathfinder, targets mid-2027.
- Sequoia, the first orbital vehicle, would lift four tons to low Earth orbit in the early 2030s.
- Denali, a heavy-lift vehicle, would carry 30 tons.

The company is buying a tested and hot-fired engine from an undisclosed supplier. Tanks, structures and guidance and control remain to be built. Slavik estimates more than $1 billion will be needed across the three vehicles and says the company will raise only enough for each next milestone. A $1.2 million round is a start, and the gap to that figure is the story to watch.
