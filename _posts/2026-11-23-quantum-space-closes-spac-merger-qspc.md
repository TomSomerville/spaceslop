Quantum Space, the Maryland company building maneuverable spacecraft for cislunar and defense work, closed its merger with the special purpose acquisition company Inflection Point Acquisition Corp. VI on November 23. The combined business is Quantum Space, Inc., and its Class A-1 common stock is expected to start trading on the NYSE on November 24 under the ticker QSPC. Warrants are expected to list on NYSE American as QSPC WS.

Inflection Point shareholders approved the deal at an extraordinary general meeting on November 20. The SPAC's Nasdaq units, shares and warrants were halted on November 23 and are expected to be voluntarily delisted.

## The money

The company says the transaction brought roughly $300 million in new capital through two private placements: a pre-funded PIPE of about $60 million in Series B convertible preferred units and warrants, which included Inflection Point Fund, and a closing PIPE of about $240 million in Series A Preferred Stock and Series A Warrants. The deal implies a pro forma equity value of about $958 million. Quantum Space says the proceeds will go toward accelerating production of its Ranger and Scout spacecraft.

## What it sells

Quantum pairs sustained maneuvering with on-orbit refueling, aimed at customers who need satellites to move rather than sit in predictable orbits. It lists agreements with the U.S. Space Force, the Department of War and NASA, and CEO Jim Bridenstine, a former NASA administrator, pointed to the company's selection to support NASA's Moon Base program. "Predictable orbits are no longer enough," Bridenstine said in the announcement.

Executive Chairman Kam Ghaffarian framed maneuverability as central to national security and U.S. leadership in space.

## Caveats

Ranger, the company's flagship spacecraft, is still in development. The release's own forward-looking statements say it has not yet been manufactured, operated or sold. The listing gives Quantum public-market money to change that, but it also puts a pre-revenue hardware program under quarterly scrutiny, a test several recent space listings have had to pass.
