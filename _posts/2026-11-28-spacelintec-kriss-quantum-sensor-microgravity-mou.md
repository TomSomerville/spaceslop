Space Lintech, a South Korean company that sells access to space experiment platforms, has signed a memorandum of understanding with the Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science (KRISS) to test quantum sensors in microgravity. Newsis reported the agreement on 27 November.

The pact pairs Space Lintech's space demonstration platform with KRISS's quantum measurement expertise. Space Lintech will provide facilities and technology for space-environment experiments, and advise on experiment design and platform operation. The two sides will jointly use the platform and demonstration infrastructure. The first named project is checking how atom-based quantum sensors behave in microgravity.

Quantum sensors exploit atomic-scale properties to measure things like gravity, acceleration and time with very high precision. Newsis notes that conditions in orbit differ from those on the ground, so testing there is considered essential to judge whether such sensors are practical for space use. If they work, they could feed into satellite navigation and precise positioning.

Choi Jae-hyuk, director of KRISS's Quantum Technology Institute, said that verifying atom-based quantum sensors in microgravity would be a starting point for securing core technologies the space industry needs. Space Lintech CEO Yoon Hak-soon said the company would pursue space science experiments, including quantum sensor verification, and grow the related business ecosystem.

The article gives no flight date, vehicle, budget or contract value, so it is unclear when any hardware would fly. This is a cooperation agreement rather than a funded mission.
