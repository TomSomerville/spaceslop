A Long March 2D rocket lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 7:41 p.m. Beijing time on November 11, placing four Pathfinder satellites, A through D, into a sun-synchronous orbit at about 600 kilometres. China Daily describes them as technology demonstration satellites for the Tanyuan project. Global Times reported that the payload entered its preset orbit successfully.

## What the satellites do

The satellites are meant to verify key technologies for future astronomy programmes, including the Hongmeng and Kuafu 2 missions, and are designed to operate for at least two years. Pathfinder A is the primary spacecraft and carries several scientific instruments. Pathfinders B, C and D form a technical test system for a space-based distributed low-frequency interferometric imaging spectrometer, a formation-flying concept in which separate spacecraft act together as one instrument.

## Launch tally

The flight was the 677th mission of the Long March family and China's 83rd space mission of 2026, according to China Daily. Global Times gave the same Long March count. Launch databases list the mission as CAS Pathfinder-1 from the SLS-2 pad; the news reports did not name the pad.

The launch itself is routine for the Long March 2D, one of China's most-flown medium-lift vehicles. The mission is notable mainly as a pathfinder for China's distributed-formation science missions; the science results are outside this site's beat, but formation-flying demonstrations at this scale also feed commercial and military constellation work.
