Rocket Lab launched its 100th Electron on 14 November, a mission it named "The Mountain God Rises". The rocket left Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand that evening and placed YAMATSUMI-II, a synthetic aperture radar imaging satellite for Japan's iQPS, into a 575-kilometer low Earth orbit.

Electron first flew in 2017. Rocket Lab says it is the fastest commercially developed rocket to reach 100 launches. That claim is the company's own, and it measures time from debut to the hundredth flight, not total launch count. Founder and CEO Peter Beck put it this way: "Few rockets reach 100 launches in their total operational lifetime." He added: "The next 100 Electrons are already booked so we're only accelerating from here."

## A loyal customer

This was iQPS's 11th Electron launch, and the company has had a perfect record on Electron since 2023, per Rocket Lab. iQPS has booked seven more dedicated launches through 2030 to complete its radar constellation, which makes it a model small-launch customer: repeat business, a standard bus and a steady cadence.

## The bigger picture

Electron's job in Rocket Lab's story is increasingly to fund and prove out the company's next vehicle. Rocket Lab calls Electron the operational base for Neutron, its medium-lift rocket that is still in development. Electron launches from New Zealand and from Virginia, and the company says it has deployed hundreds of satellites for commercial constellation operators, national security agencies and scientific institutions. Its press release says Rocket Lab's satellites and components have supported more than 1,700 missions overall.

Milestones like this are good for the brand, but the commercial news is the manifest: if Beck's claim holds, Electron has two more years of backlog than it has history.
