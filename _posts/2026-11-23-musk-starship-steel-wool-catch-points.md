Elon Musk says the ablative material that rubbed off Starship's hull during its first tower catch will be replaced by stainless steel wool, in what would be a fix aimed at making ship catches nearly maintenance-free.

Starship returned to Starbase, Texas, on 14 November, about 22.5 hours after launch, and was caught by the Mechazilla arms, as [covered at the time](/2026/11/14/starship-ship-catch-flight-15/). Footage of the catch showed ablative coating being scraped from the ship's side where it met the arms. Responding on 16 November, Musk wrote on X: "That will be replaced with stainless steel wool for future flights," according to Teslanorth, which did not report which flight would first carry the change.

A separate post, relayed by TIKR, has Musk saying woven stainless steel matting "probably solves for zero refurbishment" at the catch points. In that account, the ship, unlike the booster, has to keep its primary structure below its annealing temperature, the point at which steel loses strength, so something must protect the contact areas. We could only confirm that claim through secondary accounts of Musk's post, and the matting design details are not independently documented.

## Why it matters

Reuse is the whole point of catching the ship. Musk has said Ship 42 could probably fly again within weeks of refurbishment, but that airliner-style reuse means zero expected refurbishment apart from scheduled maintenance. Every contact point that needs patching between flights works against that. SpaceX has not set a date for a second flight of Ship 42, and it has never reflown a ship.

The booster side shows a different trade-off: Super Heavy diverted offshore on Flight 15 rather than attempting its own catch. As with most Musk statements on timelines, the design change is a stated intention, not a demonstrated fix. It needs to survive a real catch cycle on a reflown ship.
