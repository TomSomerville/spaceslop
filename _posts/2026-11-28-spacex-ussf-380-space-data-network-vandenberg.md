SpaceX is preparing to launch USSF-380 from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, the seventh Space Force mission in a series carrying an "R" designation in public filings. Liftoff is planned from Space Launch Complex 4 East during a four-hour window that opens at 8:39 p.m. PST on Saturday, Nov. 28, which is 0439 UTC on Nov. 29. The Falcon 9 will fly a southeasterly trajectory after leaving the pad, according to Spaceflight Now.

## What it carries

The payload is a batch of satellites for the Space Force's Space Data Network backbone. The service describes the system as a government-owned and controlled communication constellation meant to move data securely and at high speed around the globe. SpaceX holds a $2.29 billion contract to deliver the satellites to low Earth orbit, and the stated goal is global capability by the end of 2027.

The mission is the latest in a steady run of national security launches from California this month. Recent examples on the site include [USSF-266](/2026/11/24/ussf-266-falcon-9-launches-vandenberg/) and USSF-317. The pattern of Space Force launches from Vandenberg has been unusually dense, and the "R" series appears to be one thread of it.

## The booster

The first stage is B1082, flying for the 27th time. Its earlier missions include Transporter-18, NROL-145, USSF-62 and OneWeb Launch 20, plus 22 Starlink batches. About 8.5 minutes after liftoff it is scheduled to land on the droneship *Of Course I Still Love You* in the Pacific. Spaceflight Now counts a successful landing as the droneship's 237th and the 682nd Falcon booster landing overall.

As with other recent Space Force missions, the launch notice gives limited detail on the satellites themselves. Launch Library separately lists the mission as classified and speculates about Starshield-based LEO satellites, but that is not confirmed by the Space Force, so treat it as an inference.
