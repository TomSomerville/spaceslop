BlackSky Technology has added a new international customer: an unnamed major ministry of defense that signed a seven-figure, one-year Assured subscription. The company announced the deal on November 10, 2026, without disclosing the exact value or the country.

## What the customer gets

Assured is BlackSky's committed-capacity service. It provides priority tasking of the company's Gen-3 very high-resolution imagery, plus AI-enabled analytics, over the customer's critical areas of interest. The company says the tasking is prioritized so that other users cannot preempt it, and that the customer can task the constellation throughout the day to follow developing events instead of relying on a single snapshot or archive imagery.

Data flows into the customer's operations through BlackSky's Spectra platform, which offers web access to 35-centimeter Gen-3 imagery and tasking for daytime, nighttime, stereo and burst collections. It also includes built-in AI detection for vehicles, aircraft and vessels.

BlackSky stressed the speed of the sale. By its account the customer went from initial meetings to an Assured subscription in under nine months, and less than 12 hours after signing, BlackSky had completed in-person training and the customer had begun full operations.

## Context

"In a dynamic world, the value of intelligence is measured in minutes," said CEO Brian O'Toole, who added that the customer chose Assured for dedicated capacity and priority tasking, which gives certainty about intelligence when decisions must be made.

The company sells imagery in three ways, according to the release: On-Demand access, Assured subscriptions and full sovereign systems, in which a customer gets dedicated satellite capability. Assured sits in the middle, and a one-year term with a new ministry is the kind of entry deal that can lead to a larger commitment. The release does not suggest that is planned, and no follow-on terms are given.

BlackSky reported its third-quarter 2026 results on November 5, five days before the announcement; this article does not draw on those results. The company says it owns its full value chain, from in-house satellite design and manufacturing to constellation operations and AI software, which is the argument it makes for fast onboarding of foreign government customers.

With the customer and country unnamed, the deal is useful mainly as a data point on international appetite for assured access to commercial imagery.
