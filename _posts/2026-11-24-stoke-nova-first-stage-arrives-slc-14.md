Stoke Space's first Nova booster is at the Cape. According to local outlet Talk of Titusville, the company posted on November 22 that "Stage 1 has arrived at SLC-14," the Cape Canaveral pad it has rebuilt for the rocket. The stage shipped from Washington state in early November.

## Hardware status

The first stage will be powered by seven Zenith engines burning liquefied natural gas and liquid oxygen in a full-flow staged-combustion cycle, for roughly 700,000 pounds of combined thrust. Stoke announced on November 19 that Zenith development is complete, and the report says flight engines are passing acceptance testing and shipping to Florida weekly.

The second stage is still in Washington state's test cycle. It left Stoke's Kent factory on October 14 for Moses Lake, where it is going through cryogenic proof testing and a hot-fire campaign. Stoke has not published results from those tests.

## The pad

SLC-14 is where John Glenn launched on Friendship 7 in 1962; the complex was deactivated in 1967. The Space Force allocated it to Stoke in March 2023, and the company broke ground in October 2024. The rebuilt site includes a horizontal integration facility about 700 feet away, a launch mount rated for 1.2 million pounds of thrust, a flame trench about 30 feet deep, a roughly 128-foot umbilical tower and new propellant farms. About 50 Stoke employees are based at the Cape.

Stoke is already looking at a second pad. On November 6, Space Launch Delta 45 granted it a right of entry to study the former Launch Complex 15 site to the north, covering studies only. The report also says the Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital signed a $150 million conditional loan commitment, about two weeks earlier, for a second pad supporting the larger Nova Block 2.

## Schedule

Stoke says Nova Pathfinder will make its first orbital flight "in the first part of 2027," with no firm date. The flight was previously expected in late 2026, and the report notes some industry reporting points to mid-2027. The first flight will likely not try to recover either stage; recovery and reuse are planned for 2027 and 2028 Pathfinder flights. Announced payloads include AstroForge's Autonomy-1 spacecraft and a Celestis memorial payload.

Nova is designed to be fully reusable, which, if it works, would make it the first fully and rapidly reusable medium-lift rocket. Customer momentum is building: Planet Labs signed a multi-launch agreement on November 9 and SEOPS added Nova Block 2 flights to its Waymaker rideshare program on November 17, as covered in our [earlier reporting](/2026/11/22/seops-stoke-nova-rideshare-deal/). Stoke has raised about $2.3 billion, including a $1 billion Series E in September. It can compete for Space Force launch task orders only after a successful first flight.

This account rests on a single outlet's roundup of Stoke's own posts; we have not independently confirmed the arrival with Stoke's feed.
