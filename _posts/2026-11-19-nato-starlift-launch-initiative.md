NATO announced on 17 November, at its EDGE 26 conference in Izmir, Turkey, that STARLIFT, a multinational initiative to help allies secure launches for military satellites, has moved into implementation.

Ten allies (Belgium, Canada, Finland, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden, Turkey and the United Kingdom) and South Korea have committed to fund the effort through the NATO Support and Procurement Agency. The commitment was signed on 29 October, on the margins of a meeting of the alliance's National Armaments Directors.

## What it does

STARLIFT is meant to secure slots on commercial rockets for military payloads such as communications, navigation, intelligence and early-warning satellites. NATO says it will build on a network of spaceports across the alliance and in South Korea, pool demand and coordinate requirements across members, and act as a forum for developing and testing "responsive space" concepts, including urgent launches. It will also work on managing air and maritime traffic so launches can happen on short notice.

Breaking Defense reports that the spaceports cited include Norway's Andøya and the UK's Spaceport Cornwall, and that South Korea is reportedly studying a second launch site. Neither NATO nor Breaking Defense names specific launch providers, and program costs and individual national contributions were not disclosed.

## Who is, and isn't, in

The initiative began in October 2024 as one of five NATO multinational cooperation efforts. Breaking Defense notes that France, Hungary, Luxembourg and the United States were among the original participants but were not named in the funding announcement. A NATO official told the outlet the US is taking part as an associated member, contributing expertise and lessons learned, and that more allies and partners are expected to join.

## Why it matters

European launch capacity is thin and heavily booked, and several allies have been looking at commercial options, including small launchers, for sovereign access to orbit. Pooling demand gives launch companies a more predictable customer, which is the kind of signal the industry has asked for. Whether STARLIFT ends up buying flights from European startups, American providers or both is the open question, since no providers have been named.
