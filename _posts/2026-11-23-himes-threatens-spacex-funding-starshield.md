Rep. Jim Himes, the Connecticut Democrat expected to chair the House Intelligence Committee when the new Congress convenes in January, has warned that he may hold up funding for SpaceX-developed government systems until the company and the administration explain what happened to Starshield service in Ukraine. CNN reported the threat on Friday, based on a classified letter to Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton.

This escalates the oversight pressure we covered [earlier this week](/2026/11/20/ukraine-starshield-contract-congress-scrutiny/) when lawmakers first pressed for answers on the October disruption and Ukraine opened talks on a successor Starshield contract.

## What Himes wants

Starshield is the military variant of Starlink, which Ukraine uses for battlefield communications and drone operations. According to the reports, restrictions in October limited the speed at which Ukrainian drones and other equipment could keep their satellite links, disrupting strike missions and interceptor drones. Ukrainian military reports described lost communication when terminal speeds passed roughly 160 km/h. President Zelensky said on November 12 that the limit had returned along the front line and over occupied territory.

In the letter, Himes wrote that his committee "cannot authorize funds for the development of government systems" that the government does not fully control. In a separate letter to Elon Musk, he asked for a meeting and wanted to know what changed in October, why, and whether SpaceX acted on its own or at the direction of U.S. officials. He also wrote that it is "difficult for me to continue supporting" funding for these systems until he gets "satisfactory answers."

The mechanism he floated is the annual Intelligence Authorization Act, where the committee could add language delaying money for certain SpaceX projects, and he could also work with appropriators. Himes said investigators must determine whether SpaceX broke a contract or used authority without adequately notifying officials.

## Who decided?

Accounts conflict. SpaceX has attributed the disruptions to technical issues and denied deliberately restricting terminals, according to Militarnyi's summary. President Trump has repeatedly denied ordering the restrictions, and the Pentagon said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had no advance knowledge. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said access decisions are governed by SpaceX's contracts. CNN, citing a source, reported that SpaceX executives and defense officials have privately told some lawmakers the October decision came from the White House; Reuters reported the same in October, citing officials.

Musk has said Starshield is owned and controlled by the U.S. government, and separately cited alleged terms-of-service violations by Ukrainian forces for restrictions on commercial Starlink. Ukrainian officials disputed that. Ukraine's top diplomat in Washington, Denys Sienik, said Kyiv has contacted the Pentagon but has had no response from SpaceX.

## What's next

Senators Jeanne Shaheen and Chris Coons asked Musk to preserve company records from September 1 onward and to confirm by November 30. Senate Democrats also plan a Foreign Relations Committee investigation once they hold the majority, per Militarnyi. The Pentagon inspector general declined a bipartisan request to investigate, saying the questions belong with the Air Force and Space Force.

For SpaceX, the risk is concrete: Starshield and related national-security work are a growing revenue line, and an authorization-bill freeze would land on programs the military is counting on. CNN said it contacted SpaceX for comment; no response appeared in the coverage reviewed here.
