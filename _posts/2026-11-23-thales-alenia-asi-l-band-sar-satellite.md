Thales Alenia Space has been picked by the Italian Space Agency (ASI) to build an L-band synthetic aperture radar (SAR) Earth observation satellite, according to a French-language financial wire report dated 17 November. The company, a joint venture owned 67% by Thales and 33% by Leonardo, will act as prime contractor and take responsibility for the whole system, payload included.

## What is being built

The report gives the satellite a launch mass of roughly 2.2 tonnes and a deployable antenna more than 26 metres across. It will fly in a sun-synchronous orbit and provide systematic global coverage as well as on-demand imaging. The onboard electronics and the antenna are being developed in-house.

L-band radar uses a longer wavelength than the X- and C-band sensors Italy already operates, which lets it see through vegetation more effectively. The intended uses listed are tracking ground deformation and geological risk, measuring biomass and soil moisture, and environmental monitoring and territorial security.

## Why it matters

The mission is described as filling a continuity gap: it complements Italy's X- and C-band radar capabilities and is meant to carry on L-band data from the SAOCOM satellites. For Thales Alenia it is another institutional win in a busy month for the company, which also signed a Malaysian satcom memorandum of understanding recently (see [our earlier coverage](/2026/11/20/thales-alenia-weststar-malaysia-satcom-mou/)).

The wire report did not disclose a contract value, the satellite's name, a launch date or a launch provider. A German financial news page published the same day mentioned a Thales Alenia Space contract worth more than €240 million, but nothing read for this article ties that figure to this award, so treat any link between the two as unconfirmed. Thales shares were down about 1.1% at roughly €227 in Paris shortly before 4 p.m. on the day of the report.
