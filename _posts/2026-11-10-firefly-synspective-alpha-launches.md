Firefly Aerospace announced on November 10 that it has signed a multi-launch agreement with Synspective, the Japanese operator of a synthetic aperture radar (SAR) satellite constellation, covering up to 10 Alpha launches through 2030.

Each mission will carry two Synspective SAR satellites, so the full deal would put up to 20 satellites into orbit. Alpha is rated for 1,000 kilograms to low Earth orbit, and flying two spacecraft per rocket roughly doubles Synspective's deployment pace compared with single-satellite launches, according to the release. That should speed up growth of its StriX constellation, which is used to watch for ground changes after disasters such as earthquakes and floods and to track natural resources and urban development.

Launches would fly from Pad-0A at NASA's Wallops Flight Facility in Virginia and from Space Launch Complex 2 at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, which gives access to a range of orbital inclinations.

Firefly CEO Jason Kim said the agreement reflects "growing customer confidence in our Alpha Block II vehicle." Synspective founder and CEO Motoyuki Arai said Alpha's payload class suits the company's upcoming satellites.

The announcement came the same day Firefly reported third-quarter results. Secondary coverage of that day's releases also lists an extension of Lockheed Martin's multi-launch agreement for up to 25 Alpha launches through 2031, and a deal with SSC Space for two commercial Alpha launches from Esrange in Sweden, described as Firefly's first European launch contracts. We have not read those releases ourselves. The same coverage cites a record backlog of $1.7 billion at quarter end.

Multi-launch agreements with "up to" language are options, not firm orders, and the release does not state a contract value. Still, steady Alpha bookings matter for a vehicle that needs flight cadence to justify its Block II upgrades, and a small-satellite radar customer with its own constellation plan is a natural fit.
