ISRO launched the NVS-03 navigation satellite on a GSLV from the Second Launch Pad at Sriharikota on the night of Friday, October 30, placing it in its intended geosynchronous transfer orbit. It was the 20th flight of the Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle, according to the reports.

The Deccan Chronicle gives the liftoff time as 11:51 p.m. and the satellite's mass as 2,250 kilograms; All India Radio puts it at about 2,232 kilograms. The satellite will use its own propulsion to climb from transfer orbit to its working position, roughly 35,800 kilometers up.

## What NVS-03 adds

NVS-03 serves NavIC, India's regional navigation system, which covers the country and about 1,500 kilometers beyond its borders. ISRO says the satellite is meant to keep the service continuous and improve its availability, with uses spanning civilian, strategic and security applications.

Technically, it carries an indigenous atomic clock alongside procured clocks, and adds an L1 navigation signal (1575.42 MHz) to the L5 and S-band signals the constellation already broadcasts. L1 is the band most consumer GPS receivers listen on, so the addition matters for getting NavIC into ordinary devices. All India Radio notes that seven satellites are planned for the constellation, and that integration with mobile systems for wider public use depends on completing it.

The sources disagree on numbering: the radio report calls NVS-03 the third satellite of the second-generation series but also quotes ISRO Chairman V. Narayanan describing it as the fourth launched under the programme, and does not reconcile the two.

## Cadence

All India Radio says the 56 days between this and the previous GSLV mission is the shortest turnaround yet for the vehicle. Narayanan said the satellite was "precisely injected into its intended transfer orbit." Prime Minister Narendra Modi congratulated the teams and called the mission an important step toward strengthening NavIC.

Neither source said when NVS-03 would reach its final orbit.
