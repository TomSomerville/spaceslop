PLD Space has begun system-level testing of its Miura 5 launch vehicle after finishing manufacture of its first three vehicles, according to a 19 October report from SatNews that attributes the announcement to the company. PLD's own news page could not be loaded when this article was prepared, so the figures below come from that report and haven't been checked against a PLD release. Two qualification units and the first flight vehicle are being built at the same time.

## The numbers

The report, datelined Elche, Spain, where PLD has a 17,000 square metre facility, lists a fair amount of hardware progress:

- 13 propellant tanks completed, including liquid oxygen tanks for both stages
- avionics sets for four rockets, and two fairing qualification models tested
- initial flight units of the TEPREL-C and TEPREL-C Vac engines in test campaigns, with seven engines assembled, 35 combustion chambers, 40 gas generators and 15 turbopumps produced
- 10 in-house test benches at Teruel, including a 10.2-metre vertical stand with an eight-metre flame duct

The report also cites 493 suppliers and €42.7 million in local contracts, and ties the industrial ramp-up to a €288 million Series C round and PLD's selection under ESA's European Launcher Challenge for Miura 5 scaling.

## Kourou and the schedule

About 60 containers of ground equipment have reached the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, the report says, to validate pad strongbacks, hold-down clamps and umbilical interfaces under a partnership with CNES, the French space agency.

On timing, the report is inconsistent, giving the maiden flight as "first half of 2027" in one place and "early 2027" in another. Either way, it is a slip from earlier expectations of a 2026 debut. The next test is whether the integrated vehicles behave as the components did; component counts say little about that, and system tests are where integration problems usually surface.
