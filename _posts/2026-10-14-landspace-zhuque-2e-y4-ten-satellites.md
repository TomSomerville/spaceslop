LandSpace launched an improved Zhuque-2 rocket, designated Zhuque-2E Y4, on 14 October at 19:40 Beijing time, carrying ten satellites on a single rideshare flight. The company's own account calls the mission a complete success and describes it as the tenth flight of the Zhuque-2 series. It lifted off from LandSpace's methane/liquid-oxygen pad at the Dongfeng Commercial Aerospace Innovation Pilot Zone.

## The payloads

Eight of the satellites belong to Guoxing Aerospace: Star Era 39 and Star Era 44 through 50. LandSpace lists their test objectives as high-performance computing payloads, high-speed laser links between satellites and to the ground, and large-area radiative cooling wings.

The other two, Guangchuan 03 and 04, were built by Lantower Intelligence, a satellite company associated with LandSpace. They are next-generation laser data-transmission satellites meant to prove out constellation networking, validating a highly integrated onboard processing unit, large-aperture high-speed laser communication, an integrated laser routing module and a new solar array power system.

## Where it fits

The payload mix reflects a theme running through Chinese commercial launch: space-based computing and optical networking are being tested on small satellites before any large constellation commits to them. LandSpace says it is moving toward routine commercial launches of the improved Zhuque-2 and toward reusability validation for Zhuque-3. Through Lantower it also wants to build constellation capabilities, tying rocket and satellite work together to serve China's satellite internet ambitions.

The company's news page lists other recent milestones that this post does not detail: a Zhuque-2E launch on 15 September described as the vehicle's first scaled constellation-deployment mission, and a Zhuque-3 reusable rocket flight on 19 August whose headline reports orbit and recovery success. SpaceSlop has not independently verified those, and the Zhuque-3 claim in particular should be checked against further reporting. LandSpace's own account of the 14 October launch is the only source for this article; no independent coverage was found.
