SWISSto12 announced on November 9 that it has passed critical design review (CDR) for the S-band and Ka-band phased array antennas it is building for UNIVITY's 5G non-terrestrial network demonstration satellites. The milestone clears the project to start flight hardware production, followed by verification and acceptance testing.

The pair of antennas is what lets a single satellite offer two services. The S-band direct radiating array supports direct-to-device service, connecting standard 5G phones to the satellite, while the 26 GHz Ka-band array handles broadband and feeder links. The work is part of UNIVITY's uniShape program, backed by France 2030 and the French space agency CNES.

## How they are built

The antennas belong to SWISSto12's HummingLink product line. The passive radiating front ends are 3D printed, with radiating elements that integrate miniaturised, low-loss filters. The company also designs and builds the beamforming circuit boards and provides the digital controls, all in Switzerland and using proprietary designs.

SWISSto12 founder and CEO Emile de Rijk said UNIVITY "set us an ambitious schedule" and called reaching CDR in under a year a significant milestone. UNIVITY CEO Charles Delfieux said active antennas are central to its 5G payload architecture and support shared space-based infrastructure for telecom operators. UNIVITY is developing a very low Earth orbit constellation that lets operators offer broadband using their own 5G spectrum.

The release also gives a snapshot of the supplier: SWISSto12 says it was founded in 2011, has delivered payloads for several LEO constellations and seven HummingSat GEO platforms, reported $140 million in revenue for 2025 and holds over $500 million in contracted orders. Those are company figures. A passed CDR is a paper milestone, not an in-orbit result, but with hardware now going into production the schedule is the thing to watch.
