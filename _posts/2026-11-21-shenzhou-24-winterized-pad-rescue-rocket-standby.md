Chinese state media has detailed how Jiuquan is being readied for Shenzhou-24, the crewed flight due to carry the first foreign visitor to the Tiangong station. The launch date is still unannounced: the China Manned Space Agency says only that the mission will fly "at an appropriate time", and Global Times describes it as scheduled for the coming days.

## Cold-weather changes

Global Times reports that the Long March 2F rocket and spacecraft were rolled to the launch area on 17 November, a roughly 1.5 km trip that took about 90 minutes. Both are described as in good condition.

With winter conditions at Jiuquan, the launch team has added enclosed protective structures, heating and insulation, and temperature control around the propellant tanks and engines. Procedures have also been altered so the tower's rotating platforms are opened later, limiting how long the vehicle sits exposed to the cold.

The tower itself has had upgrades. About 20 voltage-stabilization devices were added for steadier power, and propellant filling pumps more than two decades old were replaced, according to the report.

## Rescue vehicle ready

The report also says a Long March 2F (Y25) and a Shenzhou-25 spacecraft are standing by in the vertical assembly building. If the Shenzhou-24 crew ran into trouble in orbit, the pair could be launched quickly as a rescue mission. Having a second crewed vehicle on call is a safeguard China has publicised for recent crew rotations.

## What is known about the crew

A Pakistani astronaut will fly as a payload specialist, the first foreign national to visit the Chinese space station, and will return to Earth on Shenzhou-23 after a short stay. Global Times says the report does not name the other crew members. Two Pakistani candidates have been widely reported, but China has not said who will fly, and crew announcements typically come shortly before launch.

For the background on the Pakistani flight and the handover plan, see our [earlier article](/2026/11/17/shenzhou-24-pakistani-astronaut/). Nothing in the material we read changes that picture; the new detail is the extent of the pad preparations for a launch in cold conditions.
