German launch startup HyImpulse has fired its HyPLOX-90 hybrid rocket motor with a thrust vector control (TVC) system attached for the first time. The test took place on 16 November and was disclosed in a company update on 27 November, according to European Spaceflight.

TVC is the hardware that swings the thrust direction so a rocket can steer itself while its engines burn. It is a prerequisite for an orbital vehicle, and earlier HyPLOX-90 firings did not include it. The motor is a 90-kilonewton design that burns paraffin wax with liquid oxygen, and it first ran in a hot-fire in 2024.

The test was run at a new site that HyImpulse described only as being in the "Mediterranean region". The company did not name the location.

## What it means for SL1

The motor is the building block for SL1, HyImpulse's three-stage orbital launcher, which is designed to carry up to 600 kg to low Earth orbit. In October 2025 the company said SL1 should begin delivering payloads in 2027.

HyImpulse would not commit to fresh dates this time. Asked about integrated testing and the maiden launch, it said it is progressing launch preparations and continuing regulatory, operational and campaign work with partners, and that further details on the timeline will be communicated once available. In rocket-startup language, that usually means the schedule is still moving.

The company's smaller suborbital rocket, SR75, is also running later than planned. It flew for the first time on 3 May 2024 from Koonibba Test Range in South Australia. Its second flight, planned from SaxaVord Spaceport in Scotland, has slipped from late 2026 to spring 2027, European Spaceflight reports.

Hybrid motors, which pair a solid fuel with a liquid oxidizer, are simpler and safer to handle than most liquid engines, which is the appeal. The trade-off is that hybrids have historically been harder to scale to the thrust levels an orbital rocket needs, so a working TVC on a 90-kN motor is an incremental but necessary milestone. HyImpulse is one of several European small-launcher developers competing for institutional and commercial payloads, with SaxaVord as its planned UK launch site.
