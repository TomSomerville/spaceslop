Virgin Galactic has a new customer on its 2027 manifest: Teachers in Space, a nonprofit that trains educators to build and fly their own experiments. According to a Virgin Galactic announcement dated November 18, the group plans to send two teachers on a research flight that is "expected in 2027." No specific date or flight window was given.

The announcement does not say what the flight costs or how the seats were arranged, and it does not describe the vehicle. Virgin Galactic's own materials elsewhere on the site point to its next-generation ships, and the company recently flew its first, Apeiron, to Spaceport America, with customer flights currently expected in early Q2 2027 (see our [earlier coverage](/2026/10/28/virgin-galactic-apeiron-spaceport-america/)). Whether the teachers fly on that schedule is not stated.

## What the teachers will fly

Teachers in Space, a 501(c)(3), says its teachers have done microgravity research on parabolic "zero-G" flights since 2016. This would be the first time its teachers run experiments in space itself. The payload is a 3D-printed CubeSat-style unit of roughly 3.5 by 3.5 inches built around an Arduino microcontroller, with sensors for temperature, pressure, acceleration, rotation, magnetic field, carbon dioxide, humidity and light. Data returns to the classrooms that designed the experiments.

The nonprofit says it has trained more than 800 teachers, reaching an estimated 70,000 students. It is raising $2 million for its full educational program and mission, and says $285,000 has been raised so far.

## Why it matters

Mike Moses, president of Spaceline at Virgin Galactic, said the company's mission is "to fly people whose work in space will make a difference back on Earth."

Liz Kennick, CEO of Teachers in Space, said that "putting a teacher in space is the natural evolution of our program."

The 2027 timing depends on Virgin Galactic keeping its own schedule. Until the company confirms a customer-flight start date for the new ships and the nonprofit finishes fundraising, the teachers' seats are best read as a plan rather than a booking with a date.
