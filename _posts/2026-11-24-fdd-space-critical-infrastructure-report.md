A new report from the Foundation for Defense of Democracies (FDD), a Washington think tank, argues that Congress should formally designate space-based systems as the 17th U.S. critical infrastructure sector. Payload reported the findings on November 24.

The report's case is that services such as positioning, navigation and timing, weather forecasting and communications already underpin American life and national security, yet policy treats space differently from the electric grid or the financial system. "In practice, American space architecture already functions as critical infrastructure," it says, according to Payload.

## The threat picture

FDD says China and Russia are developing and testing ways to deny the United States access to space. Those include nonkinetic methods such as jamming and spoofing, and physical ones such as anti-satellite missiles and maneuvering spacecraft. The report suggests the most damaging scenario may not be a kinetic attack on satellites at all, but a prolonged, hard-to-attribute campaign of interference.

## What it recommends

Beyond the designation itself, the report asks Congress to:

- make NASA the lead agency for space infrastructure and require it to produce a space risk management plan;
- set cybersecurity standards for systems that support national security space missions; and
- require federal agencies to run exercises on operating in a degraded space environment.

FDD argues a designation would clarify federal responsibilities, improve coordination between commercial operators and government, strengthen resilience standards and help keep services running in a crisis.

Lawmakers have tried a version of this before. Payload notes that the 2025 Space Infrastructure Act did not advance past committee, so the report revives an idea that has yet to find legislative traction. The recommendations are those of a think tank, not of Congress or the administration.
