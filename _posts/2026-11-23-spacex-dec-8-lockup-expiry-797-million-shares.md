The final tranche of SpaceX's 180-day IPO lock-up ends on Dec. 8, when up to 797.6 million shares become eligible for sale, according to an analysis from TIKR citing the company's prospectus.

That is about 5.9% of the 13.58 billion shares outstanding as of Oct. 15, and it is larger than the 638.9 million shares SpaceX sold in the offering itself. At the Nov. 17 close of $141.70, TIKR values the block at roughly $113 billion.

## A staged release

Dec. 8 is the last step in a sequence rather than a single cliff. Shares have been freed in stages since August, so a good part of the early-holder base has already been able to sell. Eligibility is not the same as selling: insiders are not obliged to sell when a lock-up lapses, though markets tend to treat the date warily.

One caveat on the numbers: other coverage of the same date cites different tranche sizes, which may reflect how the stages are counted. The 797.6 million figure here is the one TIKR attributes to the prospectus.

## Context

TIKR says the stock has fallen about 54% from its June high to its low. A much larger overhang remains after December: about 7.8 billion shares, including Elon Musk's stake, are under longer lock-ups, and Musk's stake stays locked until June 2027.

TIKR also ties the date to operations. It notes SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage on Nov. 14, although the booster catch failed, and that Musk has guided to weekly Starship flights in the first half of 2027. Whether the flight cadence arrives on that schedule is a separate question from whether a large block of stock changes hands in the meantime, but the two now share a calendar.

The sourcing here is a single analysis piece, so the share counts should be read as that outlet's reading of the prospectus rather than a figure confirmed by SpaceX.
