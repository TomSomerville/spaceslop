A Long March 2D rocket launched the Yaogan 49 group 01 satellites from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center in southwest China on 26 November, lifting off at 09:38 UTC (5:38 p.m. Beijing time) from Launch Complex 3.

Chinese state media, citing the China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC), said the satellites entered their planned orbit. Both Xinhua and China News described the mission as the 681st flight of the Long March family.

## What the satellites do

Xinhua said the satellites will serve scientific experiments, land and resource surveys, crop-yield estimates and disaster prevention. Launch trackers, however, list the Yaogan series payload as reconnaissance satellites of unknown function, and the official reports reviewed here do not say how many satellites flew or give their orbit. China News said the Fifth Academy led the satellites' development. The purpose beyond the official description is unconfirmed.

Yaogan ("remote sensing") is the name China uses for a long-running series of Earth-observation satellites that are widely assumed to include military reconnaissance spacecraft. The previous Yaogan launch in the current record, Yaogan 51-07 to 09, flew on a Long March 2C in early November.

## Context

The Long March 2D is a workhorse for small and medium payloads and has flown several times this month, including Shijian 24 on 18 November and the CAS Pathfinder-1 mission on 11 November. China's launch cadence continues with a Long March 6A and a Long March 6C both listed from Taiyuan in the coming days, though payload details for those remain to be announced.
