A day after ICEYE launched its all-cash bid for Avio, the Italian government has made clear it intends to weigh in. Defense Minister Guido Crosetto said the state's golden-power rules, which let Rome block or attach conditions to foreign takeovers of strategic assets, will apply to the offer, according to reports of his remarks on 18 November.

Crosetto also pointed at Leonardo, the defense group that holds about 18% of Avio and is partly state-controlled. "If I were Leonardo, I would think carefully before exiting," he said, as quoted by Global Banking & Finance. Leonardo's chief executive, Lorenzo Mariani, had earlier said he was "pleasantly surprised" by the bid, and the company has described it as a good sign while saying any decision must be coordinated with the government. A Leonardo board meeting to examine the offer is expected soon.

## Where the bid stands

As covered in our [earlier article](/2026/11/17/iceye-bids-for-avio/) on the offer, ICEYE is paying €41 per share, valuing Avio at roughly €2.05 billion, and says the deal would take a few more months to close. A person close to the matter told reporters that Rome had not yet received formal notification, so no assessment has begun. A second official said the government plans to take all the necessary steps.

Markets are leaning cautiously optimistic. Avio shares rose about 1.25% to €39.67 in Milan on Wednesday according to MarketScreener, still below the offer price, a gap that suggests investors see some risk of conditions or a block. Analysts at Equita told clients ICEYE has a good chance of winning authorization and read the government's silence on Tuesday as a form of tacit approval. They also don't rule out Leonardo keeping a stake to preserve an Italian presence in the company.

## Precedent and what to watch

Golden power has teeth. This year the government allowed Advent and Nextalia to take over Tinexta only on condition that its defense arm stayed in national hands; the state-backed lender CDP ultimately bought it. A similar carve-out, or a Leonardo stake that stays put, would be the likeliest compromise for Avio, which builds the Vega family of launchers and is expanding with a plant in Virginia due for completion in late 2028.

The bid also needs approvals involving France, the UK and the United States, plus EU foreign-subsidy clearance. For now the question is Leonardo: its decision on its 18% shapes both the tender's chances and how much leverage Rome ends up with.
