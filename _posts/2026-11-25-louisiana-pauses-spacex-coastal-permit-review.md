Louisiana has hit pause on the state permit for SpaceX's planned development in Vermilion Parish, saying it lacks the environmental information it needs to continue.

In a Nov. 23 letter, the Louisiana Department of Conservation and Energy told SpaceX it "cannot continue processing the company's permit application until additional information is submitted." That came less than a week after the application was declared administratively complete. SpaceX has 30 days from the letter to respond, after which the state will resume processing, though it may ask follow-up questions. This is not a rejection, and no final decision has been made on permit P20260831.

## A second letter to the Army Corps

In a separate Nov. 23 letter, the state told the Army Corps of Engineers the project is inconsistent with Louisiana's Coastal Resources Program because the application lacks enough information, and directed the Corps not to issue a federal Section 404 (Clean Water Act) permit until the issues are resolved. If they are still open six months after the review began, the state would formally notify the Corps of the inconsistency.

## What the state wants

The development includes a Gulf Coast wharf near Freshwater Bayou, inland barge facilities, canal dredging and a road linking to regional highways. Construction would excavate about 5.2 million cubic yards of material and use millions more as fill. The site sits within a quarter mile of a sensitive feature monitored by the state wildlife department.

Regulators asked for wetland impacts broken down by habitat type, plans to restore disturbed areas and construction canals and access roads, erosion prevention, protection of existing shoreline work, and whether dredging could worsen erosion or hurt drainage and boat access. They also flagged inconsistencies in the construction drawings.
