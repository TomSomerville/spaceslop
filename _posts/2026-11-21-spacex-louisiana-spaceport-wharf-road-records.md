SpaceX's proposed launch complex on the Louisiana coast is getting more concrete, and more contested, as records and permit filings show how large the build-out would be. Local reporting this week points to a public meeting on the wharf and road plans in Vermilion Parish.

## What is being built

Reporting by The Center Square describes a wharf of roughly 3,500 feet, meant to let large vessels dock and move cargo, along with hundreds of acres of industrial staging areas. The access network would include about 15.5 miles of heavy-haul road and more than a mile of elevated roadway. The article says extensive dredging would be needed for big ships, but gives no volume or depth. Several state and federal agency spokespeople declined to provide more information.

## What the state records show

Records obtained by WWNO and the Gulf States Newsroom add scale. Louisiana officials referred to a $100 billion spaceport. An April briefing from Louisiana Economic Development, the state's business agency, said the first phase could involve at least $20 billion in capital investment, potentially rising to $50 billion, and at least 3,000 jobs, centered on what officials called the world's largest launch site. The project carried the code name "Project Osprey."

An earlier March briefing listed building a new city in Vermilion Parish among the things needed to win the project. Nearly all detail was redacted, and the April update dropped the explicit reference. WWNO said it is unclear whether the idea was abandoned or moved elsewhere; the state agency and SpaceX did not comment. The day after the March briefing, a state official circulated a presentation on Starbase, Texas, the SpaceX-linked city incorporated in 2025. The records do not say Louisiana intended to copy it.

The primary and secondary sites named in the March briefing are owned by ExxonMobil and carry a 100-year hunting lease held by a local duck hunting club, per the records.

## Friction over process

Dozens of state and local officials signed nondisclosure agreements, WWNO reported, and more than 100 state employees did so with the governor's office. Residents have sued two Vermilion Parish bodies alleging Open Meetings Law violations, and a separate lawsuit challenges a police jury settlement with ExxonMobil. The governor's office says the settlement was discussed in executive session and approved in open session.

## Why it matters

SpaceX already operates the Starbase launch site in Texas, and Starship needs more pads and cargo logistics if its flight rate is to rise. A site in Louisiana would add a second Gulf Coast hub, but the permits, the Army Corps of Engineers sign-offs and the local politics are all unresolved. The records do not show whether residents had any chance to comment on the broader plan before it was announced, which is shaping how the permit process is received.
