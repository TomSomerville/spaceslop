RBC Signals, the Redmond, Washington company that rents out access to a worldwide network of satellite ground stations, announced a $10 million strategic growth investment on Thursday, led by Washington Harbour Partners. The Washington, D.C. firm invests in national security, defense technology and cybersecurity companies. No other investors were named.

RBC sells ground station as a service: operators of satellites book antenna time instead of building their own dishes. It also leases spectrum dynamically. Its customers span civil, defense and national security agencies as well as commercial constellation operators.

## Where the money goes

The company says the cash is aimed at two things: growing its government services business, including pursuing federal contracts, and commercializing newer capabilities. Those include intersatellite links, optical ground communications, space domain awareness and spectrum monitoring, along with higher-frequency and optical communications work.

Ron Faith, RBC's president and CEO, described the goal as "a global, secure network of networks" and called the company a dual-use platform serving both national defense and commercial space. Mina Faltas, founder and CEO of Washington Harbour Partners, said RBC has built a differentiated global network able to meet complex commercial and national security requirements.

## Context

The round follows a busy year. In 2025 RBC acquired 10 S- and X-band tracking antennas from Microsoft and was picked as an antenna provider for the Space Force's Joint Antenna Marketplace. In August 2026 Blue Origin selected it to support the global deployment of Blue Origin's Quartz ground network, handling site surveys, antenna procurement, deployment and hosting, according to a separate report. RBC has also started an optical ground station collaboration with Cailabs.

The Blue Origin tie is the one to watch. Antennas RBC deploys for Quartz are slated to join the RBC network, making unused capacity available to its other customers, per the company. The announcement did not say when the investment closes or give a valuation.
