Quantum Space says it is part of a team that NASA has awarded $185 million under its Moon Base Transportation program. The company announced on November 7, 2026 that it will provide orbital transfer for the effort, with its Ranger vehicle set to deliver two NASA CAPSTONE-2 spacecraft.

## The team and the work

The award is a block buy of multiple launch and in-space transportation services, issued as part of NASA's VADR contracts within the Commercial Access to Space Program. Arrow Science and Technology is the prime contractor and mission integrator. SpaceX's Falcon 9 provides the launch, and Quantum Space provides the orbital transfer leg. NASA's goal for the broader program is a sustained American presence at the lunar south pole, and the awards were announced at Kennedy Space Center by NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and Moon Base Program Manager Carlos García-Galán.

Quantum Space is a subcontractor to Arrow, and the release says the subcontract terms are still being finalized. It puts the company's share at about 25% of the total contract value, which works out to roughly $46 million on the $185 million figure. That number is an inference from the release rather than a stated contract value.

Ranger is described as built for cislunar operations, with payload flexibility, support for multiple payloads and in-orbit refuelling and resupply. The release is candid that Ranger is still in development and has not yet been manufactured, operated or sold, so the award is for a vehicle that has yet to fly.

## Context

"America is going back to the Moon to stay, and staying takes reliable transportation between the Earth and the Moon," said Jim Bridenstine, Quantum Space's CEO. Arrow founder and CEO Jim Baker said the team is "establishing a repeatable and cost-effective blueprint for the future of lunar exploration."

The award lands while Quantum Space is mid-way through going public. The SEC declared its registration statement for a business combination with Inflection Point Acquisition Corp. VI effective on October 28, 2026, according to the release, and the company has since announced a virtual analyst and investor day. A NASA contract tied to its first lunar-capable vehicle adds a concrete customer to that story, though the subcontract's final terms remain open.
