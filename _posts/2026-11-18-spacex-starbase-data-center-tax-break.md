Cameron County commissioners in South Texas turned down SpaceX's request for a property tax incentive on Tuesday, stalling the company's plan for a roughly $24 billion natural gas power plant and data center next to Starbase. According to the RGV Business Journal, the vote was unusually split, and it came after several hours of discussion in executive session. Commissioners also voted against giving public notice for a hearing on the deal, which had been set for December 29. Local outlet ValleyCentral reported the tally as 3–2 against.

## What SpaceX asked for

SpaceX filed its request on October 15 and revised it on November 9. The project would sit on 444 acres that the city of Brownsville recently removed from its city limits. In material presented to the county, SpaceX estimated a first-year taxable value of $13.8 billion and 500 new regional jobs.

The county's refusal is only one of two local tracks. A separate city notice dated November 6 shows SpaceX also asking the city of Starbase for a tax exemption under Chapter 312 of the Texas Tax Code, plus an economic development agreement, for what it calls a "super-compute facility" and a gas-fired plant. Yahoo Finance reports that the Starbase City Commission is due to consider that package on December 9, and that nothing has been approved. The city notice does not say how large the exemption would be or how long it would run, nor the plant's generating capacity, the scale of the computing, or who would use it. Yahoo Finance reports that SpaceX has not commented on the request.

## Why it matters

This is a launch company's home-base politics colliding with its pivot to power and computing. The site sits inside the Cameron County Enterprise Zone, between Boca Chica Boulevard and the Port of Brownsville. A state air filing cited by Yahoo Finance, originally reported by The 956 Report, covers 35 temporary gas turbines of about 748 megawatts, to be replaced later by eight permanent turbines of about 708 megawatts. The same report notes a SpaceX affiliate has proposed an eight-mile gas pipeline to supply Starship propellant production.

The local politics are also not simple. Yahoo Finance notes that voters rejected data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, and that Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has said she would ban new data center projects until lawmakers pass rules. It adds that a city vote would not override a state ban or the state air permit.

SpaceX's earlier county tax abatements have lapsed, so this is the first test of whether the county will extend fresh incentives to a company that is now far larger than when it first set up in Boca Chica. The company's response to the county's vote, beyond the commission's action itself, was not available in the sources used for this report. The next real signal is the Starbase commission's December 9 meeting.
