Google has signed a launch services agreement with Blue Origin to fly Project Suncatcher satellites on New Glenn, with a target of the first quarter of 2029. Google announced the deal on November 19 in a blog post by Travis Beals, a senior director at the company.

The satellites are headed for sun-synchronous orbit, and Google says each carries roughly the compute capacity of a data center rack. Google described the mission as the first heavy-lift launch for Suncatcher and "a crucial milestone on our long-term roadmap." The post does not say how many satellites will fly, what the deal is worth or how many launches it covers.

## What Suncatcher is

Suncatcher is Google's long-term effort to scale AI infrastructure in space, using the near-constant solar power available in orbit. Its satellites carry Google's Tensor Processing Units, the company's AI chips. According to Crypto Briefing, a prototype with four TPUs reached orbit on October 1, 2026, on a mission facilitated by Planet Labs, and ran limited AI inference as expected. That is a single outlet's account; Google's post says only that the new launch builds on work with other satellite and launch partners.

The same report points to the hard problems: shedding heat without air to carry it away, and keeping fast, reliable links between satellites. It also notes that SpaceX is pursuing a comparable space-computing effort called Starmind. Google has announced no timeline for an operational constellation beyond the 2029 launch.

## Why it matters for Blue Origin

For Blue Origin, this is a marquee commercial customer for New Glenn at a time when the rocket's launch cadence is the open question for every customer on its manifest. A date more than two years out leaves room for that cadence to improve, but it is a target, not a guarantee. Orbital data centers have become a crowded field of announcements; a signed launch contract with a heavy-lift provider is a more concrete step than most.
