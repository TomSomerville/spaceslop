Arianespace launched its tenth Ariane 6 on Tuesday, sending 36 Amazon Leo satellites toward low Earth orbit on a mission designated VA271, or LE-04 in Amazon's numbering. Liftoff came from the Guiana Space Centre at 8:14 a.m. Kourou time (1114 UTC), at the very end of the launch window, according to Spaceflight Now's live coverage. At the time of that report the rocket was in its first coast phase, and satellite deployment had not yet been confirmed. We will follow up once Arianespace publishes a result.

## A day late

The countdown was originally set for Nov. 23 but was halted by a ground-system fault before the gantry was rolled back. We covered that scrub earlier; the rocket and payload were reported safe, and the attempt moved a day.

## Why this flight matters to Amazon

This was the fourth Arianespace flight for the constellation and the fourth Ariane 6 flown in its most powerful Ariane 64 configuration, with four P160C solid boosters. If it succeeds, Arianespace will have delivered 136 Amazon Leo satellites in total, and the constellation will stand at 432 satellites in orbit across all launch providers, per Spaceflight Now.

The timing is awkward for Amazon. Nine days earlier, a ULA Vulcan failed to reach orbit and destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites, the first full mission failure in ULA's history, Spaceflight Now reports. Blue Origin's fourth New Glenn, which was meant to carry 48 satellites, was destroyed in a pad explosion during a static fire test with no satellites aboard, and Blue Origin now expects to return to flight in early 2027.

That leaves Ariane 6 as one of the few rockets currently available to Amazon. The company originally contracted Arianespace for 18 missions in 2022.

## Regulatory clock

The FCC exempted Amazon from its July 30, 2026 requirement to deploy half of the constellation, but the full first-generation system of 3,232 satellites is still due by July 30, 2029.

Spaceflight Now also notes that reported turbopump issues on the Ariane 6 main engine have caused schedule delays for the launcher program, without giving the length of the delay.
