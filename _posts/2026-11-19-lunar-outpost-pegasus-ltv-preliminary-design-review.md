Lunar Outpost has completed NASA's preliminary design review (PDR) for Pegasus, its Lunar Terrain Vehicle, according to a post on the company's website. The review comes four months after NASA's $220 million award to build the rover, and the company says it remains on schedule to hand NASA a flight-ready Pegasus in late 2027, ahead of a planned delivery to the Moon in 2028.

A PDR is the checkpoint where an agency confirms that a design meets its requirements before detailed engineering and hardware builds proceed. Lunar Outpost did not publish NASA's assessment, so the claim of a completed review comes from the company alone.

## What the company says it has done

Lunar Outpost says it built three Pegasus prototypes. NASA astronauts tested them in pressurized spacesuits at Johnson Space Center, judging design, usability and crew comfort. Separately, the company used NASA Glenn's SLOPE Laboratory to study soil mechanics, traction and mobility for the rover's spring mesh tires. A drivable Pegasus is due to be tested soon with a NASA crew, both with astronauts aboard and operated telerobotically, in a simulated lunar environment.

## The job ahead

Pegasus is meant to carry astronauts and cargo across the Moon's south polar terrain in support of NASA's Moon Base development and Artemis missions. Before crews arrive, the company says, it would be driven remotely from Earth to map the surface and gather data for Moon Base planning. It also lists scouting base sites, moving supplies and supporting science among its uses.

Founder and CEO Justin Cyrus said that "Building a lasting presence in space depends on our ability to turn ambitious plans into operational hardware at speed," and that Pegasus "will provide the mobility NASA Astronauts need to explore, work, and set the stage for Moon Base."

Lunar Outpost also says it plans ten lunar and cislunar missions before 2030, including a 2027 mission to Reiner Gamma, a site with a magnetic anomaly.

The company's post does not cover the wider LTV program. Reporting we found indicates NASA selected both Lunar Outpost and Astrolab in May 2026 for the first phase of lunar terrain vehicles, and that Astrolab's PDR was expected by the end of December, but we could not confirm that independently this run.
