Blue Origin used the UP.Summit conference at Kennedy Space Center to show two pieces of hardware that stretch New Glenn well beyond its current form: a third stage called GS3 and a new booster engine called Northstar. In the same news cycle, CEO Dave Limp pushed the rocket's return to flight from December 2026 to January 2027, according to a staff memo reported by CNBC.

## GS3: a hydrogen kick stage

GS3 is a liquid-hydrogen stage built around a single BE-7, the engine developed for Blue Origin's Blue Moon landers. It is designed to fit inside the interstage, so payloads keep the full fairing volume. Blue Origin claims vacuum specific impulse above 460 seconds, and quotes capacity of 8 metric tons to geostationary orbit or 10 tons to trans-lunar injection on the 7x2 configuration, rising to 14 and 20 tons on the larger 9x4 variant.

The stage uses a 5.5-meter composite liquid-hydrogen tank, which Limp said the company wasn't sure was feasible. A recent test reportedly held the tank at its full expected operating pressure for 2.5 hours and 150% of it for 10 minutes. Blue Origin says it would be the first composite hydrogen tank flown in space. A government customer has signed for a cislunar mission on the three-stage setup, targeted for 2028.

## Northstar replaces BE-4 on the booster

Northstar is a methane-fueled engine intended to take over from the BE-4 on New Glenn's first stage. Blue Origin says it has about half the parts and 30% less mass, with thrust rising from 640,000 to 715,000 pounds-force, a figure Limp called a starting point. More than 65% of it is 3D-printed in-house, and the company says it went from program approval to developmental testing in 11 months. Blue Origin puts production cost at roughly a quarter of the BE-4's but gave no dollar figures. No date has been set for a first Northstar flight. ULA will keep receiving BE-4s for Vulcan, and Blue Origin will keep building them.

## The schedule problem

The delay follows a May explosion during a ground test at Cape Canaveral that destroyed the third New Glenn first stage, and a summer of weather slowed the rebuild of Launch Complex 36. A hot-fire of the next upper stage is expected within one to two weeks at NASA's Stennis Space Center. New Glenn has flown three times. Blue Origin also announced a $330 million expansion of its Florida facilities, with capacity for up to 150 upper stages a year by summer 2027. Its backlog includes Amazon Leo satellites, NASA lunar lander missions and national-security payloads.

The announcements are a bet that demand will outlast the grounding. The details here come from a single outlet's report of the event, so figures such as the cost ratio are Blue Origin's own claims, not independently verified.
